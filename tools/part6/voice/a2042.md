@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
Running a workshop for 8 people is easy to plan; running it for 12 next month means redoing the arithmetic, and the mistakes are predictable: scissors scaled like paper, glue measured in bottles in one place and millilitres in another, a shortage nobody noticed until the morning.

This project builds a planner that keeps the arithmetic honest. Materials are either consumables (scale per person) or shared equipment (one per group of N). Units must match, or be converted with an explicit factor you supply. Shortages are computed against inventory, and anything that cannot be converted is listed as uncertain rather than guessed. A slider lets you try other participant counts with the same model.
:::
@@

@@end Step 2 · Scale consumables and shared equipment separately
:::visual Two kinds of scaling
```python run
import math
materials = [("paper", "consumable", 2, "sheet", None), ("scissors", "shared", 1, "pair", 3), ("glue", "consumable", 10, "ml", None)]
inventory = {"paper": (20, "sheet"), "scissors": (3, "pair"), "glue": (0.1, "l")}
convert = {("l", "ml"): 1000}
for n in (8, 12):
    print(f"--- {n} participants")
    for mid, kind, q, unit, share in materials:
        need = q * n if kind == "consumable" else q * math.ceil(n / share)
        have, hunit = inventory[mid]
        if hunit != unit:
            if (hunit, unit) not in convert: print(f"{mid}: need {need} {unit}, cannot convert {hunit} -> uncertain"); continue
            have = have * convert[(hunit, unit)]
        print(f"{mid:8} need {need:>5} {unit:5} have {have:>6} -> short {max(0, need - have)}")
```
:::

:::read
At 8 people: 16 sheets, ceil(8/3) = 3 pairs of scissors and 80 ml of glue, all covered (0.1 litre becomes 100 ml only because the factor 1000 was supplied). At 12: 24 sheets against 20 leaves 4 short, scissors need ceil(12/3) = 4 pairs against 3 (1 short), and glue needs 120 ml against 100 (20 short). Scaling scissors per person would have asked for 12 pairs.
:::

@@end Step 3 · Compute shortages and review uncertain conversions
:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ Shared equipment scales with the number of groups, rounded up.
- ✗ A bottle of glue can be assumed to hold 500 ml if no size is given.
  Without a supplied conversion the item is listed as uncertain.
- ✓ Exact decimal quantities avoid floating-point surprises such as 0.1 × 3 = 0.30000000000000004.
- ✗ The scenario slider uses a separate, simplified calculation.
  Every participant count is computed from the same materials model.

:::recap What to keep from this project
- Separate consumables (per person) from shared equipment (per group, rounded up).
- Units must match or convert by an explicit factor.
- Unconvertible items are uncertain, never guessed.
- Use exact decimals for quantities.
- One model drives every what-if scenario.

@@end Exercises
1. Why round shared equipment up rather than to the nearest whole number?
2. How would you add "one projector per room" for a workshop split across two rooms?
3. What should happen if inventory lists glue in "bottles"?

## Solutions

??? sol | 1. Rounding up
Eight people in groups of three is three groups, the last one of two. Rounding to the nearest gives 3 here, but at 7 people it gives 2 and leaves one person without scissors. Rounding up never does.
???

??? sol | 2. Per room
Add a third kind, "per room", scaled by an explicit room count in the plan, with its own share rule. Do not fake it as "shared, one per 15 people", which breaks when rooms are uneven.
???

??? sol | 3. Glue in bottles
Bottles and millilitres need a conversion factor (millilitres per bottle). Until someone supplies it, the item is reported as uncertain, with the reason, instead of guessing a bottle size.
???

??? sol | B1 · Projectors per person
Shared equipment must scale with groups (or rooms), not people. The project models it as `shared` with a `share` size, so 30 people would need ceil(30 / share) projectors.
???
