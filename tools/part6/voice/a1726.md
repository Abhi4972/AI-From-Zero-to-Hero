@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
Enterprise customers buy software only after checking it against **compliance frameworks**: rule sets for security and data handling. **SOC 2 Type II** shows an auditor watched your controls work for 6–12 months. **HIPAA** governs US health data and requires a signed **BAA** (business associate agreement) before patient data reaches any vendor, including your model provider. **GDPR** governs EU personal data. **PCI-DSS** covers card data. **ISO 27001** and the newer **ISO 42001** are international standards for security and AI governance. The **EU AI Act** classifies AI systems by risk; most chat and copilot products are "limited risk" (transparency duties), while hiring, credit or medical uses are "high risk" with documentation, logging and assessments. Many controls (access logs, change management, encryption, secrets) satisfy several frameworks at once, so you build each control once and map it.
:::

@@end Idea 9 · Numbers you should remember
:::worked Which frameworks does one deal need?
A US hospital network wants your LLM note-summarizer, used by its staff in the US and in its Dublin office.

1. Patient data → **HIPAA**: a BAA with you, and with your model provider (Bedrock, Azure OpenAI and Vertex offer one).
2. Dublin staff and EU patients → **GDPR**: redaction before inference, records of processing, data-subject rights.
3. B2B procurement → **SOC 2 Type II** (a Type I report will not pass).
4. EU AI Act: summarizing notes for clinicians is not one of the Annex III high-risk uses on its own, so most likely limited-risk transparency duties; if it informs diagnosis or triage decisions, check the medical-device route (Annex I, from August 2028).
5. One audit log, one access-control policy and one secrets vault serve all three frameworks' logging, access and secrets controls.
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ HIPAA data cannot go to an outside model provider without a BAA.
- ✗ Redacting personal data after the model answers satisfies GDPR.
  The model already saw it; redaction must happen before inference.
- ✓ One well-designed access-logging control maps to ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA and SOC 2 at once.
- ✗ EU AI Act high-risk obligations applied to all AI products from August 2026.
  Most chat products are limited-risk, and the high-risk dates were postponed to 2027 and 2028.

:::visual One control, many frameworks
```python run
controls = {
    "access logging": ["ISO 27001", "GDPR", "HIPAA", "SOC 2"],
    "change management": ["ISO 27001", "PCI DSS", "SOC 2"],
    "encryption in transit": ["ISO 27001", "GDPR", "HIPAA"],
    "secrets management": ["ISO 27001", "PCI DSS", "SOC 2"],
    "PII redaction before inference": ["GDPR", "HIPAA"],
}
covered = sum(len(f) for f in controls.values())
print(f"{len(controls)} controls satisfy {covered} framework requirements")
by_framework = {}
for control, frameworks in controls.items():
    for f in frameworks:
        by_framework.setdefault(f, []).append(control)
for f, cs in sorted(by_framework.items()):
    print(f"{f:10s} <- {', '.join(cs)}")
```
:::

:::read
Five controls cover fifteen framework requirements, three per control. Without mapping, each framework's audit would ask for its own evidence and teams would build parallel processes. Compliance platforms (Drata, Vanta, Secureframe) automate exactly this matrix, which is why they pay off once you hold more than two frameworks.
:::

## Solutions

??? sol | B1 · De-identified is a standard, not a feeling
HIPAA's de-identification standard (Safe Harbor) requires removing 18 identifier types, including all dates more specific than the year and any unique characteristic; rare diagnoses plus dates can re-identify patients. The notes were still patient data, so they needed a BAA with the provider (or proper de-identification, verified), plus redaction before the call.
