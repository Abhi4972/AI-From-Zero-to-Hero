@@top Problem
:::plain In plain words first
This lesson starts a track that builds a layered **safety gate** for an assistant, and it begins with naming. A **taxonomy** sorts jailbreak attempts by the **trust boundary** they abuse: the assistant's persona (**role-play**), the system prompt's authority (**instruction override**), the line between data and instructions (**context smuggling**), the conversation history (**multi-turn ramp**), the surface form of words (**encoding tricks**), and the model's next-token tendency (**prefix injection**). Each labelled example also gets a **severity**. Labels do not stop attacks, but they turn a stream of incidents into a histogram, and a histogram shows where defences are missing. All examples here are harmless fixtures for testing.
:::
@@

@@end Concept
:::worked From incidents to coverage
A week of flagged prompts: 40 instruction overrides, 25 role-plays, 20 encoding tricks, 10 prefix injections, 5 context smugglings.

1. Without labels: "100 jailbreak attempts this week". Nothing to act on.
2. With labels: the input filter catches 38 of 40 overrides but only 4 of 20 encoding tricks.
3. Coverage by category, attempts × miss rate, points at the gap: encoding tricks are 20% of attempts but the largest share of misses. That is next sprint's work.
4. Severity adds the other axis: a rare category with high-severity examples may still come first.
:::

:::check

:::visual Misses by category
```python run
attempts = {"instruction-override": (40, 38), "role-play": (25, 15), "encoding-trick": (20, 4),
            "prefix-injection": (10, 7), "context-smuggling": (5, 1)}           # (attempts, caught)
rows = sorted(((c, a - k, a, (a - k) / a) for c, (a, k) in attempts.items()), key=lambda r: -r[1])
for c, missed, a, rate in rows:
    print(f"{c:22s} missed {missed:2d} of {a:2d}  ({rate:.0%})")
```
:::

:::read
Encoding tricks lead with 16 misses, ahead of role-play's 10, although overrides are twice as common: the override filter works, the encoding coverage does not. Context smuggling shares the worst miss *rate* (80%) but on only five attempts, a signal to collect more examples before concluding. The histogram turns "we get jailbroken" into a ranked list of specific gaps.
:::

## Solutions

??? sol | B1 · A catch-all category
A catch-all hides which boundaries are attacked and which defences miss: 70% of the data shares one label, so the chart cannot point at a specific gap. Split it by the trust boundary each example abuses, and add a new category only when examples do not fit any existing one.
