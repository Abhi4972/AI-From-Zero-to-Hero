@@top Problem
:::plain In plain words first
Once an AI application is in production you need to see what it is doing: what each request cost and who made it, how slow it was, whether answers stay faithful to their sources, whether it leaks personal data, and whether the kind of questions people ask is shifting. An **observability dashboard** collects a **trace** for every request, in a standard format (OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions), stores it, runs **evaluation jobs** over a sample of traces, watches for **drift**, and raises **alerts**. The test of the whole system is concrete: inject a bug that makes the chatbot leak fake identity numbers in 1% of answers, and measure how quickly the dashboard notices. Product names are a 2026 snapshot.
:::

@@end Build it
:::math Population stability index
$$\text{PSI}=\sum_{b}\left(p_b-q_b\right)\ln\frac{p_b}{q_b}$$

Split a quantity (for example a prompt-topic cluster) into bins; $q_b$ is the share of the baseline in bin $b$ and $p_b$ the share this week. A common rule of thumb: below 0.1 little change, 0.1 to 0.25 moderate, above 0.25 large.
:::

:::worked Sampling and detection time
A bug makes 1% of responses leak PII. Traffic is 600 responses per minute; the evaluation job samples 10% of traces every 5 minutes.

1. Leaking responses per minute: 6. Sampled: 0.6 per minute, so about 3 per 5-minute window.
2. In one window, 3,000 responses arrive and 300 are sampled. Each sampled response leaks with probability 0.01, so the chance that the window's sample contains no leak at all is 0.99^300 ≈ 0.05.
3. So the first evaluation run catches the bug about 95% of the time; detection time is about one run interval, around 5 minutes.
4. Halve the sampling rate and P(no leak in a window) rises to 0.99^150 ≈ 0.22: detection becomes slower and less certain. The sampling rate is a direct trade between eval cost and time to detect.
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ Traces follow a shared schema so different SDKs land in the same tables.
- ✗ Evaluating every trace is the only reliable approach.
  Sampling, with all errored traces kept, is usually enough and much cheaper.
- ✓ PSI compares this period's distribution with a baseline.
- ✗ A low PSI means the model's answers are fine.
  PSI watches inputs or score distributions; quality needs its own evals.
:::

:::predict
Before running the code below: a baseline has prompt topics split 50/30/20 across three clusters; this week it is 40/30/30. Is the PSI above 0.1?

:::visual PSI from scratch
```python run
import math
def psi(current, baseline, eps=1e-6):
    return sum((p - q) * math.log((p + eps) / (q + eps)) for p, q in zip(current, baseline))

baseline = [0.50, 0.30, 0.20]
for name, cur in (("same mix", [0.50, 0.30, 0.20]), ("modest shift", [0.40, 0.30, 0.30]),
                  ("new topic dominates", [0.20, 0.20, 0.60])):
    print(f"{name:20s} PSI {psi(cur, baseline):.3f}")
```
:::

:::read
An unchanged mix scores 0; shifting ten points from one cluster to another gives about 0.06, under the usual 0.1 warning line; a new topic taking over gives about 0.76, a large shift. PSI is cheap and needs no labels, which is why it is the standard drift alarm, but it only says the inputs changed, not whether answers got worse.
:::

The dashboard ingests traces from three model families, attributes cost per user, raises a PII-leak alert (5 events, with the first trace id so an engineer can jump to it), and reports a PSI of 0.083, under the 0.1 drift line, so no drift alert. :::recap What to keep from this lesson
- Trace every request in a standard GenAI schema.
- Sample for evals, but keep all errored traces.
- Run faithfulness, toxicity and PII judges over samples; link results to traces.
- Watch drift with PSI on inputs and score trends.
- Measure time to detect with a deliberately injected regression.
:::

## Solutions

??? sol | P3 · Drift per application
Group traces by app id, compute each app's PSI against its own baseline, and plot them separately. A global PSI can stay flat while one small app drifts sharply, because its traffic is a small share of the total; per-app trails reveal it.
???

??? sol | B1 · Sampling away the evidence
Errors are the traces most worth keeping, and uniform 5% sampling throws 95% of them away. And a 1% PII leak is sampled at 0.05% of traffic, so detection is slow and may miss short bursts. Keep 100% of errors, raise sampling for high-risk apps, and run a cheap PII detector (regexes for identity-number formats) on every response, with the LLM judge on the sample.
???
