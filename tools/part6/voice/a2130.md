@@top The situation
:::plain In plain words first
After a benchmark misled a team, a new policy turned off prefix caching everywhere "for predictability". A customer who sends the same 2,000-token system prompt with every question saw first-token time triple. The cache was never what made the benchmark wrong; the benchmark's method was. The case trains fixing the cause of a misleading measurement instead of removing a useful feature.
:::
@@

@@before Walkthrough
:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ A warm cache can make a badly designed benchmark look faster.
- ✗ Disabling the cache in production makes benchmarks honest.
  The benchmark needed flushing and varied seeds; production needed the cache.
- ✓ On the GB10 the shared-prefix median first token rose from 691 ms to 2,542 ms without the cache.
:::
@@

@@end The lesson to carry
:::recap What to keep from this case
- Fix the measurement method, not the system being measured.
- Prefix caching is essential for repeated system prompts.
- Benchmarks should flush caches and vary seeds.
- Check who depends on a feature before removing it.
:::
@@
