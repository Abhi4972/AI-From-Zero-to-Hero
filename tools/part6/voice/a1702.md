@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
Below the big clouds sits a layer of **inference platforms**: companies whose whole business is running models fast and cheaply. Some build their own chips (Groq, Cerebras), most rent NVIDIA GPUs and tune the software (Fireworks, Together, Baseten, Modal, Anyscale), and some are marketplaces with thousands of models (Replicate). They price in different units. **Per-token** pricing charges for what you generate, so idle time is free. **Per-minute** or **per-second** pricing rents you a GPU, so you pay for idle time too but nothing extra for each token. To compare them you convert everything to one unit, usually dollars per day or dollars per million tokens, for *your* traffic. This lesson does that conversion and finds where one pricing model overtakes the other.
:::
@@

@@end Idea 10 · Numbers you should remember
:::math
**Per-token against a rented GPU.** A rented GPU costs $m$ dollars per minute and can produce $T$ tokens per minute. Per-token pricing costs $p$ dollars per token. At average utilization $u$, renting wins when

$$u \cdot T \cdot p > m \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u > \frac{m}{T \cdot p}$$

The crossover depends on how fast the GPU runs your model ($T$), not on a universal rule. A faster engine lowers the crossover.
:::

:::worked The crossover in `code/main.py`
The script uses a per-minute GPU at $0.55 a minute that produces 900,000 tokens a minute, against per-token pricing of $0.90 per million tokens.

1. Full use of the GPU for a minute on per-token pricing: 0.9 million × $0.90 = $0.81.
2. Crossover: 0.55 / 0.81 ≈ 0.68. The rented GPU wins only above about **68%** average utilization.
3. The lesson's rule of thumb says about 30%. Both are right for their inputs: with a slower model (say 400,000 tokens a minute), the crossover is 0.55 / 0.36, above 100%, so renting never wins; with a cheaper GPU or pricier tokens it drops towards 30%.

The lesson: do the division for your model, your throughput and your quotes.
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ Per-token pricing makes idle time free.
- ✗ Per-minute pricing is always cheaper at high volume.
  It is cheaper only when the GPU is busy enough; a busy *day* can still mean an idle GPU most minutes.
- ✓ Custom-chip providers (Groq, Cerebras) charge more per token but decode several times faster.
- ✗ A platform's "custom engine" is the main reason to pick it.
  Most production open-source serving runs on vLLM or SGLang; the real differences are developer experience, attribution and SLAs.
:::

:::predict
A service generates 2 million tokens a day, spread evenly. Before running the code below: is a rented GPU that can produce 900,000 tokens a minute anywhere near its crossover?
:::

:::visual Utilization of one rented GPU, from daily volume
```python run
TOK_PER_MIN = 900_000            # what one rented GPU can produce
GPU_PER_MIN, PER_TOKEN = 0.55, 0.90 / 1e6

for daily in (2e6, 100e6, 600e6, 1.2e9):
    util = daily / (TOK_PER_MIN * 60 * 24)
    rent, per_token = GPU_PER_MIN * 60 * 24, daily * PER_TOKEN
    best = "rent" if rent < per_token else "per-token"
    print(f"{daily / 1e6:7.0f}M tokens/day  utilization {util:6.1%}  rent ${rent:7.2f}  per-token ${per_token:8.2f}  -> {best}")
```
:::

:::read
At 2 million tokens a day the GPU would be busy about 0.15% of the time, so per-token pricing costs under $2 against $792 to rent. Renting only wins near 900 million tokens a day, about 68% utilization. Most new products sit far to the left of the crossover, which is why per-token platforms are the usual starting point.
:::

@@before Ship it
:::broken
A startup reads "per-minute beats per-token above 30% utilization", sees its product busy from 9 to 5, and moves to a dedicated GPU billed per minute. The bill goes up fourfold. What went wrong? (Solution B1.)
:::

:::recap What to keep from this lesson
- Three segments: custom silicon (fastest decode), GPU platforms (the middle), API marketplaces (breadth).
- Convert every quote to dollars per day for your own traffic before comparing.
- Renting beats per-token above $m / (T \cdot p)$ utilization; compute it, do not assume 30%.
- Choose on developer experience, attribution, compliance and SLAs once prices are close.
:::

## Solutions

??? sol | P1 · Baseten against Fireworks
With the script's numbers, $0.55 a minute against $0.90 per million tokens at 900,000 tokens a minute, the crossover is 0.55 / 0.81 ≈ 68% utilization. The sweep agrees: Fireworks wins at 40% and the rented GPU from about 70%. The 30% rule of thumb assumes a cheaper GPU minute or a faster model; it is not a constant.
???

??? sol | P3 · Batch tier after a price rise
If 40% of traffic moves to a batch tier at half price, the blended rate is 0.6 × 1 + 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.8 of the old rate. That 20% saving usually outweighs a $1 an hour rise on the remaining on-demand capacity unless the volume is small.
???

??? sol | B1 · Busy hours are not utilization
"Busy from 9 to 5" means traffic for a third of the day, and even then a GPU is rarely saturated. Average utilization over the 24 hours they pay for was probably under 15%, far below the crossover. They should have divided daily tokens by the GPU's daily capacity before switching, or rented only for the busy window.
???
