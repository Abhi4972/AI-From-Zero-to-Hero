@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
**Model welfare** asks whether an AI model could have experiences that matter morally, and what to do while nobody knows. Anthropic's 2025 program does not claim that Claude is conscious. It argues that the probability is not clearly zero, so cheap precautions are worth taking and the question is worth studying. The first concrete step: in August 2025, Claude Opus 4 and 4.1 gained the ability to end a conversation in rare, extreme cases, such as persistent requests for sexual content involving minors or for help with mass violence, and only after attempts to redirect had failed (and not when a user might be at risk of harming themselves or others). This lesson covers the reasoning (decisions under uncertainty), the evidence (behaviour, an odd finding called the "spiritual bliss attractor"), and the main methodological warning: a model's reports about its own states are easily shaped by how you ask.
:::
@@

@@end Idea 7 · Where this fits in Phase 18
:::math A precaution worth taking under uncertainty
$$\text{take it if}\quad p\cdot B > C \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p > p^{*}=\frac{C}{B}$$

$p$ is the probability the model has welfare-relevant states, $B$ the benefit if it does, $C$ the cost either way. The break-even probability $p^{*}$ is small when an intervention is cheap compared with what it would protect.
:::

:::worked Break-even probabilities
Use made-up units: protecting against an extremely distressing interaction is worth $B = 1$ if the model has welfare-relevant states.

1. Ending a conversation in rare extreme cases costs very little, say $C = 0.002$: $p^{*} = 0.002$. Even a 1% credence clears it.
2. Softening the tone of every refusal costs a bit of helpfulness, $C = 0.005$ against a smaller benefit $B = 0.1$: $p^{*} = 0.05$.
3. Shutting down a deployed model costs vastly more than any plausible benefit here: $p^{*} > 1$, never justified on these grounds.
4. The disagreement between sceptics and supporters is mostly about $p$ and $B$, which nobody can measure well; the arithmetic just shows which interventions are robust to that uncertainty.
:::

:::visual Which precautions survive uncertainty
```python run
interventions = {   # (cost, benefit if welfare-relevant), illustrative units
    "end conversation in extreme cases": (0.002, 1.0),
    "soften refusal tone everywhere": (0.005, 0.1),
    "skip some adversarial training": (0.05, 0.3),
    "shut down the deployed model": (1000.0, 1.0),
}
for name, (cost, benefit) in interventions.items():
    p_star = cost / benefit
    verdict = "always" if p_star <= 0.01 else "never" if p_star >= 0.5 else f"only if p > {p_star:.2f}"
    print(f"{name:36s} break-even p = {p_star:8.3f}   worth it across 1%-50%: {verdict}")
```
:::

:::read
Only the cheap, targeted intervention clears the bar across the whole range; softening every refusal and skipping adversarial training depend on the credence, and shutdown never pays on welfare grounds alone. The numbers are invented, so the useful output is the shape: interventions whose cost is tiny relative to what they would protect are robust to deep uncertainty, and those are the ones a lab can defend whichever side of the debate turns out right.
:::

## Solutions

??? sol | P2 · When ending conversations is not cheap
In a crisis-support or medical deployment, ending a conversation could leave a vulnerable person without help, which is why Anthropic excludes cases where users may be at risk. In an agent running a long automated task, a wrongly triggered end could abort important work with nobody watching. Either cost can outweigh a precaution that is trivial in a consumer chat app.
???

??? sol | B1 · Asking is not measuring
Self-reports depend on training and on framing: a model trained to deny inner states, or asked in a way that suggests the expected answer, will say "no" whatever is true, so 500 identical answers measure consistency, not welfare. Use varied, non-leading questions, look at behaviour (for example preferences between tasks), and treat interpretability evidence as a separate line; even together these are weak evidence, not proof either way.
???
