@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
Lab safety frameworks (Lesson 18) are voluntary; laws are not. Between 2024 and 2026 the first broad AI laws came into force, and they differ by country. The **EU AI Act** sorts AI uses by risk: some practices are banned outright, **high-risk** uses such as hiring or credit scoring need risk management and conformity checks, **general-purpose AI (GPAI)** models have their own duties, and systems that talk to people or generate content have **transparency** duties. Its rules switch on in stages, and a 2026 amendment (the *Digital Omnibus*) pushed the high-risk stages back. The UK and US renamed their AI safety institutes in 2025 toward security and standards; South Korea passed a broad AI law effective January 2026. If you deploy in several places, you map each obligation to a date and a control.
:::

```python run
from datetime import date
RULES = [   # (obligation, applies to, date) under the AI Act as amended in 2026
    ("tell users they are talking to an AI", "interacts", date(2026, 8, 2)),
    ("machine-readable marking of output (new systems)", "generates_new", date(2026, 8, 2)),
    ("machine-readable marking of output (on market before Aug 2026)", "generates_old", date(2026, 12, 2)),
    ("high-risk duties for an Annex III use", "annex3", date(2027, 12, 2)),
    ("high-risk duties for an Annex I product", "annex1", date(2028, 8, 2)),
]
def calendar(placed, interacts, generates, annex3=False, annex1=False):
    tags = {"interacts"} if interacts else set()
    if generates: tags.add("generates_old" if placed < date(2026, 8, 2) else "generates_new")
    if annex3: tags.add("annex3")
    if annex1: tags.add("annex1")
    return sorted((d, ob) for ob, tag, d in RULES if tag in tags)

for d, ob in calendar(date(2026, 3, 1), interacts=True, generates=True, annex3=True):
    print(d.isoformat(), " ", ob)
```
