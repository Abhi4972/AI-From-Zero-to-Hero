@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
RLHF needs a separate reward model and an unstable reinforcement-learning loop. **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)** noticed that the best policy under the RLHF objective has a closed form, so the reward can be written in terms of the policy itself: how much more likely the model makes an answer than the reference model did. Plugging that into the preference loss gives a plain classification-style loss on (preferred, rejected) pairs: raise the preferred answer's probability relative to the reference, lower the rejected one's. No reward model, no RL loop. A family of variants followed, each fixing one weakness: **IPO** bounds how far the gap can grow, **KTO** learns from single thumbs-up or thumbs-down labels, **SimPO** drops the reference and normalizes by length, **ORPO** folds preference into supervised fine-tuning, **BPO** stops the preferred answer's own probability from falling. All of them still over-optimize, just like RLHF.
:::

@@end Idea 8 · Choosing among them (2026)
:::worked The DPO loss can fall while the good answer gets less likely
Write $\Delta_w$ and $\Delta_l$ for the change in log-probability of the preferred and rejected answers relative to the reference, with β = 0.1. The DPO loss is $-\log\sigma\big(\beta(\Delta_w - \Delta_l)\big)$.

1. Start: $\Delta_w = \Delta_l = 0$. Margin 0, loss $\log 2 ≈ 0.693$.
2. Training raises the good answer: $\Delta_w = +2$, $\Delta_l = -2$. Margin 0.4, loss ≈ 0.513.
3. Or training lowers both: $\Delta_w = -3$, $\Delta_l = -8$. Margin 0.5, loss ≈ 0.474, *lower* than in step 2.
4. In step 3 the preferred answer became $e^{3} ≈ 20$ times less likely than under the reference, and the loss calls it progress. That is the "degraded chosen response" BPO penalizes.
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ DPO needs no separate reward model.
- ✗ DPO's implicit reward gap is bounded.
  It is unbounded; IPO's squared loss bounds it at $1/(2\beta)$.
- ✓ KTO can train on unpaired thumbs-up and thumbs-down data.
- ✗ Direct alignment algorithms escape reward over-optimization.
  They over-optimize the policy-to-reference ratio instead; the 2024 scaling results show the same curve.
:::

:::predict
Before running the code below: for a pair with margin $\Delta_w - \Delta_l = 30$ and β = 0.1, which loss is lower, DPO's or IPO's?
:::

:::visual DPO and IPO on the same margins
```python run
import math
beta = 0.1
dpo = lambda m: -math.log(1 / (1 + math.exp(-beta * m)))
ipo = lambda m: (m - 1 / (2 * beta)) ** 2
print("margin   DPO loss   IPO loss   (IPO's target margin is 1/(2*beta) = 5)")
for m in (0, 2, 5, 10, 30, 100):
    print(f"{m:6d}   {dpo(m):8.4f}   {ipo(m):8.2f}")
```
:::

:::read
DPO's loss keeps shrinking as the margin grows, so it always rewards pushing the pair further apart, even at a margin of 100, far beyond what one preference label justifies. IPO's loss is zero at its target margin of 5 and grows again beyond it, so it stops pushing. That is why IPO is steadier when many pairs are only weak preferences.
:::

Every method moves probability towards action 1, the one with the highest true utility, as the takeaway says. Compare each row with the "true optimum" line: closest is not the one with the highest win rate.

A team switches from PPO to DPO and reports a falling training loss and a rising preference win rate. Users say answers got worse, and the logs show the chosen responses' log-probability dropping steadily. What is happening, and which variant targets it? (Solution B1.)

:::recap What to keep from this lesson
- DPO turns the RLHF objective into a loss on preference pairs: no reward model, no RL loop.
- IPO bounds the margin; KTO uses unpaired labels; SimPO removes the reference and length bias; ORPO is single-stage; BPO protects the chosen answer.
- A falling DPO loss can hide a falling chosen probability.
- All of them still over-optimize; keep β conservative and stop early.
- Choose by data shape and the failure you observe.
:::

## Solutions

??? sol | P2 · Equal-strength pairs
When every pair is an equally strong preference, IPO's bounded target matches the data well and stays stable, while DPO keeps widening margins with no ceiling and over-commits. Methods that ignore the reference (SimPO, ORPO) depend most on their margin or λ settings.
???

??? sol | P3 · Longer rejected answers
Log-probabilities of long answers are larger negative numbers, so with rejected answers twice as long, DPO can "win" pairs partly through length alone; the learned policy then prefers shorter answers for the wrong reason. SimPO divides each log-probability by length, which removes that shortcut.
???

??? sol | B1 · Loss down, quality down
DPO is lowering both log-probabilities, the rejected ones faster, so the margin and the loss improve while the preferred answers also become less likely: degraded chosen responses. BPO adds a penalty on downward moves of the chosen log-probability; a lower learning rate, a larger β and early stopping on a held-out quality measure help too.
???
