@@top Problem
:::plain In plain words first
Searching hours of video for "when does the chef pour the sauce?" needs an index, not just a large model. The standard pipeline cuts each video into **scenes**, and for each scene produces three things: a **caption** written by a vision-language model, an **embedding** of a keyframe, and the **transcript** of what was said, with word-level timestamps. A question searches all three, the results are fused, and a **temporal grounding** step narrows the answer to a precise (start, end) window inside the best scene, which the answer cites. Some questions are much harder than others: models often get **counting** ("how many people enter?") and **order of actions** ("pour before stir?") wrong, so those are measured separately. Model names are a 2026 snapshot.
:::
@@

@@end Build it
:::math Temporal IoU
$$\text{tIoU}=\frac{\left|[s_p,e_p]\cap[s_g,e_g]\right|}{\left|[s_p,e_p]\cup[s_g,e_g]\right|}$$

The overlap between the predicted window $[s_p, e_p]$ and the true one $[s_g, e_g]$, divided by their combined span. Grounding benchmarks report the share of answers with tIoU above 0.5 or 0.7.
:::

:::worked Narrowing a window
The true moment is 02:20–02:35 (15 s). Retrieval returns the scene 02:12–02:50 (38 s).

1. Scene as the answer: overlap 15 s, union 38 s, tIoU ≈ 0.39. Below 0.5: counts as a miss.
2. After grounding narrows it to 02:15–02:40 (25 s): overlap 15 s, union 25 s, tIoU = 0.60. A hit at 0.5, not at 0.7.
3. A tighter 02:18–02:36 gives overlap 15, union 18, tIoU ≈ 0.83.
4. Scene retrieval finds *where to look*; grounding is what turns "somewhere in this scene" into a citable moment.
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ Each scene gets caption, keyframe and transcript representations.
- ✗ A long-context model removes the need for an index over 100 hours of video.
  Ingesting a whole library per question is too slow and costly; scenes are indexed once.
- ✓ Counting and action-order questions are reported separately because they fail more often.
- ✗ Returning the whole matching scene is precise enough.
  Grounding narrows the window so citations point at the actual moment.
:::

:::visual Temporal IoU from scratch
```python run
def t(m): mm, ss = m.split(":"); return int(mm) * 60 + int(ss)
def tiou(pred, gold):
    (ps, pe), (gs, ge) = [(t(a), t(b)) for a, b in (pred, gold)]
    inter = max(0, min(pe, ge) - max(ps, gs))
    union = max(pe, ge) - min(ps, gs)
    return inter / union

gold = ("02:20", "02:35")
for pred in (("02:12", "02:50"), ("02:12", "02:42"), ("02:15", "02:40"), ("02:30", "03:00")):
    v = tiou(pred, gold)
    print(f"predicted {pred[0]}-{pred[1]}  tIoU {v:.2f}  {'hit' if v >= 0.5 else 'miss'} at 0.5")
```
:::

:::read
The whole scene misses (0.39); the toy's grounded window 02:12–02:42 scores 0.50, a hit only just at the 0.5 threshold; a tighter window scores 0.60; and a window that is shifted late scores 0.12 although it overlaps the event. tIoU punishes both windows that are too wide and windows in the wrong place, which is why it is the standard grounding metric.
:::

@@before Ship it
:::read
For each question the toy finds a sensible top scene and narrows the window inside it, for example 02:12–02:50 down to 02:12–02:42 for the pour-or-stir question. Look at the scores, though: the fused scores of the top three scenes differ only in the third decimal place, and unrelated scenes ("cars stopped at a red light") come second. Reciprocal rank fusion over three tiny lists produces near-ties; on a real corpus, a re-ranker or the VLM itself must separate the top scene from the rest.
:::

:::broken
A team reports 81% accuracy on their video QA set and calls the hallucination problem solved. Their set is 90% descriptive questions. What should the report have included? (Solution B1.)
:::

:::recap What to keep from this lesson
- Cut video into scenes; caption, embed and transcribe each.
- Search all three representations and fuse the results.
- Ground the answer to a precise window; measure with temporal IoU.
- Cite (video, start, end) with a frame preview.
- Report counting and action-order questions separately.
:::

## Solutions

??? sol | P3 · Counting you can check
Make the synthesizer list each counted instance with its own timestamp ("person 1 enters at 01:12, person 2 at 01:20…"), and show those frames in the UI so the user can confirm or reject each. Measure count accuracy with and without the per-instance list; listing usually exposes double counting and missed instances that a single number hides.
???

??? sol | B1 · An average dominated by easy questions
With 90% descriptive questions, the overall score mostly reflects the easy class; counting and action-order accuracy could be very low and barely move it. Report accuracy per class with the number of questions in each, and build the hard classes up to a size where their accuracy is measured with a useful margin of error.
