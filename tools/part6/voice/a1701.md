@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
A **managed LLM platform** is a cloud service that runs models for you, so you send a request and pay for the tokens instead of renting GPUs yourself. The three big clouds each sell one, and they made different bets. **AWS Bedrock** is a shop with many brands of model behind one API. **Azure OpenAI** sells OpenAI's models only, inside Azure. **Vertex AI** is built around Google's Gemini models. You pay in one of two ways. **On-demand** means you share capacity with everyone else and pay per token. **Provisioned throughput** (Azure calls a unit of it a **PTU**) means you rent a reserved slice of capacity by the hour, used or not. This lesson shows how to choose a platform and a payment mode from the facts you have: which models you need, how fast the first token must arrive, how steady your traffic is, and how clearly you need to see who spent what.
:::
@@

@@end Idea 7 · Numbers you should remember
:::math
**When does reserved capacity pay off?** Let a reservation cost $R$ dollars per hour and serve at most $C$ tokens per hour. On-demand costs $p$ dollars per token. If your traffic fills a fraction $u$ of the reservation on average, on-demand would cost $u \cdot C \cdot p$ per hour, so the reservation wins when

$$u \cdot C \cdot p > R \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad u > u^{*} = \frac{R}{C \cdot p}$$

The break-even utilization $u^{*}$ is the reservation's price divided by what its full capacity would cost on demand.
:::

:::worked Where a reservation starts to pay
A reservation costs R = $10 an hour and can produce C = 2 million output tokens an hour. On-demand output costs p = $10 per million tokens.

1. Full capacity on demand would cost 2 × $10 = $20 an hour.
2. Break-even: $u^{*} = 10 / 20 = 0.5$. Below 50% average utilization, on-demand is cheaper.
3. At 80% utilization, on-demand costs 0.8 × $20 = $16 an hour against the reservation's $10: you save $6 an hour, about $4,300 a month.
4. At 30%, on-demand costs $6 an hour, and the reservation wastes $4 an hour.

The same arithmetic explains the "40–60% band" quoted for PTUs: real prices put $u^{*}$ somewhere in that range.
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✗ Azure is faster than Bedrock because its GPUs are better.
  The roughly 50 ms against 75 ms gap comes from reserved capacity against shared on-demand capacity, not from the hardware.
- ✓ A reservation costs the same per hour whether you send traffic or not.
- ✗ Reserving capacity always saves money on a busy product.
  It saves money only when the *average* utilization is above break-even. A product that is busy for two hours a day can still lose money on a reservation.
- ✓ Running each critical call on two providers costs little and protects you during a regional outage.
:::

:::predict
Your traffic peaks at 100% of one reservation for 4 hours a day and sits at 20% the rest of the time. The reservation's break-even is 50%. Before you run the code below, guess: does the reservation pay off over a whole day?
:::

:::visual A day of traffic against a reservation
```python run
# Hourly traffic as a share of one reservation's capacity: busy 9:00-13:00, quiet otherwise.
traffic = [0.2] * 9 + [1.0] * 4 + [0.2] * 11
R, C, p = 10.0, 2_000_000, 10 / 1_000_000      # $/hour, tokens/hour, $/token

on_demand = sum(u * C * p for u in traffic)
reserved = R * len(traffic)
print(f"average utilization: {sum(traffic) / len(traffic):.0%}")
print(f"on-demand for the day: ${on_demand:7.2f}")
print(f"reservation for the day: ${reserved:7.2f}")
print()
for hour, u in enumerate(traffic):
    if hour % 3 == 0:
        print(f"{hour:02d}:00 {'#' * int(u * 20):20s} {u:.0%}")
```
:::

:::read
Average utilization is only about 33%, under the 50% break-even, so on-demand costs about $160 for the day against $240 for the reservation. The four busy hours do not decide it; the twenty quiet ones do. If you still need fast first tokens in the busy window, the usual answer is a small reservation for the steady base load plus on-demand for the peaks.
:::

:::recap What to keep from this lesson
- The three platforms differ in **catalog**: Bedrock is a marketplace, Azure OpenAI is OpenAI-only, Vertex is Gemini-first.
- Latency gaps between them come mostly from **reserved versus shared capacity**.
- A reservation pays off when average utilization is above $R / (C \cdot p)$, usually 40–60%.
- Cost **attribution** differs: Bedrock tags requests through inference profiles, Vertex uses projects plus BigQuery, Azure needs extra instrumentation.
- Keep **two providers** for any product-critical call.
:::
