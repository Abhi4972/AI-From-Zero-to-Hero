@@top The situation
:::plain In plain words first
A security review said a function "calls nothing", citing an empty list of callees returned by a tool. The sentence happened to be true, but an empty list is not proof: it can mean "none found" or "could not resolve". The case trains telling "known empty" from "unknown", and citing the evidence that actually supports a claim (here, the assembly with no call instruction).
:::
@@

@@before Walkthrough
:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ An empty list can mean the tool looked and found nothing, or could not look.
- ✗ The original sentence was wrong.
  The claim was right; the evidence cited under it was not enough.
- ✓ Coverage fields in the response say how complete the list is.
:::
@@

@@end The lesson to carry
:::recap What to keep from this case
- Separate known-empty from unknown.
- Read the coverage and completeness fields.
- Cite evidence that supports the claim directly.
- State what remains unknown.
:::
@@
