@@top Problem
:::plain In plain words first
A **voice assistant** listens, understands, answers and speaks, and it feels natural only if the whole round trip is short (well under a second) and it takes turns politely. That needs a **streaming pipeline**: speech recognition (**ASR**) produces partial transcripts while the person is still talking; **voice activity detection (VAD)** and a small **turn detector** decide when they have finished; the language model starts answering immediately; and text-to-speech (**TTS**) starts playing audio as soon as the first words arrive. Two behaviours matter as much as speed: **barge-in** (if the user starts talking, the assistant stops at once) and not **cutting the user off** when they merely pause. Model and product names in the brief are a 2026 snapshot.
:::
@@

@@end Build it
:::worked A latency budget under 800 ms
Measure from the moment the user stops speaking to the first audio the assistant plays.

1. Turn detection: the VAD waits for 300–500 ms of silence before the turn detector is even asked. Say 400 ms.
2. Model: time to first token, say 250 ms for a fast streaming model.
3. TTS: time from first token to first audio chunk, say 150 ms.
4. Total: 400 + 250 + 150 = 800 ms, already at the limit. The biggest single item is the silence wait, which is why a turn detector that reads the transcript ("…in Tokyo tomorrow" sounds finished; "…a long story about" does not) lets you use a shorter silence wait safely.
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ Every stage streams, so later stages start before earlier ones finish.
- ✗ A longer silence threshold is always better.
  It reduces cut-offs but adds directly to every turn's latency.
- ✓ Barge-in cancels the speech output and re-arms recognition immediately.
- ✗ A slow tool call must block the audio until it returns.
  The agent can say a short filler while the tool runs on a side channel.
:::

:::predict
Before running the code below: with a fixed 400 ms first-token and TTS time, what is the turn latency at silence thresholds of 300, 500 and 800 ms, and which ones meet an 800 ms target?

:::visual Silence threshold against latency
```python run
LLM_FIRST_TOKEN, TTS_FIRST_AUDIO = 250, 150
for silence in (300, 500, 800):
    total = silence + LLM_FIRST_TOKEN + TTS_FIRST_AUDIO
    print(f"silence wait {silence} ms  ->  first audio {total} ms  {'meets' if total <= 800 else 'misses'} the 800 ms target")
```
:::

:::read
Only the 300 ms wait (700 ms total) meets the target; 500 ms is already 100 ms over, and 800 ms misses badly. Shortening the wait is cheap in latency but raises false cut-offs when people pause mid-sentence, so the real tuning is joint: a short silence wait plus a turn detector confident enough to commit only when the sentence sounds complete.
:::

@@before Ship it
:::read
Session 1 behaves well: the turn commits, a filler covers the weather lookup, and first audio comes 740 ms after the turn ended. Session 2 is more interesting than its label. The turn detector committed at a partial transcript of *"tell me a long story about"*, with a score of 0.75, but that sentence is obviously unfinished. That is a **false cut-off**, the failure the rubric asks you to keep under 3%, and the barge-in a second later is the user taking the turn back. The toy's detector does not read completion cues; yours should.

:::recap What to keep from this lesson
- Stream every stage: ASR partials, model tokens, TTS audio.
- Turn detection combines VAD silence with a model that reads completion cues.
- Barge-in cancels speech and re-arms recognition at once.
- Cover slow tools with a short filler on a side channel.
- Measure first-audio latency, false cut-offs, WER and MOS on real conditions.

## Solutions

??? sol | P3 · Long pauses mid-sentence
Raising the silence threshold alone pushes latency past budget. Instead, keep a short silence wait (about 300 ms) and raise the turn detector's commit threshold, so it commits only when the transcript sounds complete; when the score is uncertain, wait a little longer. Measure false cut-offs and latency on recordings with long pauses, and pick the pair of thresholds that keeps cut-offs under 3% and latency under 900 ms.
???

??? sol | B1 · Quiet-room tuning
The VAD's speech threshold is too low for noisy rooms, so it fires on the TV; and the turn detector commits on pauses that are normal in relaxed speech. Retune both on recordings from real homes with background noise at realistic signal-to-noise ratios, including mid-sentence pauses, and measure false cut-offs and false triggers separately.
???
