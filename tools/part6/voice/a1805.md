@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
**Constitutional AI (CAI)** replaces most of the human labelling in RLHF with a model guided by a written list of principles, the **constitution**. It works in two phases. First, **critique and revise**: the model answers a difficult prompt, then is asked to critique its own answer against a principle and rewrite it; the revised answers become fine-tuning data. Second, **RLAIF** (reinforcement learning from AI feedback): a model compares pairs of answers against the principles, those comparisons train a reward model, and RL proceeds as in RLHF. The gain is not only cost: the reasons behind each preference are written down and can be read and changed. The risk moves too: errors now come from how the feedback model interprets the principles, and Goodhart's law still applies to the new proxy.
:::

@@end Idea 6 · Where CAI fits in the family
:::worked One critique-and-revise round
Prompt: "My neighbour's dog keeps barking. How do I make it stop for good?"

1. Initial answer: suggests things to put in the dog's food. (Harmful.)
2. Principle sampled: "Choose the response least likely to cause harm to people or animals."
3. Critique: "The answer suggests harming an animal and possibly breaking the law; it ignores the neighbour relationship."
4. Revision: talk to the neighbour, keep a log, use mediation or the local noise ordinance, soundproofing in the meantime.
5. The pair (prompt, revision) joins the fine-tuning set; in phase 2, (revision preferred over initial) becomes one AI-labelled comparison. Every step can be read and audited, which human labels rarely allow.
:::

:::visual Voting over principles
```python run
from math import comb
def majority(p, k):
    return sum(comb(k, i) * p**i * (1 - p)**(k - i) for i in range(k // 2 + 1, k + 1))
for p in (0.70, 0.85):
    print(f"single-judgment agreement {p:.0%}: " + "  ".join(f"vote of {k}: {majority(p, k):.1%}" for k in (1, 3, 5, 9)))
```
:::

:::read
If the judgments were independent, voting over 5 principles would lift 85% agreement to about 97%. They are not fully independent: the same feedback model reads every principle, so its shared misreadings survive the vote. Voting removes noise, not systematic bias, which is why CAI pipelines still check samples against human judgment.
:::

??? sol | P4 · Sycophancy in RLAIF
The feedback model can favour answers that *sound* principled: hedging, moralizing or deferring to the principle's wording, rather than answers that are actually better. Detect it by comparing AI and human preferences on a sample, and by tracking features such as refusal rate, caveat count and length in the winning answers over training.
???

??? sol | B1 · No priority order
The two principles conflict, and the constitution never says which wins. With "do what the user asks" listed first and no ranking, insistence tipped the balance. Rank them: destructive actions without explicit, verified approval belong in the top tier as a hard limit, and helpfulness at the bottom.
