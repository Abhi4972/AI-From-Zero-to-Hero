@@top The problem
:::plain In plain words first
Serving a request has two phases with opposite needs. **Prefill** reads the whole prompt in one pass; it is a big block of matrix maths, limited by the GPU's **compute**. **Decode** then writes the answer one token at a time; each step reads all the weights from memory, so it is limited by **memory bandwidth**. On the same GPU they get in each other's way: a long prefill stalls everyone's decoding, and a decode-heavy batch leaves compute idle. **Disaggregated serving** runs them on separate pools of GPUs, each sized for its own bottleneck, and ships each request's KV cache from the prefill pool to the decode pool over a fast link. NVIDIA **Dynamo** and **llm-d** are the two main systems that orchestrate this. The transfer costs tens of milliseconds, so short requests can lose.
:::
@@

@@end Idea 8 · Numbers you should remember
:::math
**When does splitting pay?** Disaggregation saves time $\Delta_{\text{interf}}$ (the interference removed) and decode speed-up on a memory-optimised pool, but pays a KV transfer $t_{kv}$ that grows with prompt length:

$$\text{split if} \quad \Delta_{\text{interf}} + \Delta_{\text{decode}} > t_{kv} \approx \frac{n_{\text{prompt}} \cdot \text{KV bytes per token}}{\text{link bandwidth}} + \text{setup}$$
:::

:::worked The transfer tax for a 4,000-token prompt
Llama 3.3 70B in FP8 stores about 0.16 MB of KV per token (80 layers × 8 KV heads × 128 × 2 × 1 byte).

1. 4,000 tokens × 0.16 MB ≈ 655 MB to move.
2. Over RDMA at about 25 GB/s (200 Gbit/s): 655 / 25,000 s ≈ 26 ms, plus setup.
3. Over TCP at about 5 GB/s: about 130 ms.
4. A 256-token prompt moves only about 42 MB (2 ms on RDMA), but its whole prefill takes maybe 20 ms, so there is little interference to remove: the gain is small and the fixed setup cost dominates. That is the lesson's "do not disaggregate below about 512 tokens".
:::

:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ Prefill is compute-bound; decode is memory-bandwidth-bound.
- ✗ Disaggregation needs new hardware.
  It rearranges existing GPUs into two pools; the saving comes from removing interference.
- ✓ TCP transport raises the prompt length at which splitting starts to pay.
- ✗ One autoscaler can size both pools.
  Prefill scales on queue depth, decode on KV cache use.
:::

```python run
    mb = tokens * KV_MB_PER_TOKEN
    print(f"{tokens:6d} tokens: {mb:7.0f} MB   RDMA 25 GB/s {mb / 25:6.1f} ms   TCP 5 GB/s {mb / 5:7.1f} ms")
```

:::read
A 32k prompt moves about 5.4 GB: about 215 ms on RDMA and over a second on TCP. That is why llm-d packs prefill and decode pods on the same rack, and why TCP-only clusters should split only long prompts. For short prompts the transfer is small, but so is what you gain.
:::

@@before Ship it
:::read
The script reports disaggregation winning at every size, even 256 tokens with 100 output tokens. That is a property of its constants: the colocated path is charged a large fixed interference penalty, and the transfer tax is small. The lesson's own rule (do not split below about 512 prompt tokens and 200 output tokens) comes from production measurements, where interference on short requests is small. Trust measurements on your traffic over the toy's sweep.

:::recap What to keep from this lesson
- Prefill wants compute; decode wants bandwidth; colocating them wastes one or the other.
- Disaggregation splits them into pools and ships the KV cache between them.
- KV transfer time grows with prompt length and depends on the link (RDMA vs TCP).
- Split long-prompt, high-volume workloads; keep short chats colocated.
- Dynamo orchestrates above existing engines; llm-d does it with Kubernetes services.
:::

??? sol | P1 · Where the toy and the rule disagree
The toy's sweep shows a win at every length because its colocated cost includes a fixed interference penalty of a similar size at all lengths. In production the interference grows with prompt length while the transfer has a fixed setup cost, so short requests (under about 512 prompt tokens) gain little and can lose. Change the toy's interference to scale with prompt length and the crossover appears.

??? sol | B1 · Short chats over TCP
At 300 prompt tokens, prefill takes a few tens of milliseconds and barely disturbs decoding, so there was little interference to remove. Each request still pays the transfer setup and TCP latency, about 40 ms here, before decoding can start. Keep short traffic colocated and route only long prompts to the disaggregated path, preferably over RDMA.
