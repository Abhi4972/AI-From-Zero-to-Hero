@@top The situation
:::plain In plain words first
A team picked one speculative decoding setting for three products after benchmarking random prompts. The gain from speculation depends on how predictable the text is, so random prompts said little about math answers or code. Measured per product, the right choice differed. The case trains benchmarking with each product's own traffic.
:::
@@

@@before Walkthrough
:::check
Which statements are right?

- ✓ Speculation pays off when the next tokens are predictable.
- ✗ N-gram speculation has no gain because it did nothing on random prompts.
  Random prompts have no repeated text for n-grams to reuse.
- ✓ Each product needs its own measurement and setting.
:::
@@

@@end The lesson to carry
:::recap What to keep from this case
- Benchmark with the real traffic of each product.
- Speculation gain is a property of the text.
- Pick settings per product when they differ.
- Reopen tickets closed on unrepresentative data.
:::
