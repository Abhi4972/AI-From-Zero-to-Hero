The idea in one line

OpenAI's CLIP (2021) proved a single idea big enough to power the next five years: align an image encoder and a text encoder in the same vector space using only noisy web image-caption pairs and a contrastive loss. Zero supervised labels. 400M pairs. The resulting embedding space does zero-shot classification, image-text retrieval, and plugs into every 2026 VLM as its vision tower. Term What people say What it actually means
InfoNCE "Contrastive loss" Cross-entropy over a batch's similarity matrix; each item's positive is its paired item, negatives are everything else
Sigmoid loss "SigLIP loss" Per-pair binary cross-entropy; no softmax, no all-gather, scales cheaply in distributed training

The problem

In plain words first

CLIP learns to put a picture and a sentence that describes it at the same point in one shared space. It has two encoders, one for images and one for text, each producing a vector. Training shows the model a batch of images with their captions and rewards it when every image's vector is closest to its own caption's vector rather than to the other captions'. That needs no labels, only image–caption pairs from the web. Once trained, the space can classify images it was never trained on: write "a photo of a dog", "a photo of a cat", and pick the caption closest to the image. This lesson derives the two training losses (CLIP's softmax and SigLIP's sigmoid), then uses the real CLIP ViT-B/32 to classify photos zero-shot and to compute both losses on real vectors. No model is trained.

Pre-CLIP vision was supervised. Collect labeled datasets (ImageNet: 1.2M images, 1000 classes), train a CNN, ship it. Labels are expensive, labels bias to what labelers can agree on, and labels do not transfer to new tasks without finetuning.

The image-caption web has one billion-plus loosely-labeled pairs for free. A picture of a golden retriever with alt text "my dog Max in the park" carries a supervisory signal — the text describes the image. The question: can you turn this into useful training?

CLIP's answer: treat image-caption pairs as a matching task. Given a batch of N images and N captions, learn to match each image to its own caption against N-1 distractors. The supervision is "these two things belong together; these N-1 do not." No class labels. No human annotation. Just a contrastive loss.

The resulting embedding space does more than CLIP was trained for. ImageNet zero-shot works because "a photo of a cat" embeds near pictures of cats that were never explicitly labeled cats. This is the bet that spawned every 2026 VLM.

Idea 1 · The dual encoder

CLIP has two towers:

  • Image encoder f: ViT or ResNet, outputs a D-dim vector per image.
  • Text encoder g: small transformer, outputs a D-dim vector per caption.

Both towers normalize their outputs to unit length. Similarity is cos(f(x), g(y)) = f(x)^T g(y) since both are unit-norm.

For a batch of N (image, caption) pairs, build the similarity matrix S of shape (N, N):

S[i,j]=cos(f(xi​),g(yj​))/τ

where tau is a learned temperature (CLIP initializes to 0.07; learned in log-space).

Idea 2 · InfoNCE loss

CLIP uses a symmetric cross-entropy over rows and columns:

loss_i2t = CE(S, labels=identity)     # each image's positive is its own caption
loss_t2i = CE(S^T, labels=identity)   # each caption's positive is its own image
loss = (loss_i2t + loss_t2i) / 2

This is InfoNCE. The softmax in CE forces each image to match its caption more than every other caption in the batch. The "negatives" are all other batch items. Bigger batches = more negatives = stronger signal. CLIP trained at batch 32k; scale matters.

Idea 3 · Temperature

tau controls the sharpness of the softmax. Low τ → sharp distribution, hard negative mining effect. High τ → soft, all samples contribute. CLIP learns log(1/τ), clipped so that 1/τ never exceeds 100 (the released models sit at that cap, as the run below shows). SigLIP learns a temperature too, initialised at t=10, and adds a learned bias b, initialised at −10, which sets how likely a pair is to match before the model has seen it.

Idea 4 · Why sigmoid scales better (SigLIP)

Softmax needs the whole similarity matrix in sync. In distributed training you must all-gather every embedding to every replica, then do the softmax. This is quadratic in world size for communication.

SigLIP replaces softmax with element-wise sigmoid: for each pair (i, j), the loss is a binary classification of "are these the matching pair?" positive class labels are the diagonal, everything else is negative. The loss is:

L=−N1​(i,j)∑​[yij​logsigmoid(S[i,j])+(1−yij​)logsigmoid(−S[i,j])]

y_ij = 1 if i == j, else 0. Each pair's loss is independent, so no normalisation over the whole batch is needed. SigLIP's paper exploits this with a chunked implementation: each device scores its local images against its local texts, then passes its text embeddings to the next device in a ring and repeats, so no device ever holds the full N×N matrix. That makes very large batches cheap in memory; the SigLIP paper also found that gains level off around a batch of 32k.

Idea 5 · Zero-shot classification

Given N class names, for each class build a text template:

"a photo of a {class}"

Embed each template with the text encoder. Embed your image with the image encoder. Argmax cosine similarity = predicted class. No training on the target classes.

Prompt templates matter. CLIP's original paper used 80 templates per class (plain, artistic, photo, painting, etc.) and averaged the embeddings. +3 ImageNet points. Modern usage typically picks one or two templates.

The same idea as a formula

Similarity. Unit vectors ui​=f(xi​)/∥f(xi​)∥ and vj​=g(yj​)/∥g(yj​)∥, logits sij​=ui⊤​vj​/τ.

InfoNCE (CLIP), symmetric over images and texts:

L=−2N1​i=1∑N​[log∑j​esij​esii​​+log∑j​esji​esii​​].
A model that cannot tell pairs apart scores lnN; a perfect one approaches 0.

Sigmoid loss (SigLIP) with zij​=+1 for matching pairs and −1 otherwise:

L=−N1​i,j∑​logσ(zij​(tui⊤​vj​+b)).
There are N positives and N2−N negatives, which is why the bias b starts strongly negative.

Zero-shot classification: c^=argmaxc​u⊤vˉc​, where vˉc​ is the (normalised) average of the text vectors for class c's prompts.

Worked example
Why 1/τ = 100 matters
  • In the run below, a photo's cosine with its own class prompt averages 0.295, and with the best wrong class 0.205: a gap of only 0.09.
  • Unscaled, a softmax over 10 classes would barely prefer the right one: e0.295/(e0.295+9e0.205)≈0.11, hardly above 1/10.
  • Scaled by 100 the gap becomes 9 logits: e29.5/(e29.5+9e20.5)≈0.9989 in the worst case where all nine wrong classes sit at the best wrong cosine.
  • So the absolute cosines of a CLIP space are small and look unimpressive; what matters is their order, which the temperature turns into confident probabilities.
Check your mental model
  • ✗ "A cosine of 0.3 means the image and caption barely match."
    ✓ In CLIP space, matching pairs sit around 0.3 and non-matching around 0.2; only the ranking is meaningful.
  • ✗ "Zero-shot means the model has never seen the class."
    ✓ It means no training on your labels; the concept was almost certainly in the web data.
  • ✗ "Sigmoid loss needs no communication between devices."
    ✓ It needs no global softmax; text embeddings still travel around a ring of devices, in chunks.

Zero-shot classification with the real CLIP

OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/32 (151M parameters) embeds 300 photos from Imagenette (10 ImageNet classes) and the class prompts with three prompt styles. Then it reports accuracy, the cosine gap between right and wrong classes, the effect of the learned temperature, and the most common confusion. Inference only. About a minute on CPU.

import time, torch
from datasets import load_dataset
from transformers import CLIPModel, CLIPProcessor, logging
logging.set_verbosity_error()

# Zero-shot classification with OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/32 on 300 Imagenette photos (10 ImageNet classes), no training
ds = load_dataset("Multimodal-Fatima/Imagenette_validation", split="validation").shuffle(seed=0).select(range(300))
classes = ds.features["label"].names
model = CLIPModel.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32").eval()
proc = CLIPProcessor.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32")

t = time.time()
with torch.no_grad():
    img = torch.cat([model.get_image_features(**proc(images=[r.convert("RGB") for r in ds[i:i + 50]["image"]], return_tensors="pt")).pooler_output
                     for i in range(0, len(ds), 50)])
img = img / img.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
labels = torch.tensor(ds["label"])
print(f"{len(ds)} photos embedded in {time.time() - t:.0f} s; image vectors {tuple(img.shape)}; learned 1/tau = {model.logit_scale.exp():.1f}\n")

def class_vectors(templates):
    with torch.no_grad():
        v = torch.stack([torch.nn.functional.normalize(model.get_text_features(**proc(text=[t.format(c) for t in templates], return_tensors="pt", padding=True)).pooler_output, dim=-1).mean(0)   # average unit vectors
                         for c in classes])
    return v / v.norm(dim=-1, keepdim=True)
TEMPLATES = {"class name only": ["{}"],
             "'a photo of a {}.'": ["a photo of a {}."],
             "5-template ensemble": ["a photo of a {}.", "a blurry photo of a {}.", "a photo of the large {}.",
                                     "a photo of the small {}.", "a bad photo of a {}."]}
for name, tpl in TEMPLATES.items():
    txt = class_vectors(tpl)
    acc = ((img @ txt.T).argmax(1) == labels).float().mean()
    print(f"  zero-shot, {name:<22} accuracy {acc:.1%}")

txt = class_vectors(TEMPLATES["'a photo of a {}.'"])
sims = img @ txt.T
probs = (model.logit_scale.exp() * sims).softmax(-1)
print(f"\ncosine similarity to the right class: mean {sims[torch.arange(len(ds)), labels].mean():.3f}; "
      f"to the best wrong class: mean {sims.scatter(1, labels[:, None], -1).max(1).values.mean():.3f}")
print(f"after scaling by 1/tau and softmax, mean probability on the predicted class: {probs.max(1).values.mean():.0%}")
pred = sims.argmax(1)
conf = torch.zeros(10, 10, dtype=torch.long)
for g, p in zip(labels, pred): conf[g, p] += 1
worst = max(((conf[g, p].item(), classes[g], classes[p]) for g in range(10) for p in range(10) if g != p))
print(f"most common confusion: {worst[1]!r} predicted as {worst[2]!r} ({worst[0]} photos)")

Reading this output

Zero-shot works, and on easy classes it is nearly perfect. With nothing but the ten class names, CLIP ViT-B/32 labelled 99.3% of the 300 photos correctly. It was never trained on Imagenette's labels. These ten classes (fish, dog, church, chainsaw, parachute…) are very different from one another, so this is close to a best case. On all 1,000 ImageNet classes the same model scores about 63%.

The templates made no difference here. The class name alone, "a photo of a {}." and a 5-prompt ensemble all land at 99.0–99.3%: one or two photos either way, which is noise. With so little left to fix there is nothing for prompt engineering to gain. The CLIP paper's gains from templates (about 5 points on ImageNet) come from hard, fine-grained classes and ambiguous names. Measure templates on your own classes before adopting them.

The cosines are small, and that is normal. A photo's cosine with its own class prompt averages 0.295, with the best wrong class 0.205. The learned temperature (1/τ = 100, the cap CLIP's training allows) multiplies that gap into confident predictions: the mean probability on the predicted class is 99%.

The one confusion is understandable. A gas pump predicted as a garbage truck: both are street scenes with large machines and signage.

Both losses on real CLIP vectors

The same image and text vectors, in contrastive batches. A clean batch of 10 pairs (one photo per class) scored with InfoNCE at three temperatures and with the sigmoid loss at several temperature and bias settings. Then a batch of 64 photos whose captions repeat, because there are only 10 classes. Instant.

import math, torch
import torch.nn.functional as F
# uses img (unit image vectors), txt (unit class-prompt vectors), labels and classes from the run above

def info_nce(S):                     # symmetric cross-entropy, positives on the diagonal
    y = torch.arange(len(S))
    return ((F.cross_entropy(S, y) + F.cross_entropy(S.T, y)) / 2).item()
def sigmoid_loss(cos, t, b):         # SigLIP: every (image, text) pair is its own yes/no question
    y = 2 * torch.eye(len(cos)) - 1  # +1 on the diagonal, -1 elsewhere
    return (-F.logsigmoid(y * (t * cos + b)).sum() / len(cos)).item()

# one photo per class, each paired with its own class prompt: a clean batch of 10 pairs
first = torch.stack([(labels == c).nonzero()[0, 0] for c in range(10)])
cos = img[first] @ txt.T
print(f"batch of 10 pairs (one per class); chance-level InfoNCE = ln 10 = {math.log(10):.2f}")
for s in (1, 10, 100):
    print(f"  InfoNCE with 1/tau = {s:>3}: {info_nce(s * cos):.3f}")
print("\nsigmoid loss on the same batch (SigLIP starts at t = 10, b = -10 and learns both)")
for t, b in [(10, -10), (100, 0), (100, -10), (100, -25)]:
    p = torch.sigmoid(t * cos + b)
    print(f"  t = {t:>3}, b = {b:>4}: loss {sigmoid_loss(cos, t, b):7.3f} | P(match) for true pairs {p.diag().mean():.2f}, "
          f"for wrong pairs {(p.sum() - p.diag().sum()) / 90:.2f}")

# a realistic batch: 64 random photos, captions from only 10 classes, so many 'negatives' are actually matches
g = torch.Generator().manual_seed(0)
idx = torch.randperm(len(labels), generator=g)[:64]
same = (labels[idx][:, None] == labels[idx][None, :]).float()
cos64 = img[idx] @ txt[labels[idx]].T
print(f"\nbatch of 64 photos with class-prompt captions: {(same.sum() - 64) / (64 * 63):.0%} of the off-diagonal "
      f"'negatives' are the same class")
print(f"  InfoNCE (1/tau = 100), diagonal-only positives: {info_nce(100 * cos64):.3f}  (chance ln 64 = {math.log(64):.2f})")
y_multi = same / same.sum(1, keepdim=True)          # treat every same-class caption as a positive
soft = -(y_multi * F.log_softmax(100 * cos64, 1)).sum(1).mean().item()
print(f"  same loss, all same-class captions counted as positives: {soft:.3f}")

Reading this output

The temperature decides whether the loss sees the signal. On a clean batch of 10 pairs, InfoNCE with 1/τ = 1 is 2.193, barely below chance (ln 10 = 2.30), although the model ranks every pair correctly. At 1/τ = 100 the same vectors give 0.044. A loss stuck near ln N at the start of training is often a temperature problem, not a model problem.

The sigmoid loss needs its bias. Each of the 100 pairs is a yes/no question, and 90 of them are "no". At SigLIP's starting point (t = 10, b = −10) every pair is a confident "no": wrong pairs are right, true pairs wrong, loss 7.2. With t = 100 and no bias, every pair looks like a match (loss 145.8). A bias of −25 puts the decision boundary between the cosines of matching (about 0.3) and non-matching (about 0.2) pairs: true pairs get 0.83, wrong pairs 0.01, loss 0.548. In training, b and t are learned for exactly this reason.

Repeated captions become false negatives. In a batch of 64 photos from 10 classes, 10% of the off-diagonal pairs show a photo and a caption of the same class. InfoNCE scores them as negatives, so even this well-trained model is charged 3.099. That is closer to chance (4.16) than to zero, and the gradient would push matching pairs apart. Counting every same-class caption as a positive drops the loss to 1.987. Web captions rarely repeat exactly, which is why CLIP works; curated datasets with few distinct captions need deduplicated batches or multi-positive losses.

Idea 6 · Linear probes and finetuning

Zero-shot is a baseline. A linear probe (train one linear layer on top of frozen CLIP features for your target classes) beats zero-shot on in-domain tasks. Full finetuning beats linear probe on in-domain but can hurt zero-shot transfer. Three regimes with three trade-offs.

Idea 7 · SigLIP 2: NaFlex and dense features

SigLIP 2 (2025) adds: - NaFlex: single model handles variable aspect ratios and resolutions. - Better dense features for segmentation and depth estimation, targeting use as a frozen backbone in VLMs. - Multilingual: trained on 100+ languages where CLIP was English-only. - 1B param scale where CLIP topped out at 400M.

SigLIP and SigLIP 2 So400m/14 are among the most used vision towers in open VLMs (Lesson 12.1). For pure image–text retrieval, CLIP-family checkpoints remain common, including OpenCLIP models trained on LAION-2B and DataComp.

Idea 8 · ALIGN, BASIC, OpenCLIP, EVA-CLIP

ALIGN (Google, 2021): same idea as CLIP, 1.8B pair scale, 90% noisy. Proved noisy data scales. OpenCLIP (LAION): open reproduction of CLIP on LAION-400M / 2B, multiple scales, the go-to open checkpoint. EVA-CLIP: initializes from masked image modeling; strong backbone for VLMs. BASIC: Google's CLIP+ALIGN hybrid. All the same family, different data and tuning.

Idea 9 · The zero-shot ceiling

OpenAI's best CLIP (ViT-L/14 at 336 px) reached 76.2% ImageNet zero-shot; OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 reached 80.1% on LAION-2B. SigLIP 2 reaches 84.1% with So400m/14 at 384 px and 85.0% with its 1B-parameter g/16. The benchmark is saturating; the real value is the embedding space that downstream VLMs consume.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • SigLIP 2 (February 2025): sigmoid loss plus captioning, self-distillation and masked-prediction losses; multilingual; up to 85.0% ImageNet zero-shot (g/16); the usual choice for new open VLMs and retrieval.
  • Data curation as the main lever: DataComp and MetaCLIP showed that how image–text pairs are filtered and balanced matters as much as model size; OpenCLIP trains on these public sets.
  • Contrastive towers inside multimodal embeddings and RAG: text–image embedding models (Lesson 12.24) and visual document retrieval such as ColPali (Lesson 12.23) build on the same objective.
  • Hosted multimodal embedding APIs, such as Voyage's multimodal models and Cohere Embed v4, place text and images in one space for search.

Sources: SigLIP 2 (arXiv 2502.14786), SigLIP (arXiv 2303.15343), CLIP (arXiv 2103.00020), OpenCLIP, DataComp (arXiv 2304.14108).

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Two encoders map images and texts into one space; similarity is a dot product of unit vectors.
  • InfoNCE treats every other item in the batch as a negative; chance level is ln N.
  • SigLIP scores each pair independently, with a learned temperature and bias.
  • The temperature turns small cosine gaps into confident probabilities.
  • Repeated captions in a batch are false negatives; deduplicate or use multi-positive targets.

Ship it

This lesson produces outputs/skill-clip-zero-shot.md. Given a set of images (via path) and a list of target classes, it builds text prompts with the CLIP template, embeds both sides with a stated checkpoint (e.g., openai/clip-vit-large-patch14), and returns top-1 / top-5 predictions with similarity scores. The skill refuses to make claims about classes not in the prompt list.

Given a list of images (file paths or URLs) and a list of candidate class names, produce a ranked zero-shot classification using a declared CLIP or SigLIP checkpoint. The skill is pure-prediction; it does not train or finetune.

Produce:

1. Prompt construction. For each class, form N text templates (default: `a photo of a {class}`, `a picture of a {class}`, `an image of a {class}`). Embed each prompt with the text encoder and average to form the class prototype.
2. Image embedding. Embed each input image with the stated vision encoder. Normalize both sides to unit length.
3. Ranked predictions. Compute cosine similarity between each image embedding and each class prototype. Return top-1 and top-5 with scores.
4. Checkpoint metadata. Name the exact Hugging Face checkpoint used (e.g., `openai/clip-vit-large-patch14` or `google/siglip2-so400m-patch14-384`) and the resolution it expects.
5. Honesty notice. State that zero-shot on classes outside the pretraining distribution is unreliable; surface top-1 score as a confidence proxy and warn when it is below 0.2.

Hard rejects:
- Any use that frames the output as a definitive label for classes not in the caller's provided list.
- Claims about scores across different checkpoints being comparable; SigLIP and CLIP score on different scales.
- Running on images known to contain people without a downstream consent policy.

Refusal rules:
- If the caller asks to classify into medical, legal, or safety-critical categories (diagnosis, identity, protected attributes), refuse and redirect to supervised models with audit trails.
- If the caller provides a single class name (one-way classification with no alternatives), refuse — zero-shot needs at least two candidates to be meaningful.
- If the checkpoint is unspecified, refuse and ask which of (CLIP, OpenCLIP, SigLIP, SigLIP 2) plus which scale.

Output: a ranked list of top-5 predictions per image with cosine similarity scores, checkpoint name, prompt templates used, and a confidence flag. End with a "what to read next" paragraph pointing to Lesson 12.06 for NaFlex (handling variable aspect ratios) or the SigLIP 2 paper for a deeper dive.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Nearly perfect, so the templates had nothing left to add

99.3% with the bare class names, and the 5-template ensemble added nothing (99.0%). The ten Imagenette classes are so distinct that CLIP gets almost every photo right without help, so prompt templates have no errors left to fix. On harder label sets, such as fine-grained species or ambiguous names, the CLIP paper found templates and ensembles worth several points. Test them where the errors are.

SolutionB1 · Most negatives are positives in disguise

With 20 categories and a batch of 256, each caption appears about 13 times in a batch. For any image, about 12 of the 255 "negatives" carry exactly its own caption. InfoNCE asks the model to rank its own caption above identical copies of itself, which is impossible, so the loss cannot fall much below ln 13 ≈ 2.6. Part of every gradient pushes an image away from correct descriptions. The run above shows the effect at a smaller scale: 10% false negatives lifted the loss from near zero to 3.1. Fixes: sample batches with at most one item per category; use a multi-positive target that counts all same-category pairs as matches (as in supervised contrastive learning); or, best, write captions that actually differ per item (colour, brand, style), so that the contrastive task carries information. With only 20 categories, a plain classifier or a linear probe on frozen CLIP features may simply be the better tool.
