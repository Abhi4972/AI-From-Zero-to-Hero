Why this came about

Diffusion models (2020 onward) made image generation practical, and video and audio generation followed. As generated media became realistic, labeling its origin (provenance standards such as C2PA content credentials) became a requirement as well as a feature.

In plain words first
Making images, audio and video, responsibly

Generative media models create images, audio and video from descriptions, mostly with diffusion: start from noise and remove it step by step, guided by the prompt. In products you mostly call hosted services, so the engineering is in prompts, cost, safety filters, rights to the output, and provenance: labeling generated media (for example with content credentials) so people can tell it was made by AI.

Generative media: how diffusion models work

Image, video and much audio generation uses diffusion models. Training teaches a network to remove a little noise from a noisy example; generation starts from pure noise and removes noise step by step, guided by the prompt, until an image (or audio clip, or video) remains. The guidance comes from a text encoder, and classifier-free guidance controls how strictly the output follows the prompt. Modern systems run diffusion in a compressed latent space (latent diffusion) and often use a transformer as the denoiser (diffusion transformers); flow matching is a closely related training method used by several recent image models.

The whole idea fits in a few lines on one-dimensional data. The "model" here is the exact denoising direction (the score), which a real system learns with a neural network:

rng = np.random.default_rng(1)
# Data: a mixture of two 1-D clusters (think: two styles of image, flattened to one number)
data = np.concatenate([rng.normal(-2, 0.3, 5000), rng.normal(2, 0.3, 5000)])

T = 200
betas = np.linspace(1e-4, 0.05, T)
alpha_bar = np.cumprod(1 - betas)                                  # how much signal survives to step t

def add_noise(x0, t):
    """Forward process: blend data with Gaussian noise; at the last step only noise remains."""
    return np.sqrt(alpha_bar[t]) * x0 + np.sqrt(1 - alpha_bar[t]) * rng.normal(size=x0.shape)

def score(x, t):
    """d/dx log p_t(x) for the noised mixture. A real model LEARNS this with a neural network."""
    mus, var = np.sqrt(alpha_bar[t]) * np.array([-2.0, 2.0]), alpha_bar[t] * 0.09 + (1 - alpha_bar[t])
    w = np.exp(-(x[:, None] - mus) ** 2 / (2 * var))
    w /= w.sum(axis=1, keepdims=True)
    return (w * (mus - x[:, None]) / var).sum(axis=1)

print(f"after full noising: mean {add_noise(data, T - 1).mean():+.2f}, std {add_noise(data, T - 1).std():.2f}  (pure noise)")
x = rng.normal(size=10_000)                                        # generation starts from noise...
for t in reversed(range(T)):                                       # ...and denoises step by step
    beta = betas[t]
    x = (x + beta * score(x, t)) / np.sqrt(1 - beta)
    if t > 0:
        x += np.sqrt(beta) * rng.normal(size=x.shape)
print(f"generated samples: {np.mean(x < 0):.0%} near -2, {np.mean(x > 0):.0%} near +2, "
      f"cluster centers {x[x < 0].mean():+.2f} and {x[x > 0].mean():+.2f}")
What you should see
after full noising: mean -0.01, std 0.99  (pure noise)
generated samples: 50% near -2, 50% near +2, cluster centers -1.99 and +2.00

Ten thousand samples of pure noise were denoised, in 200 small steps, into the two clusters of the training data. Replace "one number" with "the pixels of an image", the exact score with a trained network, and add a text condition, and you have the core of an image generator. The many steps are why diffusion is slower than text generation, and why distillationlater · 11.10 into few-step models is an active area.

What enterprise projects need around the model:

  • Rights and licensing: read each model's terms on commercial use and indemnity; avoid prompts that imitate living artists' styles or real people's likenesses; keep brand assets you are allowed to use as references.
  • Provenance and labeling: mark generated media with C2PA content credentials and, where available, invisible watermarks (Google's SynthID, for example); the EU AI Actlater · 10.4 requires machine-readable marking of synthetic media from August 2026 (Lesson 10.4).
  • Safety filters: providers filter prompts and outputs for sexual content, violence, public figures and minors; add your own brand-safety review for anything published.
  • Evaluation: automatic scores (prompt-image alignment with CLIP-style models, aesthetic predictors) catch regressions, but publishing decisions need human review against a brief. Track the rejection rate per prompt template.
  • Cost and latency: images take seconds and videos minutes; generate asynchronously, cache approved assets, and batch variants.
Industry practice
What teams that ship voice and media agents do

Voice teams obsess over the latency budget and measure it per stage in production, not only in demos; they test with real callers' audio from day one, because benchmark audio hides accent, noise and code-mixing problems. Media teams treat generation as a pipeline with human approval at the end, keep every prompt, seed and model version with the asset for reproducibility, and attach provenance metadata automatically so no generated asset leaves without it.

Video, world models and robots: the same ideas, with time and action added

Three areas extend what this chapter has built. None of them needs a new principle. Each adds one thing to a model you already understand.

Area What is added How it works Examples to recognise
Video generation Time The diffusion process of Lesson 9.4 runs over a block of frames at once, cut into patches across space and time, so that objects stay consistent from one frame to the next. Some models generate the sound together with the picture. Sora (OpenAI), Veo (Google DeepMind), Runway, Kling
World models Action A model that predicts the next frames given what you do: press a key or move, and the scene responds. It is a learned simulator. Agents and robots can practise inside it, which is cheaper and safer than the real world. Genie (Google DeepMind)
Vision-language-action models (VLA) A body A model that reads camera images and an instruction and writes motor commands in place of text. It is the tool-calling loop of Lesson 3.2 with a robot arm as the tool. RT-2 and Gemini Robotics (Google DeepMind), OpenVLA, π0 (Physical Intelligence), GR00T (NVIDIA)

Read these as an agent engineer and the differences from the rest of the course are practical ones:

  • The loop is the same and the clock is not. A robot observes, acts and observes again, like any agent, but it must act many times a second. That is why these systems often pair a slow model that plans with a fast one that moves.
  • An action cannot be rolled back. A wrong tool call can be compensated (Lesson 12.7). A dropped glass cannot. Limits enforced outside the model matter even more here: speed and force limits, a stop button, and a person nearby.
  • Checking is harder. A refund either appears in the database or does not. "Did the robot fold the shirt?" needs another model or a person to judge, so evaluation is slower and noisier than anything in Chapter 12.
  • A generated video is not evidence. Video and audio can now be made of events that never happened. The provenance marks described earlier in this lesson exist for this reason.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Diffusion in one line

Almost pure noise. The model learns to predict the noise (or the clean image) at every level, so generation can start from noise and remove it step by step.

SolutionB1 · Synthetic media needs disclosure

Customers may be misled about the real product, and platforms or laws may require labeling AI-generated content. Use content credentials (C2PA) and clear disclosure, and never show generated images as photos of the actual item.

SolutionD1 · Model capabilities differ

Use a model known for text rendering, keep text short, or add text as an overlay after generation. Evaluate candidate models on your use case.

SolutionM1 · A media policy

Allowed: lifestyle backgrounds, banners. Not allowed: altering the product itself or real people without consent. Required: provenance metadata and disclosure, human review before publishing, and logs of prompts and models.

Check your understanding

Explain the mechanismHow do diffusion models generate images?

Worked answer. They are trained to reverse a noising process: given a noisy image and the noise level, predict the noise. Generation starts from random noise and repeatedly removes predicted noise, guided by a text prompt through conditioning and classifier-free guidance. Latent diffusion does this in a compressed latent space for efficiency.

A plausible wrong answer. "They copy and blend training images." They learn a denoising function, although memorization of some training images is possible.

Follow-ups. What is classifier-free guidance?

Debug a failureGenerated images vary too much between runs for a product campaign. What do you control?

Worked answer. The seed, the model version, the sampler and steps, guidance strength, reference images or style controls, and prompt templates. Pin all of them for repeatable output.

A plausible wrong answer. "Generation is random; accept it." Most variation is controllable.

Follow-ups. How would you keep a consistent visual style?

Defend a designGenerated imagery or photography for product listings?

Worked answer. Photography for the product itself, because customers buy what they see; generated imagery for backgrounds, variations and concepts, with labeling and review. The risk of misrepresentation outweighs the savings on the product shot.

A plausible wrong answer. "Generated everything, it is cheaper." Returns and trust cost more.

Follow-ups. What review would you require?

Q1How does a diffusion model generate an image?

The network learns to denoise; generation runs that process from pure noise.
