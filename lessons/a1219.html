In plain words first
The idea in one line

Whisper (Radford et al., December 2022) settled speech recognition — 680k hours of weakly-supervised multilingual speech, a simple encoder-decoder transformer, a benchmark that made every subsequent ASR release cite it. But recognition is not reasoning. Asking "what instruments are in this recording" or "what emotion is the speaker expressing" or "what happened at minute 3" requires audio understanding, not transcription. The problem

In plain words first

Whisper (OpenAI, 2022) made speech-to-text reliable, but turning audio into words throws away everything that is not words: the music, the mood, who is speaking, when the door slammed. Audio language models keep that information. An audio encoder (usually Whisper's, sometimes trained further on music and sounds) turns a spectrogram into vectors. A small adaptor maps them into a language model, which answers questions about the sound itself. The line runs from SALMONN and Qwen-Audio to Audio Flamingo 3 (2025). This lesson covers the spectrogram, the encoder, the adaptor, the model lineage, when a simple transcribe-then-reason pipeline is enough, and the MMAU benchmark. It runs Whisper's real feature extractor and encoder shapes, and computes the token cost of audio. No model is trained or downloaded.

Speech recognition was solved by Whisper. OCR-of-audio is a commodity. But "commodity" stops at transcription. If the model cannot reason over what it heard — timing, speakers, emotion, music structure, environmental sounds — transcription alone cannot drive product features.

Three obvious routes:

  1. Cascade: Whisper transcribes, LLM reasons over the transcript. Works for pure-speech scenarios. Fails for music, environmental audio, multi-speaker overlap, emotion.

  2. End-to-end audio-LLM: an audio encoder feeds audio tokens directly into an LLM, skipping transcription. Preserves acoustic information (emotion, speaker, environment). Needs new training data.

  3. Hybrid: audio encoder + text decoder that can both transcribe and reason. Qwen-Audio and Audio Flamingo pick this route.

Idea 1 · Log-Mel spectrogram: the input feature

Every audio encoder starts with the same feature: a log-Mel spectrogram.

  1. Resample to 16 kHz.
  2. Short-time Fourier transform with 25ms windows, 10ms hop.
  3. Take magnitude of the FFT result.
  4. Apply Mel filter banks (typically 80 filters log-spaced 0-8000 Hz) to warp to perceptual frequency.
  5. Log compress for dynamic range (Whisper takes log10​ of the clamped power, limits the range to 8 below the maximum, and rescales).

Result: a 2D array of shape (T, 80) where T is the number of time frames. For a 30-second clip at 100 Hz frame rate: (3000, 80).

Idea 2 · Whisper's encoder

Whisper's encoder is a transformer over time frames: two 1D convolutions (the second with stride 2) turn the 3,000 spectrogram frames of a 30-second window into 1,500 vectors, one per 20 ms, followed by 4 to 32 transformer layers depending on model size (measured below). Large-v3 uses 128 Mel bins instead of 80. Output: one hidden-state vector per 20 ms.

For ASR, Whisper's decoder is a cross-attention transformer that generates text tokens conditioned on the encoder output. Standard encoder-decoder.

For ALMs (audio-LLMs), you want the encoder output as input to a different LLM. The pattern: Whisper encoder frozen, Q-former trainable, LLM frozen or tuned.

The same idea as a formula

Log-Mel spectrogram. For window w (25 ms) and hop h (10 ms) at 16 kHz, the short-time Fourier transform X(τ,f) is pooled by Mel filters Mm​(f):

S(τ,m)=log10​max(ϵ,f∑​Mm​(f)∣X(τ,f)∣2),τ=1,…,100 per second, m=1,…,80.

Encoder frames. A stride-2 convolution halves the frame rate: 100→50 vectors per second; a 30-second window gives 1,500.

Tokens for the LLM. With pooling factor p (AF3: p=2), a clip of D seconds becomes 50D/p audio tokens: 25 per second, 1,500 per minute.

Worked example
A 10-minute meeting recording
  • Whisper encoder output: 10×60×50=30,000 vectors, processed in twenty 30-second windows.
  • As audio-LLM input at 25 tokens per second: 15,000 tokens.
  • Transcribed instead: about 150 words per minute × 10 ≈ 1,500 words ≈ 2,000 tokens, 7.5 times fewer.
  • So: if the question is about what was said, transcribe and reason over text; if it is about how it was said (tone, speakers, sounds), the audio tokens are worth their cost.

Predict before you run

See it drawn
Whisper's input and encoder, measured

Whisper's real feature extractor (default settings) turns 3 seconds of a two-tone test signal into a log-Mel spectrogram. A Whisper-tiny-shaped encoder (random weights) maps it to frame vectors, and the encoders of all five Whisper sizes are built on the meta device to count parameters. Then the run compares the token cost of audio and of its transcript. A few seconds.

import math, numpy as np, torch
from transformers import WhisperConfig, WhisperFeatureExtractor, logging
from transformers.models.whisper.modeling_whisper import WhisperEncoder
logging.set_verbosity_error()
torch.manual_seed(0)

# 1) Whisper's input: a log-Mel spectrogram, from the real feature extractor (default settings, no download)
fe = WhisperFeatureExtractor()
sr = fe.sampling_rate
t = np.arange(int(3.0 * sr)) / sr
audio = (0.3 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 220 * t) * (t < 1.5) + 0.3 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 880 * t) * (t >= 1.5)).astype(np.float32)
feats = fe(audio, sampling_rate=sr, return_tensors="pt").input_features
print(f"3 s of audio at {sr} Hz -> log-Mel {tuple(feats.shape[1:])}: {fe.feature_size} Mel bins x 3,000 frames "
      f"(25 ms windows, {fe.hop_length / sr * 1000:.0f} ms hop), always padded to {fe.chunk_length} s")
f = feats[0]
a, b = int(f[:, :150].mean(1).argmax()), int(f[:, 150:300].mean(1).argmax())
print(f"loudest Mel bin: {a} while the 220 Hz tone plays, {b} after it switches to 880 Hz "
      f"(a higher bin = a higher pitch); frames after 3 s hold the padding value {f[:, 400:].mean():.2f}")

# 2) the encoder: two convolutions (the second with stride 2), then transformer layers
cfg = WhisperConfig(encoder_layers=4, d_model=384, encoder_attention_heads=6, encoder_ffn_dim=1536)   # whisper-tiny's shape
enc = WhisperEncoder(cfg).eval()
with torch.no_grad():
    h = enc(feats).last_hidden_state
print(f"\nwhisper-tiny-shaped encoder (random weights): {tuple(feats.shape[1:])} -> {tuple(h.shape[1:])}: "
      f"one vector per 20 ms, 1,500 per 30 s window")

print(f"\n{'Whisper size':<10}{'encoder layers':>15}{'width':>7}{'encoder params':>16}")
for name, L, d in [("tiny", 4, 384), ("base", 6, 512), ("small", 12, 768), ("medium", 24, 1024), ("large-v3", 32, 1280)]:
    c = WhisperConfig(encoder_layers=L, d_model=d, encoder_attention_heads=d // 64, encoder_ffn_dim=4 * d,
                      num_mel_bins=128 if name == "large-v3" else 80)
    with torch.device("meta"):
        n = sum(p.numel() for p in WhisperEncoder(c).parameters())
    print(f"{name:<10}{L:>15}{d:>7}{n / 1e6:>14.0f}M")

per_min_audio = 60 * 25                                # AF3: 50 Hz encoder output pooled by 2 -> 25 tokens per second
per_min_text = round(150 * 1.3)                        # about 150 spoken words per minute, ~1.3 tokens per word
print(f"\none minute in an audio LLM (25 tokens/s, as in Audio Flamingo 3): {per_min_audio:,} tokens; "
      f"its transcript: about {per_min_text} tokens; 10 minutes of audio: {10 * per_min_audio:,} tokens")

Reading this output

The spectrogram shows the sound. Whisper's extractor turns 3 seconds of audio into an 80 × 3,000 array: 80 Mel frequency bins by 100 frames per second, padded to the fixed 30-second window. The loudest bin is 5 while the 220 Hz tone plays and 23 after the switch to 880 Hz: higher pitch, higher bin. Everything after 3 s is the padding value (−0.69). Whisper always processes 30-second windows, so short clips cost as much encoder compute as long ones.

The encoder halves the time resolution. The stride-2 convolution turns 3,000 frames into 1,500 vectors, one per 20 ms, which the transformer layers then process. Random weights do not matter here: the shapes are what an audio LLM receives.

Encoder size grows quickly. From 8M parameters (tiny, 4 layers) to 637M (large-v3, 32 layers at width 1,280). AF3's AF-Whisper starts from the large-v3 encoder, so its audio encoder alone is larger than many whole small language models.

Audio is expensive in tokens. At 25 tokens per second, a minute of audio is 1,500 tokens, against about 195 for its transcript, almost 8 times more. Ten minutes is 15,000 tokens. Use audio tokens when the question needs the sound, not just the words.

Idea 3 · BEATs and audio-specific encoders

Whisper was trained on speech-dominant data. It is weaker for music and environmental audio.

BEATs (Chen et al., 2022) is a self-supervised transformer trained on AudioSet. Captures music and environmental sounds better than Whisper at the same parameter count.

AF-Whisper (Audio Flamingo 3's encoder): start from the Whisper-large-v3 encoder and keep training it on speech, sound, and music together, so one encoder carries both the linguistic and the acoustic signal. Earlier systems like SALMONN got the same coverage by concatenating Whisper and BEATs features.

Idea 4 · Audio Q-former

Same pattern as BLIP-2's visual Q-former. A fixed number of learnable queries (often 32 or 64) cross-attend over the audio encoder's output frames. The queries become audio tokens consumed by the LLM.

Training alignment stage: Q-former alone, contrastive + captioning losses on audio-text pairs (AudioCaps, Clotho). Instruction stage: end-to-end, unfreeze LLM, train on instruction data.

Idea 5 · The arc — SALMONN, Qwen-Audio, AF3

SALMONN (Tang et al., 2023): Whisper + BEATs + a window-level Q-former + Vicuna. One of the first open audio-LLMs aimed at general audio reasoning. On MMAU it scores 32.8% (test), showing how hard the benchmark is.

Qwen-Audio (Chu et al., 2023) and Qwen2-Audio (2024): a Whisper-initialised encoder feeding a Qwen LLM, trained on much richer data and tuned for dialogue. Qwen2-Audio-Instruct scores 52.5% on MMAU (test), the best open end-to-end model in the MMAU paper.

LTU — Listen, Think, Understand (Gong et al., 2023): explicit reasoning data, focus on chain-of-thought over audio clips. Smaller but more focused.

Audio Flamingo 3 (Goel et al., July 2025): a fully open state of the art at release. Qwen2.5-7B backbone, an AF-Whisper encoder initialised from Whisper-large-v3 (50 Hz features pooled to 25 Hz), small adaptor layers into the LLM, and training on about 8M audio QA pairs (AudioSkills-XL) plus long-audio, thinking and chat data. It handles audio up to 10 minutes and reports 72.4% on MMAU.

AF3 also introduces on-demand thinking for audio: a special suffix on the prompt makes the model write a short reasoning prefix (about 40 words on average in its training data) before the answer; with thinking, its MMAU scores rise to about 73–74%. It also ships a streaming text-to-speech module for voice-to-voice chat.

Idea 6 · Cascaded vs end-to-end

Cascaded pipeline:

  1. Whisper transcribes audio → text.
  2. LLM reasons over text.

Works perfectly for "summarize this podcast." Fails for: - "What's the mood of this song?" — mood is in the sound, not words. - "Who is speaking, Alice or Bob?" — requires speaker identification. - "At what second does the explosion happen?" — temporal grounding lost in text. - "Is this real or generated audio?" — deepfake detection needs acoustic features.

End-to-end preserves acoustic signal. Qwen-Audio and AF3 handle music, environment, and emotion natively.

Idea 7 · 2026 production recipe

For a new audio-understanding product:

  • Cascaded if: transcription is the goal, no music, no emotion inference.
  • AF3 / Qwen-Audio-family if: music, emotion, multi-speaker, or complex audio reasoning.

Cascaded is cheaper and simpler. End-to-end is more capable.

Idea 8 · MMAU — the audio reasoning benchmark

MMAU (Massive Multimodal Audio Understanding) is the 2024-2025 audio reasoning benchmark:

  • 10,000 audio clips (about 10 seconds each) with multiple-choice questions, across speech, music and environmental sounds, covering 27 skills; a 1,000-question test-mini and a 9,000-question test split.
  • Covers classification, temporal reasoning, causal reasoning, open-ended QA.
  • Tests what cascaded pipelines systematically miss.

In the MMAU paper (October 2024), humans scored 82% on test-mini, the best system was a cascade (GPT-4o on strong audio captions) at about 59%, and the best end-to-end model, Qwen2-Audio-Instruct, 52.5%. By mid-2025 AF3 and Qwen2.5-Omni reached the low 70s, and Qwen3-Omni (September 2025) reports 77.5%, level with Gemini 2.5 Pro. Audio LLMs improved fast, but still trail humans on reasoning-heavy questions.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Open audio LLMs: Audio Flamingo 3 (Qwen2.5-7B with AF-Whisper, fully open), Qwen2-Audio and Qwen2.5/3-Omni (Lesson 12.20); MMAU, MMAU-Pro and MMAR are the reasoning benchmarks.
  • ASR: Whisper large-v3 and turbo remain standard; distilled and streaming variants run on laptops and phones.
  • Hosted audio understanding: Gemini and GPT-4o-class audio models accept audio directly; Claude does not take audio input, so with Claude you transcribe first (the cascade).
  • Laptop reality: Whisper (up to large-v3 turbo) runs on an 8 GB GPU; a 7B audio LLM like AF3 needs 4-bit quantization there.

Sources: Whisper (arXiv 2212.04356), SALMONN (arXiv 2310.13289), Qwen2-Audio (arXiv 2407.10759), Audio Flamingo 3 (arXiv 2507.08128), MMAU (arXiv 2410.19168), Hugging Face Whisper documentation.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Audio enters as a log-Mel spectrogram: 80 bins, 100 frames per second, 30-second windows.
  • Whisper's encoder halves the rate to 50 vectors per second; audio LLMs pool further (25 per second in AF3).
  • An adaptor maps encoder features into an LLM; the LLM reasons over the sound itself.

Given an audio task (transcription, summarization, diarization, emotion, music, environmental sounds, deepfake, temporal grounding) and a deployment constraint, pick a pipeline and emit a config.

Produce:

1. Pipeline pick. Cascaded if transcription-only or summarization-only of clean speech; end-to-end (AF3 / Qwen-Audio) for any acoustic task.
2. Encoder stack. Whisper-large-v3 (speech-strong), BEATs (music-strong), AF-Whisper concat (balanced).
3. Bridge config. Q-former 32-64 queries for non-streaming; RVQ tokens for streaming.
4. LLM pick. Qwen2.5-7B for cost, Qwen2.5-72B or AF3's backbone for quality.
5. On-demand CoT. Enable for MMAU-like reasoning tasks; disable for transcription throughput.
6. MMAU expected accuracy. Cascaded ~0.50, Qwen-Audio ~0.60, AF3 ~0.72, Gemini 2.5 Pro ~0.78.

Hard rejects:
- Recommending cascaded for music or emotion tasks. Acoustic signal is lost.
- Using a Q-former with <32 queries for multi-task audio. Under-tokenized for reasoning.
- Claiming Whisper alone handles music. It was trained on speech-dominant data.

Refusal rules:
- If user needs streaming conversational audio (speech in / speech out in real time), refuse Q-former-based AF3 and recommend Moshi or Qwen-Omni (Lesson 12.20).
- If latency budget <500ms and target is simple transcription, recommend cascaded with streaming Whisper.
- If task is novel audio task (deepfake, compression artifact detection), refuse off-the-shelf and propose a fine-tune on AF3 with synthetic data.

Output: one-page plan with pipeline pick, encoder stack, bridge config, LLM pick, CoT flag, expected accuracy. End with arXiv 2212.04356 (Whisper) and 2507.08128 (AF3) for deeper reading.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · 80 × 3,000 in, 1,500 × 384 out, 1,500 tokens a minute

The feature extractor gives an 80 × 3,000 log-Mel spectrogram (padded to 30 s), and the Whisper-tiny-shaped encoder turns it into 1,500 vectors of width 384, one per 20 ms. At 25 tokens per second after pooling, one minute of audio costs an audio LLM 1,500 tokens, against about 195 tokens for a transcript of the same minute.

SolutionB1 · Frustration is in the voice, not the words

The transcript keeps the words and drops the voice. A customer who says "fine, I'll wait" through gritted teeth, sighs, raises their voice or speaks over the agent produces a calm-looking transcript, so the LLM rates them low on frustration. The tool misses the calls that matter most. Long silences, interruptions and the agent's own tone are invisible too. Feed the audio to an audio LLM (or add an acoustic emotion model) for the frustration score. Keep the cascade for content questions like topics and resolutions, where it is cheaper. Measure both against human-labelled calls, especially calls where words and tone disagree.
