In plain words first
The idea in one line

Vision-native document RAG is one slice. Production multimodal RAG goes wider — retrieving across text, images, audio, and video for workflows like trip planning ("find me a quiet vegan brunch with natural light"), medical triage ("what injury matches this photo + these notes"), e-commerce ("outfits similar to this selfie, in my size"), and field service ("diagnose this engine sound plus photo of the part"). Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means
Cross-modal retrieval "Query one modality, retrieve another" Text query retrieves images; image query retrieves text; requires a shared space or translator
Score fusion "Combine scores" Weighted sum of per-modality retrieval scores; simplest fusion
MoE fusion "Modality-routed experts" Gating network picks which modality's scores to trust per query
Grounded generation "Cite your sources" Each claim in the answer tagged with the source index
MuRAG "First multimodal RAG" 2022 paper that established the multimodal RAG pattern
Agentic multi-hop "Reformulate and retry" LLM re-queries retrievers when first-pass confidence is low

The problem

In plain words first

Many collections hold more than text: product photos with short descriptions, posts with pictures, videos with transcripts, recordings with notes. Multimodal RAG searches all of these together and hands what it finds to a model that writes an answer with sources. Three questions come up. How does a text question find a photo (a shared space such as CLIP's)? How do you combine results from separate retrievers whose scores are on different scales (fusion)? And how does the answer say which photo or passage each claim came from (grounding)? This lesson covers the patterns from the 2025 surveys, then builds a small two-retriever search over 200 real photos with captions. It compares fusion methods, tunes their weights and handles posts that have no photo. No model is trained.

Single-modality RAG is a solved pattern: embed query, embed chunks, retrieve, stuff into LLM. Multimodal RAG requires:

  1. Multiple retrieval heads (each modality needs embeddings in a compatible space).
  2. Fusion of retrieval results across modalities.
  3. Generation grounding that cites sources across modalities.
  4. Evaluation metrics that cover cross-modal signal.

The 2025 surveys all arrive at the same taxonomy.

Idea 1 · Cross-modal retrieval

Retrieve documents of modality B given a query of modality A. Three patterns:

  1. Shared embedding space. CLIP and CLAP produce text + image / text + audio embeddings in a shared space. Cosine similarity across modalities works directly. Limited to CLIP-trained pairs.

  2. Per-modality encoder + translation. Separate text and image encoders plus a small learned module that maps one space into the other, much as BLIP-2's Q-Former bridges an image encoder and an LLM (Lesson 12.3). Flexible but adds a component to train.

  3. VLM as encoder. Use a VLM's hidden states as the retrieval representation. Any modality the VLM supports works. Higher quality, more expensive.

Choice: CLIP / SigLIP 2 for text+image; CLAP for text+audio; VLM-hidden-states for cross-modal at frontier quality.

Idea 2 · Fusion strategies

You retrieved 10 results: 5 images, 3 text passages, 2 audio clips. How do you merge?

Score fusion (cheapest). Each modality has its own retriever, each returns scores. Normalize scores within-modality then sum. Simple, often works.

Attention-based fusion. Concatenate all retrieved items, let a small attention network weight them. Needs training.

MoE fusion. Gating network routes to modality-specific experts. Different query types route differently — a visual question weights images higher.

Production default: score fusion after normalising each retriever's scores per query (min-max or z-score), or reciprocal rank fusion when scores cannot be compared; tune the weights on labelled queries (measured below). Upgrade to a learned gate (MoE-style) only if an A/B test shows clear wins on your domain.

The same idea as a formula

Per-query normalisation. For retriever m with scores sm​(q,d) over the candidate set:

s^m​(q,d)=maxd′​sm​(q,d′)−mind′​sm​(q,d′)sm​(q,d)−mind′​sm​(q,d′)​∈[0,1].

Weighted score fusion: s(q,d)=∑m​wm​s^m​(q,d), with ∑m​wm​=1.

Reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), which uses only each retriever's rank rm​(d) of the item:

RRF(d)=m∑​k+rm​(d)1​,k=60 by convention.

R-precision: if a query has R relevant items, the share of the top R results that are relevant. It equals recall@R.

Worked example
Three ways to add two scores
  • One query, two posts. Caption cosines (bge) span 0.32 to 0.80 across the collection; photo cosines (CLIP) span 0.07 to 0.36. Post A: caption 0.70, photo 0.10. Post B: caption 0.40, photo 0.34.
  • Raw sum: A =0.80, B =0.74. A wins, because the caption scores vary more and so dominate the sum.
  • Min-max, then 0.5 / 0.5: A =0.5⋅0.480.70−0.32​+0.5⋅0.290.10−0.07​=0.40+0.05=0.45. B =0.5⋅0.17+0.5⋅0.93=0.08+0.47=0.55. B wins: its photo match is near the top of its scale.
  • RRF with ranks (1, 30) for A and (5, 5) for B: A =611​+901​=0.0275, B =652​=0.0308. B wins: RRF rewards items that both retrievers rank well, and ignores how confident either was.
Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Cosine scores from two models can simply be added."
    ✓ Each model has its own scale; the one whose scores vary more decides the ranking.
  • ✗ "Reciprocal rank fusion is always the safe choice."
    ✓ It throws away confidence, so a retriever that is guessing gets as much say as one that knows.
  • ✗ "Multimodal RAG needs one model trained on every pair of modalities."
    ✓ Separate retrievers plus fusion work; a shared space is needed only where a query in one modality must find items in another.
  • ✗ "If the prompt asks for citations, the answer's sources are right."
    ✓ Check them in code: every cited label must exist among the retrieved items.
Predict before you run

200 posts, each a real photo with a caption. Most captions are vague ("Weekend vibes"), and almost half the photos are tiny and blurred. A caption retriever and a photo retriever each answer 10 queries. Which one does better alone, and does fusing them beat both? Which fusion method wins? (Solution P1.)

See it drawn
Two retrievers and three ways to fuse them

200 Imagenette photos (10 classes, 20 each) become community posts. About 4 in 10 captions name what is in the photo; the rest are vague. About half the photos are shrunk to 8×8 pixels and scaled back up. bge-small searches the captions; CLIP ViT-B/32 searches the photos from the text query (cross-modal). The table shows R-precision for each retriever alone and for three fusion methods. About half a minute on CPU.

import numpy as np, torch
from datasets import load_dataset
from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
from transformers import CLIPModel, CLIPProcessor, logging
logging.set_verbosity_error()
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# 200 community posts: a real photo (Imagenette, 10 classes x 20) plus the caption its poster wrote.
ds = load_dataset("Multimodal-Fatima/Imagenette_validation", split="validation").shuffle(seed=0)
classes = ds.features["label"].names
idx = [i for c in range(10) for i in np.where(np.array(ds["label"]) == c)[0][:20]]
posts = ds.select(idx)
labels = np.array(posts["label"])
SAYS = {  # how people describe each class in a caption
    "tench": ["Caught a big tench at the lake this morning", "Green tench, released after the photo"],
    "English springer": ["Our springer spaniel loves the beach", "Springer puppy, eight weeks old"],
    "cassette player": ["Old cassette player, still plays tapes", "Portable tape player from the 80s"],
    "chain saw": ["Petrol chainsaw, starts first pull", "Cutting logs with the chainsaw"],
    "church": ["Village church on a Sunday morning", "The old stone chapel near our house"],
    "French horn": ["Brass French horn, school band size", "Horn practice before the concert"],
    "garbage truck": ["Bin lorry doing the morning collection", "Refuse truck on our street"],
    "gas pump": ["Vintage petrol pump, restored", "Fuel pump at the old station"],
    "golf ball": ["Box of used golf balls, mixed brands", "Found these golf balls in the rough"],
    "parachute": ["First tandem skydive, canopy opened perfectly", "Landing at the drop zone"]}
VAGUE = ["What a day!", "Look what I found", "Weekend vibes", "Can't believe this", "Throwback",
         "New post", "Saturday morning", "Guess what this is", "Not bad at all", "Sharing this one"]
# Half the photos are poor: tiny, blurry phone shots (8 x 8 pixels, scaled back up).
from PIL import Image
poor = rng.random(len(posts)) < 0.5
photos = [(r.convert("RGB").resize((8, 8)).resize((224, 224), Image.BILINEAR) if p else r.convert("RGB"))
          for r, p in zip(posts["image"], poor)]
captions = [rng.choice(SAYS[classes[c]]) if rng.random() < 0.4 else rng.choice(VAGUE) for c in labels]
print(f"{len(posts)} posts; {sum(c not in VAGUE for c in captions)} captions say what is in the photo, "
      f"the rest are vague (e.g. {VAGUE[2]!r}); {poor.sum()} photos are tiny and blurred")

queries = ["freshwater fish someone caught", "a spaniel dog", "device for playing tapes", "power tool for cutting trees",
           "a place of worship", "brass musical instrument", "waste collection vehicle",
           "where you fill up a car with fuel", "golf equipment", "skydiving"]       # query c wants class c

# Text retriever over captions (bge-small) and cross-modal retriever over photos (CLIP ViT-B/32).
bge = SentenceTransformer("BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5", device="cpu")
cap = bge.encode(captions, normalize_embeddings=True)
qt = bge.encode(["Represent this sentence for searching relevant passages: " + q for q in queries], normalize_embeddings=True)
S_text = qt @ cap.T
clip = CLIPModel.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32").eval()
proc = CLIPProcessor.from_pretrained("openai/clip-vit-base-patch32")
with torch.no_grad():
    im = torch.cat([clip.get_image_features(**proc(images=photos[i:i + 50],
                                                   return_tensors="pt")).pooler_output for i in range(0, 200, 50)])
    qi = clip.get_text_features(**proc(text=["a photo of " + q for q in queries], return_tensors="pt", padding=True)).pooler_output
im = torch.nn.functional.normalize(im, dim=-1).numpy(); qi = torch.nn.functional.normalize(qi, dim=-1).numpy()
S_img = qi @ im.T

def minmax(S):                         # rescale each query's scores to [0, 1]
    lo, hi = S.min(1, keepdims=True), S.max(1, keepdims=True)
    return (S - lo) / (hi - lo)
def rrf(*Ss, k=60):                    # reciprocal rank fusion: only ranks matter
    return sum(1 / (k + 1 + np.argsort(np.argsort(-S, 1), 1)) for S in Ss)
def r_prec(S):                         # each query has 20 right posts: what share of the top 20 is right?
    top = np.argsort(-S, 1)[:, :20]
    return np.mean([(labels[top[c]] == c).mean() for c in range(10)])

print(f"\n{'retrieval':<40}{'R-precision':>13}")
for name, S in (("captions only (bge-small)", S_text), ("photos only (CLIP text -> image)", S_img),
                ("fusion: raw cosine sum", S_text + S_img),
                ("fusion: min-max, then 0.5 / 0.5", 0.5 * minmax(S_text) + 0.5 * minmax(S_img)),
                ("fusion: reciprocal rank (k = 60)", rrf(S_text, S_img))):
    print(f"{name:<40}{r_prec(S):>12.0%}")
findable = np.mean(~poor | np.array([c not in VAGUE for c in captions]))
print(f"ceiling: {findable:.0%} of posts have a clear photo or an informative caption")
print(f"\ncosine ranges: captions {S_text.min():.2f} to {S_text.max():.2f}, photos {S_img.min():.2f} to {S_img.max():.2f}")

Reading this output

Each retriever alone sees only part of the collection. Captions find 36% of the right posts in each query's top 20: only 72 of the 200 captions say what the photo shows. CLIP finds 62% from the photos, even though 88 of them are blurred to 8×8 pixels; a clear photo is a strong signal, and some blurred ones keep enough colour and shape.

A raw sum is worse than the photos alone. Adding the two cosines gives 61%. Caption scores spread from 0.32 to 0.80 and photo scores only from 0.07 to 0.36, so the weaker caption retriever decides most of the ranking.

Normalising first helps. Min-max scaling per query, then equal weights, reaches 65%, above both retrievers alone.

Reciprocal rank fusion falls behind. RRF gives 55%. It uses only ranks, and for the many vague captions the caption ranks are noise ("Weekend vibes" is as close to "skydiving" as to anything else). RRF counts that noise as much as the photo ranks.

The ceiling is 70%. Only 70% of the posts have either a clear photo or an informative caption; no fusion can find the rest, because neither modality says what they are.

Tuning the fusion, and posts with no photo

This block continues from the one above and reuses its scores. It sweeps the photo weight, tries a per-query weight taken from each retriever's confidence, and removes the photo from 30% of the posts to compare three ways of scoring a missing modality. Instant.

# Continues from the block above: S_text, S_img, minmax, rrf, r_prec, labels and poor are already defined.
import numpy as np

T, I = minmax(S_text), minmax(S_img)
print("1. One fixed weight for every query")
for w in (0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 1.0):
    print(f"   photo weight {w:.2f}: R-precision {r_prec((1 - w) * T + w * I):.0%}")

def confidence(S, top=20):            # how far the best scores stand out from the rest, per query
    z = (S - S.mean(1, keepdims=True)) / S.std(1, keepdims=True)
    return np.sort(z, 1)[:, -top:].mean(1)
ct, ci = confidence(S_text), confidence(S_img)
w_img = (ci / (ct + ci))[:, None]
print(f"\n2. A weight per query, from each retriever's confidence (no training)")
print(f"   photo weights chosen: {' '.join(f'{w:.2f}' for w in w_img.ravel())}")
print(f"   R-precision {r_prec((1 - w_img) * T + w_img * I):.0%}")

print("\n3. 30% of posts have no photo at all (photo weight 0.6)")
missing = np.random.default_rng(1).random(len(labels)) < 0.3
I_m = I.copy(); I_m[:, missing] = np.nan
fills = {"missing photo scores 0": np.nan_to_num(I_m, nan=0.0),
         "missing photo scores the query's median": np.where(np.isnan(I_m), np.nanmedian(I_m, 1, keepdims=True), I_m)}
for name, Ifill in fills.items():
    print(f"   {name:<42} R-precision {r_prec(0.4 * T + 0.6 * Ifill):.0%}")
avg = np.where(np.isnan(I_m), T, 0.4 * T + 0.6 * np.nan_to_num(I_m))   # use the caption alone where there is no photo
print(f"   {'missing photo: caption score alone':<42} R-precision {r_prec(avg):.0%}")
print(f"   posts with no photo among each query's top 20: "
      f"{np.mean([missing[np.argsort(-avg[c])[:20]].mean() for c in range(10)]):.0%} (overall {missing.mean():.0%})")

Reading this output

The best fixed weight reaches the ceiling. R-precision rises from 36% (captions only) to 70% at a photo weight of 0.75, then falls to 62% with photos only. 70% is the ceiling from the first run. But that weight was chosen on the same 10 queries it is scored on, so the number is optimistic: tune weights on one set of labelled queries and report on another.

Confidence-based weights did not help here. Measuring how far each retriever's top scores stand out gave weights close to 0.5 for every query, and the result (65%) equals plain equal weights. Both retrievers looked equally "sure" on these queries, even though one was usually guessing. A gate that works needs labelled data to learn from, which is what MoE-style fusion trains.

A missing photo must be handled on purpose. Scoring a missing photo as 0 drops R-precision to 53%, because every post without a photo sinks. Using the query's median photo score gives 59%, and using the caption score alone gives 58%. Even then, posts without photos make up 19% of the top results against 28% of the collection: they are still under-ranked, so check the share of each kind of item in your results.

Idea 3 · Generation grounding

The LLM should cite which retrieved item drove each claim. For multi-modal:

  • Text source: standard citation [1].
  • Image source: [img 3] with a short caption.
  • Audio: [audio 2 at 0:34].

Frontier VLMs cite reliably when the prompt numbers each source and asks for those labels; fine-tuning on answers tagged with source indices helps smaller models. Either way, check citations in code: every label in the answer must exist among the retrieved items, and a claim with no label is unsupported.

Idea 4 · The 2025 surveys

Abootorabi et al. (arXiv:2502.08826, "Ask in Any Modality"): taxonomy for multimodal RAG. Covers retrieval, fusion, generation. Broadest coverage.

Mei et al. (arXiv:2504.08748, "A Survey on Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation"): organises the field in stages, from converting everything to captions, to keeping native images and video, to document screenshots with search planning. Covers parsing, search planning, retrieval, generation, datasets and evaluation. Useful for evaluation design.

Zheng et al. (arXiv:2503.18016, "Retrieval Augmented Generation and Understanding in Vision"): vision-focused survey covering visual understanding (including document understanding), visual generation and embodied AI.

Reading all three gives you the state of the art as of spring 2025. Most of the sub-problems are still open.

Idea 5 · MuRAG — the foundational paper

MuRAG (Chen et al., Google, 2022) described itself as the first multimodal retrieval-augmented transformer: a ViT plus T5 model (527M parameters) that retrieves images and text from a memory and generates answers, 10–20 points better than earlier models on WebQA and MultimodalQA. Showed feasibility before the VLM wave. Modern systems (VisRAG, M3DocRAG) follow the same retrieve-then-generate pattern with VLMs.

Idea 6 · A production trip-planner example

Query: "find me a quiet vegan brunch with natural light."

Pipeline:

  1. Decompose query. "quiet" → audio/review keyword; "vegan brunch" → menu item; "natural light" → image feature.
  2. Retrieve per modality: - Text retrieval on reviews: "vegan brunch, quiet ambiance." - Image retrieval on restaurant photos: "natural light, airy." - Audio retrieval on ambient-sound clips: "low decibel, no music."
  3. Fuse scores. Each restaurant has a composite score.
  4. Top-k restaurants → VLM generator with all evidence → answer with citations.

This is well beyond text-RAG. Each modality adds signal that text alone misses.

Idea 7 · Agentic multimodal RAG

Multi-hop: if the first retrieval does not return high-confidence answers, the LLM reformulates and retrieves again. Agentic RAG patterns from Phase 14 apply here. Examples:

  • Retrieve initial top-10 → LLM asks "too noisy, filter for <40 dB" → re-retrieve.
  • Retrieve images → LLM sees one has a menu → retrieve the menu text → answer.

Adds complexity but handles queries that single-shot retrieval cannot.

Idea 8 · Evaluation

Cross-modal evaluation is still immature. Common proxies:

  • Recall@k per modality.
  • Fused top-k accuracy.
  • Human-judged end-to-end satisfaction.
  • Task-specific (bookings completed, purchases made).

No standard benchmark spans all modalities. Most papers evaluate on domain-specific tasks.

The audio model's cosines all sit between 0.85 and 0.95, while text scores spread from 0.3 to 0.8. Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Hybrid search with built-in fusion is standard in search engines and vector databases: Elasticsearch, OpenSearch, Qdrant and Weaviate offer reciprocal rank fusion and score-based fusion of several retrievers in one query.
  • Shared text–image spaces: SigLIP 2 and OpenCLIP models for self-hosted search (CLIP B/32 and SigLIP 2 base run on a laptop CPU); hosted multimodal embedding APIs such as Cohere Embed v4 and Voyage's multimodal models embed text and images in one space.
  • Captions as a bridge: many production systems caption or transcribe images, audio and video with a VLM or speech model at indexing time and search the text, keeping the original media for the generator. The Mei et al. survey calls this the first stage of multimodal RAG.
  • VLM generators with numbered sources: Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL (2B–8B fit a laptop GPU, larger ones in 4-bit) or hosted VLMs read the retrieved images and text, and cite them by label; the application checks the labels.

Sources: Abootorabi et al. (arXiv 2502.08826), Mei et al. (arXiv 2504.08748), Zheng et al. (arXiv 2503.18016), MuRAG (arXiv 2210.02928), CLIP (arXiv 2103.00020), SigLIP 2 (arXiv 2502.14786).

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Multimodal RAG = retrievers per modality (or a shared space) + fusion + a generator that cites sources.
  • A shared space (CLIP, CLAP) lets a text query find photos or sounds directly.
  • Never add raw scores from different models; normalise per query first.
  • RRF needs no calibration but ignores confidence; weighted fusion needs labelled queries to tune.
  • Decide explicitly how to score an item that lacks a modality.
  • Check every citation in code.

Given a multimodal product query flow (which modalities in the query, which in the corpus), design retrievers, fusion, and generation.

Produce:

1. Per-modality retrievers. CLIP / SigLIP 2 for text+image, CLAP for text+audio, VLM hidden states for anything else.
2. Fusion pick. Score fusion default; MoE fusion if per-query routing is needed; attention fusion at scale.
3. Grounded generator. Qwen2.5-VL or Claude 4.7 with training on source-tagged outputs.
4. Evaluation. Recall@k per modality + fused top-k accuracy + human-judged end-to-end.
5. Agentic multi-hop. When to re-query; confidence threshold to trigger.
6. Storage estimate. Per-modality vector counts and compression.

Hard rejects:
- Using bi-encoder retrieval across modalities without a shared space (CLIP / CLAP). Scores are meaningless.
- Proposing MoE fusion without training data. MoE needs supervision to route correctly.
- Claiming score-fusion weights transfer across domains. They do not.

Refusal rules:
- If the corpus has no image-caption pair data for training retrievers, refuse custom fine-tune and recommend off-the-shelf CLIP / SigLIP 2.
- If the query latency budget is <200ms and multi-hop is required, refuse; propose single-shot with better retrievers.
- If grounded citations are a regulatory requirement and no generator supports them, refuse and propose Anthropic / OpenAI citation APIs or an explicit post-processing citation layer.

Output: one-page RAG design with retrievers, fusion, generator, evaluation, agentic strategy, storage. End with arXiv 2502.08826, 2504.08748, 2503.18016.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Photos win alone; tuned fusion wins overall; RRF disappoints

The photo retriever wins alone: 62% R-precision against 36% for captions, because a clear photo shows the object while most captions say nothing about it. Fusion beats both only if the scores are made comparable first: the raw sum (61%) is slightly worse than photos alone, min-max with equal weights reaches 65%, and a tuned photo weight of 0.75 reaches 70%, the ceiling set by posts with neither a clear photo nor a useful caption.

Reciprocal rank fusion came last of the fusion methods (55%). It treats the caption retriever's ranks as just as meaningful as the photo ranks, even for vague captions where they are noise.

SolutionB1 · Spread decides, and equal weights amplify noise

With a raw sum, the audio retriever contributes almost nothing to the ranking. Its scores differ by at most 0.1 between clips, while text scores differ by up to 0.5, so text decides the order. The high level of the audio scores does not matter: adding nearly the same amount to every item does not change who is first. If audio is the most useful signal for the query ("engine knocking sound"), it is ignored.

Min-max scaling every retriever and weighting them equally overcorrects. Now the audio retriever's tiny differences are stretched to the full 0–1 range, as large as the text differences. When the query has nothing to do with sound, those differences are noise, and the noise gets a third of the vote.

What works: normalise per query, then set the weights from labelled queries (held out from the evaluation), per query type if needed. Or learn a gate on those labels, which is what MoE-style fusion does. Measure each retriever alone too: if one never helps, remove it.
