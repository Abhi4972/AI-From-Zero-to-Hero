In plain words first
The idea in one line

Score every sentence of Pride and Prejudice for sentiment and list the most positive and most negative ones.

The problem

A lexicon-based sentiment scorer gives each sentence a polarity from -1 to 1. Looking at the sentences it rates as perfectly positive or negative is a quick way to see what such a model gets right and wrong.

The book's text must be downloaded first, so this project is shown, not run.

Step 1 · Score each sentence and list the extremes

Download the book's plain text from Project Gutenberg, save it as pride.txt, then run:

from textblob import TextBlob

# You should download the book text, clean it, and import it here
with open("pride.txt", encoding="utf8") as f:
    file_contents = f.read()

book_pride = TextBlob(file_contents)
positive_sentiment_sentences = []
negative_sentiment_sentences = []

for sentence in book_pride.sentences:
    if sentence.sentiment.polarity == 1:
        positive_sentiment_sentences.append(sentence)
    if sentence.sentiment.polarity == -1:
        negative_sentiment_sentences.append(sentence)

print("The " + str(len(positive_sentiment_sentences)) + " most positive sentences:")
for sentence in positive_sentiment_sentences:
    print("+ " + str(sentence.replace("\n", "").replace("      ", " ")))

print("The " + str(len(negative_sentiment_sentences)) + " most negative sentences:")
for sentence in negative_sentiment_sentences:
    print("- " + str(sentence.replace("\n", "").replace("      ", " ")))

Exercises

  1. How many sentences score exactly 1 or -1? Read ten of each. How many would a human agree with?
  2. Average polarity per chapter and plot it across the book.
  3. Run the same sentences through NLTK's VADER. Where do the two scorers disagree most?
Recap
What to keep
  • Lexicon sentiment is fast and explainable but blind to context.
  • Reading the extremes is a quick quality check.
  • Compare two scorers before trusting either.
