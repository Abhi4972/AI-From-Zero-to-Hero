In plain words first
The idea in one line

Every voice agent lives or dies on two decisions: is the user speaking now, and are they done? VAD answers the first. Turn-detection (VAD + silence-hangover + semantic endpoint model) answers the second. Get either wrong and your assistant either cuts users off or never shuts up.

The problem

In plain words first

A voice assistant hears a continuous stream of sound and must decide, many times a second, two things: is someone speaking right now? and have they finished? The first is voice activity detection (VAD): a small model labels each 20–32 ms frame as speech or not. The second is turn detection, and it is harder, because people pause in the middle of sentences. The simple rule, "wait for N milliseconds of silence", forces a trade: a short wait interrupts people, a long wait makes the assistant feel slow. Production systems add a third piece, a model that judges from the words or the sound of the voice whether the thought is complete. This lesson measures each piece on real LibriSpeech speech: an energy threshold, WebRTC VAD and Silero VAD in three kinds of noise; how often fixed silence timers cut a speaker off; and Pipecat's open Smart Turn model, plus the scoring rule behind LiveKit's text model. Nothing is trained.

Three distinct decisions a voice agent makes on every 20 ms chunk:

  1. Is this frame speech? — VAD. Binary, per-frame.
  2. Has the user started a new utterance? — onset detection.
  3. Has the user finished? — end-pointing (turn-end).

The naive answer (energy threshold) fails on any noise — traffic, keyboards, crowd babble. The 2026 answer: Silero VAD (open, deep-learned) + a turn-detection model (semantic endpointing) + a VAD-calibrated silence hangover.

The idea

The same idea as a formula

Frame decisions. Split audio into frames xt​. Energy VAD scores et​=10log10​(N1​∑n​xt​[n]2) dBFS; Silero outputs pt​∈[0,1]. A threshold τ turns a score into a decision y^​t​=[st​>τ].

ROC and TPR at fixed FPR. With true labels yt​:

TPR(τ)=#{t:yt​=1}#{t:y^​t​=1, yt​=1}​,FPR(τ)=#{t:yt​=0}#{t:y^​t​=1, yt​=0}​

Sweeping τ traces the ROC curve; "TPR at 5% FPR" picks the τ that flags 5% of non-speech frames and reports how much speech is caught.

Mixing at a chosen SNR. To add noise n to speech s at SNR ρ dB, scale the noise by

g=Pn​⋅10ρ/10Ps​​​,Ps​=s2 over speech frames,Pn​=n2

The turn-end trade-off. With a silence hangover h, a turn is cut off if any pause inside it lasts at least h:

P(cut off)=P(kmax​pausek​≥h),reply delay≥h+VAD lag+STT finalisation

Raising h lowers the first and raises the second. A semantic model replaces one fixed h with a short wait when the turn sounds complete and a long one when it does not.

Worked example
A latency budget by hand

Time from the end of the user's speech to a final transcript (before the LLM starts):

  • Fixed timer + streaming STT with 500 ms delay (Kyutai STT 1B): 500 ms hangover + 500 ms STT delay = 1,000 ms.
  • Add the flush trick: the STT processes its 500 ms buffer at about 4× real time, so 500 + 500/4 = 625 ms.
  • Semantic turn detection + flush: wait 200 ms of silence, run a turn model (Pipecat reports about 65 ms for Smart Turn on its cloud instances), then flush: 200 + 65 + 125 = 390 ms, when the model says "done". When it says "not done", fall back to a longer wait (LiveKit's default maximum is 3 s).

Then add the LLM's time to first token and the TTS's time to first audio; Lesson 6.12 measured those.

Idea 1 · The three-tier VAD cascade

Tier 1: energy gate. Cheapest. Threshold RMS at -40 dBFS. Filters obvious silence but fires on any noise above the threshold.

Tier 2: Silero VAD (MIT licence; the silero-vad package is at version 6). About 0.46 million parameters, trained (its authors say) on data covering over 6,000 languages, and under 1 ms per 32 ms chunk on one CPU thread (0.7 ms on this page's machine). The open-source default, and the VAD that LiveKit Agents and Pipecat ship with.

Tier 3: semantic turn detector. LiveKit's turn-detection model (text, reads the transcript), Pipecat's Smart Turn (audio, hears intonation and pace), the end-of-turn signals built into streaming STT services, or your own small classifier. Distinguishes "pause mid-sentence" from "done talking." Uses linguistic context (intonation + recent words), not just silence.

Idea 2 · Key parameters and their defaults

  • Threshold. Silero outputs a probability; classify speech at > 0.5 (default) or > 0.3 (sensitive). Lower threshold = fewer first-word clips, more false positives.
  • Minimum speech duration. Reject speech shorter than 250 ms — usually coughs or chair noise.
  • Silence hangover (end-pointing). After VAD returns to 0, wait 500-800 ms before declaring end-of-turn. Too short → interrupt user. Too long → feels sluggish.
  • Pre-roll buffer. Keep 300-500 ms of audio before VAD fires. Prevents "hey" being clipped.

Idea 3 · The flush trick (Kyutai 2025)

Streaming STT models have a look-ahead delay (500 ms for Kyutai STT-1B, 2.5 s for STT-2.6B). Normally you'd wait that long after end-of-speech for the transcript. Flush trick: when VAD fires end-of-speech, send a flush signal to the STT that forces immediate output. STT processes at ~4× realtime, so the 500 ms buffer finishes in ~125 ms.

End-to-end: 125 ms VAD + flush STT = conversational latency.

Step 1 · the energy gate

def energy_vad(chunk, threshold_dbfs=-40.0):
    rms = (sum(x * x for x in chunk) / len(chunk)) ** 0.5
    dbfs = 20.0 * math.log10(max(rms, 1e-10))
    return dbfs > threshold_dbfs

Energy, WebRTC and Silero in noise, measured

Ten LibriSpeech utterances joined with random 0.4–1.5 s gaps (130 s), labelled speech or non-speech from the clean audio's energy (pauses under 200 ms filled in, 64 ms either side of each boundary not scored). import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

raw = db > db.max() - 35                   # speech = within 35 dB of the loudest frame...
from scipy.ndimage import binary_closing, binary_dilation
lab = binary_closing(raw, np.ones(7))      # ...with pauses under ~200 ms filled in
edge = binary_dilation(lab ^ np.roll(lab, 1), np.ones(5))
keep = ~edge                               # a 64 ms "collar" either side of each boundary is not scored
truth_all = lab
print(f"{len(clean)/16000:.0f} s of audio, {lab.mean():.0%} of frames are speech, {keep.mean():.0%} scored")

def pink(n):
    spec = np.fft.rfft(rng.standard_normal(n)); spec /= np.sqrt(np.arange(1, len(spec) + 1))
    return np.fft.irfft(spec, n).astype(np.float32)
def keyboard_and_fan(n):
    x = 0.3 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * 120 * np.arange(n) / 16000) + 0.2 * rng.standard_normal(n)
    for t in rng.integers(0, n - 800, size=int(n / 16000 * 6)):          # ~6 key clicks per second
        x[t:t + 800] += 4 * rng.standard_normal(800) * np.exp(-np.arange(800) / 120)
    return x.astype(np.float32)
def at_snr(noise, snr_db):
    p_s = (clean[np.repeat(lab, FR)] ** 2).mean(); p_n = (noise ** 2).mean()
    return clean + noise * np.sqrt(p_s / (p_n * 10 ** (snr_db / 10)))

conditions = {"clean": clean, "pink noise, 10 dB SNR": at_snr(pink(len(clean)), 10),
              "pink noise, 0 dB SNR": at_snr(pink(len(clean)), 0),
              "keyboard + fan, 5 dB SNR": at_snr(keyboard_and_fan(len(clean)), 5)}

truth = lab[keep]
def tpr_at_fpr(score, fpr_target=0.05):
    thr = np.quantile(score[~truth], 1 - fpr_target)           # threshold that lets 5% of non-speech through
    return (score[truth] > thr).mean()
def roc(score):
    ts = np.quantile(score, np.linspace(0, 1, 200))
    return [(score[~truth] > t).mean() for t in ts], [(score[truth] > t).mean() for t in ts]

Reading this output

On the clean recordings all three work. As noise rises, the energy threshold collapses: at 5% false positives it catches 70% of speech frames at 10 dB SNR and 25% at 0 dB, because loud noise frames are as energetic as speech. WebRTC VAD's fixed modes either flag almost everything in heavy noise (100% of non-speech at 0 dB in modes 0–2) or, in mode 3, still flag about a quarter; it has no threshold to tune. Silero stays at 95–99% TPR at 5% FPR in every condition, and its default threshold of 0.5 keeps false positives at 0–3%. Picovoice's figure for Silero (87.7%) is lower than ours: real DEMAND noise is harder than synthetic pink noise, and our labels are derived from clean audio. Absolute numbers depend on the test set; the ranking is what generalises.

Step 3 · turn-end state machine

class TurnDetector:
    def __init__(self, silence_hangover_ms=500, min_speech_ms=250):
        self.state = "idle"
        self.speech_ms = 0
        self.silence_ms = 0
        self.silence_hangover_ms = silence_hangover_ms
        self.min_speech_ms = min_speech_ms

    def update(self, is_speech, chunk_ms=20):
        if is_speech:
            self.speech_ms += chunk_ms
            self.silence_ms = 0
            if self.state == "idle" and self.speech_ms >= self.min_speech_ms:
                self.state = "speaking"
                return "START"
        else:
            self.silence_ms += chunk_ms
            if self.state == "speaking" and self.silence_ms >= self.silence_hangover_ms:
                self.state = "idle"
                self.speech_ms = 0
                return "END"
        return None

Reading this output

Read speech is full of long pauses: the median internal pause is about 290 ms and the 90th percentile about 670 ms. A 200 ms hangover would cut half of these utterances off mid-sentence; 500 ms, a quarter; 800 ms, 5%; only at 1–1.5 s does it reach almost zero, which means the assistant waits a second or more before every reply. Conversational speech has fewer long pauses than audiobook narration but more hesitations ("um, let me see..."), so the same trade-off appears. No single number is right, which is why production systems add a semantic model.

Step 4 · the flush trick skeleton

def flush_on_end(stt_client, audio_buffer):
    stt_client.send_audio(audio_buffer)
    stt_client.send_flush()
    return stt_client.recv_transcript(timeout_ms=150)

STT (Kyutai, Deepgram, AssemblyAI) must support flush for this to work. Whisper streaming does not — it's block-based and always waits for chunks.

Semantic turn detection: an audio model and a text scoring rule

Audio: Pipecat's Smart Turn v3 (Whisper-tiny encoder with a linear head, 8 MB ONNX, BSD-2 licence) on every moment a 200 ms silence timer would fire in the LibriSpeech utterances: at natural pauses (the reader is not done) and at the true ends, plus cuts in the middle of speech as a sanity check. Text: LiveKit's scoring rule, the probability that the user's turn closes next, applied to the untuned Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct. (LiveKit's own model may only be used inside LiveKit Agents, so it is not run here.)

import re, onnxruntime as ort
from huggingface_hub import hf_hub_download
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score
from transformers import WhisperFeatureExtractor, AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM

# Audio model: Pipecat's Smart Turn v3 (Whisper-tiny encoder + linear head, 8 MB ONNX, BSD-2 licence).
st = ort.InferenceSession(hf_hub_download("pipecat-ai/smart-turn-v3", "smart-turn-v3.2-cpu.onnx"))
fe = WhisperFeatureExtractor(chunk_length=8)
def smart_turn(audio):
    audio = audio[-8 * 16000:]
    audio = np.pad(audio, (8 * 16000 - len(audio), 0))            # left-pad: the turn's audio sits at the end
    f = fe(audio, sampling_rate=16000, return_tensors="np", padding="max_length",
           max_length=8 * 16000, truncation=True, do_normalize=True).input_features.astype(np.float32)
    return float(st.run(None, {"input_features": f})[0].ravel()[0])

# Every moment a 200 ms silence timer would fire: each internal pause of 200 ms or more (the reader is NOT done)
# and the end of each utterance (done). Plus, as a sanity check, cuts in the middle of speech with no pause.
STOP = 0.2
pad = np.zeros(int(STOP * 16000), np.float32)
ends, pauses_mid, cuts = [], [], []
for i, seg in enumerate(segs):
    x = clip(i)
    for a, b in zip(seg, seg[1:]):
        if (b["start"] - a["end"]) / 16000 >= STOP:
            pauses_mid.append((i, smart_turn(x[: a["end"] + len(pad)])))
    ends.append(smart_turn(np.concatenate([x[: seg[-1]["end"]], pad])))
    if seg[-1]["end"] > 56000:
        cuts.append(smart_turn(np.concatenate([x[: seg[-1]["end"] - 24000], pad])))
p_mid = np.array([p for _, p in pauses_mid]); ends = np.array(ends); cuts = np.array(cuts)
print("Smart Turn v3 says 'done' (p > 0.5) at:")
print(f"  {len(ends):3d} true ends of utterances       {np.mean(ends > 0.5):5.0%}")
print(f"  {len(p_mid):3d} natural mid-sentence pauses   {np.mean(p_mid > 0.5):5.0%}")
print(f"  {len(cuts):3d} cuts in the middle of speech  {np.mean(cuts > 0.5):5.0%}")
print(f"  AUC, true ends vs natural pauses: {roc_auc_score([1]*len(ends) + [0]*len(p_mid), np.r_[ends, p_mid]):.2f}")

# Turn-end policy: reply 200 ms after speech stops if Smart Turn says 'done', otherwise wait for a 1 s fallback.
cut_off = len({i for i, p in pauses_mid if p > 0.5}) / len(segs)
print(f"Silero + Smart Turn: {cut_off:.0%} of turns cut off mid-sentence, mean wait at the true end "
      f"{np.where(ends > 0.5, 200, 1000).mean():.0f} ms")

tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct")
lm = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct").eval()
END = tok.convert_tokens_to_ids("<|im_end|>")
def p_end_of_turn(text, agent="How can I help you today?"):
    text = re.sub(r"[^\w\s'-]", "", text.lower()).strip()            # LiveKit-style normalisation
    prompt = tok.apply_chat_template([{"role": "assistant", "content": agent}, {"role": "user", "content": text}], tokenize=False)
    prompt = prompt[: prompt.rfind("<|im_end|>")]                   # leave the user's turn open
    with torch.no_grad():
        logits = lm(**tok(prompt, return_tensors="pt")).logits[0, -1]
    return float(torch.softmax(logits, -1)[END])

Reading this output

Smart Turn does separate speech cut off mid-flow (39% "done") from true ends (75%), so it hears that a voice is still mid-word. But at the natural pauses of audiobook narration it says "done" 64% of the time, almost as often as at true ends (AUC 0.57, where 0.5 is chance): a reader's pause at a clause boundary sounds final. Used as the only turn signal, it would cut off 40% of these utterances, worse than a fixed 500 ms timer (25%), although its mean wait at true ends is shorter. Pipecat's published benchmark on conversational speech reports 4.7% false positives; that our result is far worse shows how much the domain matters, so measure on audio like your users'. The text rule shows the idea behind LiveKit's model: even untuned, the LM gives the finished version a higher end-of-turn probability in 4 of 6 pairs, but every probability is around 10⁻⁹ to 10⁻¹¹, because an untuned chat model expects punctuation and a closing turn. LiveKit fine-tunes this same base model (distilling a 7B teacher) so that the probability becomes a usable signal. In production, the two kinds of signal are combined with VAD and a maximum wait.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Silero VAD remains the default open VAD; LiveKit Agents and Pipecat both ship it as their VAD.
  • Semantic turn detection is standard in voice-agent frameworks. LiveKit's turn detector (text; fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, distilled from a 7B teacher; licensed for use only within LiveKit Agents) and Pipecat's Smart Turn v3 (audio; 8 MB, BSD-2; run once the VAD reports silence).
  • Hosted APIs build it in. OpenAI's Realtime API offers server_vad (silence-based) and semantic_vad (a classifier on the user's words, with an eagerness setting); Deepgram's Flux STT emits EndOfTurn and an optional early EagerEndOfTurn so the LLM can start speculatively.
  • Kyutai's flush trick (in Unmute): on end of speech, process the STT's buffered audio at about 4× real time, so a 500 ms delay costs about 125 ms.
  • Typical defaults: LiveKit waits at least 0.5 s and at most 3 s of silence and treats speech of 0.5 s or more as an interruption; Deepgram Flux forces an end of turn after 5 s of silence by default.

Sources: Silero VAD, LiveKit turn detector, LiveKit turn tuning, Pipecat Smart Turn, OpenAI Realtime VAD, Deepgram Flux, Kyutai STT, Picovoice VAD.

Pitfalls

  • Fixed threshold. Works in quiet, fails in noisy. Either calibrate on-device or switch to Silero.
  • Too-short silence hangover. Agent interrupts mid-sentence. 500-800 ms is the sweet spot for conversational speech.
  • Too-long hangover. Feels sluggish. A/B test with target users.
  • No pre-roll buffer. First 200-300 ms of user audio lost. Always keep a rolling pre-roll.
  • Ignoring semantic endpointing. "Hmm, let me think..." contains long pauses. Users hate being cut off mid-thought. Use LiveKit's turn-detector or similar.
Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • VAD answers "is this speech?"; turn detection answers "are they done?", and needs more than VAD.
  • Compare VADs at a fixed false-positive rate, in noise like your users'; energy thresholds fail in noise, Silero holds up.
  • A silence hangover trades interruptions against latency; read speech shows a quarter of turns cut off at 500 ms.
  • Semantic turn models (audio or text) shorten the wait when a turn sounds complete; check them on your own domain.
  • Pre-roll, minimum speech duration, a maximum wait and the flush trick complete the design.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Energy collapses, Silero holds

At 0 dB SNR the energy threshold catches only about 25% of speech frames at 5% false positives, because noise frames are as loud as speech. Silero catches about 95%: it has learned what speech sounds like, not just how loud it is.

SolutionB1 · Silero needs 512 samples

Silero raises ValueError: Input audio chunk is too short. At 16 kHz it expects exactly 512-sample (32 ms) chunks, 256 at 8 kHz. Re-buffer the stream: collect incoming frames into a buffer and hand Silero 512 samples at a time, or use get_speech_timestamps / a framework's VAD wrapper, which does this for you.
