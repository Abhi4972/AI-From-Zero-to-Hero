The problem

In plain words first

Whisper is one neural network that listens to 30 seconds of audio and writes what was said. Its first half (the encoder) turns the log-mel spectrogram into a sequence of vectors, 50 per second; its second half (the decoder) writes text one token at a time, looking back at those vectors through cross-attention. A few special tokens at the start of the text tell it what to do: which language, whether to transcribe or translate into English, whether to write timestamps. Because the window is fixed at 30 seconds, longer recordings have to be cut up and stitched back together, and how you do that changes the result. This lesson opens the model up on real recordings: its sizes and shapes, what the decoder attends to as it writes each word, how three ways of handling a two-minute recording compare, what happens if the input features are computed even slightly differently, and what fine-tuning with LoRA would change. No model is trained here.

Whisper, released by OpenAI in September 2022, was the first ASR model to ship as a commodity: paste audio, get text, 99 languages, robust to noise, runs on a laptop. By 2024 OpenAI had shipped Large-v3 and Turbo variants; Whisper remains the most common open baseline for everything from podcast transcription to voice assistants to subtitles.

But Whisper is not a pipeline you can treat as a black box forever. Domain shift kills it — technical jargon, speaker accents, proper nouns, short clips, silence. You need to know:

  1. What it actually is inside.
  2. How to give it chunked, streaming, or long-form audio correctly.
  3. When to fine-tune and how.

The idea

Architecture. Standard transformer encoder-decoder.

  • Input: 30-second log-mel spectrogram, 80 mels (128 for large-v3 and turbo), 10 ms hop → 3000 frames. Clips shorter are zero-padded, clips longer are chunked.
  • Encoder: conv-downsample (stride 2) + N transformer blocks. For Large-v3: 32 layers, 1280-dim, 20 heads.
  • Decoder: N transformer blocks with causal self-attn + cross-attn to encoder output. Same size as encoder.
  • Output: BPE tokens over a vocabulary of about 51,865 (51,864 for the English-only models, 51,866 for large-v3).

Large-v3 has 1.55B params. Turbo keeps large-v3's encoder and cuts the decoder from 32 layers to 4 (809M params); OpenAI's README lists it at about 8× the speed of large, with a small accuracy loss, and notes that it was not trained for translation.

The prompt format. Whisper is a multitask model steered by special tokens in the decoder prompt:

<|startoftranscript|><|en|><|transcribe|><|notimestamps|> Hello world.<|endoftext|>
  • <|en|>: language tag; names the language spoken in the audio. Leave it out and Whisper detects the language first.
  • <|transcribe|> or <|translate|>: task tag; transcribe the speech in its source language, or translate it to English text.
  • <|notimestamps|>: skip word-level timestamps (faster).

The prompt is what lets one model do many tasks. Change <|en|> to <|fr|> and it transcribes French.

30-second window. Everything is pinned to 30 seconds. Longer clips need chunking; shorter clips are padded. Windows are not streamed natively — this is why WhisperX, Whisper-Streaming, and faster-whisper exist.

Log-mel normalisation. Whisper takes $\log_{10}$ of the mel power, clips everything more than 8 below the clip's own maximum, and maps the result with $(x + 4)/4$ (rebuilt exactly in Lesson 6.2). No corpus statistics are involved. You must use Whisper's preprocessing (whisper.audio.log_mel_spectrogram), not librosa.feature.melspectrogram.

The same idea as a formula

Encoder. The log-mel input X∈R80×3000 (128 for large-v3) passes two 1-D convolutions (kernel 3, the second with stride 2) and GELU, giving 1500 vectors of width d, one per 20 ms; sinusoidal position embeddings are added, then Le​ transformer blocks.

Decoder. P(y∣X)=∏u​P(yu​∣y<u​,Enc(X)). Each decoder block has causal self-attention over the tokens so far, then cross-attention, softmax(QK⊤/dh​​)V with queries from the text and keys and values from the 1,500 encoder vectors, then an MLP.

Prompt. The text starts with control tokens, ⟨sot⟩⟨lang⟩⟨task⟩⟨notimestamps⟩; with timestamps enabled, the model writes time tokens ⟨t⟩ for t=0.00,0.02,…,30.00 s, 1,501 of them, around each segment.

Decoding safeguards in OpenAI's transcribe: a window's output is rejected and re-decoded at a higher temperature if its gzip compression ratio exceeds 2.4 (repetitive text compresses well) or its average token log-probability is below −1; it is treated as silence if P(no speech)>0.6 and the log-probability is also low.

Word timestamps. A few cross-attention heads (the alignment heads) look at the frames being spoken as each token is written. Dynamic time warping finds the monotonic path through that token × frame matrix that maximises total attention; token u's time is where the path crosses row u.

LoRA. A frozen weight W∈Rd×d is used as W+rα​BA with trainable B∈Rd×r, A∈Rr×d, B initialised to zero so training starts from the original model. Each adapted matrix adds 2dr parameters.

Worked example
Frames, tokens and adapter sizes by hand

Frames. 30 s × 100 frames/s = 3,000 log-mel frames; the stride-2 convolution halves that to 1,500 encoder positions, 20 ms each. Cross-attention therefore compares every text token with 1,500 positions: for large-v3 (20 heads, 32 decoder layers) that is 1500×20×32=960,000 attention weights per generated token.

Long audio. 134 seconds needs at least ⌈134/30⌉=5 windows; with 5 s of overlap between 30 s chunks, the step is 25 s, so ⌈(134−30)/25⌉+1=6 chunks.

LoRA on turbo, r=16, targets q_proj and v_proj (d=1280): each adapted matrix adds 2×1280×16=40,960 parameters. The name matches the encoder's self-attention (32 layers × 2) and the decoder's self- and cross-attention (4 layers × 4): 80 matrices, 80×40,960=3.28 M, about 0.4% of 809 M. Counting only the decoder gives 16×40,960=0.66 M, the figure main.py prints.

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Whisper's input is normalised with dataset statistics."
    ✓ It is log10​, clipped to 8 below the clip's maximum and rescaled; nothing is learned from a corpus.
  • ✗ "To transcribe a long file, cut it into 30-second pieces and join the texts."
    ✓ Cuts fall mid-word. Use Whisper's sequential long-form decoding or overlapping chunks with stitching, and VAD.
  • ✗ "Turbo is large-v3, only faster."
    ✓ It has a 4-layer decoder, is a little less accurate, and is not trained for translation.

import io, time
import numpy as np, soundfile as sf, torch
from datasets import load_dataset, Audio
from transformers import WhisperProcessor, WhisperForConditionalGeneration

libri = load_dataset("hf-internal-testing/librispeech_asr_dummy", "clean", split="validation").cast_column("audio", Audio(decode=False))
def clip(i):
    return sf.read(io.BytesIO(libri[i]["audio"]["bytes"]), dtype="float32")[0]

def load(name):
    return WhisperProcessor.from_pretrained(name), WhisperForConditionalGeneration.from_pretrained(name).eval()

proc, model = load("openai/whisper-base.en")
c = model.config
print(f"whisper-base.en: {c.encoder_layers} encoder + {c.decoder_layers} decoder layers, width {c.d_model}, "
      f"{c.encoder_attention_heads} heads, {c.num_mel_bins} mel bins, vocabulary {c.vocab_size:,}, "
      f"{sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters()) / 1e6:.0f}M parameters")
feats = proc(clip(1), sampling_rate=16000, return_tensors="pt").input_features
with torch.no_grad():
    enc = model.model.encoder(feats).last_hidden_state
print(f"{len(clip(1)) / 16000:.1f} s clip -> log-mel {tuple(feats.shape)} (padded to 30 s) -> encoder output {tuple(enc.shape)}")
with torch.no_grad():
    out = model.generate(feats, return_timestamps=True)
print("decoder output with timestamps:", proc.batch_decode(out, skip_special_tokens=False, decode_with_timestamps=True)[0][:160])

def corpus_wer(p, m, idx):
    norm = p.tokenizer.normalize; e = n = 0
    for i in idx:
        with torch.no_grad():
            hyp = p.batch_decode(m.generate(p(clip(i), sampling_rate=16000, return_tensors="pt").input_features), skip_special_tokens=True)[0]
        r, h = norm(libri[i]["text"]).split(), norm(hyp).split()
        d = np.arange(len(h) + 1)
        for a in r:                                   # word-level edit distance, one row at a time
            nd = d.copy(); nd[0] = d[0] + 1
            for j, b in enumerate(h, 1):
                nd[j] = min(d[j] + 1, nd[j - 1] + 1, d[j - 1] + (a != b))
            d = nd
        e += d[-1]; n += len(r)
    return e / n

t0 = time.time()
for name in ["openai/whisper-tiny.en", "openai/whisper-base.en"]:
    p, m = (proc, model) if name.endswith("base.en") else load(name)
    t1 = time.time(); w = corpus_wer(p, m, range(20))
    print(f"{name:24s} {sum(x.numel() for x in m.parameters()) / 1e6:4.0f}M params: WER {w:.2%} on 20 utterances, {time.time() - t1:.0f} s")

4.8 s clip -> log-mel (1, 80, 3000) (padded to 30 s) -> encoder output (1, 1500, 512)
decoder output with timestamps: <|0.00|> Nor is Mr. Quilter's manner less interesting than his matter.<|4.40|>

Reading this output

base.en is 6 + 6 layers of width 512 with 8 heads, reading 80 mel bins, 73M parameters. A 4.8 s clip becomes a padded 80 × 3,000 log-mel and then 1,500 encoder vectors, whatever its length. With timestamps on, the decoder brackets the segment with time tokens, <|0.00|> … <|4.40|>. On these 20 utterances tiny.en (5.3%) happens to beat base.en (6.8%), and it took half the time. Twenty utterances are about 400 words, so one or two errors move WER by half a point: on large test sets the bigger model is better, which is why the table above reports sizes and speeds from OpenAI and not a WER column we cannot verify.

Idea 2 · Fine-tuning

Canonical workflow in 2026:

  1. Collect 10–100 hours of target-domain audio with aligned transcripts.
  2. Run transformers.Seq2SeqTrainer with predict_with_generate=True, so that evaluation decodes text and computes WER.
  3. Parameter-efficient: LoRA on the attention projections (q_proj, v_proj) trains well under 1% of the weights and needs far less GPU memory than full fine-tuning; compare its WER with full fine-tuning on your own data.
  4. Freeze the encoder if you have <10 hours. Only tune the decoder.
  5. Use Whisper's own tokenizer and prompt format; never swap tokenizers.

Reported gains vary widely with language, domain and amount of data. The number that counts is WER on held-out audio from your own domain, measured before and after, with the same normaliser.

Step 1 · run Whisper out of the box

import whisper
model = whisper.load_model("large-v3-turbo")
result = model.transcribe(
    "clip.wav",
    language="en",
    task="transcribe",
    temperature=0.0,
    condition_on_previous_text=False,  # prevents runaway repetition
)
print(result["text"])
for seg in result["segments"]:
    print(f"[{seg['start']:.2f}–{seg['end']:.2f}] {seg['text']}")

Know the defaults. temperature defaults to the fallback chain 0.0, 0.2, … 1.0: decode greedily, and retry with sampling only when the output looks degenerate. condition_on_previous_text=True feeds each window the previous window's text, which keeps style consistent but lets one hallucination cascade, so many pipelines set it to False. no_speech_threshold=0.6 marks a window as silent when the no-speech probability is high and the text's average log-probability is low.

Not run here: it needs OpenAI's whisper package and the 1.6 GB turbo checkpoint. The runs below use whisper-base.en through transformers, which exposes the same model.

Three ways to transcribe two minutes

Twelve consecutive LibriSpeech utterances joined into one recording, transcribed by whisper-base.en: (a) as a single input, (b) with sequential long-form decoding (truncation=False, timestamps on), (c) with the transformers pipeline's overlapping chunks.

from transformers import pipeline
# About two minutes of audio: 12 consecutive LibriSpeech utterances joined with 0.3 s gaps.
gap = np.zeros(int(0.3 * 16000), np.float32)
long_audio = np.concatenate([np.concatenate([clip(i), gap]) for i in range(12)])
ref = " ".join(libri[i]["text"] for i in range(12))
norm = proc.tokenizer.normalize
def wer_words(r, h):
    r, h = norm(r).split(), norm(h).split(); d = np.arange(len(h) + 1)
    for a in r:
        nd = d.copy(); nd[0] = d[0] + 1
        for j, b in enumerate(h, 1):
            nd[j] = min(d[j] + 1, nd[j - 1] + 1, d[j - 1] + (a != b))
        d = nd
    return d[-1] / len(r)
print(f"{len(long_audio) / 16000:.0f} s of audio, {len(ref.split())} reference words")

# (a) Feed only the first 30 s, the way a naive caller would.
f = proc(long_audio, sampling_rate=16000, return_tensors="pt").input_features      # truncated to 3000 frames
with torch.no_grad():
    first = proc.batch_decode(model.generate(f), skip_special_tokens=True)[0]
print(f"(a) one 30 s window only:      {len(first.split()):3d} words out, WER {wer_words(ref, first):.0%}")

# (b) Sequential long-form decoding: Whisper's own algorithm, sliding the window by its timestamps.
f = proc(long_audio, sampling_rate=16000, return_tensors="pt", truncation=False, padding="longest",
         return_attention_mask=True)
t0 = time.time()
with torch.no_grad():
    seq = proc.batch_decode(model.generate(f.input_features, attention_mask=f.attention_mask, return_timestamps=True),
                            skip_special_tokens=True)[0]
print(f"(b) sequential long-form:      {len(seq.split()):3d} words out, WER {wer_words(ref, seq):.1%}  ({time.time() - t0:.0f} s)")

# (c) Chunked: independent 30 s chunks with overlapping strides, stitched (the pipeline's chunk_length_s).
asr = pipeline("automatic-speech-recognition", model=model, tokenizer=proc.tokenizer, feature_extractor=proc.feature_extractor)
t0 = time.time()
chunked = asr(long_audio.copy(), chunk_length_s=30, stride_length_s=5, batch_size=4)["text"]
print(f"(c) chunked, 30 s + 5 s stride: {len(chunked.split()):3d} words out, WER {wer_words(ref, chunked):.1%}  ({time.time() - t0:.0f} s)")
What you should see
134 s of audio, 303 reference words
(a) one 30 s window only:       73 words out, WER 76%
(b) sequential long-form:      297 words out, WER 8.4%  (11 s)
(c) chunked, 30 s + 5 s stride: 292 words out, WER 12.3%  (30 s)
Reading this output

(a) The processor silently truncates to the first 30 s: 73 words out of 303, WER 76%, with no error message. (b) Sequential long-form decoding, Whisper's own algorithm, moves the window forward to the last complete segment's end timestamp, so no word is cut: 8.4%. (c) Independent chunks with 5 s overlaps, merged by aligning the overlapping text, scored 12.3% here: words at chunk edges are heard twice, once without context, and the merge does not always pick the right copy. Chunking parallelises well on a GPU (batch_size), which is why WhisperX and the pipeline use it with VAD-placed boundaries rather than fixed ones; sequential decoding is the accuracy reference.

Reading this output

The block above runs Step 4 on a real clip with the model loaded in "eager" attention mode (the fused kernel does not return weights). Averaged over the five alignment heads OpenAI identified for base.en, each output token's attention is a sharp spot, and the spots march diagonally through the audio: "Nor" at 0.62 s, "manner" at 1.9 s, "than" at 3.3 s. Sub-word pieces ("Qu", "il", "ter") step through the name a few frames apart. The timestamps from return_token_timestamps=True, which run dynamic time warping over the same heads, land within about 0.1 s of the attention peaks (the first token's 0.0 is an artefact of where the path starts). This is the mechanism behind Whisper's word timestamps; WhisperX replaces it with a wav2vec 2.0 forced alignment, which is more precise.

Break it on purpose

Compute the log-mel yourself with librosa for the same 4.8 s clip (80 mels, n_fft=400, hop_length=160, padded to 30 s) and give it to model.generate in place of the processor's features: once as natural log, once as librosa.power_to_db. What does Whisper write? (Solution B1.)

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Which Whisper to run. large-v3-turbo for fast, accurate transcription; large-v3 when you need translation into English (turbo was not trained for it) or the best multilingual accuracy; distil-whisper and the small English-only models for CPUs and edge devices.
  • Which runtime. faster-whisper (CTranslate2) on servers and laptops, whisper.cpp on CPUs and Apple hardware, transformers when you need the model inside a PyTorch pipeline, WhisperX for long recordings with word timestamps and speakers.
  • Fine-tuning practice. The Hugging Face fine-tuning guide with Seq2SeqTrainer (full or LoRA with peft) is the common recipe; evaluation uses the same normaliser as the baseline.

Sources: OpenAI Whisper README, large-v3-turbo discussion, faster-whisper, whisper.cpp, Fine-tune Whisper guide.

Pitfalls that still ship in 2026

  • Hallucinated text on silence. Whisper trained on captions includes "Thanks for watching!", "Subscribe!", song lyrics. Always VAD-gate before calling.
  • condition_on_previous_text cascade. One hallucination pollutes subsequent windows. Set False unless you need fluency across chunks.
  • Short-clip padding. A 2-second clip padded to 30 seconds can hallucinate in the trailing silence. Trim with VAD, and treat text after the last speech segment with suspicion.
  • Wrong mel stats. Using librosa's mels instead of Whisper's produces nonsense (see the broken-on-purpose box). Use whisper.audio.log_mel_spectrogram or the transformers feature extractor.
Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Encoder: log-mel (80 or 128 bins) → 1,500 vectors per 30 s window; decoder: text tokens with cross-attention, steered by language, task and timestamp tokens.
  • Use Whisper's exact feature extractor; a different log or scale produces nonsense, not an error.
  • Long audio: sequential long-form decoding is the accuracy reference; chunking is faster in batches; a single call silently truncates to 30 s.
  • Word timestamps come from alignment heads in cross-attention plus DTW; forced alignment is more precise.
  • Fine-tune (full or LoRA) only after measuring the baseline on your own data with the same normaliser.

Exercises

  1. Easy. Run code/main.py. It tokenizes a Whisper-style prompt, computes decoded shape budgets, and prints the chunk schedule for a 10-minute clip.
  2. Medium. Install faster-whisper, transcribe a 10-minute podcast, compare WER against a human transcript. Try language="auto" vs forced language="en".

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Sequential, then chunked, then truncated

Sequential long-form decoding is best (8.4%), overlapping chunks next (12.3%), and the single call is far behind (76%) because it only ever sees the first 30 seconds and says nothing about it.

SolutionB1 · No error, nonsense output

With the processor's features Whisper writes the sentence correctly. With natural-log librosa mels it writes a row of apostrophes; with power_to_db it writes "God God God …". Both inputs have the right shape, so nothing complains; they are on the wrong scale (Whisper expects log10​, clipped to an 80 dB range and mapped to roughly [−1,1]), and the model is outside anything it was trained on. Always use the model's own feature extractor.
