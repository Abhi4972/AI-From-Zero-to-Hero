In plain words first
The idea in one line

Goodfellow's trick in 2014 was to skip density entirely. Two networks. One makes fakes. One catches them. They fight until the fakes are indistinguishable from real. It shouldn't work. It often doesn't. When it does, the samples are still the sharpest in the literature for narrow domains.

Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means
Generator "G" Noise-to-sample network, G: z → x̂.
Discriminator "D" Classifier D: x → [0, 1], real vs fake.
Minimax "The game" $\min_G \max_D$ of a joint objective.
Non-saturating loss "The fix" Use $-\log D(G(z))$ for G instead of $\log(1 - D(G(z)))$.
Mode collapse "G memorized one thing" Generator produces few distinct outputs despite diverse data.
WGAN "Wasserstein" Replace BCE with Earth-Mover distance + gradient penalty; smoother gradient.
Spectral norm "Lipschitz trick" Constrain D's weight norms to bound its slope; stabilizes training.
StyleGAN "The one that works" Mapping network + style modulation (AdaIN, then weight demodulation in StyleGAN2); strong and fast for faces and other narrow domains.

The problem

In plain words first

A generative adversarial network (GAN) trains two networks against each other. The generator turns random numbers into a sample, say an image. The discriminator looks at a sample and guesses whether it is real or made up. Each improves by exploiting the other's mistakes, and if the contest goes well the generator ends up making samples the discriminator cannot tell from real ones, without anyone ever writing down how likely an image is. Once trained, a GAN makes an image in a single pass, which is still its great strength. Training is the hard part: the contest can stall, oscillate, or settle on a few kinds of output. This lesson checks the theory behind the contest numerically with the lesson's tiny networks (untrained, since this page trains nothing), then runs BigGAN, a large GAN trained on ImageNet, to show the quality, the diversity dial and the one-pass speed.

VAEs produce blurry samples because their MSE decoder loss is Bayes-optimal for the mean image — and the mean of many plausible digits is a fuzzy digit. You want a loss that rewards plausibility, not pixel-wise proximity to any one target. There is no closed-form for plausibility. You have to learn it.

Goodfellow's idea: train a classifier D(x) to distinguish real images from fakes. Train a generator G(z) to fool D. The loss signal for G is whatever D currently thinks makes something look real. This signal updates as G improves, chasing a moving target. If both networks converge, G has learned the data distribution without ever writing down log p(x).

This is adversarial training. The math is a minimax game:

Gmin​Dmax​Ereal​[logD(x)]+Efake​[log(1−D(G(z)))]

GANs are no longer the state of the art for open-ended generation (diffusion and flow matching took that), but the adversarial idea is everywhere. The VAE in front of a latent-diffusion model is trained with a patch discriminator, which is what keeps its decodes sharp; neural audio codecs and vocoders use discriminators the same way; and adversarial distillation (SDXL-Turbo, SD3.5 Large Turbo, FLUX.1-schnell) turns slow diffusion models into 1–4-step generators. StyleGAN-family models remain strong and cheap within narrow domains such as faces.

The idea

Generator G(z). Maps a noise vector z∼N(0,I) to a sample x^. A decoder-shaped network (dense or transposed conv).

Discriminator D(x). Maps a sample to a scalar probability (or score). Real → 1, fake → 0.

Loss. Two alternating updates:

  • Train D: lossD​=−[logD(x)+log(1−D(G(z)))]. Binary cross-entropy on real=1, fake=0.
  • Train G: lossG​=−logD(G(z)). This is the non-saturating form Goodfellow used (original log(1−D(G(z))) saturates and kills gradients when D is confident).

Training loop. One step of D, one step of G. Repeat.

Why it works. If G perfectly matches pdata​, then D cannot do better than chance and outputs 0.5 everywhere; G gets no more gradient. Equilibrium.

Why it breaks. Mode collapse (G finds one mode D can't classify and mints it forever), vanishing gradient (D learns too fast and logD saturates), training instability (learning rates, batch sizes, anything).

The same idea as a formula

The game. With real data x∼pdata​ and fakes G(z), z∼N(0,I), whose distribution is pg​:

V(G,D)=Ex∼pdata​​[logD(x)]+Ex∼pg​​[log(1−D(x))],Gmin​Dmax​V(G,D)

The best discriminator for a fixed generator maximises pdata​(x)logD+pg​(x)log(1−D) at every x, which gives

D∗(x)=pdata​(x)+pg​(x)pdata​(x)​

What the generator then minimises. Substituting D∗:

V(G,D∗)=2JSD(pdata​∥pg​)−log4

The Jensen–Shannon divergence is zero only when pg​=pdata​, so the global minimum is the data distribution, where D∗=21​ everywhere.

Why the generator's loss was changed. With D=σ(ℓ) (ℓ the logit), the gradient of the original generator loss is ∂log(1−D)/∂ℓ=−D, which vanishes when the discriminator confidently rejects fakes (D→0), exactly early in training. The non-saturating loss −logD has gradient −(1−D)→−1 there.

Why JSD stalls and Wasserstein does not. If pdata​ and pg​ do not overlap, JSD=log2 however far apart they are, so it gives no direction. The Wasserstein-1 distance

W1​(p,q)=∥f∥L​≤1sup​Ep​[f(x)]−Eq​[f(x)]

grows with the distance. WGANs make the discriminator (the "critic" f) approximately 1-Lipschitz with weight clipping, a gradient penalty or spectral normalisation; modern GANs such as StyleGAN2 and R3GAN instead penalise ∥∇x​D∥2 on real data (R1) and fakes (R2).

Worked example
The optimal discriminator by hand

At a point where the data density is pdata​(x)=0.30 and the generator's is pg​(x)=0.10:

  • D∗(x)=0.30/(0.30+0.10)=0.75: three real samples land there for every fake.
  • Where only fakes land (pdata​=0): D∗=0. Where only real data lands: D∗=1.
  • If the two distributions never overlap, D∗ is 1 on the data and 0 on the fakes, JSD=log2=0.693, and V=2×0.693−log4=0, the largest value V(G,D∗) can take.

The gradients at D(G(z))=0.01 (fakes confidently rejected):

  • Saturating loss: −D=−0.01.
  • Non-saturating loss: −(1−D)=−0.99, 99 times stronger.
Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Falling GAN losses mean training is going well."
    ✓ The two losses measure a contest, not quality; they oscillate even in good runs. Judge with samples and metrics such as FID (Lesson 8.14).
  • ✗ "The discriminator should be as strong as possible."
    ✓ A perfect discriminator on non-overlapping distributions gives the generator no useful signal (JSD stuck at log2); the non-saturating loss, gradient penalties and balanced learning rates keep the signal alive.
  • ✗ "Mode collapse means the generator outputs one fixed image."
    ✓ More often it covers only some modes (some classes, poses, colours) while each output still looks fine, which is why it is easy to miss.

Year Innovation Fix
2015 DCGAN Conv/deconv, batch norm, LeakyReLU — the first stable architecture.
2017 WGAN, WGAN-GP Replace BCE with Wasserstein distance + gradient penalty. Fixes vanishing gradient.
2018 Spectral normalization Lipschitz-bound the discriminator. Still common in discriminators.

2021 StyleGAN3 Alias-free, translation- and rotation-equivariant; removes "texture sticking" when the image moves.

Step 1 · non-saturating loss

code/main.py trains a tiny GAN on 1-D data: a mixture of two Gaussians. Generator and discriminator are single-hidden-layer MLPs. We implement forward, backward, and the minimax loop by hand. The goal is to see the two key failure modes (mode collapse + vanishing gradient) as they happen.

The vanilla Goodfellow loss log(1−D(G(z))) goes to 0 when D classifies G's fake as fake with high confidence. At that point the gradient for G is basically zero — G cannot improve. The non-saturating form −logD(G(z)) has the opposite asymptote: it blows up when D is confident, giving G a strong signal.

def g_loss(d_fake):
    # maximize log D(G(z))  <=>  minimize -log D(G(z))
    return -sum(math.log(max(p, 1e-8)) for p in d_fake) / len(d_fake)

The theory, checked numerically

import math, random, runpy
m = runpy.run_path("main.py")            # the lesson's networks; main() is not run, because it trains

# 1. The best possible discriminator for a fixed generator: D*(x) = p_data(x) / (p_data(x) + p_g(x)).
rng = random.Random(1)
G = m["init_mlp"](4, 16, 1, rng)         # the untrained generator, initialised as in main()
fakes = [m["forward_g"](z, G)[0][0] for z in m["sample_noise"](20_000, 4, rng)]
mu_g = sum(fakes) / len(fakes); sd_g = (sum((f - mu_g) ** 2 for f in fakes) / len(fakes)) ** 0.5
bw = 1.06 * sd_g * len(fakes) ** -0.2
npdf = lambda x, mu, s: math.exp(-0.5 * ((x - mu) / s) ** 2) / (s * math.sqrt(2 * math.pi))
grid = [-6 + 12 * i / 600 for i in range(601)]; dx = 12 / 600
p_data = [0.5 * npdf(x, -2, 0.4) + 0.5 * npdf(x, 2, 0.4) for x in grid]
sub = fakes[:2000]
p_g = [sum(npdf(x, f, bw) for f in sub) / len(sub) for x in grid]      # kernel estimate of the generator's density
print(f"untrained generator: mean {mu_g:+.2f}, std {sd_g:.2f} (the data: modes at -2 and +2, std 0.4)")
d_star = [a / (a + b) if a + b > 1e-12 else 0.5 for a, b in zip(p_data, p_g)]
for x in (-2.0, -1.0, 0.0, 1.0, 2.0):
    i = round((x + 6) / dx)
    print(f"  D*({x:+.0f}) = {d_star[i]:.3f}")
value = sum((a * math.log(max(d, 1e-12)) + b * math.log(max(1 - d, 1e-12))) * dx
            for a, b, d in zip(p_data, p_g, d_star))
mix = [(a + b) / 2 for a, b in zip(p_data, p_g)]
kl = lambda p, q: sum(a * math.log(a / c) * dx for a, c in zip(p, q) if a > 1e-12)
jsd = 0.5 * kl(p_data, mix) + 0.5 * kl(p_g, mix)
print(f"V(G, D*) = {value:.4f};  2 * JSD - log 4 = {2 * jsd - math.log(4):.4f};  JSD = {jsd:.4f} (max log 2 = {math.log(2):.4f})")

# 2. The generator's gradient through the discriminator's logit, for the two generator losses.
print("\nD(G(z))   saturating log(1 - D): d/dlogit   non-saturating -log D: d/dlogit")
for d in (0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 0.9):
    print(f"{d:7.3f}   {-d:>30.3f}   {-(1 - d):>33.3f}")

# 3. JSD against the Wasserstein-1 distance, for two narrow bumps a distance theta apart.
print("\ntheta    JSD      W1")
for theta in (0.0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0):
    xs = [-2 + 8 * i / 4000 for i in range(4001)]; h = 8 / 4000
    p = [npdf(x, 0, 0.1) for x in xs]; q = [npdf(x, theta, 0.1) for x in xs]
    mm = [(a + b) / 2 for a, b in zip(p, q)]
    j = 0.5 * sum(a * math.log(a / c) * h for a, c in zip(p, mm) if a > 1e-300) \
      + 0.5 * sum(b * math.log(b / c) * h for b, c in zip(q, mm) if b > 1e-300)
    print(f"{theta:5.2f}  {j:.4f}  {theta:6.2f}")     # W1 between two shifted copies of one shape is the shift

Reading this output

The untrained generator puts its samples in one narrow bump near 0, where the data has almost none, so the optimal discriminator is nearly certain everywhere: D∗≈1 at both real modes, 0 at the generator's bump, and in between only where the two densities overlap a little. The value V(G,D∗) and 2JSD−log4 agree to four decimals, the identity from the formula box, and the JSD, 0.663, is already close to its ceiling log2=0.693. The gradient table shows why the lesson's generator uses the non-saturating loss: when fakes are rejected (D=0.001 to 0.1), the original loss gives 0.001 to 0.1 and the non-saturating one 0.9 to 0.999. The last table is the WGAN argument: as two narrow bumps are pulled apart, JSD climbs to log2 by a distance of about 1 and then stays flat, so a generator twice or four times as far away gets the same score, while the Wasserstein distance keeps growing with the gap and points the way back.

Pitfalls

  • Discriminator too strong. Cut D's learning rate by 2-5x, or add instance/layer noise. If D reaches >95% accuracy, G is dead.
  • Generator memorizes a mode. Add noise to D inputs, use a minibatch-discriminator layer, or switch to WGAN-GP.
  • Batch norm leaking statistics. Real batch + fake batch flowing through the same BN layer mixes their statistics. Use instance norm or spectral norm instead.
  • Inception-score gaming. FID and IS are noisy at low sample counts. Use ≥10k samples at eval.
  • One pass is not always one try. Conditional GANs still trade quality against diversity with the truncation trick (the run under "Use it"), and products often generate several candidates and filter them.

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

The 2026 GAN stack:

Situation Pick
Photoreal faces or another narrow domain, in real time StyleGAN2/3 (one pass, small)
Stylised images with text control A diffusion model with a LoRA (Lesson 8.8)
Image-to-image translation Pix2Pix / CycleGAN (Phase 8 · 04) or ControlNet (Phase 8 · 08)
Fast 1–4-step text-to-image Adversarial distillation of diffusion (SDXL-Turbo, SD3.5 Large Turbo, FLUX.1-schnell)
Sharp decodes from an autoencoder or vocoder A discriminator as an extra loss, as in Stable Diffusion's VAE, EnCodec, DAC and HiFi-GAN-style vocoders
Anything multi-modal, open-ended Don't — use diffusion or flow matching

GANs are sharp but narrow. Once your domain opens up — photos, arbitrary text prompts, video — switch to diffusion. The adversarial trick lives on as a component (perceptual losses, distillation), not a standalone generator.

import time, torch, numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from huggingface_hub import hf_hub_download
from pytorch_pretrained_biggan import BigGAN, BigGANConfig, one_hot_from_int, truncated_noise_sample

torch.set_num_threads(2)
cfg = BigGANConfig.from_json_file(hf_hub_download("osanseviero/BigGAN-deep-128", "config.json"))
gan = BigGAN(cfg); gan.load_state_dict(torch.load(hf_hub_download("osanseviero/BigGAN-deep-128", "pytorch_model.bin"))); gan.eval()
print(f"BigGAN-deep generator: {sum(p.numel() for p in gan.parameters()) / 1e6:.1f}M parameters, "
      f"z of {cfg.z_dim} numbers + one of 1,000 ImageNet classes -> 128 x 128 image")
gen = lambda z, y, t: gan(torch.tensor(z), torch.tensor(y), t).clamp(-1, 1)

rows, titles = [], []
with torch.no_grad():
    y = one_hot_from_int([207] * 6, batch_size=6)                  # class 207: golden retriever
    for t in (0.2, 0.5, 1.0):                                       # truncation: resample |z| > t
        z = truncated_noise_sample(truncation=t, batch_size=6, seed=3)
        imgs = gen(z * 1.0, y, t)
        flat = imgs.flatten(1)
        div = torch.cdist(flat, flat).sum() / (6 * 5) / flat.shape[1] ** 0.5
        rows.append(imgs); titles.append(f"truncation {t}: retriever, 6 seeds")
        print(f"truncation {t}: mean pixel distance between the 6 images {div:.3f}")
    z = truncated_noise_sample(truncation=0.5, batch_size=2, seed=11)
    a = np.linspace(0, 1, 6)[:, None]
    zs = (1 - a) * z[0] + a * z[1]                                   # walk in z, class fixed
    rows.append(gen(zs.astype(np.float32), one_hot_from_int([207] * 6, batch_size=6), 0.5)); titles.append("straight line in z (retriever)")
    yc = (1 - a) * one_hot_from_int([207], 1) + a * one_hot_from_int([281], 1)   # walk between classes: retriever -> tabby cat
    rows.append(gen(np.repeat(z[:1], 6, 0), yc.astype(np.float32), 0.5)); titles.append("straight line between class vectors: retriever -> tabby cat")
    for bs in (1, 8):
        zb = truncated_noise_sample(truncation=0.5, batch_size=bs, seed=0); yb = one_hot_from_int([207] * bs, batch_size=bs)
        gen(zb, yb, 0.5)                                             # warm-up
        t0 = time.perf_counter(); gen(zb, yb, 0.5); dt = time.perf_counter() - t0
        print(f"batch {bs}: {dt:.2f} s per pass, {bs / dt:.1f} images per second, one pass per image")

fig, axes = plt.subplots(len(rows), 6, figsize=(9, 1.78 * len(rows)))
for r, (imgs, title) in enumerate(zip(rows, titles)):
    for c in range(6):
        axes[r, c].imshow(((imgs[c] + 1) / 2).permute(1, 2, 0).numpy()); axes[r, c].axis("off")
    axes[r, 0].set_title(title, loc="left", fontsize=9)

Reading this output

Truncation is BigGAN's quality–diversity dial: noise values beyond the threshold are redrawn, so a small threshold keeps every image close to the most typical retriever. At 0.2 the six dogs are similar centred portraits; at 1.0 they vary in pose, framing and number of dogs, and the average pixel distance between them rises from 0.40 to 0.66, with occasional odd details. The two straight-line rows show what Lesson 8.2's VAE could not do: walking between two noise vectors turns one retriever smoothly into another, and walking between the retriever and tabby-cat class vectors passes through single animals that are part dog, part cat, not a double exposure. The generator has 50 million parameters and makes each image in one pass; a batch of 8 takes far less than 8 times as long as a single image, so throughput rises with batch size, and every request costs the same, the production note's point.

Break it on purpose

In update_g, change dL_dpre2 = p - 1.0 (the gradient of the non-saturating loss −logD(G(z)) with respect to D's logit) to dL_dpre2 = -p (the gradient of the original loss log(1−D(G(z)))). Training still runs. What happens in the first hundred steps, when the discriminator easily spots the generator's bump near 0? (Solution B1.)

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Adversarial distillation in text-to-image. SDXL-Turbo, SD3.5 Large Turbo and FLUX.1-schnell are diffusion models distilled with a discriminator to sample in 1–4 steps; they power fast preview and real-time drawing features.
  • Discriminators inside autoencoders and vocoders. The VAEs of latent-diffusion models, neural audio codecs (EnCodec, DAC, Mimi) and GAN vocoders such as HiFi-GAN and NVIDIA's BigVGAN are all trained with adversarial losses; every text-to-speech system with a neural vocoder runs a GAN generator at inference.
  • GAN upscalers and restorers. Real-ESRGAN and GFPGAN remain standard upscaling and face-restoration nodes in ComfyUI and Automatic1111 workflows.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Generator and discriminator play minG​maxD​E[logD(x)]+E[log(1−D(G(z)))].
  • The optimal discriminator is pdata​/(pdata​+pg​); against it, the generator minimises 2JSD−log4.
  • Train the generator with −logD(G(z)): its gradient stays near 1 when fakes are rejected.
  • JSD saturates for non-overlapping distributions; Wasserstein critics and gradient penalties keep the signal.
  • Trained GANs: one pass per image, a truncation dial between quality and diversity, smooth and semantic latent spaces.

Save outputs/skill-gan-debugger.md. Skill takes a failing GAN run (loss curves, sample grid, dataset size) and outputs a ranked list of likely causes, one-line fixes, and a rerun protocol.

Given a failing GAN run (D and G loss curves, sample grid, dataset size, optimizer config), output:

1. Diagnosis. One root cause from: mode collapse, D too strong, D too weak, vanishing gradient, batch-norm leakage, overfit D, learning-rate mismatch, bad init.
2. Evidence. Pointer to the telltale in the loss curves or samples (e.g. "D(fake) &lt; 0.05 by step 500 = D too strong").
3. Fix. One concrete change. Examples: `lr_D = lr_G / 2`, replace BN with IN, add spectral norm to D, switch to WGAN-GP with lambda=10, cut batch size by 2, add 0.1 Gaussian noise to D inputs.
4. Rerun protocol. Seeds to try, number of steps before re-evaluation, acceptance criterion (e.g. "FID drops below baseline by step 20k").
5. Fallback. If the fix doesn't land in one rerun, what to try next. Usually: switch architecture (StyleGAN, R3GAN) or switch paradigm (diffusion, flow matching) if dataset is too diverse.

Refuse to recommend increasing G learning rate when D is already saturated. Refuse to add regularization to G when the real failure is D - fix D first. Flag any run that shows training collapse within 100 steps as likely bad init or lr blowup, not a deep algorithmic issue.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · A single animal, not a double exposure

A single, plausible-looking animal partway between a dog and a cat (the third and fourth images of the last row), not two pictures overlaid. BigGAN's generator was trained so that every point it is given maps to an image the discriminator accepts as real; a blend of the two class vectors is just another input, and the output stays on the "realistic image" manifold. The VAE of Lesson 8.2 was trained only to reconstruct, so its latents behave like a compressed copy of the pixels, and averaging them averages the pictures. Smooth, semantic latent spaces are one of the GAN family's lasting strengths, and the reason StyleGAN latents became a tool for editing faces.

SolutionB1 · The generator stalls

At the start the generator's samples sit in a bump near 0 where there is no real data, so the discriminator quickly learns to reject them and D(G(z)) falls toward 0. With the original loss, the generator's gradient through the logit is −D, so as the discriminator gets better the generator's signal shrinks: at D=0.01 it is 0.01, against 0.99 with the non-saturating loss (the table in the run after Step 3). The generator barely moves while the discriminator becomes ever more confident, which makes the gradient smaller still. Both losses have the same equilibrium; only the non-saturating one gives the generator a usable signal when it is losing.

Production note: one-shot inference is GAN's lasting advantage

GANs no longer win on sample quality for open-domain generation, but they still win on inference cost. In production-inference literature vocabulary a GAN has:

  • No prefill, no decode stages. A single G(z) forward pass. TTFT ≈ total latency.
  • No KV-cache pressure. The only state is the weights. Batch size is bounded by activation memory, not cache.
  • Trivial continuous batching. Since every request takes the same fixed FLOPs, a static batch at the server's target occupancy is usually optimal. No in-flight scheduler needed.

This is why adversarial distillation (ADD in SDXL-Turbo; latent ADD in SD3.5 Large Turbo and FLUX.1-schnell) is a leading technique for fast text-to-image: it collapses a 20–50-step diffusion pipeline into 1–4 forward passes while staying close to the diffusion model's outputs. Consistency distillation (LCM) reaches the same few-step range without a discriminator. The adversarial loss survives as a training-time knob for turning slow generators into fast ones.
