In plain words first
The idea in one line

Unified multimodal models have an unavoidable tension. Understanding wants semantic features — SigLIP or DINOv2 output vectors rich with concept-level information. Generation wants reconstruction-friendly codes — VQ tokens that compose back into crisp pixels. The two goals are not compatible in a single encoder. Janus (DeepSeek, October 2024) and Janus-Pro (DeepSeek, January 2025) argue the fix is to stop trying: decouple the two encoders. Share the transformer body between tasks, but route understanding through SigLIP and genera

Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means
Decoupled encoding "Two visual encoders" Separate tokenizer or encoder per direction: semantic for understanding, reconstruction for generation
Shared body "One transformer" Single transformer processes either encoder's output; no modality-specific weights
SigLIP for understanding "Semantic features" CLIP-family vision tower providing rich conceptual features but poor reconstruction
VQ for generation "Reconstruction codes" Vector-quantized tokens that decode cleanly back to pixels
JanusFlow "Rectified-flow variant" Janus-Pro with a continuous flow-matching generation head instead of VQ
Path selection "Which encoder" The task picks the path: understood images go through SigLIP, generated images come out as VQ codes from the image head

The problem

In plain words first

A model that both understands and generates images needs two different things from its image encoder. Understanding wants features that capture meaning ("a dog on a sofa"), the kind CLIP-style encoders produce. Generation wants codes that rebuild exact pixels, the kind a VQ tokenizer produces. One encoder is not good at both. Janus (October 2024) and Janus-Pro (January 2025) from DeepSeek use two. A SigLIP encoder reads images for understanding, a VQ tokenizer writes images as codes for generation, and one shared language model sits in the middle. Janus-Pro scales the data and model to 7B and beats DALL-E 3 on the GenEval benchmark. This lesson explains the split and the scaling, and builds the real Janus-Pro 1B and 7B models from their configurations to see where the parameters sit. No model is trained or downloaded.

Unified models share a transformer body across understanding and generation. Previous attempts (Chameleon, Show-o, Transfusion) all use one visual tokenizer for both directions. The tokenizer is a compromise:

  • Optimized for reconstruction (generation): VQ-VAE captures fine-grained pixel detail but produces tokens with weak semantic coherence.
  • Optimized for semantics (understanding): SigLIP embeddings group "cat" images near "cat" tokens but do not permit good reconstruction.

Show-o and Transfusion pay for this with a visible quality tax on one direction. Janus-Pro asks: why require one tokenizer when the tasks have different needs?

Idea 1 · Decoupled visual encoding

Janus-Pro's architecture separates the two encoders:

  • Understanding path. Input image → SigLIP-Large (patch 16, 384 px) → 2-layer MLP → transformer body.
  • Generation path. Input image (if conditioning on an existing image) → VQ tokenizer → token IDs → transformer body.
  • Output generation. Image tokens predicted by the transformer → VQ decoder → pixels.

The transformer body is shared. Everything upstream and downstream of the body is task-specific.

The task decides the path: images given to be understood go through SigLIP and the understanding adaptor; images to be generated are produced as VQ codes (a 16,384-entry codebook, 16× downsampling, 576 codes per 384-px image) by a separate image head, after a begin-of-image marker in the sequence.

Idea 2 · Why this works

Understanding loss gets SigLIP features, which CLIP-style pretraining has tuned for semantic similarity. The model's perception benchmarks improve over Show-o / Transfusion because the input features are better for the task.

Generation loss gets VQ tokens, which a tokenizer has tuned for reconstruction. Image quality improves over Show-o because VQ codes compose back to pixels cleanly.

The shared transformer body sees two input distributions (SigLIP and VQ) and learns to work with both. The claim: enough data + enough parameters, the body absorbs the switching.

Idea 3 · Data scaling — Janus vs Janus-Pro

Janus (original, arXiv 2410.13848) introduced the decoupling but at small scale (1.3B params, limited data). Janus-Pro (arXiv 2501.17811) scaled:

  • Model size: 1B and 7B (DeepSeek-LLM 1.5B and 7B bases) instead of 1.3B.
  • Training: a longer stage 1 on ImageNet, which was enough to learn pixel dependencies with the LLM frozen; stage 2 drops ImageNet for real text-to-image data; the stage-3 data ratio (multimodal : text : text-to-image) moves from 7:3:10 to 5:1:4.
  • Understanding data: about 90M more samples (captions, documents, charts, tables).
  • Generation data: about 72M synthetic aesthetic images, bringing the real-to-synthetic ratio to 1:1.

The upshot: Janus-Pro-7B scores 79.2 on MMBench (Janus: 69.4) and 0.80 on GenEval, ahead of DALL-E 3 (0.67) and SD3-Medium (0.74). Its reasoning is still limited by its size: MMMU is 41.0, far below large understanding-only VLMs. One open model, competitive on generation and solid on everyday understanding.

The same idea as a formula

Two input paths into one body. For understanding, image features f=SigLIP(I)∈R576×1024 become h=Au​(f); for generation, codes c∈{0,…,16383}576 become h=Ag​(E[c]). Both Au​ and Ag​ are 2-layer MLPs into the LLM width.

Two output heads. Text: p(yt​)=softmax(Wtext​ht​) over the text vocabulary. Images: p(ck​)=softmax(Himg​(hk​)) over 16,384 codes, then I^=VQDec(c).

Training: one next-token loss, applied to text tokens in understanding samples and to image codes in generation samples:

L=−t∑​logpθ​(yt​∣y<t​)or−k∑​logpθ​(ck​∣c<k​,prompt).

Worked example
Janus-Pro-7B at a glance
  • 384-px image in: 384/16=24, so 242=576 SigLIP features into the understanding adaptor.
  • 384-px image out: 576 VQ codes, each one of 16,384 (log2​16384=14 bits), generated token by token: 576 sequential steps (each run twice with classifier-free guidance).
  • Parameters (measured below): about 0.33B for understanding input, 0.17B for the generation path (tokenizer, adaptor, image head), 6.49B for the shared language model.
  • GenEval: 0.80 against DALL-E 3's 0.67; MMMU: 41.0, a reminder that 7B of language model sets the reasoning ceiling.

See it drawn
Janus-Pro's two paths, counted

Janus-Pro 1B and 7B are built from their published configurations on PyTorch's meta device, with no weights downloaded. Parameters are grouped into the understanding input path (SigLIP and adaptor), the generation path (VQ tokenizer, code embeddings, adaptor and image head) and the shared language model. The token counts per image follow. A few seconds.

import torch
from transformers import AutoConfig, JanusForConditionalGeneration, logging
logging.set_verbosity_error()

# Janus-Pro 1B and 7B built from their published configs on the meta device (no weights): the two visual paths
PATHS = {"understanding in": ["vision_model", "aligner"],
         "generation in / out": ["vqmodel", "generation_embeddings", "generation_aligner", "generation_head"],
         "shared language model": ["language_model"]}
for repo in ["deepseek-community/Janus-Pro-1B", "deepseek-community/Janus-Pro-7B"]:
    cfg = AutoConfig.from_pretrained(repo)
    with torch.device("meta"):
        m = JanusForConditionalGeneration(cfg)
    part = lambda name: sum(p.numel() for n, p in m.named_parameters()
                            if (n.split(".")[1] if n.startswith("model.") else n.split(".")[0]) == name)
    total = sum(p.numel() for p in m.parameters())
    print(f"{repo.split('/')[1]}: {total / 1e9:.2f}B parameters")
    for path, names in PATHS.items():
        n = sum(part(x) for x in names)
        detail = ", ".join(f"{x} {part(x) / 1e6:,.1f}M" for x in names)
        print(f"   {path:<22}{n / 1e6:>8,.0f}M ({n / total:.0%})   {detail}")

v, q = cfg.vision_config, cfg.vq_config
print(f"\nunderstanding: SigLIP at {v.image_size} px, {v.patch_size}-px patches -> {(v.image_size // v.patch_size) ** 2} feature vectors")
print(f"generation: VQ tokenizer, codebook {q.num_embeddings:,}, {q.num_patches} x {q.num_patches} = {q.num_patches ** 2} codes "
      f"per {v.image_size}-px image, predicted one at a time by a separate image head (text keeps its own head)")

Reading this output

Most of Janus-Pro is the language model. In the 7B model the shared DeepSeek-LLM is 6.49B parameters, 93% of 6.99B. Both visual paths together are under 0.5B. Decoupling the encoders makes the architecture more complex but barely changes its size.

The understanding path is the larger of the two. SigLIP-Large is 304M parameters plus a 21M adaptor. The generation path is the VQ tokenizer (72M, encoder and decoder), a tiny code embedding, a 17M adaptor and an 84M image head that predicts one of 16,384 codes.

The 1B model is shaped differently. With a 1.44B language model, the visual paths are 23% of the total, and SigLIP alone is 16%. Small unified models spend a larger share on vision, as small VLMs do (Lesson 12.7).

Both paths see the same 576-token grid. A 384-px image gives 576 SigLIP features in and 576 VQ codes out (24 × 24 at 16 px each). Generation means 576 sequential predictions from the image head, twice per step with classifier-free guidance.

Idea 4 · JanusFlow — the rectified flow variant

JanusFlow (arXiv 2411.07975) swaps the VQ generation path for a rectified-flow generation path (continuous). The split becomes SigLIP-for-understanding + rectified-flow-for-generation. Quality ceilings lift further. The architecture remains decoupled-encoders-shared-body.

Idea 5 · The shared body's job

The transformer body processes a unified sequence but with two input distributions. Its job is to:

  • For understanding: consume SigLIP features + text tokens → emit text autoregressively.
  • For generation: consume text tokens + (optional image VQ tokens) → emit image VQ tokens autoregressively.

The body has no modality-specific weights per block. It is the text-style transformer you'd expect to find inside Qwen or Llama, plus the two input adapters.

Interestingly, this means Janus-Pro's body can be initialized from a pretrained LLM, and it is: DeepSeek-LLM (1.5B and 7B). That choice matters: the LLM contributes language and reasoning ability that from-scratch unified models struggle to reach.

Idea 6 · Compared to InternVL-U

InternVL-U (Lesson 12.10, March 2026) is a 4B unified model that pairs an InternVL-style understanding model with an MMDiT-based diffusion head for generation and editing. It keeps the same principle: separate representations for understanding and for generation, one shared language backbone.

The idea of using different visual representations for understanding and generation is now common in unified models (BAGEL, for example, uses a ViT for understanding and a VAE for generation).

Idea 7 · Limitations

Decoupled encoders add architectural complexity. Two tokenizers to train, two input paths to maintain, two sets of fail modes. For products that do not need generation, Janus-Pro is over-engineered — pick a LLaVA-family understanding model.

For products that do not need understanding, Janus-Pro is overqualified — pick a Stable Diffusion 3 / Flux model.

For products that need both, Janus-Pro is a useful open reference architecture; newer unified models usually add a diffusion or flow head for image quality.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Janus-Pro (January 2025): 1B and 7B, open weights; GenEval 0.80, MMBench 79.2; the 1B fits an 8 GB laptop GPU in bf16, the 7B needs 4-bit quantization there.
  • JanusFlow: SigLIP for understanding, rectified flow in an SDXL-VAE latent space for generation.
  • Separate understanding and generation representations are widespread in unified models: BAGEL (ViT plus VAE), InternVL-U (MMDiT head), and others.
  • Reasoning stays with the language model: unified models at 7B trail larger understanding-only VLMs on reasoning benchmarks like MMMU.

Sources: Janus-Pro (arXiv 2501.17811), Janus (arXiv 2410.13848), JanusFlow (arXiv 2411.07975), Hugging Face Janus documentation, InternVL-U (arXiv 2603.09877).

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Understanding needs semantic features; generation needs reconstructable codes.
  • Janus-Pro: SigLIP in for understanding, VQ codes out for generation, one shared LLM.
  • Two adaptors, two heads, one next-token loss.
  • Scaling data (90M understanding, 72M synthetic aesthetic images) and model (7B) drove the gains.
  • GenEval 0.80 beats DALL-E 3; reasoning is capped by the LLM size.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · About 7%, and understanding is larger

About 7%: 0.33B for understanding (SigLIP-Large and its adaptor) and 0.17B for generation (VQ tokenizer, code embeddings, adaptor and image head), out of 6.99B. The understanding path is the larger, mostly because of SigLIP's 304M parameters. The shared language model is the other 93%.

SolutionB1 · Meaning without pixels

SigLIP vectors encode what an image means, not what it looks like. They are trained to match captions, so two different photos of "a red car" map to similar vectors, and the details that distinguish them (exact shape, lighting, background texture) are mostly discarded. There is no decoder that turns them back into pixels, because SigLIP was never trained to reconstruct. A decoder trained after the fact gets images that are semantically right but blurry, generic and inconsistent in detail. This is the tension Janus resolves: use SigLIP where meaning matters, and a reconstruction-trained tokenizer (VQ codes, or a VAE latent in JanusFlow and BAGEL) where pixels must come out. Some newer work does generate from semantic features by training a dedicated diffusion decoder on them, but that is a second model, not SigLIP itself.
