In plain words first
The idea in one line

Diffusion models take 20-50 sampling steps because they walk a curved path from noise to data. Flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) trained straight paths. Straighter paths mean fewer steps mean faster inference. Stable Diffusion 3, FLUX.1 and FLUX.2, Wan and F5-TTS all train with flow matching.

Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means
Flow matching "Straight-line diffusion" Train $v_\theta(x, t)$ to match $x_1 - x_0$ along an interpolant.
Rectified flow "Reflow" Iterative procedure that straightens learned flows.
Velocity field "v_θ" Output of the model — the direction to move $x_t$.
Straight-line interpolant "The path" $x_t = (1-t) \cdot x_0 + t \cdot x_1$; trivial target derivative.
Euler sampler "1st order ODE solver" Simplest integrator; works well when paths are straight.
Logit-normal t "SD3 sampling" Concentrate $t$ sampling toward mid-values where gradients are strongest.
Consistency distillation "1-step sampler" Train a student to map any $x_t$ directly to $x_0$.
CFG with velocity "v-CFG" $v_{\text{cfg}} = (1+w) v_{\text{cond}} - w v_{\text{uncond}}$; same trick, new variable.

The problem

In plain words first

A diffusion sampler moves a sample from noise to data in many small steps because the route it follows bends; take big steps on a bending road and you leave it. Flow matching trains the model on the simplest possible route instead: a straight line from each noise sample to a data sample, with the model learning which direction to move (the "velocity") at every point and time. Training is a plain regression, as simple as diffusion's, and sampling is following the learned velocity with a few large steps. It is the training method behind most current image and video models. This lesson computes the best possible velocity for the lesson's two-mode data exactly, samples with 1 to 32 steps, compares with diffusion's sampler, measures how straight the paths really are, and then drives the same velocity with the real schedulers of two production flow-matching models. Nothing is trained.

DDPM's reverse process is a 1000-step stochastic walk from N(0,I) back to the data distribution. DDIM collapsed it to 20-50 deterministic steps. You want fewer steps — ideally one. The blocker is that the ODE solving the reverse process is stiff; the path is curved.

If you could train the model such that the path from noise to data was a straight line, a single Euler step from t=1 to t=0 would work. Flow matching builds this directly: define a straight-line interpolation from x1​∼N(0,I) to x0​∼data, train a vector field vθ​(x,t) to match its time derivative, integrate at inference.

Rectified flow (Liu 2022) goes further: iteratively straighten the paths with a reflow procedure that produces a progressively closer-to-linear ODE. In the rectified-flow papers, reflow followed by distillation brought sampling down to one or a few steps (InstaFlow).

The idea

Idea 1 · Straight-line flow

Define:

xt​=t⋅x1​+(1−t)⋅x0​,t∈[0,1]

where x0​∼data and x1​∼N(0,I). The time derivative along this straight line is constant:

dtdxt​​=x1​−x0​

Define a neural vector field vθ​(xt​,t) and train it to match this derivative:

L=Ex0​,x1​,t​∥vθ​(xt​,t)−(x1​−x0​)∥2

This is the conditional flow matching loss (Lipman 2023). Training is simulation-free: you never unroll the ODE. Just sample (x0​,x1​,t) and regress.

Idea 2 · Sampling

At inference, integrate the learned vector field backwards in time:

xt−Δt​=xt​−Δt⋅vθ​(xt​,t)

Start at x1​∼N(0,I), Euler-step down to t=0.

Idea 3 · Rectified flow (Liu 2022)

Straight-line flow works but the learned paths are not actually straight — they curve because many x0​'s can map to the same x1​. Rectified flow's reflow step:

  1. Train flow model v1​ with random pairings.
  2. Sample N pairs (x1​,x0​) by integrating v1​ from x1​ to its landing x0​.
  3. Train v2​ on those paired examples. Because the pairs are now "ODE-matched", the straight-line interpolant between them is genuinely flatter.
  4. Repeat.

In practice one or two reflow iterations make the paths nearly straight. Most fast production models are distilled in other ways, though: SDXL-Turbo by adversarial distillation of a diffusion model (SDXL), LCM by consistency distillation, FLUX.1-schnell and SD3.5 Large Turbo by adversarial distillation of flow-matching models.

Idea 4 · Why this won for images in 2024

Three reasons:

  1. Simulation-free training — no ODE unrolling during training, trivial to implement.
  2. Better loss geometry — straight paths have consistent signal-to-noise, whereas DDPM ε-loss has bad SNR at edges of the schedule.
  3. Faster inference — 4-8 steps at SDXL-Turbo quality; 1 step with consistency distillation.

Flow matching vs DDPM — the exact connection

Flow matching with a Gaussian-conditional path is diffusion with a specific noise schedule. Pick the xt​=α(t)x0​+σ(t)x1​ schedule and flow matching predicts that path's time derivative, α′(t)·x₀ + σ′(t)·x₁ (for the straight line, x₁ − x₀). Knowing the velocity at a point is equivalent to knowing the predicted noise or the predicted clean image there, so with Gaussian noise, flow matching and diffusion differ only in the schedule and in what the network outputs.

What flow matching added: the clarity of the target (a plain velocity), a cleaner loss, and the license to experiment with non-Gaussian interpolants.

The same idea as a formula

The path and its velocity. With data x0​, noise x1​∼N(0,I) and t∈[0,1] (1 = noise, the lesson's convention):

xt​=(1−t)x0​+tx1​,dtdxt​​=x1​−x0​

The training target is conditional; the learned field is an average. The loss E∥vθ​(xt​,t)−(x1​−x0​)∥2 is minimised by

v∗(x,t)=E[x1​−x0​∣xt​=x]

the average direction over all (data, noise) pairs whose straight lines pass through x at time t. Where lines from different modes cross, the average bends, so the paths of v∗ are curved even though every training line is straight.

Sampling solves dx/dt=v∗(x,t) from t=1 to 0; Euler with step Δt: x←x−Δtvθ​(x,t).

One step is the conditional mean. At t=1, x1​ is independent of x0​, so v∗(x,1)=x−E[x0​] and a single Euler step returns E[x0​] for every sample: the average of the data, not a sample from it.

Connection to diffusion. For xt​=αt​x0​+σt​ε the velocity is v=α˙t​x0​+σ˙t​ε; given xt​, any one of v, ε or x^0​ determines the other two. Flow matching is diffusion with αt​=1−t, σt​=t and a velocity output.

Time shift. Production models reparameterise noise levels as σ′=1+(s−1)σsσ​ (shift s; s=3 in SD3 and Z-Image-Turbo) or set the shift from the image size, spending more steps at high noise, where large images need it.

Worked example
Straight lines and Euler by hand

One training example. x0​=2 (data), x1​=−0.5 (noise), t=0.4: xt​=0.6×2+0.4×(−0.5)=1.0, target velocity x1​−x0​=−2.5.

Euler with a perfect straight field. If the field at (1.0,0.4) were exactly −2.5, one step of Δt=0.4 gives 1.0−0.4×(−2.5)=2.0=x0​. Straight paths are integrated exactly by Euler, in any number of steps.

Why real paths are not straight. At (xt​,t)=(0,0.5), a line from data −2 to noise +2 and a line from data +2 to noise −2 both pass through 0, with velocities +4 and −4. The learned field averages them to 0 there, and paths must bend to sort themselves into the two modes.

Shift by hand. s=3, σ=0.5: σ′=1.5/(1+2×0.5)=0.75: the middle of the schedule moves toward the noisy end.

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Flow matching learns straight paths, so one Euler step is enough."
    ✓ It trains on straight lines, but the field it learns averages them and its paths curve; one step returns the data's mean (the run after Step 3). Few-step sampling needs reflow or distillation.
  • ✗ "Flow matching and diffusion are different kinds of model."
    ✓ With Gaussian noise they are the same family with different schedules and output parameterisations; the gains are in the schedule and the simplicity of the target.
  • ✗ "A larger time shift always helps."
    ✓ It moves steps toward high noise, which suits large images; on the lesson's one-dimensional data it makes 4-step sampling worse (the run under "Use it").

    x1 = rng.gauss(0, 1)
    t = rng.random()
    x_t = t * x1 + (1 - t) * x0
    target = x1 - x0
    pred = net_forward(x_t, t)

Step 2 · multi-step inference

def sample(net, num_steps):
    x = rng.gauss(0, 1)
    for i in range(num_steps):
        t = 1.0 - i / num_steps
        dt = 1.0 / num_steps
        x -= dt * net_forward(x, t)
    return x

import math, random, runpy, numpy as np
m = runpy.run_path("main.py")          # the lesson's data and conventions; main() is not run, because it trains a network
MU, S = 2.0, 0.3                        # main.py's data: modes at -2 and +2, spread 0.3
rng = random.Random(0)
data = np.sort([m["sample_data"](rng) for _ in range(20_000)])

def v_exact(x, t):
    """The best possible velocity for x_t = t*x1 + (1-t)*x0: E[x1 - x0 | x_t = x], worked out with Bayes' rule."""
    a, b = 1 - t, t                                       # x_t = a*x0 + b*x1
    var = a * a * S * S + b * b
    out = np.zeros_like(x); w = np.zeros_like(x)
    for mu in (-MU, MU):
        r = np.exp(-(x - a * mu) ** 2 / (2 * var))          # weight of this mode (equal priors)
        e_x0 = mu + a * S * S / var * (x - a * mu)
        e_x1 = b / var * (x - a * mu)
        out += r * (e_x1 - e_x0); w += r
    return out / w

def fm_sample(n_steps, n=20_000, seed=1):                 # Step 2's Euler sampler from t = 1 down to t = 0
    x = np.random.default_rng(seed).normal(size=n); dt = 1 / n_steps
    for i in range(n_steps):
        x = x - dt * v_exact(x, 1 - i * dt)
    return x

alphas, abar = (lambda b: (1 - b, np.cumprod(1 - b)))(np.linspace(1e-4, 0.02, 1000))
def eps_exact(x, ab):                                     # the same data under DDPM's schedule (Lesson 8.6)
    v = ab * S * S + 1 - ab; num = 0; den = 0
    for mu in (-MU, MU):
        r = np.exp(-(x - math.sqrt(ab) * mu) ** 2 / (2 * v))
        num = num + r * (mu + math.sqrt(ab) * S * S / v * (x - math.sqrt(ab) * mu)); den = den + r
    return (x - math.sqrt(ab) * num / den) / math.sqrt(1 - ab)
def ddim_sample(n_steps, n=20_000, seed=1):
    ts = np.linspace(999, 0, n_steps + 1).round().astype(int)
    x = np.random.default_rng(seed).normal(size=n)
    for t, t_prev in zip(ts[:-1], ts[1:]):
        e = eps_exact(x, abar[t]); x0 = (x - math.sqrt(1 - abar[t]) * e) / math.sqrt(abar[t])
        x = math.sqrt(abar[t_prev]) * x0 + math.sqrt(1 - abar[t_prev]) * e if t_prev > 0 else x0
    return x

Reading this output

With one Euler step every sample lands at 0, the data's mean, exactly as the formula box predicts: the distance to the data (W1 = 2.0) is as bad as it can be. Two steps already separate the modes but put them too close together (∣x∣ averages 1.39 instead of 2.0) and too wide; four steps get close (W1 0.21) and eight are good (0.12). At 2 and 4 steps flow matching's sampler beats DDIM on DDPM's schedule with an equally perfect model (0.61 against 1.20, 0.21 against 0.32), the practical advantage of its schedule; by 8 steps they are level. The paths are not straight: on the way from noise to its endpoint, a typical trajectory strays 0.42 from the straight chord (0.56 for the 10% most curved), because the exact field averages lines that cross between the modes. Straightening these paths is what reflow and distillation are for.

Pitfalls

  • Time parameterization. Flow matching uses t∈[0,1] with t=0 at data, t=1 at noise. DDPM uses t∈[0,T] with t=0 at data, t=T at noise. Same direction, different scale. Papers get this wrong constantly.
  • Schedule choice. Rectified flow's straight line is "the" flow-matching schedule, but you can use cosine or logit-normal t-sampling (SD3 does this) for better scale coverage.
  • Reflow cost. Generating the paired dataset for reflow is a full inference pass per sample. Only do reflow when you really need 1-2 step inference.
  • Classifier-free guidance still applies. Just swap ϵ for v in the linear combination: vcfg​=(1+w)vcond​−wvuncond​.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Flow-matching transformers are the default base. FLUX.2, Qwen-Image, Z-Image, SD3.5 and video models such as Wan, HunyuanVideo and LTX-2 train with flow matching (rectified flow), with logit-normal time sampling and resolution-dependent time shifts.
  • Few-step versions are distilled. FLUX.1-schnell, SD3.5 Large Turbo and Z-Image-Turbo reach 1–8 steps through adversarial or distribution-matching distillation; samplers in diffusers and ComfyUI (Euler, DPM-Solver++ and UniPC with flow schedules) cover the rest.
  • Speech too. F5-TTS and the acoustic stages of CosyVoice 2 generate mel spectrograms by flow matching in tens of steps.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Interpolate xt​=(1−t)x0​+tx1​; regress vθ​(xt​,t) on x1​−x0​; no simulation during training.

Practice problem · Rectified flow loss and Euler sampler

From the source repository: write the function, then press "Run the checks". They run as real Python in your browser (the first run loads Python, about 10 MB) and tell you which part is wrong and why. Hints open one at a time; the reference solution opens when every check passes.

medium25 minresearch

Rectified flow moves samples from a source distribution to a target distribution along straight lines. A network learns a velocity field v(x,t) for t from 0 to 1. An ODE solver then follows this field from a source sample x0​ to a target sample. Straight paths need very few solver steps, so the sampler is fast.

Task

Write four functions. Here x0​ is the source sample at t=0 and x1​ is the target sample at t=1.

  1. interpolate(x0, x1, t) returns the point on the straight path: xt​=(1−t)x0​+tx1​.
  2. target_velocity(x0, x1) returns the velocity of that path: x1​−x0​.
  3. flow_matching_loss(velocity_fn, x0, x1, t) returns the loss as a float:

L=B1​b=1∑B​​(x1(b)​−x0(b)​)−v(xt(b)​,t(b))​2

The squared norm sums over every axis after the first. Call velocity_fn once with the whole batch: velocity_fn(x_t, t).

  1. euler_sample(velocity_fn, x0, num_steps) integrates dx/dt=v(x,t) from t=0 to t=1 with n = num_steps equal Euler steps. Let Δt=1/n. For k=0,1,…,n−1:

x←x+Δt⋅v(x,kΔt)

At step k, pass t as a float array of shape (B,) with every entry equal to kΔt. Return x after the last step.

Time. In interpolate, t is a float or a float array of shape (B,). An array gives one time to each row and broadcasts over the other axes.

Input and output

Name Type Shape Meaning
x0 float array (B, ...) the source samples
x1 float array same as x0 the target samples, row b pairs with row b of x0
t float or float array scalar or (B,) the time of each row, from 0 to 1
velocity_fn function velocity_fn(x, t) takes x of shape (B, ...) and t of shape (B,) and returns an array with the shape of x
num_steps int the number of Euler steps
  • interpolate and target_velocity return float arrays with the shape of x0.
  • flow_matching_loss returns a Python float. In this function t is always an array of shape (B,).
  • euler_sample returns a float array with the shape of x0.

Constraints

  • 1 <= B <= 256 and 1 <= num_steps <= 1000.
  • x0 can hold integers. Work in float.
  • Do not change the input arrays.
  • Use numpy only.

Common mistakes the checks catch
  • Updates x with += on an array that shares memory with x0, so the input of the caller changes.
  • Loops over num_steps - 1 steps, so the sample stops before t = 1.
  • Reads the velocity at the end of each step, (k + 1) / n, not at its start.
  • Puts the weight t on x0 and 1 - t on x1, so the path runs backward.
  • Takes the mean of the squared error over every element, not the sum over features and the mean over the batch.
  • Uses x0 - x1 as the target velocity, so the sign is wrong.
Hint 1

To give one time to each row, reshape t to (B, 1, ...) with one 1 for each axis of x0 after the first. A scalar t needs no reshape.

Hint 2

For the loss, compute the error array first. Reshape it to (B, -1), sum the squares over axis 1, and take the mean of the B values.

Hint 3

The time of step k is the start of the step, k/n, not its end. Use np.full(B, k / n) for the time array.

Hint 4

Start the sampler from a new float array, for example np.array(x0, dtype=float). Then write x = x + dt * v, so the array of the caller does not change.

Reference solution and notes
import numpy as np


def expand(t, ndim):
    t = np.asarray(t, dtype=float)
    if t.ndim == 0:
        return t
    return t.reshape((-1,) + (1,) * (ndim - 1))


def interpolate(x0, x1, t):
    t = expand(t, np.ndim(x0))
    return (1.0 - t) * x0 + t * x1


def target_velocity(x0, x1):
    return x1 - x0


def flow_matching_loss(velocity_fn, x0, x1, t):
    x_t = interpolate(x0, x1, t)
    diff = velocity_fn(x_t, t) - target_velocity(x0, x1)
    per_sample = np.sum(diff.reshape(diff.shape[0], -1) ** 2, axis=1)
    return float(np.mean(per_sample))


def euler_sample(velocity_fn, x0, num_steps):
    x = np.array(x0, dtype=float)
    dt = 1.0 / num_steps
    for k in range(num_steps):
        t = np.full(x.shape[0], k * dt)
        x = x + dt * velocity_fn(x, t)
    return x

Why the target is x1 minus x0

The path xt​=(1−t)x0​+tx1​ is a straight line with constant speed. Its derivative with respect to t is x1​−x0​ at every point. The paper shows that the minimizer of this loss is the conditional mean of x1​−x0​ given xt​. That field moves the source distribution to the target distribution.

Why straight paths need few steps

Euler steps follow the tangent of the path. On a straight line the tangent does not change, so one step is exact. The learned field is not exactly straight, because many lines cross at the same xt​. The reflow step of the paper trains again on pairs that the model itself generates. The new paths cross less and the sampler needs fewer steps.

The direction convention

This problem puts the source at t=0 and integrates forward, as in the paper. The lesson puts the data at t=0 and the noise at t=1, and it integrates backward from t=1. The math is the same with the roles of x0​ and x1​ swapped. Check the convention of a model before you reuse its sampler.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · At the data's average, 0

All at 0, the data's average, between the two modes where there is no data at all (the run's first row: ∣x∣ = 0.00, W1 = 2.0). At t=1 the noisy point carries no information about which data point it will become, so the best velocity there points from the noise to the average data point, v∗(x,1)=x−E[x0​], and a full-length step lands exactly on that average. This is the regression-to-the-mean blur of Lesson 8.4, in another form. A one-step generator has to be trained for it specifically: by reflow, so that each noise point is paired with one particular data point, or by distillation.

SolutionB1 · Back to noise

With the loop reversed, the sampler starts on noise at t=0, where the field is trained to describe data, and moves against the direction it should: subtracting Δtv while t increases follows the flow backwards. Even with a perfect field the result is not data. Integrating the ODE in the correct direction for the clock (from t=0 to 1 with x += dt * v) would turn data into noise, which is how flow models encode images into noise for editing and inversion; mixing the two directions produces neither. The lesson's pitfall about time conventions is about exactly this: t=0 is data and t=1 is noise in flow matching, and libraries differ in how they name and order the steps.

Production note: Flux.1-schnell is flow matching at its fastest

Flow matching's production win is Flux.1-schnell — a flow-matched DiT distilled to 1-4 inference steps while keeping Flux-dev-grade quality.
