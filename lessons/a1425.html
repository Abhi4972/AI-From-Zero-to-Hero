The problem

In plain words first

Multi-agent debate asks several copies of a model (or several models) the same question, lets each read the others' answers, and asks them to answer again; the final answer is the one most agents end up with. The hope is that agents catch each other's mistakes. Two simpler ideas sit inside it: sampling several answers (diversity) and a majority vote. Debate only earns its extra calls if the reading-and-revising step adds something the vote alone does not. This lesson measures exactly that with a small model on eight word problems, and compares a full mesh (everyone reads everyone) with a star (one hub).

Self-Refine (Lesson 14.20) is one model critiquing itself — risks groupthink. CRITIC (Lesson 14.20) grounds critique in external tools — not always available. Debate introduces a third mode: multiple instances, cross-critique, convergence by disagreement.

Idea 1 · Society of Minds (Du et al., ICML 2024)

  • N model instances independently propose answers to the same question.
  • Over R rounds, each model reads the others' proposals and critiques them.
  • Models update their answers based on the critiques.
  • After R rounds, return the convergent answer.

Original experiments used N=3, R=2 due to cost. Accuracy improves with more agents and more rounds on hard problems (MMLU, GSM8K, Chess Move Validity, biography generation).

Cross-model combinations beat single-model debates: ChatGPT + Bard together > either alone.

Idea 2 · Sparse topology

"Improving Multi-Agent Debate with Sparse Communication Topology" (arXiv:2406.11776, Findings of EMNLP 2024) showed full-mesh debate is not always optimal. Sparse topologies (star, ring, hub-and-spoke) can match accuracy at lower token cost. Each debater sees only a subset of peers.

Implications:

  • Full mesh N=5, R=3 = 5 × 3 = 15 proposals, each reading 4 peers = 60 critique ops.
  • Star N=5, R=3 (one hub + 4 spokes) = 15 proposals, spokes read only the hub = 12 critique ops.

Idea 3 · When debate helps

  • Factuality. N independent proposals, cross-check reduces hallucination.
  • Rule-following. Chess move validity — one model misses a rule, others catch it.
  • Open-ended reasoning. Multiple framings narrow in on the right answer.

Idea 4 · When debate hurts

  • Latency-sensitive UX. N × R serial rounds is latency you may not have.
  • Cost-sensitive scale. N × R tokens per question.
  • Simple factual lookups. One lookup is cheaper than five debates.

Idea 5 · 2026 practical instantiations

  • Anthropic orchestrator-workers (Lesson 14.49) — one variant of debate with a synthesis step.
  • LangGraph supervisor (Lesson 14.50) — central router + specialist agents can implement debate as a node.
  • OpenAI Agents SDK (Lesson 14.78) — agents handoff back and forth for iterative critique.
  • Multi-agent evals — pair debate + evaluator-optimizer for eval signal.

Idea 6 · Where this pattern goes wrong

  • Convergence collapse. All agents converge on the first wrong answer. Mitigate with required disagreement rounds.
  • Hub failure. In a star topology, a bad hub corrupts everyone. Rotate or use multiple hubs.
  • Prompt homogenization. All agents use the same prompt; they produce the same answers. Use diverse prompts and/or models.

The same idea as a formula

Cost. With N agents, R debate rounds and each agent reading k peers per round, a question costs N(R+1) model calls and NRk peer messages to read: full mesh has k=N−1, a star has k=N−1 for the hub and k=1 for each spoke.

Why a vote helps. If three independent answers are each right with probability p, the majority is right with probability

Pmaj​=p3+3p2(1−p).
At p=0.5 that is still 0.5; at p=0.6 it is 0.648. The vote amplifies whatever accuracy the agents already have, provided their errors are not all the same.

Debate's own contribution is the change from the first-answer vote to the after-debate vote. Each agent's revision moves some answers wrong→right and some right→wrong; debate helps only if the first count beats the second.

Worked example
Counting what debate did
  • 8 problems × 3 agents = 24 individual answers. After one round, 4 went wrong→right and 5 went right→wrong.
  • One agent alone was right on 3 of 8; the majority of the three first answers on 5 of 8; the majority after debate on 5 of 8.
  • So all of the gain came from sampling three answers and voting. The debate round cost 2,430 more prompt tokens (plus its outputs) and changed nothing net.
  • The star round read 32 peer answers instead of 48 but saved only about 5% of prompt tokens (2,304 vs 2,430), because each peer message here is one number; the saving grows when agents pass their full reasoning.
Check your mental model
  • ✗ "More agents talking means more accuracy."
    ✓ Most of the gain usually comes from sampling several answers and voting.
  • ✗ "Agents only change wrong answers."
    ✓ Here five right answers were talked into wrong ones in one round.
  • ✗ "A star is the cheap version of a mesh."
    ✓ It is cheaper and puts the hub's mistakes into every spoke.
  • ✗ "Debate always converges on the truth."
    ✓ It converges on whatever the group finds persuasive, right or wrong.

# Multi-agent debate with a real model. Three Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct "agents" (the same model, sampled with
# different seeds and personas) answer eight short word problems independently, then read each other's
# answers once and answer again (full mesh). Compared: one agent alone, a majority vote of the three first
# answers (no debate), and the majority after debate. Generation is batched: one call per round.
import os, re, warnings, collections
os.environ["HF_HUB_OFFLINE"] = "1"
warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")
import torch
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM, logging as hf_logging
hf_logging.set_verbosity_error(); hf_logging.disable_progress_bar()
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct", padding_side="left")
llm = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct").eval()

PROBLEMS = [
    ("Pens cost 3 dollars each. Maya buys 4 pens and pays with a 20-dollar bill. How much change does she get?", 8),
    ("A train travels at 60 km per hour for 2.5 hours. How many km does it travel?", 150),
    ("Tom has 15 apples. He gives away 6 and then buys 9 more. How many apples does he have?", 18),
    ("A box holds 12 eggs. There are 7 full boxes and 5 eggs break. How many unbroken eggs are left?", 79),
    ("24 students are split into groups of 4. Each group gets 3 markers. How many markers are handed out?", 18),
    ("A rectangle is 8 m long and 5 m wide. What is its perimeter in metres?", 26),
    ("Sara reads 15 pages a day. How many pages does she read in 2 weeks?", 210),
    ("An item costs 40 dollars and is discounted by 25 percent. What is the new price in dollars?", 30),
]
PERSONAS = ["You are a careful maths tutor.", "You are a quick mental-arithmetic expert.", "You are a sceptical checker who verifies each step."]
FORMAT = "Think in at most three short steps, then end with 'Answer: <number>'."

def generate(chats, seed):
    torch.manual_seed(seed)
    batch = tok.apply_chat_template(chats, add_generation_prompt=True, return_tensors="pt", padding=True, return_dict=True)
    with torch.no_grad():
        out = llm.generate(**batch, max_new_tokens=110, do_sample=True, temperature=0.7, top_p=0.95)
    texts = tok.batch_decode(out[:, batch["input_ids"].shape[1]:], skip_special_tokens=True)
    return texts, int(batch["attention_mask"].sum())

def answer_of(text):
    m = re.findall(r"Answer:\s*\$?(-?\d+(?:\.\d+)?)", text) or re.findall(r"(-?\d+(?:\.\d+)?)", text)
    return float(m[-1]) if m else None

def majority(answers):
    votes = collections.Counter(a for a in answers if a is not None)
    return votes.most_common(1)[0][0] if votes else None

def first_round(seed=0):
    chats = [[{"role": "system", "content": PERSONAS[i]}, {"role": "user", "content": f"{q}\n{FORMAT}"}]
             for q, _ in PROBLEMS for i in range(3)]
    texts, tokens = generate(chats, seed)
    return [[answer_of(texts[p * 3 + i]) for i in range(3)] for p in range(len(PROBLEMS))], texts, tokens

def debate_round(prev, prev_texts, peers_of, seed=1):
    """peers_of(i) -> which agents' answers agent i reads."""
    chats = []
    for p, (q, _) in enumerate(PROBLEMS):
        for i in range(3):
            others = "\n".join(f"Agent {j + 1} answered {prev[p][j]:g}." if prev[p][j] is not None
                               else f"Agent {j + 1} gave no answer." for j in peers_of(i))
            chats.append([{"role": "system", "content": PERSONAS[i]},
                          {"role": "user", "content": f"{q}\nYour earlier answer was {prev[p][i] if prev[p][i] is not None else 'missing'}.\n"
                                                      f"Other agents:\n{others}\nCheck the reasoning and give your final answer. {FORMAT}"}])
    texts, tokens = generate(chats, seed)
    return [[answer_of(texts[p * 3 + i]) for i in range(3)] for p in range(len(PROBLEMS))], tokens

r0, texts0, tok0 = first_round()
mesh = lambda i: [j for j in range(3) if j != i]
r1, tok1 = debate_round(r0, texts0, mesh)
truth = [a for _, a in PROBLEMS]
acc = lambda answers: sum(a == t for a, t in zip(answers, truth))
print(f"{'problem':<4}{'truth':>6}   {'first answers':<20}{'after debate':<20}")
for p, t in enumerate(truth):
    f = lambda xs: ", ".join("-" if x is None else f"{x:g}" for x in xs)
    print(f"{p + 1:<4}{t:>6}   {f(r0[p]):<20}{f(r1[p]):<20}")
print()
print(f"one agent alone (agent 1, first answer)   {acc([r[0] for r in r0])}/8")
print(f"majority of three first answers           {acc([majority(r) for r in r0])}/8")
print(f"majority after one full-mesh debate round  {acc([majority(r) for r in r1])}/8")
flips = collections.Counter()
for p, t in enumerate(truth):
    for i in range(3):
        before, after = r0[p][i] == t, r1[p][i] == t
        flips[("right" if before else "wrong") + " -> " + ("right" if after else "wrong")] += 1
print("individual answers:", dict(flips))
collapse = sum(len({a for a in r1[p]}) == 1 and r1[p][0] != truth[p] for p in range(8))
print(f"problems where all three agreed on a wrong answer after debate: {collapse}")
print(f"prompt tokens: first round {tok0}, debate round {tok1}")

Reading this output

Voting does the work. One agent alone gets 3 of 8; the majority of the three first answers gets 5 of 8. Different personas and sampling give different mistakes, and the vote picks the answer two agents share.

The debate round adds nothing net. After reading the others, the majority is still right on 5 of 8. Part 1 of 3 · Debater, _make_debater, full_mesh_round, sparse_star_round

"""Multi-agent debate: full mesh vs sparse (star) topology.

Scripted debaters with different opinion drifts. Measures convergent answer,
rounds to consensus, and total critique ops (as a cost proxy).
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from collections import Counter
from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import Any, Callable


@dataclass
class Debater:
    name: str
    drift: Callable[[str, list[str]], str]


def _make_debater(name: str, bias: str,
                  corrections: dict[str, str]) -> Debater:
    def drift(question: str, peer_answers: list[str]) -> str:
        current = corrections.get(question, bias)
        if peer_answers:
            common = Counter(peer_answers).most_common(1)[0][0]
            if common != current and common != bias:
                return common
        return current
    return Debater(name=name, drift=drift)


def full_mesh_round(debaters: list[Debater], question: str,
                    prior: dict[str, str]) -> tuple[dict[str, str], int]:
    new_answers: dict[str, str] = {}
    ops = 0
    for debater in debaters:
        peers = [prior[d.name] for d in debaters if d.name != debater.name]
        new_answers[debater.name] = debater.drift(question, peers)
        ops += len(peers)
    return new_answers, ops


def sparse_star_round(hub: Debater, spokes: list[Debater], question: str,
                      prior: dict[str, str]) -> tuple[dict[str, str], int]:
    new_answers: dict[str, str] = {}
    ops = 0
    spoke_names = [s.name for s in spokes]
    new_answers[hub.name] = hub.drift(
        question, [prior[n] for n in spoke_names]
    )
    ops += len(spoke_names)
    for spoke in spokes:
        new_answers[spoke.name] = spoke.drift(
            question, [prior[hub.name]]
        )
        ops += 1
    return new_answers, ops

Part 2 of 3 · run_debate

def run_debate(debaters: list[Debater], question: str, rounds: int,
               topology: str) -> tuple[str, int, int]:
    prior: dict[str, str] = {}
    for debater in debaters:
        prior[debater.name] = debater.drift(question, [])

    total_ops = 0
    converged_round = -1
    hub = debaters[0]
    spokes = debaters[1:]
    for r in range(rounds):
        if topology == "full_mesh":
            new, ops = full_mesh_round(debaters, question, prior)
        else:
            new, ops = sparse_star_round(hub, spokes, question, prior)
        total_ops += ops
        if all(v == list(new.values())[0] for v in new.values()) and converged_round == -1:
            converged_round = r + 1
        prior = new

    votes = Counter(prior.values()).most_common(1)[0][0]
    return votes, converged_round, total_ops


def main() -> None:
    print("=" * 70)
    print("MULTI-AGENT DEBATE: Phase 14, Lesson 25")
    print("=" * 70)

    questions_and_truth = {
        "capital_of_portugal": "Lisbon",
        "is_2_plus_2_equal_4": "yes",
        "chess_legal_e4": "legal",
    }

    debaters = [
        _make_debater(
            "alpha", bias="Lisbon",
            corrections={"is_2_plus_2_equal_4": "yes",
                         "chess_legal_e4": "legal"},
        ),
        _make_debater(
            "beta", bias="Madrid",
            corrections={"capital_of_portugal": "Lisbon",
                         "is_2_plus_2_equal_4": "yes",
                         "chess_legal_e4": "legal"},
        ),
        _make_debater(
            "gamma", bias="Porto",
            corrections={"capital_of_portugal": "Lisbon",
                         "is_2_plus_2_equal_4": "yes",
                         "chess_legal_e4": "legal"},
        ),
    ]

    for q, truth in questions_and_truth.items():
        print(f"\n--- {q}  (truth: {truth}) ---")
        for topology in ("full_mesh", "sparse_star"):
            answer, converged, ops = run_debate(
                debaters, q, rounds=3, topology=topology,
            )
            correct = "CORRECT" if answer == truth else "WRONG"
            print(f"  {topology:12}  answer={answer:10}  "
                  f"converged_round={converged}  ops={ops}  {correct}")

    print()
    print("sparse star matches full mesh on accuracy with fewer critique ops.")
    print("debate helps factual and rule-based tasks; adds latency and cost.")

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Vote first. "Debate or Vote" (NeurIPS 2025 spotlight) found that majority voting alone accounts for most of the gains attributed to multi-agent debate across seven benchmarks, and modelled plain debate as not improving expected correctness. Earlier, "Should we be going MAD?" (2023) found debate does not reliably beat self-consistency at equal cost.
  • Where debate-style setups are used is in heterogeneous panels (different models or tools, so errors are less correlated), in evaluator-optimizer loops and LLM-as-judge panels (lessons 14.49 and 14.133), and in research agents that cross-check sources, rather than as a default reasoning booster.
  • Reasoning models already sample and check internally, which shrinks what an external debate can add for single-answer questions.
  • On your laptop: everything here is one 0.5B model on the CPU, batched. On the RTX 4060, a 1.5B to 3B model in bf16 with three agents and batching costs a few seconds per question.

Sources: Du et al., multiagent debate (arXiv:2305.14325), Sparse communication topology (arXiv:2406.11776), Debate or Vote (arXiv:2508.17536), Should we be going MAD? (arXiv:2311.17371).

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Separate the vote from the debate when you measure.
  • Sampling several answers and voting is the cheap, reliable part.
  • Revisions break right answers as well as fixing wrong ones.
  • A star saves reading but spreads the hub's errors.
  • Diverse models or tools make disagreement worth more.

Given a question class and accuracy target, scaffold a debate protocol.

Produce:

1. `Debater` with different prompts (and ideally different models) to avoid homogenization.
2. Round runner: full mesh, star, or ring topology.
3. Convergence rule: majority-vote, weighted by confidence, or supermajority-with-fallback.
4. Round 1 forced disagreement: every debater returns a distinct proposal if possible.
5. Cost accounting: total critique ops + token cost per question.

Hard rejects:

- All debaters with the same prompt AND same model. Guaranteed groupthink.
- Full mesh with N >= 6 without checking cost. Debate ops scale O(N*R).
- No convergence rule. Returning the round-R answer of debater 0 is not convergence.

Refusal rules:

- If the product is latency-sensitive (<1s budget), refuse debate. Use Self-Refine (Lesson 05) or parallel voting (Lesson 12) instead.
- If the question class is simple factual lookup (capital, date, definition), refuse debate. Lookup + CRITIC (Lesson 05) is cheaper.
- If the debaters have no disagreement after round 1 on any question in the eval set, refuse the protocol. You need model/prompt diversity.

Output: `debater.py`, `topology.py`, `convergence.py`, `runner.py`, `README.md` explaining N/R choice, topology rationale, and cost-vs-accuracy measurements on the eval set. End with "what to read next" pointing to Lesson 12 (workflow patterns) if the task is simpler, or Lesson 28 (orchestration patterns) for embedding debate in a larger system.

Exercises

  1. Implement a "forced disagreement" rule: in round 1, every debater must produce a distinct proposal. Measure effect on convergence speed.
  2. Add a confidence-weighted aggregation: debaters return (answer, confidence); aggregator weights by confidence. Does it help?
  3. Swap one "agent" for a different scripted LLM with different opinions. Does heterogeneity improve accuracy?
  4. Measure token cost for full mesh vs sparse on your 3 questions. Plot cost vs accuracy.
  5. Read the Society of Minds paper. Port your toy to N=5, R=3. What breaks? What gets better?

Solutions

SolutionP1 · The vote, not the debate

The majority vote of the first answers. One agent alone is right on 3 of 8; voting over three sampled answers lifts that to 5 of 8; the debate round leaves the majority at 5 of 8 while turning four wrong answers right and five right answers wrong. This is what "Debate or Vote" (NeurIPS 2025) found at scale: most of debate's gain is the vote.

SolutionB1 · The missing baseline is a vote

The comparison against sampling the old bot several times and taking a majority vote (self-consistency) at similar cost, and ideally against the same agents with the debate rounds removed. If three independent samples plus a vote give most of the 9 points, the debate rounds are paying for little. Also check: the same model and prompts for both arms, a large enough test set (on a few hundred questions, 9 points can be within noise), right→wrong flips counted separately from wrong→right, and latency, since two debate rounds are serial calls a user waits for.
