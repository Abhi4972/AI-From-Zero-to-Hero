In plain words first
The idea in one line

LDA: documents are mixtures of topics, topics are distributions over words. BERTopic: documents cluster in embedding space, clusters are topics. Same goal, different decompositions.

Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means

The problem

In plain words first

Topic modeling finds the themes in a pile of documents without anyone labelling them. You give it, say, two thousand forum posts. It gives back a handful of topics, each described by its most typical words ("space, nasa, launch, orbit"), and tells you which topics each post is about. LDA, the classic method, imagines that every document was written by picking topics from a personal mix and then picking words from those topics, and works backwards from the words to the mix. BERTopic, the modern method, turns each document into a meaning vector, groups similar vectors into clusters, and describes each cluster by the words that are unusually common in it. This lesson writes out LDA's generative story and its sampling update, works through the word-weighting that names BERTopic's clusters, and runs both on real newsgroup posts whose true categories are known, so you can check what each method found.

You have 10,000 customer support tickets, 50,000 news articles, or 200,000 tweets. You need to know what the collection is about without reading it. You do not have labeled categories. You do not even know how many categories exist.

Topic modeling answers that without supervision. Give it a corpus, get back a small set of coherent topics and, for each document, a distribution over those topics.

Two algorithmic families dominate. LDA (2003) treats each document as a mixture of latent topics and each topic as a distribution over words. Inference is Bayesian. It still ships in production where you need mixed-membership topic assignments and explainable word-level probability distributions.

BERTopic (2020) encodes documents with BERT, reduces dimensionality with UMAP, clusters with HDBSCAN, and extracts topic words via class-based TF-IDF. It tends to win on short text, social media, and anything where semantic similarity matters more than word overlap, because two posts can share meaning without sharing words. One document gets one topic, which is a limitation for long-form content.

This lesson builds intuition for both and names which one to pick for a given corpus.

The idea

LDA generative story. Each topic is a distribution over words. Each document is a mixture of topics. To generate a word in a document, sample a topic from the document's mixture, then sample a word from that topic's distribution. Inference reverses this: given observed words, infer the topic distribution per document and the word distribution per topic. Collapsed Gibbs sampling or variational Bayes does the math.

Key LDA output:

  • doc_topic: matrix (n_docs, n_topics), each row sums to 1 (document's topic mixture).
  • topic_word: matrix (n_topics, vocab_size), each row sums to 1 (topic's word distribution).

BERTopic pipeline.

  1. Encode each document with a sentence transformer (e.g., all-MiniLM-L6-v2). 384-dim vectors.
  2. Reduce dimensionality with UMAP to ~5 dimensions. BERT embeddings are too high-dim for clustering.
  3. Cluster with HDBSCAN. Density-based, produces variable-size clusters and an "outlier" label.
  4. For each cluster, compute class-based TF-IDF over the cluster's documents to extract top words.

Output is one topic per document (plus a -1 outlier label). Optionally, a soft membership via HDBSCAN's probability vector.

The same idea as a formula

LDA's generative story (Blei, Ng and Jordan, 2003), for K topics and vocabulary size V:

  1. For each topic k: draw a word distribution ϕk​∼Dirichlet(β).
  2. For each document d: draw a topic mixture θd​∼Dirichlet(α).
  3. For each word position i in d: draw a topic zd,i​∼Categorical(θd​), then a word wd,i​∼Categorical(ϕzd,i​​).

The Dirichlet priors control sparsity: a small α (below 1) says each document uses few topics; a small β says each topic uses few words. Fitting means computing the posterior p(θ,ϕ,z∣w), which has no closed form, so it is approximated: scikit-learn uses variational inference; collapsed Gibbs sampling is the easiest to understand. It integrates out θ and ϕ and resamples each word's topic given all the others:

P(zi​=k∣z−i​,w)∝(nd,k−i​+α)⋅nk−i​+Vβnk,wi​−i​+β​

where nd,k​ counts words in document d assigned to topic k, nk,w​ counts how often word w is assigned to k across the corpus, and −i means "not counting word i itself". The first factor says "this document likes topic k"; the second says "topic k likes this word".

BERTopic's class-based TF-IDF. After clustering, all documents of cluster c are joined into one long document. For term t with frequency tft,c​ in cluster c, total frequency ft​ across all clusters, and A the average number of words per cluster:

Wt,c​=tft,c​⋅log(1+ft​A​)

A word frequent in this cluster and rare overall gets a high weight; a word frequent everywhere ("the", "would") gets a low one.

Coherence. Normalised pointwise mutual information of two top words, NPMI(a,b)=−logP(a,b)logP(a)P(b)P(a,b)​​, ranges from −1 to 1; topic coherence (Cv​) aggregates NPMI-based vectors over each topic's top words and compares them with cosine similarity.

Worked example
One Gibbs update by hand

Two topics, α=0.1, β=0.01, vocabulary V=1000. We resample the topic of the word "orbit" in a document whose other words are assigned 8 to topic 1 (space) and 2 to topic 2 (cars). Across the corpus, excluding this word, "orbit" is assigned 30 times to topic 1 and once to topic 2; topic 1 has 5,000 words in total, topic 2 has 6,000.

  • Topic 1: (8+0.1)×5000+1030+0.01​=8.1×0.00599=0.0485.
  • Topic 2: (2+0.1)×6000+101+0.01​=2.1×0.000168=0.000353.
  • Normalise: P(z=1)=0.0485/(0.0485+0.000353)=0.993.

Both factors point the same way, so "orbit" almost surely goes to topic 1. Thousands of sweeps of updates like this let the topics sharpen themselves out of random initial assignments.

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "LDA finds the true topics of a corpus."
    ✓ It finds the K word distributions that best explain the counts under its assumptions; K is your choice, and different K or seeds give different, equally "valid" topics.
  • ✗ "BERTopic gives each document a mixture of topics, like LDA."
    ✓ Its clustering assigns each document to one topic (or to the outlier group); mixtures need extra steps.
  • ✗ "High coherence means the topics are useful."
    ✓ Coherence measures whether top words co-occur; a topic of boilerplate words ("subject, writes, article") can be very coherent and useless.

Step 1 · LDA via scikit-learn

from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import CountVectorizer
from sklearn.decomposition import LatentDirichletAllocation
import numpy as np


def fit_lda(documents, n_topics=5, max_features=1000):
    cv = CountVectorizer(
        max_features=max_features,
        stop_words="english",
        min_df=2,
        max_df=0.9,
    )
    X = cv.fit_transform(documents)
    lda = LatentDirichletAllocation(
        n_components=n_topics,
        random_state=42,
        max_iter=50,
        learning_method="online",
    )
    doc_topic = lda.fit_transform(X)
    feature_names = cv.get_feature_names_out()
    return lda, cv, doc_topic, feature_names


def print_top_words(lda, feature_names, n_top=10):
    for idx, topic in enumerate(lda.components_):
        top_idx = np.argsort(-topic)[:n_top]
        words = [feature_names[i] for i in top_idx]
        print(f"topic {idx}: {' '.join(words)}")

Notice: stopwords removed, min_df and max_df filter rare and ubiquitous terms, CountVectorizer (not TfidfVectorizer) because LDA expects raw counts.

Step 2 · BERTopic (production)

import json
from huggingface_hub import hf_hub_download
from bertopic import BERTopic

# 1,800 posts from six newsgroups (300 each); their true groups are kept only to check the topics afterwards
keep = ["comp.graphics", "rec.autos", "rec.sport.hockey", "sci.med", "sci.space", "talk.politics.guns"]
rows = [json.loads(l) for l in open(hf_hub_download("SetFit/20_newsgroups", "train.jsonl", repo_type="dataset"))]
picked = {g: [r for r in rows if r["label_text"] == g and len(r["text"].split()) >= 20][:300] for g in keep}
documents = [r["text"][:2000] for g in keep for r in picked[g]]
true_group = [g for g in keep for _ in picked[g]]

from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import CountVectorizer
topic_model = BERTopic(
    embedding_model="sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2",
    vectorizer_model=CountVectorizer(stop_words="english", min_df=2),   # without this, topic words are "the, to, of"
    min_topic_size=15,
    verbose=False,
)

topics, probs = topic_model.fit_transform(documents)
info = topic_model.get_topic_info()
print(info[["Topic", "Count", "Name"]].head(20).to_string())
valid_topics = info[info["Topic"] != -1]["Topic"].tolist()
for topic_id in valid_topics[:5]:
    print(f"topic {topic_id}: {[w for w, _ in topic_model.get_topic(topic_id)[:10]]}")

Reading this output

The table lists BERTopic's clusters with their sizes and automatic names (the top c-TF-IDF words joined by underscores). Five of the six newsgroups appear as one clean topic each: guns, space, hockey, graphics, medicine. Cars split into three, general car talk, radar and lights, oil and diesel engines, because those posts really do form separate clusters of meaning. Topic -1 is the outlier bucket: about 90 posts (5%) that HDBSCAN would not place in any dense region. One detail matters in the code: without a stop-word list in vectorizer_model, every topic is named "the_to_of", because c-TF-IDF only down-weights words that are frequent across clusters, and "the" is frequent everywhere but still dominates raw counts.

The filter on Topic != -1 drops BERTopic's outlier bucket (documents HDBSCAN could not cluster). min_topic_size controls HDBSCAN's minimum cluster size; BERTopic's library default is 10. This example sets it to 15 explicitly for the lesson's scale. For corpora over 10,000 documents, increase to 50 or 100.

Step 3 · evaluation

Both methods output topic words. The question is whether those words cohere.

  • Topic coherence (c_v). Combines NPMI (normalized pointwise mutual information) of top-word pairs over sliding-window contexts, aggregates the scores into topic vectors, and compares those vectors via cosine similarity. Higher is better. Use gensim.models.CoherenceModel with coherence="c_v".
  • Topic diversity. Fraction of unique words across all topics' top words. Higher is better (topics do not overlap).
  • Qualitative inspection. Read the top words of each topic. Do they name a real thing? Human judgment is still the last line of defense.

def purity(assign, labels):
    groups = {}
    for a, l in zip(assign, labels):
        groups.setdefault(a, Counter())[l] += 1
    return sum(c.most_common(1)[0][1] for a, c in groups.items() if a != -1) / sum(1 for a in assign if a != -1)
bt = np.array(topics)
print(f"purity (share of documents in their topic's majority group): LDA {purity(lda_topic, true_group):.2f}, "
      f"BERTopic {purity(bt, true_group):.2f} on the {(bt != -1).mean():.0%} it did not call outliers")

def crosstab(assign, labels, topic_ids):
    M = np.array([[sum(1 for a, l in zip(assign, labels) if a == t and l == g) for g in keep] for t in topic_ids], float)
    return M / np.maximum(M.sum(1, keepdims=True), 1)

What makes a topic "coherent"

Topic coherence (Cv​, computed with gensim over the corpus) and topic diversity (share of unique words among all topics' top 10) for both methods, plus the c-TF-IDF formula recomputed by hand for one BERTopic topic.

from gensim.corpora import Dictionary
from gensim.models import CoherenceModel
analyzer = cv.build_analyzer()
texts = [[w for w in analyzer(d)] for d in documents]
dictionary = Dictionary(texts)
lda_top = [[feature_names[i] for i in np.argsort(-t)[:10]] for t in lda.components_]
bt_top = [t for t in ([w for w, _ in topic_model.get_topic(t)[:10] if w in dictionary.token2id] for t in bt_ids) if len(t) >= 2]
for name, top in (("LDA", lda_top), ("BERTopic", bt_top)):
    cvs = CoherenceModel(topics=top, texts=texts, dictionary=dictionary, coherence="c_v").get_coherence()
    diversity = len({w for t in top for w in t}) / sum(len(t) for t in top)
    print(f"{name:9s} coherence C_v {cvs:.3f}   diversity {diversity:.2f}   ({len(top)} topics)")

t0 = bt_ids[0]
in_c = [d for d, t in zip(documents, bt) if t == t0]
tf_c = Counter(w for d in in_c for w in analyzer(d))
tf_all = Counter(w for d in documents for w in analyzer(d))
A = sum(tf_all.values()) / len(set(bt))
ctfidf = sorted(((tf_c[w] * np.log(1 + A / tf_all[w]), w) for w in tf_c), reverse=True)[:8]
print(f"\nby hand, topic {t0} ({len(in_c)} posts): " + ", ".join(f"{w} {s:.0f}" for s, w in ctfidf))
print(f"BERTopic's own words:      {[w for w, _ in topic_model.get_topic(t0)[:8]]}")

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • LLMs name the topics. BERTopic can pass each cluster's top documents and words to an LLM (OpenAI, local models via its representation models) to write a readable label and description; this is the usual production setup for exploring support tickets, reviews and survey answers.
  • Embeddings got better, and the pipeline stayed. Swapping MiniLM for a stronger embedding model is the simplest upgrade; the embed, reduce, cluster, describe pipeline is now also the standard way to explore unlabelled text before building a classifier.
  • LDA is still in use where interpretable word distributions are needed and compute is tight (Gensim, scikit-learn).

Sources: BERTopic, BERTopic LLM representations, Gensim LDA.

When to pick which

Situation Pick
Short text (tweets, reviews, headlines) BERTopic
Long documents with topic mixtures LDA
No GPU / limited compute LDA or NMF
Need document-level multi-topic distributions LDA
LLM integration for topic labeling BERTopic (direct support)
Resource-constrained edge deployment LDA
Max semantic coherence BERTopic

The biggest practical consideration is document length. BERT embeddings truncate; LDA counts work on whatever length. For documents longer than the embedding model's context, either chunk + aggregate or use LDA.

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

The 2026 stack:

  • BERTopic. Default for short text and anything where semantics matter.
  • gensim.models.LdaModel. Classic LDA for production, mature, battle-tested.
  • sklearn.decomposition.LatentDirichletAllocation. Easy LDA for experiments.
  • NMF. Non-negative matrix factorization. Fast alternative to LDA, comparable quality on short text.
  • Top2Vec. Similar design to BERTopic. Smaller community but good on some benchmarks.
  • FASTopic (NeurIPS 2024). Newer; models topics with optimal transport between document, topic and word embeddings, and its authors report it is faster than BERTopic on large corpora with competitive quality.
  • LLM-based labeling. Run any clustering, then prompt a model to name each cluster.
Break it on purpose

In fit_lda, replace the CountVectorizer with a TfidfVectorizer (keeping max_features=2000). scikit-learn's LDA accepts the matrix without complaint and prints topics as before. Which assumption of LDA's generative story have you broken, and what should you expect to happen to each document's topic mixture? (Solution B1.)

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • LDA: documents are Dirichlet mixtures of topics, topics are Dirichlet distributions over words; fit by variational inference or Gibbs sampling.
  • The Gibbs update multiplies "the document likes this topic" by "the topic likes this word".
  • BERTopic: embed, reduce with UMAP, cluster with HDBSCAN, name clusters with class-based TF-IDF; one topic per document plus outliers.
  • Evaluate with coherence and diversity, and always by reading the topics.
  • Pick by document length and by whether you need mixtures (LDA) or semantic clusters (BERTopic).

Solutions

SolutionP1 · BERTopic matches better, but leaves documents out

BERTopic matches the groups far more cleanly (purity 0.92 against 0.60): five groups come out as one topic each, and cars split into three sub-themes because BERTopic chooses its own number of clusters. LDA, forced to 6 topics, merges the graphics posts into others and spends one topic on generic forum words. What BERTopic does that LDA cannot is refuse: 5% of posts land in the outlier topic -1 and get no topic. LDA instead gives every post a full mixture, with a real second topic for about half of them.

SolutionB1 · LDA needs counts

LDA models a document as a sequence of word draws: each occurrence picks a topic, then a word, so the input must be counts of occurrences, and a document's total count is how much evidence it carries. TF-IDF rows are real-valued weights, and after the vectorizer's L2 normalisation each row's entries sum to only a few units, so a 300-word post looks to LDA like a document of a handful of words. Its posterior mixture is then dominated by the Dirichlet prior, and mixtures drift towards the flat prior (topics spread evenly), while the IDF weighting also inflates rare words inside the topics. Keep LDA on raw counts and use TF-IDF for models that expect it.
