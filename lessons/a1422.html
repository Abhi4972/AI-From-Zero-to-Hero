The problem

In plain words first

A voice agent listens, thinks and talks, and people notice delays above about half a second. The usual design is a cascade: a voice activity detector (VAD) decides when the caller has finished speaking, speech-to-text (STT) turns the audio into words, a language model writes the reply, and text-to-speech (TTS) speaks it. Every stage adds delay, so real systems stream: the model's first words go to TTS before the sentence is finished, and if the caller interrupts (barge-in), everything downstream must stop at once. Frameworks such as Pipecat and LiveKit Agents provide these pipelines. This lesson builds a frame pipeline in plain Python, then runs a real cascade on a CPU and times every stage.

Voice agents are not a text loop with TTS bolted on. Latency budgets are brutal (~600ms), partial audio is the default, turn detection is a model, and transports range from telephony SIP to WebRTC. Either you build a frame-based pipeline (Pipecat) or you lean on a platform (LiveKit).

Idea 1 · Pipecat (pipecat-ai/pipecat)

  • Python frame-based pipeline framework.
  • Frame → FrameProcessor chain.
  • Two flow directions:
  • DOWNSTREAM — source → sink (audio in, TTS out).
  • UPSTREAM — feedback and control (cancellation, metrics, barge-in).
  • PipelineTask manages lifecycle with events (on_pipeline_started, on_pipeline_finished, on_idle_timeout) and observers for metrics/tracing/RTVI.

Typical pipeline:

VAD (Silero) → STT → LLM (context alternates user/assistant) → TTS → transport

Transports: Daily, LiveKit, SmallWebRTCTransport, FastAPI WebSocket, WhatsApp.

Pipecat Flows adds structured conversations (state machines). Pipecat Cloud is the managed runtime.

Idea 2 · LiveKit Agents (livekit/agents)

  • Bridges AI models to users over WebRTC.
  • Key concepts: Agent, AgentSession, entrypoint, AgentServer.
  • Two voice agent classes:
  • MultimodalAgent — direct audio via OpenAI Realtime or equivalent.
  • VoicePipelineAgent — STT → LLM → TTS cascade; gives text-level control.
  • Both are the 0.x API: since Agents 1.0 they are deprecated, and one AgentSession runs either a realtime speech-to-speech model or an STT → LLM → TTS cascade.
  • Semantic turn detection via a transformer model.
  • Native MCP integration.
  • Telephony via SIP.
  • 50+ models with no API keys via LiveKit Inference; 200+ more via plugins.

Idea 3 · Commercial platforms

Vapi (vendor-reported ~450–600ms on an optimized premium stack) and Retell (vendor-reported ~600ms end-to-end across 180 test calls) build on top of these. Pick a platform when you want a managed voice stack without a WebRTC team.

Idea 4 · Where this pattern goes wrong

  • No barge-in handling. User interrupts; agent keeps talking. Requires UPSTREAM cancel frames in Pipecat, equivalent in LiveKit.
  • STT confidence ignored. Low-confidence transcripts fed to the LLM as if gospel. Gate on confidence or request confirmation.
  • TTS mid-sentence cutoff. When the pipeline cancels mid-utterance, TTS needs to know or cut audio.
  • Latency budget ignored. Every component adds 50–200ms. Sum your chain before shipping.

Idea 5 · Typical 2026 latencies

  • VAD: 20–60ms
  • STT partial: 100–250ms
  • LLM first token: 150–400ms
  • TTS first audio: 100–200ms
  • Transport RTT: 30–80ms

End-to-end 450–600ms is premium. 800–1200ms is common. Anything > 1500ms feels broken.

The same idea as a formula

Time to first audio in a cascade is the sum of the stages up to the first sound:

tfirst audio​=tVAD​+tSTT​+tLLM​(first chunk)+tTTS​(first chunk)+ttransport​.
Streaming shrinks the two middle terms by making the "first chunk" a clause instead of the whole reply.

Barge-in. If the caller starts speaking at time b after playback began and the reply lasts L seconds, a pipeline without cancellation keeps talking for L−b seconds over the caller; with cancellation that drops to the cancel latency tc​ (typically tens of milliseconds).

Worked example
Where the 3 seconds went, and how much streaming saves
  • On this CPU: the VAD waits its 0.3 s of silence, STT takes about 0.5 s, the model's first full sentence about 1 s and TTS of that sentence about 1 s: roughly 2.9 s to first audio, five times the lesson's premium budget.
  • The model's first token arrives in about 0.25 s, inside the budget; waiting for the whole sentence costs about 0.8 s more, and synthesising all ten words costs another second.
  • Speaking the first clause instead ("Your order has been shipped and"): the model needs about 0.5 s for six words and TTS about 0.7 s for them, so first audio falls to roughly 2 s, about 0.9 s saved only by changing when we hand text to TTS.
  • The rest of the gap is hardware and the silence rule: GPUs bring STT, LLM and TTS each to the 100 to 300 ms range, and model-based turn detection replaces most of the fixed silence wait, which is how hosted stacks reach 0.5 to 1 s.
Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Voice latency is the model's latency."
    ✓ It is the sum of VAD, STT, model, TTS and transport, and waiting points between them.
  • ✗ "Wait for the full reply, then speak it."
    ✓ Stream: speak the first clause while the model writes the rest.
  • ✗ "STT is reliable."
    ✓ Whisper heard "order of 1, 0, 0, 1" for "A one zero zero one"; gate low-confidence or critical values with a read-back.
  • ✗ "Interruptions are rare."
    ✓ People interrupt constantly; without cancellation the agent talks over them.

# A real voice cascade on the CPU, every stage timed. A synthetic caller (MMS-TTS) asks about an order; an
# energy VAD finds the end of the turn; whisper-tiny.en transcribes; Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct streams a reply;
# MMS-TTS speaks the reply's first sentence. Times are compared with the lesson's 2026 budgets.
import os, re, threading, time, warnings
os.environ["HF_HUB_OFFLINE"] = "1"
warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")
import numpy as np, torch
from transformers import (AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM, TextIteratorStreamer, VitsModel,
                          WhisperProcessor, WhisperForConditionalGeneration, logging as hf_logging)
hf_logging.set_verbosity_error(); hf_logging.disable_progress_bar()
torch.manual_seed(0)
SR = 16_000
tts_tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("facebook/mms-tts-eng"); tts = VitsModel.from_pretrained("facebook/mms-tts-eng").eval()
stt_proc = WhisperProcessor.from_pretrained("openai/whisper-tiny.en")
stt = WhisperForConditionalGeneration.from_pretrained("openai/whisper-tiny.en").eval()
llm_tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct")
llm = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct").eval()

def speak(text):
    """Text to 16 kHz audio with MMS-TTS; returns (waveform, seconds taken)."""
    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    with torch.no_grad():
        wav = tts(**tts_tok(text, return_tensors="pt")).waveform[0].numpy()
    return wav, time.perf_counter() - t0

def vad_end_of_turn(audio, frame_ms=30, silence_ms=300, threshold=0.01):
    """Energy VAD: the turn ends after silence_ms of frames whose RMS is under threshold."""
    n = SR * frame_ms // 1000
    quiet, last_voice = 0, None
    for i in range(0, len(audio) - n, n):
        if np.sqrt(np.mean(audio[i:i + n] ** 2)) >= threshold:
            quiet, last_voice = 0, i + n
        elif last_voice is not None:
            quiet += frame_ms
            if quiet >= silence_ms:
                return last_voice, i + n               # end of speech, the point where we decide
    return last_voice, len(audio)

def transcribe(audio):
    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    feats = stt_proc(audio, sampling_rate=SR, return_tensors="pt").input_features
    with torch.no_grad():
        ids = stt.generate(feats, max_new_tokens=40)
    return stt_proc.batch_decode(ids, skip_special_tokens=True)[0].strip(), time.perf_counter() - t0

SYSTEM = "You are a phone support agent for an online shop. Order A-1001 shipped on 20 November. Answer briefly."
def stream_reply(user_text, stop_when):
    """Stream the model's reply; return (text so far when stop_when(text) is true, first-token s, that s)."""
    ids = llm_tok.apply_chat_template([{"role": "system", "content": SYSTEM}, {"role": "user", "content": user_text}],
                                      add_generation_prompt=True, return_tensors="pt", return_dict=True)["input_ids"]
    streamer = TextIteratorStreamer(llm_tok, skip_prompt=True, skip_special_tokens=True)
    t0 = time.perf_counter()
    th = threading.Thread(target=llm.generate, kwargs=dict(input_ids=ids, max_new_tokens=60, do_sample=False, streamer=streamer))
    th.start()
    text, first, done_at = "", None, None
    for piece in streamer:
        if first is None and piece.strip():
            first = time.perf_counter() - t0
        text += piece
        if done_at is None and stop_when(text):
            done_at = time.perf_counter() - t0
            chunk = text
    th.join()
    if done_at is None:
        done_at, chunk = time.perf_counter() - t0, text
    return chunk.strip(), text.strip(), first, done_at

# The caller: silence, the question, silence (as a phone line would deliver it).
question, _ = speak("Hi, what is the status of my order A one zero zero one?")
caller = np.concatenate([np.zeros(SR // 2, np.float32), question, np.zeros(SR, np.float32)])

t0 = time.perf_counter(); speech_end, decided = vad_end_of_turn(caller); t_vad = (decided - speech_end) / SR * 1000
heard, t_stt = transcribe(caller[:speech_end])
sentence, full, t_first, t_sentence = stream_reply(heard, lambda s: re.search(r"[.!?](\s|$)", s) is not None)
reply_audio, t_tts = speak(sentence)

print(f"caller said : 'Hi, what is the status of my order A one zero zero one?'")
print(f"STT heard   : {heard!r}")
print(f"agent reply : {full!r}")
print(f"first sentence ({len(sentence.split())} words) spoken as {len(reply_audio) / SR:.1f} s of audio\n")
rows = [("VAD end-of-turn wait", t_vad, "20-60"), ("STT (whisper-tiny.en)", t_stt * 1000, "100-250"),
        ("LLM first token", t_first * 1000, "150-400"), ("LLM first sentence", t_sentence * 1000, "-"),
        ("TTS first audio", t_tts * 1000, "100-200")]
print(f"{'stage':<28}{'measured ms':>12}   lesson budget ms")
for name, ms, budget in rows:
    print(f"{name:<28}{ms:>12.0f}   {budget}")
total = t_vad + (t_stt + t_sentence + t_tts) * 1000
print(f"{'to first audio':<28}{total:>12.0f}   450-600 premium, 800-1200 common, >1500 feels broken")

Reading this output

It works end to end, slowly. The synthetic caller's question goes in as audio and a spoken answer comes out about 2.9 seconds after the caller stops talking. On this CPU every stage except the model's first token is over the lesson's budget.

The silence rule is a fixed cost. The VAD cannot know the caller has finished until 300 ms of quiet have passed, five times the lesson's VAD budget, before any model runs. That is why production stacks use a turn-detection model instead of a timer.

The model's first token is not the problem. About 0.25 s, within the budget. Waiting for the whole first sentence (about 1 s) and then synthesising all ten words of it (about 1 s for 3.9 s of audio) is.

Speech recognition changed the facts. Whisper tiny heard "I, what is the status of my order of 1, 0, 0, 1?". The model never repeated the order number and answered with the one order it knew, adding "currently in transit", which nothing in its prompt says. Voice adds a transcription error channel to everything the text agents in earlier lessons already got wrong.

Part 1 of 4 · Frame, Processor, VAD, STT, LLM

"""Toy Pipecat-style voice pipeline: VAD  STT  LLM  TTS  transport.

Frames travel DOWNSTREAM (source to sink) and UPSTREAM (cancel/control).
A scripted input shows normal flow plus a barge-in cancel that stops TTS.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass, field
from typing import Any, Callable


@dataclass
class Frame:
    kind: str
    payload: Any
    direction: str = "downstream"


class Processor:
    def __init__(self, name: str) -> None:
        self.name = name
        self.next: Processor | None = None
        self.prev: Processor | None = None
        self.trace: list[str] = []

    def process(self, frame: Frame) -> None:
        self.trace.append(f"{self.name} saw {frame.kind}")
        if self.next is not None and frame.direction == "downstream":
            self.next.process(frame)
        elif self.prev is not None and frame.direction == "upstream":
            self.prev.process(frame)


class VAD(Processor):
    def process(self, frame: Frame) -> None:
        if frame.kind == "audio_chunk":
            is_speech = bool(frame.payload)
            self.trace.append(f"VAD: speech={is_speech}")
            if is_speech:
                super().process(Frame("vad_speech", frame.payload))
        else:
            super().process(frame)


class STT(Processor):
    def process(self, frame: Frame) -> None:
        if frame.kind == "vad_speech":
            transcript = str(frame.payload)
            self.trace.append(f"STT: -> {transcript!r}")
            super().process(Frame("transcript", transcript))
        else:
            super().process(frame)


class LLM(Processor):
    def __init__(self, name: str, replies: dict[str, str]) -> None:
        super().__init__(name)
        self.replies = replies

    def process(self, frame: Frame) -> None:
        if frame.kind == "cancel":
            self.trace.append("LLM: cancelled")
            super().process(frame)
            return
        if frame.kind == "transcript":
            text = str(frame.payload)
            reply = self.replies.get(text, "[no canned reply]")
            self.trace.append(f"LLM: {text!r}  -> {reply!r}")
            super().process(Frame("text", reply))
        else:
            super().process(frame)

Part 2 of 4 · TTS, Transport, link

class TTS(Processor):
    def __init__(self, name: str) -> None:
        super().__init__(name)
        self.cancelled = False

    def process(self, frame: Frame) -> None:
        if frame.kind == "cancel":
            self.cancelled = True
            self.trace.append("TTS: cancel received; drop pending audio")
            super().process(frame)
            return
        if frame.kind == "text":
            self.cancelled = False
            words = str(frame.payload).split()
            emitted: list[str] = []
            for w in words:
                if self.cancelled:
                    self.trace.append(f"TTS: cut mid-word after {emitted}")
                    break
                emitted.append(w)
            self.trace.append(f"TTS: emitted {emitted}")
            super().process(Frame("tts_audio", emitted))
        else:
            super().process(frame)


class Transport(Processor):
    def __init__(self, name: str) -> None:
        super().__init__(name)
        self.delivered: list[list[str]] = []

    def process(self, frame: Frame) -> None:
        if frame.kind == "tts_audio":
            self.delivered.append(list(frame.payload))
            self.trace.append(f"transport: sent {len(frame.payload)} words")
        else:
            super().process(frame)


def link(*processors: Processor) -> None:
    for a, b in zip(processors, processors[1:]):
        a.next = b
        b.prev = a

Part 3 of 4 · setup

def main() -> None:
    print("=" * 70)
    print("VOICE PIPELINE (PIPECAT-SHAPED): Phase 14, Lesson 22")
    print("=" * 70)

    vad = VAD("vad")
    stt = STT("stt")
    llm = LLM("llm", replies={
        "hello": "hi there, how can I help today?",
        "refund please": (
            "sure, I can help with a refund; what order number should I look up?"
        ),
    })
    tts = TTS("tts")
    transport = Transport("transport")
    link(vad, stt, llm, tts, transport)

    print("\nscenario 1: normal flow")
    vad.process(Frame("audio_chunk", "hello"))
    print(f"  transport delivered: {transport.delivered[-1]}")

    print("\nscenario 2: barge-in mid-utterance")
    tts.cancelled = False
    vad.process(Frame("audio_chunk", "refund please"))
    transport.process(Frame("cancel", None, direction="upstream"))

    print("  trace across pipeline")
    for proc in (vad, stt, llm, tts, transport):
        for line in proc.trace:
            print(f"    {proc.name}: {line}")

    print()
    print("barge-in needs UPSTREAM cancel frames that propagate back to TTS+LLM.")
    print("sum latency per stage; premium stack lands at 450-600ms end-to-end.")

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

  • Pipecat for full control — custom processors, Python-first, pluggable providers.
  • LiveKit Agents for WebRTC-first deployments and telephony.
  • Vapi / Retell for hosted voice agents without a WebRTC team.
  • OpenAI Realtime / Gemini Live for direct audio-in/audio-out (MultimodalAgent).
Break it on purpose

A voice agent reads back order numbers and confirms refunds by voice. In testing, one call in twenty refunds the wrong order. Using the first block's transcript, what is likely going on, and what would you change in the pipeline? (Solution B1.)

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Frameworks: Pipecat (Python, frame pipelines, Pipecat Cloud) and LiveKit Agents (Python and Node, AgentSession since 1.0, telephony via SIP, turn-detection models) are the common open-source stacks; Vapi, Retell and Bland are hosted platforms built on similar parts.
  • Speech-to-speech models (OpenAI's realtime models, Gemini Live) skip the text in the middle and cut latency, at the cost of text-level control; cascades remain common where tools, logging and exact wording matter.
  • Turn detection moved from silence timers to small models that judge whether the caller has finished (LiveKit's turn detector, Pipecat's smart turn), which reduces both interruptions of the caller and slow responses.
  • On your laptop: everything here runs on the CPU at two to three seconds per turn. On the RTX 4060, faster-whisper (small or base), a 1.5B to 3B instruct model in bf16 and a fast TTS such as Kokoro or Piper bring a local cascade to around one second.

Sources: Pipecat, LiveKit Agents, LiveKit Agents v0 to v1 migration, Whisper (arXiv:2212.04356), MMS (arXiv:2305.13516), OpenAI Realtime API.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • A cascade's delay is the sum of its stages and its waiting points.
  • Stream: hand the first clause to TTS, not the finished reply.
  • Barge-in needs a cancel path back through TTS and the model.
  • Transcripts of numbers and names need confirmation.
  • Measure each stage on your hardware before promising latency.

Given a voice product spec (language, transport, providers), scaffold a frame-based pipeline.

Produce:

1. `Frame` type with `kind`, `payload`, `direction` (downstream / upstream).
2. Processors: `VAD`, `STT`, `LLM`, `TTS`, `Transport`. Each with `process(frame)`.
3. `link()` helper chaining processors forward and backward.
4. Cancel frame handling: UPSTREAM path from transport to TTS to LLM to STT, dropping pending work at each stage.
5. Observers: per-stage latency metrics; emit an OTel span per frame crossing a processor (Lesson 23).
6. Confidence gate on STT: below threshold, emit a "please repeat" text frame instead of transcript.

Hard rejects:

- Pipeline without UPSTREAM handling. Barge-in is not optional for voice.
- LLM calls without streaming. First-token latency dominates; must be streamed.
- Confidence-blind STT. Feeding wrong transcripts to the LLM produces wrong replies.

Refusal rules:

- If end-to-end latency exceeds 1500ms on a cold run, refuse to ship. Optimize the chain or use a MultimodalAgent (LiveKit direct-audio).
- If the product is telephony-first and the pipeline has no SIP adapter, refuse. Route through LiveKit SIP or a platform (Vapi/Retell).
- If the product carries PII audio without encryption in transit, refuse.

Output: `frames.py`, `processors.py`, `pipeline.py`, `observers.py`, `README.md` explaining the latency budget, barge-in design, and transport choice. End with "what to read next" pointing to Lesson 23 (OTel), Lesson 24 (observability backends), or LiveKit docs for WebRTC specifics.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · About three seconds, spread over every stage

About 3 seconds to first audio, five times the premium budget. No single stage dominates: the fixed 0.3 s silence wait, about 0.5 s of STT, about 1 s for the model to finish its first sentence (its first token arrives in about a quarter of a second) and about 1 s of TTS for that sentence. Streaming the first clause instead of the first sentence brings the total to about 2 s on the same hardware; the rest needs faster hardware and a turn-detection model.

SolutionB1 · Mishearing order numbers

Speech recognition is turning order numbers into something else, as Whisper turned "A one zero zero one" into "order of 1, 0, 0, 1"; with similar numbers in the account, the model can match the wrong one, and the agent acts on it. Fluent replies hide the error.

Changes: never act on a spoken identifier without a read-back ("I heard order A-1001, is that right?") and an explicit yes; use STT confidence and word-level timestamps to flag low-confidence digits; constrain the STT with expected formats or a list of the caller's actual order numbers (keyword boosting or a post-processing match); prefer keypad entry (DTMF) for account and order numbers on phone lines; and require a second confirmation for money-moving actions. Log the audio and transcript of every refund for audit.
