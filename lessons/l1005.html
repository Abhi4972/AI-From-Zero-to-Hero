Safety and security

FoundationWhat is prompt injection, and what is indirect prompt injection?

Prompt injection is text that tries to override the agent's instructions. Direct injection comes from the user; indirect injection hides in content the agent reads: web pages, emails, documents, tool results. Indirect injection is more dangerous because the user may be innocent and the agent may have tools.

AdvancedHow would you secure an agent that reads customer emails and can issue refunds?

Recognize the lethal trifecta: private data, untrusted content and an external action. Apply the Rule of Two: require human approval for refunds above a threshold, or split reading and acting into separate agents with a constrained interface. Add layered controls: screen emails for injection (Prompt Shieldslater · 14.127, Model Armorlater · 14.129, Bedrock Guardrails, or a classifier), enforce refund limits and allowed accounts in the tool or a policy enginelater · 14.125, least-privilege credentials, logging and alerts on unusual refund patterns, and red-team tests in CI.

IntermediateWhat do guardrails cover, and where do they run?

Input checks (injection, topic, PII), tool-level checks (permissions, argument validation, approval for risky actions), output checks (PII, toxicity, groundedness, format) and system checks (rate limits, budgets). They run in the application, in a gateway, or in the cloud service (Bedrock Guardrails, Azure Content Safety, Model Armor). Deterministic rules go in code; judgment calls use classifiers or LLM checks, measured for false positives.

IntermediateWhat is the difference between a jailbreak and a prompt injection?

A jailbreak tries to make the model break its own safety policies ("pretend you have no rules"); the attacker is usually the user and the target is the model's behavior. Prompt injection tries to make the application do something its developer did not intend, often through content the agent reads; the target is the system's data and actions. Jailbreak defenses live mostly in the model and content filters; injection defenses must live in the system design: privileges, approvals, data-flow controls.

IntermediateWhat is the OWASP Top 10 for agentic applications?

Published in December 2025, it lists: agent goal hijack, tool misuse, identity and privilege abuse, agentic supply chain vulnerabilities, unexpected code execution, memory and context poisoning, insecure inter-agent communication, cascading failures, human-agent trust exploitation, and rogue agents. In a design review, pick the two or three most relevant to the system being discussed and say how you would mitigate each, rather than reciting the list.

AdvancedHow do you stop an agent from leaking data through its answers?

Assume injected instructions will sometimes succeed, so control the exits: strip or allowlist link and image domains in rendered output (markdown image exfiltration), disable or allowlist outbound HTTP tools, scan outputs for PII, secrets and canary tokens, and keep untrusted content away from agents that hold private data and external tools at the same time. Taint tracking or the dual-LLM pattern gives stronger guarantees for high-risk flows.

AdvancedHow would you run generated code safely?

In an isolated sandbox: a container with no network by default, read-only file system except a scratch directory, CPU, memory and time limits, no credentials in the environment, and ideally a stronger boundary such as gVisor or a Firecracker microVM. Managed options include AgentCore Code Interpreter, Azure container sessions, E2B and Modal. Log the code and its outputs, and treat outputs as untrusted data on the way back into the agent.

IntermediateHow do you red-team an agent?

Threat-model first: list the untrusted inputs and the harmful actions. Build an attack suite per category (direct and indirect injection, privilege abuse, data exfiltration, harmful content), mutate attacks automatically (encodings, paraphrase, multi-turn escalation) with PyRIT, garak or promptfoo, and measure attack success in terms of harm (did a forbidden tool run, did data leave), not words. Run it in CI and before launch, add every successful attack as a regression test, and do periodic human red-teaming for creative attacks.

AdvancedYour guardrail blocks 6% of legitimate requests. How do you fix it without weakening safety?

Sample the blocked requests and label them: which category fired, and why it was wrong. Usually one or two categories cause most false positives. Tune thresholds per category on labeled traffic, add allow-rules for known legitimate patterns, and put a second, more accurate check behind the cheap one so the final decision is made only for uncertain cases. Keep the design controls (permissions, approvals) unchanged so safety does not depend on the classifier, and track false-positive and miss rates together on a fixed test set.

Governance and regulation

IntermediateWhat does the EU AI Act require of a company deploying an AI agent?

It depends on the risk class. Most business agents are limited-risk: transparency duties from 2 August 2026 (tell users they are interacting with AI; label synthetic media), plus AI literacy for staff. High-risk uses (hiring, credit scoring, essential services and others in Annex III) carry risk management, data governance, logging, human oversight, accuracy and documentation duties for providers and deployers; after the Digital Omnibus these apply from 2 December 2027. It applies to non-EU companies whose systems are used in the EU. Always say you would confirm the current text with legal counsel.

IntermediateHow does India's DPDP Act affect an agent that handles customer data?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its 2025 Rules require a lawful basis (usually consent with a clear notice, or a listed legitimate use), purpose limitation, reasonable security safeguards, breach reporting (to the Board within 72 hours under the Rules), and data principal rights such as access, correction and erasure. Most obligations phase in by about May 2027. For an agent, that means mapping every store of personal data (logs, traces, memories, vector indexes, caches), redacting where possible, short retention, and an erasure path that reaches all of them.

AdvancedWhat goes into an AI governance program, practically?

A register of every AI system with owner, purpose, data, models and risk tier; a short risk-tiering questionnaire; review gates proportional to risk; documentation generated from evidence (system cards with eval and red-team results and known limitations); monitoring, an incident process and a kill switch; training and named accountability. Frameworks to anchor it: NIST AI RMF and its generative AI profile, ISO/IEC 42001 for certification. The success test is that teams use the low-risk path instead of routing around governance.
