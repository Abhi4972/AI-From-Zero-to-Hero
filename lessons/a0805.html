In plain words first
The idea in one line

Most generators stir z into every layer at the same time. StyleGAN split it apart: first map z to an intermediate w, then inject w at every resolution level through AdaIN. That change made the latent space far more controllable and set the standard for photorealistic faces for years.

Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means
Mapping network "The MLP" $f: Z \to W$, 8 layers, decouples latent geometry from data statistics.
W space "The style space" Output of the mapping network; roughly disentangled.
AdaIN "Adaptive instance norm" Normalize feature map, then scale + shift by $w$-projection.
Truncation trick "Psi" $w = \text{mean} + \psi \cdot (w - \text{mean})$, $\psi<1$ trades diversity for quality.
Path-length regularization "PL reg" Penalizes large changes in image per unit change in $w$; makes $W$ smoother.
Weight demodulation "The StyleGAN2 fix" Normalize conv weights instead of activations; kills droplet artifacts.
Alias-free "StyleGAN3's trick" Windowed sinc filters; eliminates texture sticking to the pixel grid.
Inversion "Find w for a real image" Optimize or encode $x \to w$ so $G(w) \approx x$.

The problem

In plain words first

In an ordinary GAN, the random input numbers are fed in once at the start and every property of the picture (layout, lighting, colour, texture) ends up tangled together in them. StyleGAN feeds them in differently. A small network first converts the random numbers into a "style" vector, and that style is applied at every stage of image building, from the coarse low-resolution stages that settle the layout to the fine ones that settle textures. Because each stage reads its own copy of the style, you can take the coarse stages' style from one image and the fine stages' style from another. StyleGAN2 and StyleGAN3 fixed artefacts in the original. This lesson runs the lesson's toy version, checks StyleGAN2's normalisation trick numerically, and then runs a real StyleGAN3 trained on landscape photos: the truncation trick, style mixing, and StyleGAN3's ability to move a picture smoothly. Nothing is trained.

A DCGAN maps z to an image through a stack of transposed convolutions. The problem: z controls everything — pose, lighting, identity, background — entangled together. Move along one axis of z, all four change. You cannot ask the model "same person, different pose" because the representation does not factor that way.

Karras et al. (2019, NVIDIA) proposed: stop feeding z directly into conv layers. Feed a constant 4×4×512 tensor as the network input. Learn an 8-layer MLP that maps z∈Z→w∈W. Inject w at every resolution via adaptive instance normalization (AdaIN): normalize each conv feature map, then scale and shift by affine projections of w. Add per-layer noise for stochastic detail (skin pores, hair strands).

The result: W has roughly orthogonal axes for "high-level style" (pose, identity) vs "fine style" (lighting, color). You can swap styles between two images by using image A's w for the low-resolution levels and image B's w for the high. This unlocked editing, cross-domain stylization, and the entire "StyleGAN-inversion" line of research.

The idea

Mapping network. f:Z→W, an 8-layer MLP. Z=N(0,I)512. W is not forced to be Gaussian — it learns a data-adapted shape.

Synthesis network. Starts from a learned constant 4×4×512. Each resolution block: upsample → conv → AdaIN(w_i) → noise → conv → AdaIN(w_i) → noise. Resolutions double: 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024.

AdaIN.

AdaIN(x,y)=yscale​⋅std(x)x−mean(x)​+ybias​

where yscale​ and ybias​ come from affine projections of w. Normalize per feature map, then restyle. "Style" here is the first- and second-order statistics of the feature map.

Per-layer noise. Single-channel Gaussian noise added to each feature map, scaled by a learned per-channel factor. Controls stochastic detail without affecting global structure.

Truncation trick. At inference, sample z, compute w=mapping(z), then w′=w^+ψ⋅(w−w^) where w^ is the mean w over many samples. ψ<1 trades diversity for quality. Almost every StyleGAN demo uses ψ≈0.7.

The same idea as a formula

Mapping and styles. w=f(z) with z∼N(0,I512​) and f an 8-layer MLP. Layer i of the synthesis network receives its own copy wi​ (all equal unless mixing) and turns it into per-channel styles with a learned affine map, si​=Ai​wi​+bi​.

AdaIN (StyleGAN). For feature map c of activation x:

AdaIN(xc​,s)=scscale​⋅σ(xc​)xc​−μ(xc​)​+scbias​

Every map leaves with the statistics the style dictates, whatever it came in with.

Modulation and demodulation (StyleGAN2). Scale the convolution weights instead of the activations, then normalise each output channel:

wijk′​=si​⋅wijk​,wijk′′​=∑i,k​(wijk′​)2+ε​wijk′​​

(i input channel, j output channel, k kernel position). For unit-variance, independent inputs the output of channel j then has unit variance again, whatever the styles, so scales cannot compound across layers.

Truncation. With wˉ=Ez​[f(z)]: w′=wˉ+ψ(w−wˉ), ψ∈[0,1].

Style mixing. Use wA​ for layers 0,…,k−1 and wB​ for layers k,…; trained with random mixing so that no layer relies on its neighbours sharing its w.

Path-length regularisation (StyleGAN2). Penalise (∥Jw⊤​y∥2​−a)2 with y random image-space noise and a a running average, so a fixed step in W changes the image by a fixed amount everywhere.

Worked example
Demodulation by hand

A 3×3 convolution with 256 input channels, weights of variance 1/(256×9), unit-variance inputs:

  • Without styles: output variance =256×9×256×91​=1.
  • With every style si​=10: weights scale by 10, output variance by 100, standard deviation 10.
  • Through 14 such layers with styles of 2: standard deviation 214=16,384; with styles of 0.5, 0.514≈6×10−5.
  • Demodulation divides output channel j's weights by ∑i,k​(si​wijk​)2​, here 100×1​=10 when all si​=10, restoring standard deviation 1.

Truncation by hand. wˉ=(0.2,0.0), w=(1.2,−2.0), ψ=0.5: w′=(0.2+0.5×1.0,0+0.5×(−2.0))=(0.7,−1.0).

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "The mapping network makes w Gaussian."
    ✓ z is Gaussian; w takes whatever shape suits the data, which is why truncation pulls toward the measured average wˉ, not toward zero (the run under "Use it").
  • ✗ "Truncation makes generation cheaper or safer to serve."
    ✓ It only moves the input closer to the average; the network and its cost are unchanged.
  • ✗ "In every StyleGAN, the first few layers decide layout and the last ones decide colour."
    ✓ That clean split holds roughly for StyleGAN1 and 2 on faces; in the StyleGAN3 run under "Use it", the first five copies of w change almost nothing and the middle layers carry layout and colour.

StyleGAN 1 → 2 → 3

Version Year Innovation
StyleGAN 2019 Mapping network + AdaIN + noise + progressive growing.
StyleGAN2 2020 Weight demodulation replaces AdaIN (fixes droplet artifacts); skip/residual architecture; path-length regularization.
StyleGAN3 2021 Alias-free convolution + equivariant kernels; eliminates texture sticking to pixel grid.
StyleGAN-XL 2022 Class-conditional, 1024², ImageNet.
R3GAN 2024 Regularised relativistic loss and a modern ConvNet; matches or beats StyleGAN2 on FFHQ and ImageNet benchmarks without its tricks.

StyleGAN2 and StyleGAN3 remain good choices for (a) narrow-domain photorealism at high frame rates, (b) training on small datasets (StyleGAN2-ADA's augmentation works with a few thousand images or fewer), (c) inversion-based editing (find the w that reconstructs a real photo, then edit that w). For open-domain text-to-image they are not the tool; diffusion is.

AdaIN against weight demodulation

A styled 3×3 convolution, as in StyleGAN2, applied to unit-variance features with styles of 0.1, 1, 10 and random values, with and without demodulation; then AdaIN on eight feature maps of different sizes.

import torch, torch.nn.functional as F
torch.manual_seed(0)
cin, cout = 256, 256
x = torch.randn(4, cin, 32, 32)                        # unit-variance input features
weight = torch.randn(cout, cin, 3, 3) / (cin * 9) ** 0.5
def styled_conv(x, s, demod):
    # StyleGAN2: scale the weights by the style (one factor per input channel) instead of the activations...
    w = weight[None] * s[:, None, :, None, None]              # (batch, out, in, 3, 3)
    if demod:                                                  # ...then rescale each output channel to unit gain
        w = w / (w.pow(2).sum((2, 3, 4), keepdim=True) + 1e-8).sqrt()
    out = F.conv2d(x.reshape(1, -1, 32, 32), w.reshape(-1, cin, 3, 3), padding=1, groups=x.shape[0])
    return out.reshape(x.shape[0], cout, 32, 32)
styles = torch.stack([torch.full((cin,), 0.1), torch.ones(cin), torch.full((cin,), 10.0), torch.rand(cin) * 5])
names = ["all styles 0.1", "all styles 1", "all styles 10", "random 0-5"]
print("output std per image:      " + "  ".join(f"{n:>15}" for n in names))
for demod in (False, True):
    out = styled_conv(x, styles, demod)
    print(f"  {'with' if demod else 'without'} demodulation:  " + "  ".join(f"{v:15.3f}" for v in out.std((1, 2, 3)).tolist()))

# AdaIN, StyleGAN1's version, for comparison: normalise each feature map, then impose the style's statistics.
feat = torch.randn(1, 8, 16, 16) * torch.arange(1, 9).view(1, 8, 1, 1) + 3      # maps with different scales
scale, bias = torch.full((8,), 2.0), torch.zeros(8)
mu, sd = feat.mean((2, 3), keepdim=True), feat.std((2, 3), keepdim=True)
ada = scale.view(1, 8, 1, 1) * (feat - mu) / sd + bias.view(1, 8, 1, 1)
print("\nfeature-map std before AdaIN:", " ".join(f"{v:.1f}" for v in feat.std((2, 3))[0].tolist()))
print("feature-map std after AdaIN: ", " ".join(f"{v:.1f}" for v in ada.std((2, 3))[0].tolist()),
      "(the relative sizes of the maps are gone)")

  without demodulation:            0.098            0.980            9.761            2.744
  with demodulation:            0.978            0.980            0.976            0.977

feature-map std before AdaIN: 1.0 2.0 2.9 4.1 5.2 5.2 7.2 7.8

Reading this output

Without demodulation the output's standard deviation is simply the style: 0.1, 1, 10, and 2.7 for random styles averaging 2.5. Stack 14 layers and these factors multiply, which is why StyleGAN1 normalised activations instead. With demodulation every case comes out at 0.98, the scale of an unstyled layer, so styles can change which features are emphasised without changing the overall scale. The AdaIN line shows what StyleGAN1 paid for its normalisation: eight maps with standard deviations from 1 to 8 all leave with 2. The information about which map was strongest is gone, and StyleGAN1's generator learned to smuggle it through a large local spike, the droplet artefact in the pitfalls.

Step 1 · mapping network

code/main.py implements a toy "style-GAN lite" in 1-D: a mapping MLP, a synthesis function that takes a learned constant vector and modulates it with w-derived scale/bias, and per-layer noise. It shows that injecting w via affine-modulation matches or beats concatenating z into the generator's input.

def mapping(z, M):
    h = z
    for i in range(num_layers):
        h = leaky_relu(add(matmul(M[f"W{i}"], h), M[f"b{i}"]))
    return h

Step 2 · adaptive instance normalization

def adain(x, w_scale, w_bias):
    mu = mean(x)
    sd = std(x)
    x_norm = [(xi - mu) / (sd + 1e-8) for xi in x]
    return [w_scale * xi + w_bias for xi in x_norm]

Per-feature-map scale and bias come from w via linear projection.

Step 3 · per-layer noise

def add_noise(x, sigma, rng):
    return [xi + sigma * rng.gauss(0, 1) for xi in x]

Sigma per-channel is learnable.

Reading this output

With AdaIN, outputs for five different z spread about 13 times more widely (std 0.152 against 0.012): the style reaches the output through the scale and bias at every layer, while without AdaIN the constant input barely depends on w. The truncation table moves smoothly from the average-w output at ψ=0 to the full sample at ψ=1, the formula wˉ+ψ(w−wˉ) at work. The noise rows keep w fixed and change only the noise seed: the outputs change too, by more than truncation moves them, because this toy's noise scale is not trained. In a trained StyleGAN the learned per-channel noise strengths are small, and noise changes only details such as hair strands or ripples, as the run under "Use it" shows for landscapes.

Pitfalls

  • Droplet artifacts. StyleGAN 1 produced a blobby droplet in the feature maps because AdaIN normalises each feature map separately, destroying the information about their relative sizes; the generator smuggled it past with a strong local spike. StyleGAN 2's weight demodulation fixes it by scaling the convolution weights instead (both are run after the version table).
  • Texture sticking. StyleGAN 1 and 2 textures followed pixel coordinates, not object coordinates (visible when interpolating). StyleGAN 3's alias-free convolutions fix this with windowed sinc filters.
  • Mode coverage. Truncation ψ < 0.7 looks clean but samples from a narrow cone; use ψ = 1.0 if you need diversity.
  • Inversion is lossy. Inverting a real photo into W is usually done through optimization or an encoder (e4e, ReStyle, HyperStyle). Results drift over many iterations.

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

Use case Approach
Photoreal human faces (anime, product, narrow) StyleGAN3 FFHQ / custom fine-tune
Face editing from a photo e4e inversion + StyleSpace / InterFaceGAN directions
Face swap / reenactment StyleGAN + encoder + blending
Avatar pipelines StyleGAN3 w/ ADA for low-data fine-tune
Domain adaptation from a few images Freeze mapping network, fine-tune synthesis
Multi-modal or text-conditioned generation Don't — use diffusion

For narrow products where the answer is always "a photo of this kind of thing", StyleGAN beats diffusion on inference cost: one forward pass, milliseconds on a modern GPU.

StyleGAN3 on landscapes

StyleGAN3-T trained on LHQ (90,000 landscape photos) at 256×256, with NVIDIA's code on this page's CPU: the shape of W, the truncation trick, style mixing, and the input translated by one-eighth of its range. Each image takes several seconds on a CPU; on a GPU, milliseconds.

Show the code that draws this 
import os, sys, subprocess, pickle, time, numpy as np, torch
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from huggingface_hub import hf_hub_download

# NVIDIA's StyleGAN3 code is needed to unpickle the network (it runs on a CPU with reference kernels).
if not os.path.isdir("stylegan3"):
    subprocess.run(["git", "clone", "-q", "--depth", "1", "https://github.com/NVlabs/stylegan3.git"], check=True)
sys.path.insert(0, "stylegan3")
torch.set_num_threads(2)
# StyleGAN3-T trained on LHQ, 90,000 landscape photos, at 256 x 256 (Justin Pinkney's release).
with open(hf_hub_download("justinpinkney/stylegan3-t-lhq-256", "lhq-256-stylegan3-t-25Mimg.pkl"), "rb") as f:
    G = pickle.load(f)["G_ema"].float().eval()
print(f"generator {sum(p.numel() for p in G.parameters()) / 1e6:.1f}M parameters "
      f"(mapping network {sum(p.numel() for p in G.mapping.parameters()) / 1e6:.1f}M); "
      f"z and w have {G.z_dim} numbers; the synthesis network takes {G.mapping.num_ws} copies of w")

with torch.no_grad():
    # The mapping network turns Gaussian z into w. w is not Gaussian, and its average is the "typical" image.
    zs = torch.randn(2000, G.z_dim, generator=torch.Generator().manual_seed(0))
    ws = G.mapping(zs, None)[:, 0]
    print(f"z: mean {zs.mean():+.2f}, std {zs.std():.2f} | w: mean {ws.mean():+.2f}, std {ws.std():.2f}, "
          f"distance of w from the average w: {(ws - G.mapping.w_avg).norm(dim=1).mean():.1f} on average")
    za, zb = torch.randn(2, G.z_dim, generator=torch.Generator().manual_seed(4)).split(1)
    wa, wb = G.mapping(za, None), G.mapping(zb, None)
    trunc = lambda w, psi: G.mapping.w_avg + psi * (w - G.mapping.w_avg)    # the truncation trick, in W
    synth = lambda w: G.synthesis(w, noise_mode="const").clamp(-1, 1)
    t = time.perf_counter()
    imgs = {"A, psi = 1": synth(wa)}
    print(f"one 256 x 256 image: {time.perf_counter() - t:.1f} s on this CPU")
    imgs["A, psi = 0.5"] = synth(trunc(wa, 0.5))
    imgs["average w (psi = 0)"] = synth(G.mapping.w_avg.repeat(1, G.mapping.num_ws, 1))
    imgs["B, psi = 0.5"] = synth(trunc(wb, 0.5))
    # Style mixing: the first k of the 16 copies of w (the low-resolution layers) from one image, the rest from the other.
    for k, first, rest in ((5, wa, wb), (9, wa, wb), (9, wb, wa)):
        mix = trunc(rest, 0.5).clone(); mix[:, :k] = trunc(first, 0.5)[:, :k]
        imgs[f"w 0-{k - 1} from {'A' if first is wa else 'B'}, {k}-15 from {'B' if first is wa else 'A'}"] = synth(mix)
    # StyleGAN3's selling point: translate the input Fourier features and the whole picture translates.
    base = imgs["A, psi = 0.5"]
    tr = torch.eye(3); tr[0, 2] = -0.125                 # shift the input coordinates by 1/8 of their range
    G.synthesis.input.transform.copy_(tr)
    shifted = synth(trunc(wa, 0.5)); G.synthesis.input.transform.copy_(torch.eye(3))
    errs = {k: float((shifted[..., :, 40:216] - torch.roll(base, k, dims=3)[..., :, 40:216]).abs().mean()) for k in range(-64, 65)}
    k = min(errs, key=errs.get)
    print(f"shifted input -> picture moves {k} pixels; mean |difference| from the moved original: {errs[k]:.4f} "
          f"(unshifted: {errs[0]:.3f}; pixel range -1..1)")
    imgs[f"input shifted: moved {k} px"] = shifted

fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 4, figsize=(9, 5))
for ax, (name, im) in zip(axes.ravel(), imgs.items()):
    ax.imshow(((im[0] + 1) / 2).permute(1, 2, 0).numpy()); ax.set_title(name, fontsize=8); ax.axis("off")
plt.subplots_adjust(left=0.01, right=0.99, top=0.95, bottom=0.01, wspace=0.04, hspace=0.12)
plt.savefig("v2.png", dpi=60); plt.show()

Reading this output

The generator has 28.5M parameters, only 0.5M of them in the mapping network. The z inputs are standard Gaussian, but the w outputs are not: their mean is 0.21, their spread 0.76, and a typical w sits about 16 units from the average wˉ. Truncation pulls toward that measured average: at ψ=1, image A is a turquoise lake below snowy peaks; at ψ=0.5 it becomes a calmer, more typical version of the same scene; and the average w itself (ψ=0) decodes to a bland "typical landscape" of green slopes and grey mountains. The mixing row answers the prediction: taking the first 5 copies of w from A changes little: the result is still a rocky plain like B; taking the first 9 gives A's lake, sky and colours, with B's later layers adding only fine texture; and the reverse 9-from-B mix looks like B. In this StyleGAN3 the layout lives in the middle layers (the 52×52 to 148×148 stages), not the first few. The last image shifts the input's coordinate grid by one-eighth: the picture slides 32 pixels and matches the shifted original almost exactly (mean difference below 0.001 against 0.19 unshifted). That is StyleGAN3's alias-free design: details are tied to the scene, not to the pixel grid, so they do not "stick" when the image moves.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • StyleGAN priors in face restoration. GFPGAN restores degraded faces using a pretrained StyleGAN2 face generator as its prior; it ships as the face-restoration option in Automatic1111, ComfyUI nodes and face tools such as FaceFusion.
  • Identity-preserving generation moved to diffusion. InstantID, PhotoMaker and IP-Adapter-FaceID give a diffusion model a person's identity from one photo, the job StyleGAN inversion and editing used to do.
  • StyleGAN where cost dominates. Narrow, high-volume generators (avatars, synthetic training data for face and document models) still use StyleGAN2/3 for its one-pass cost; NVIDIA's StyleGAN3 code remains the reference implementation.

Sources: GFPGAN, InstantID, IP-Adapter, StyleGAN3, StyleGAN2-ADA.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Map z to w with an MLP; give every synthesis layer its own copy of w as per-channel styles.
  • StyleGAN: AdaIN; StyleGAN2: modulate and demodulate the weights (unit output variance whatever the style).
  • Truncation w′=wˉ+ψ(w−wˉ) trades diversity for typicality at no change in cost.
  • Style mixing works because each layer has its own w; which layers carry layout depends on the model.
  • StyleGAN3's alias-free design makes images translate cleanly with their input coordinates.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · The k = 9 mix

The k=9 mix looks like A; the k=5 mix still looks like B. In this StyleGAN3, the first five copies of w feed the input and the layers working at 36×36 on abstract Fourier features, and changing them alters little that is visible. Layout and colour are settled by copies 5–8, the middle layers, and copies 9–15 (the 148 and 256-pixel stages) add fine texture. The classic StyleGAN picture, coarse layers for pose and fine layers for colour, comes from StyleGAN1 and 2 trained on faces; which layers carry what is learned, differs between models and datasets, and has to be measured, as here.

SolutionB1 · Scales compound

Each layer multiplies the standard deviation by the style, so 14 layers with styles of 2 give 214≈16,000 times the input's scale, and styles of 0.5 give 0.514≈6×10−5: the signal either overflows half-precision arithmetic or drowns in rounding error and noise. Any style that is not exactly 1 on average compounds exponentially with depth. Demodulation removes the problem at its source by rescaling each output channel's weights to unit gain, so the style changes the direction of the features (which channels matter) but not their overall size; StyleGAN1's AdaIN achieved stability by renormalising the activations instead, at the cost of the droplet artefacts.

Production note: why StyleGAN still ships in 2026

StyleGAN3 generates a 1024² face in one forward pass on a modern GPU, in tens of milliseconds or less: num_steps = 1, no VAE decode, no cross-attention pass. A 30–50-step SDXL pipeline at the same resolution takes seconds, two orders of magnitude more, so for narrow-domain products (avatar services, stock face generation) StyleGAN wins on cost.

Two operational consequences:

  • No scheduler, no batcher. Static batch at the target occupancy is optimal. Continuous batching (essential for LLMs and diffusion) provides zero benefit because every request takes the same FLOPs.
  • Truncation ψ is the quality–diversity knob. ψ < 0.7 samples from a narrow region around the average w: cleaner, less varied images. It does not change the cost; every ψ runs the same network.
