In plain words first
The idea in one line

OpenAI Preparedness Framework v2 (April 15, 2025) introduces Research Categories — Long-range Autonomy, Sandbagging, Autonomous Replication and Adaptation, Undermining Safeguards, Nuclear and Radiological — distinct from Tracked Categories (Biological and Chemical, Cybersecurity, AI Self-improvement). Tracked Categories trigger Capabilities Reports plus Safeguards Reports reviewed by the Safety Advisory Group. DeepMind's FSF v3 (September 2025) folds autonomy into its ML R&D and Cyber domains; its ML R&D autonomy level 1 Critical Capability Level is a model that can fully automate the AI R&D pipeline at competitive cost compared with humans using AI tools. FSF v3.1 (April 17, 2026) added Tracked Capability Levels below the CCLs, for CBRN and for stealth and situational awareness, and merged misalignment into an ML R&D and Misalignment domain. The honest note: Research Categories in PF v2 (including Long-range Autonomy) do not automatically trigger mitigations, and DeepMind's own framework warns that automated monitoring may stop being enough if models' instrumental reasoning keeps strengthening.

The problem

In plain words first

OpenAI and Google DeepMind publish their own versions of a scaling policy: OpenAI's Preparedness Framework and DeepMind's Frontier Safety Framework. Each sorts risky capabilities into tiers and says what each tier triggers, but they sort differently, so the same capability can be gated at one lab and only watched at another. Responsible AI and governance are covered in Lesson 15.26 (linked above). This lesson checks the lesson's comparison table against the published documents, then tests whether a small model can read these policies reliably.

Lesson 15.19 read Anthropic's scaling policy closely. This lesson completes the picture by reading OpenAI's and DeepMind's. The three documents are cousin artifacts addressing the same question — when should a frontier lab pause or gate a model — and they converge on a small set of categories and diverge in specific places that matter.

The convergence: all three track some form of autonomous AI R&D, and all three acknowledge deceptive behavior (alignment faking, sandbagging, evading oversight) as a class of risk. All three have an internal review process. The divergence: OpenAI splits categories into "Tracked" (mandatory mitigation) and "Research" (no automatic trigger). DeepMind folds autonomy into its domains rather than naming it separately, and adds a lower Tracked Capability Level tier. Anthropic's v3.0 names few thresholds and separates its own commitments from recommendations. The operational consequence of which bucket a capability lives in differs across labs.

Reading them together is the useful exercise. The same capability can be behind a gate at one lab, "monitored but not triggering" at OpenAI, and folded into a broader domain at DeepMind. The policy posture matters.

Idea 1 · OpenAI Preparedness Framework v2 (April 2025)

Structure:

  • Tracked Categories: trigger Capabilities Reports (what the model can do) plus Safeguards Reports (what mitigations are in place). Reviewed by the Safety Advisory Group before deployment.
  • Research Categories: model capabilities the lab is tracking but not yet committing to specific mitigations: Long-range Autonomy, Sandbagging, Autonomous Replication and Adaptation, Undermining Safeguards, and Nuclear and Radiological.

Key framing in v2: Research Categories do not automatically trigger mitigations. The policy language is "potential" mitigations. This is the specific delta to watch: a category's location in Tracked vs Research is the hinge between "gated" and "observed."

Idea 2 · DeepMind Frontier Safety Framework v3 (September 2025; Tracked Capability Levels added April 17, 2026)

Structure:

  • Critical Capability Levels (CCLs): capability thresholds in three groups: misuse (CBRN, Cyber, Harmful Manipulation), ML R&D, and an exploratory misalignment track. Autonomy is not its own domain; it is folded into ML R&D and Cyber.
  • Tracked Capability Levels (TCLs): added in v3.1 on April 17, 2026, below the CCLs, for "significant" rather than "severe" harm. A TCL triggers a proportionate assessment and mitigation process. The first two cover CBRN and stealth and situational awareness. (ML R&D autonomy level 1, a model that can fully automate the AI R&D pipeline at competitive cost, is a CCL, not a TCL.)
  • Deceptive alignment monitoring: explicit commitment to automated monitoring for instrumental-reasoning misuse.

The autonomy framing differs from OpenAI's. DeepMind does not keep "Autonomy" as a top-level domain; it is folded into the domains where autonomy would cause harm (ML R&D and Cyber). The argument is that autonomy without a domain is capability without risk; the counter-argument is that autonomy across domains is a meta-risk the framework should name.

Idea 3 · What all three converge on

  • An internal review process before deploying high-capability models: OpenAI's Safety Advisory Group; Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Officer, with board and Long-Term Benefit Trust approval for some Risk Reports; Google DeepMind's Responsibility and Safety Council.
  • Explicit mention of deceptive alignment / alignment faking as a risk class.
  • Standing artifacts on a declared cadence (Anthropic: Frontier Safety Roadmap, Risk Report; OpenAI: Capabilities and Safeguards Reports; DeepMind: FSF update cycle).
  • Acknowledgement that monitoring-only defenses have a ceiling. DeepMind's framework says automated monitoring may stop being enough as models' instrumental reasoning strengthens.

Idea 4 · Where they diverge

  • Anthropic: pause commitment removed in v3.0; one automated-AI-R&D threshold replaces AI R&D-4 and -5, and most of the strong measures there are recommendations.
  • OpenAI: Tracked vs Research split; Research Categories (including Long-range Autonomy) do not automatically gate.
  • DeepMind: autonomy folded into other domains; v3.1 adds Tracked Capability Levels below the CCLs in April 2026.

Idea 5 · Sandbagging: a specific capability that complicates all three

Sandbagging (a model strategically underperforming on evaluations) is one of OpenAI's Research Categories. Anthropic does not make it a separate RSP threshold; it reports evaluation awareness and sandbagging tests in system cards and Risk Reports. DeepMind's closest named category is the v3.1 Stealth and Situational Awareness TCL.

If a model sandbags on evaluations, every framework's capability thresholds are underestimated. The framework works only if the measurement works. This is why external measurement (Lesson 15.21, METR) and adversarial evaluation are necessary in addition to lab self-assessment.

Idea 6 · The policy-reading skill

  • Locate: every capability you care about should be findable in the policy. If not findable, the policy does not cover it.
  • Classify: is it Tracked (triggers mitigation) or Research (tracked but not triggering)? OpenAI names this; Anthropic and DeepMind have their own equivalents.
  • Cadence: is the policy updated on a declared schedule, or only after specific events? Declared cadence is stronger.
  • Independence: is external review mandatory or optional? All three give some outside evaluators pre-deployment access (government institutes such as the US CAISI and the UK AI Security Institute, and groups such as METR and Apollo Research). None makes outside review a binding gate for every release; Anthropic's v3.0 requires third-party review of heavily redacted Risk Reports for highly capable models.

The same idea as a formula

A crosswalk is a table of gate levels. For capability c and framework f, write g(c,f)∈{gated,assessed,research,none}. A capability's coverage is the number of frameworks that gate it:

cov(c)=#{f:g(c,f)=gated}.
Capabilities with cov(c)=0 are the gaps that every lab leaves open at once.

Score a reader against a baseline. A reader that answers yes/no for each cell gets accuracy a. If a fraction q of the cells are "no", then always answering "no" scores q without reading anything. A reader is useful only if

a>max(q,1−q).

Check the quote, not the claim. Ask for the supporting sentence and accept it only if it appears in the source, word for word or nearly so. A claim with no matching quote is a claim the reader made up.

Worked example
Counting coverage
  • Biological uplift: OpenAI Tracked, Anthropic's ASL-3 protections and DeepMind's CBRN CCL are all gated. cov=3.
  • Cyber uplift: OpenAI Tracked and DeepMind's Cyber CCL are gated; GovAI's summary of Anthropic's unilateral commitments names no cyber threshold. cov=2.
  • Undermining safeguards: Anthropic's high-stakes sabotage threshold is gated; it is a Research Category at OpenAI and a TCL ("assessed") at DeepMind. cov=1.
  • Long-range autonomy, autonomous replication and sandbagging: no framework gates them. cov=0 for all three.

# This block continues from the one above: a small model as a policy reader. Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (greedy, CPU) gets
# a short excerpt summarising each framework's rules and is asked, for each of the seven capabilities, whether the
# framework requires safeguards for it. Its answers are scored against the checked table above. Then it is asked to
# quote the sentence that supports each answer: each quoted span is checked against the excerpt, and against the words
# that name the capability. About 2 minutes.
import os, re, warnings
os.environ["HF_HUB_OFFLINE"] = "1"
warnings.filterwarnings("ignore")
import torch
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM, logging as hf_logging
hf_logging.set_verbosity_error(); hf_logging.disable_progress_bar()
tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct")
llm = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained("Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct").eval()

EXCERPTS = {
    "OpenAI PF v2": ("Tracked Categories are Biological and Chemical, Cybersecurity, and AI Self-improvement; they require "
                     "Capabilities Reports and Safeguards Reports reviewed by the Safety Advisory Group. Research Categories "
                     "are Long-range Autonomy, Sandbagging, Autonomous Replication and Adaptation, Undermining Safeguards, and "
                     "Nuclear and Radiological; they do not yet meet the criteria to be Tracked Categories."),
    "Anthropic RSP v3.0": ("Anthropic will maintain ASL-3 protections at the chemical and biological weapons thresholds. At the "
                           "high-stakes sabotage threshold it will detail model capabilities and monitoring. At the automated "
                           "AI R&D threshold it will pursue its Frontier Safety Roadmap goals. Other risks are discussed in Risk "
                           "Reports."),
    "DeepMind FSF v3.1": ("Critical Capability Levels cover CBRN, Cyber, Harmful Manipulation, and ML R&D and Misalignment, "
                          "including ML R&D autonomy level 1. Tracked Capability Levels for CBRN and for Stealth and Situational "
                          "Awareness trigger a proportionate assessment."),
}
NAMES = {"bio_uplift": "biological weapons uplift", "cyber_uplift": "cyberattack uplift",
         "rnd_automation": "automating AI research", "long_range_autonomy": "long-range autonomy",
         "autonomous_replication": "autonomous replication", "sandbagging": "sandbagging on evaluations",
         "undermining_safeguards": "undermining safeguards or human control"}
def ask(prompt, n):
    ids = tok.apply_chat_template([{"role": "user", "content": prompt}], add_generation_prompt=True, return_tensors="pt", return_dict=True)["input_ids"]
    with torch.no_grad():
        out = llm.generate(ids, max_new_tokens=n, do_sample=False)
    return tok.decode(out[0, ids.shape[1]:], skip_special_tokens=True).strip()
KEYWORDS = {"bio_uplift": ["biological", "Biological", "CBRN"], "cyber_uplift": ["Cyber"],
            "rnd_automation": ["AI Self-improvement", "AI R&D", "ML R&D"], "long_range_autonomy": ["Long-range Autonomy"],
            "autonomous_replication": ["Autonomous Replication"], "sandbagging": ["Sandbagging", "Stealth"],
            "undermining_safeguards": ["Undermining Safeguards", "sabotage", "Stealth"]}
norm = lambda t: re.sub(r"[^a-z0-9 ]", "", t.lower()).strip()
right, total, grounded, quoted, relevant, invented = 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0
for policy, text in EXCERPTS.items():
    row = []
    for cap, name in NAMES.items():
        truth = CHECKED[policy][cap][0] == "gated"
        a = ask(f"Policy excerpt: {text}\n\nUnder this policy, are safeguards required for {name}? Answer yes or no.", 4)
        said = a.lower().startswith("yes")
        right += said == truth; total += 1
        reply = ask(f"Policy excerpt: {text}\n\nQuote, word for word, the one sentence of the excerpt that says whether "
                    f"safeguards are required for {name}.", 60)
        quotes = [x for x in re.findall(r'"([^"]{20,})"', reply) if norm(x) in norm(text)]   # quoted spans really in the excerpt
        keys = [k for k in KEYWORDS[cap] if k in text]                                     # words that name this capability
        quoted += 1
        grounded += bool(quotes)
        relevant += bool(quotes) and bool(keys) and any(k in x for x in quotes for k in keys)
        if not keys and quotes:
            invented += 1                                                                  # no sentence covers it, yet it quoted one
        row.append(f"{'Y' if said else 'n'}{'' if said == truth else '!'}")
    print(f"{policy:<20} answers (Y/n, ! = wrong): {' '.join(row)}")
print(f"\nyes/no answers right: {right}/{total}")
print(f"replies that quote a real sentence of the excerpt: {grounded}/{quoted}; quoting the sentence that names the "
      f"capability: {relevant}/{quoted}")
print(f"cases where no sentence names the capability, yet the model quoted one as the answer: {invented}")
print(f"always answering 'no' would score {sum(CHECKED[p][c][0] != 'gated' for p in EXCERPTS for c in NAMES)}/{total}")

Reading this output

The small model is no better than a constant. It got 12 of 21 yes/no answers right, exactly what always answering "no" scores. It said no to OpenAI's three Tracked Categories and yes to two Research Categories, which is the exact inversion of the policy.

Its quotes are real sentences, mostly the wrong ones. 17 of 21 replies quoted a sentence that is really in the excerpt, but only 9 quoted the sentence that names the capability. Asked about long-range autonomy under Anthropic's excerpt, it quoted the chemical and biological weapons sentence and argued from it. In 3 of the 6 cases where no sentence covers the capability, it still quoted one as the answer.

Check the claim against its quote, and the quote against the text. A quote that exists is not a quote that supports the answer. This is the check to run on any summary of a policy, from a model or from a person.

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

code/main.py implements a small decision-table diff tool. Given a capability (autonomy, deceptive alignment, R&D automation, cyber uplift, etc.), it outputs how each of the three policies classifies the capability, and what mitigations trigger. It's a reading aid, not a policy tool.

The whole of code/main.py follows in parts. Each part runs on this page, in order, in one Python session; the last part does what python3 code/main.py does.

"""Cross-policy decision-table diff — stdlib Python.

Reads three small tables encoding how OpenAI PF v2, Anthropic RSP v3.0,
and DeepMind FSF v3 classify a short list of capabilities. Outputs a
side-by-side comparison. The tables are pedagogical distillations of
the three source documents; real policy reads require the documents.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass
class Policy:
    name: str
    # capability -> (classification, trigger-action)
    table: dict[str, tuple[str, str]]

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • OpenAI Preparedness Framework v2 (April 15, 2025) is the current version: three Tracked Categories, five Research Categories, Capabilities and Safeguards Reports, and a Safety Advisory Group that recommends to leadership. It allows OpenAI to adjust requirements if another developer ships a high-risk system without comparable safeguards, after saying so publicly.
  • Google DeepMind's Frontier Safety Framework v3.1 (April 17, 2026) adds Tracked Capability Levels, a Security Level 2+ baseline and a merged ML R&D and Misalignment domain. DeepMind publishes FSF reports for major models, such as Gemini 3 Pro.
  • Anthropic's RSP v3.0 (February 24, 2026) is in Lesson 15.19.
  • Laws now require such frameworks. California's SB 53 requires large frontier developers to publish one, and the EU's General-Purpose AI Code of Practice asks signatories for a safety and security framework.
  • On your laptop: keep your own crosswalk as a small table like the one above, with a link to the clause behind every cell.

Sources: OpenAI, Updating our Preparedness Framework, Google DeepMind, Strengthening our Frontier Safety Framework, Frontier Risk, DeepMind FSF v3.1, GovAI, RSP v3.0.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • OpenAI gates three Tracked Categories and only watches five Research Categories, including Long-range Autonomy and Sandbagging.
  • DeepMind gates CCLs by domain and, since v3.1, assesses lower Tracked Capability Levels.
  • Several capabilities, including long-range autonomy and sandbagging, are gated by none of the three.
  • Build your own crosswalk with a source clause for every cell; summaries, including this lesson's original table, drift.
  • Do not let a small model read policy for you: check its answers against a baseline and its quotes against the text.

Given a specific frontier capability (e.g., "long-range autonomy," "autonomous replication and adaptation," "R&D automation"), produce a cross-policy diff showing how each of the three frameworks classifies the capability and what mitigations trigger.

Produce:

1. **OpenAI PF v2 classification.** Tracked or Research. If Tracked, name the Capabilities + Safeguards Report triggers. If Research, note the policy language is "potential" mitigations.
2. **Anthropic RSP v3.0 classification.** Which threshold (ASL-3, AI R&D-4, hardcoded prohibition)? Which mitigation (affirmative case, security + deployment)? Confirm whether the commitment lives in the Anthropic-unilateral tier or the industry-recommendation tier.
3. **DeepMind FSF v3 classification.** Which domain (CBRN, Cyber, Harmful Manipulation, ML R&D)? Which CCL or Tracked Capability Level? Is deceptive alignment monitoring invoked?
4. **Convergence summary.** Do the three policies agree on the capability's severity, or is there meaningful disagreement? Which classification is most rigorous, which least?
5. **Measurement dependency.** Every classification depends on capability measurement. Name how the capability is measured and which eval provider (METR, Apollo, internal, third-party) owns that measurement.

Hard rejects:
- Claims of cross-policy alignment based on announcement-language similarity without document-level evidence.
- Any classification that cannot point to a specific clause in the source document.
- Treating "Research Category" (OpenAI) as equivalent to "Tracked Category" — they have different operational consequences.

Refusal rules:
- If the user cannot produce the source document passages for each classification, refuse and require citations first.
- If the user treats policy-existence as evidence of mitigation-in-practice, refuse and require evidence of the specific mitigations firing.
- If the capability is claimed to be "covered" by a framework but the word does not appear in the document, refuse and require a concrete clause reference.

Output format:

Return a diff document with:
- **Capability definition** (one sentence)
- **OpenAI PF v2 row** (classification, trigger, source clause)
- **Anthropic RSP v3.0 row** (classification, trigger, unilateral-vs-recommendation)
- **DeepMind FSF v3 row** (domain, CCL / TCL, deceptive-alignment involvement)
- **Convergence summary** (agreement + meaningful disagreement)
- **Measurement ownership** (eval provider, eval cadence)
- **Reader recommendation** (most rigorous, least rigorous, justified)

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Two everywhere, three nowhere, and no better than "no"

Two are gated in all three, bio uplift and automating AI research. Three are gated in none: long-range autonomy, autonomous replication and sandbagging.

The small model does not beat the baseline. It scores 12 of 21, the same as always answering "no", and it inverts OpenAI's Tracked and Research Categories. A reader has to beat the trivial answer before its answers mean anything.

SolutionB1 · Coverage that no framework provides

No framework covers autonomy as its own gated risk. At OpenAI, Long-range Autonomy is a Research Category, watched with only potential mitigations. Anthropic's RSP v3.0 has no separate autonomy threshold, and DeepMind folds autonomy into its ML R&D and Cyber domains.

The team should have: - Asked for the clause behind each cell of the vendor's table, and read it. - Checked which tier the clause is in: gated with required safeguards, assessed, research-only, or recommended for the industry. - Checked the version and date: a table written for the earlier RSP or FSF v3.0 is out of date after February and April 2026. - Asked what the vendor itself does for autonomy risk, since frontier labs' frameworks govern the labs' own models, not a vendor's product.
