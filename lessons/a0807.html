In plain words first
The idea in one line

Pixel-space diffusion on 512×512 images is a computational war crime. Rombach et al. (2022) noticed that you do not need all 786k dimensions to generate an image — you need enough to capture semantic structure, and a separate decoder for the rest. Run diffusion inside a VAE's latent space. That one idea is Stable Diffusion.

Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means
First stage "The VAE" Trained encoder/decoder pair; compresses 512² to 64².
Second stage "The U-Net" Diffusion model over the latent space.
CFG "Guidance scale" $(1+w) \cdot \epsilon_{\text{cond}} - w \cdot \epsilon_{\text{uncond}}$; tunes conditioning strength.
Null token "Empty prompt embed" Unconditional embed used for $\epsilon_{\text{uncond}}$.
Cross-attention "How text gets in" Each U-Net block attends to text tokens as K and V.
DiT "Diffusion Transformer" Replace U-Net with a transformer over latent patches; scales better.
MMDiT "Multi-modal DiT" SD3's architecture: text and image streams with joint attention.
VAE scaling factor "Magic number" Divides latents by ~5.4 so diffusion operates in unit-variance space.

The problem

In plain words first

Running diffusion directly on the pixels of a large image is very expensive: every one of dozens of denoising steps pushes the whole image through a big network. Latent diffusion, the idea behind Stable Diffusion, first squeezes the image with a VAE (Lesson 8.2) into a much smaller grid of numbers, runs all the denoising steps on that small grid, and decodes to pixels once at the end. Text steers the result through cross-attention: at every layer, each position of the image grid looks up the parts of the prompt that matter to it. A trick called classifier-free guidance then strengthens the prompt's pull by comparing the model's prediction with and without the prompt. This lesson computes guidance exactly on the lesson's one-dimensional example, runs a real cross-attention layer, and generates a picture on this page's CPU with a small distilled Stable Diffusion, with and without guidance. Nothing is trained.

Pixel-space diffusion at 512² means the U-Net runs on tensors of shape [B, 3, 512, 512]. Each sampling step is ~100 GFLOPS for a 500M-param U-Net. Fifty steps is 5 TFLOPS per image. Train on a billion images and the compute bill is absurd.

Most of those FLOPs go to pushing perceptually unimportant details through the net — the high-frequency texture that a lossy VAE could compress away. Rombach's idea: train a VAE once (the first stage), freeze it, and run diffusion entirely in the 4-channel 64×64 latent space (the second stage). Same kind of U-Net, on 64 times fewer spatial positions (48 times fewer numbers), so each step costs a small fraction of its pixel-space equivalent for comparable quality.

This is the Stable Diffusion recipe. SD 1.x / 2.x used an 860M U-Net over 64×64×4 latents, SDXL used a 2.6B U-Net over 128×128×4, SD3 swapped the U-Net for a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with flow matching. Flux.1-dev (Black Forest Labs, 2024) ships a 12B-param DiT-MMDiT. All run on the same two-stage substrate.

The idea

Two stages, separately trained.

  1. Stage 1 — VAE. Encoder E(x)→z, decoder D(z)→x. Target compression: 8× downsample in each spatial axis + adjust channels so the latent holds about 1/48th as many numbers as the image (Lesson 8.2). Loss = reconstruction (L1 + LPIPS perceptual) + KL (small weight so z isn't forced too Gaussian, because we do not need exact sampling from z). Often trained with an adversarial loss so decoded images are sharp.

  2. Stage 2 — diffusion on z. Treat z=E(xreal​) as the data. Train a U-Net (or DiT) to denoise zt​. At inference: sample z0​ via diffusion, then x=D(z0​).

Text conditioning. Two additional components. A frozen text encoder (CLIP-L for SD 1.x, OpenCLIP-H for SD 2.x, CLIP-L + OpenCLIP-G for SDXL, T5-XXL for SD3 and Flux). A cross-attention injection: every U-Net block takes [Q = image features, K = V = text tokens] and mixes them in. The tokens are the only way text influences the image.

The loss function is identical to Lesson 06. Same DDPM / flow matching MSE on noise. You just swap the data domain.

The same idea as a formula

Two stages. A VAE with encoder E and decoder D is trained first and frozen. The diffusion model is then trained on latents z0​=s⋅E(x) (s the scaling factor, 0.18215 for SD 1.x):

L=Ex,c,ε,t​​ε−εθ​(zt​,t,τ(c))​2,zt​=αˉt​​z0​+1−αˉt​​ε

with τ the frozen text encoder. Generation: sample z0​ by reverse diffusion, output D(z0​/s).

Cross-attention. With image features h (one row per latent position) and text tokens e:

CrossAttn(h,e)=softmax(d​(hWQ​)(eWK​)⊤​)eWV​

Each image position takes a weighted average of the tokens' value vectors.

Classifier-free guidance. Bayes' rule for the scores: ∇logp(z∣c)=∇logp(z)+∇logp(c∣z). Guidance multiplies the classifier part by 1+w, which corresponds to sampling from p(z)p(c∣z)1+w. Since the noise prediction is a scaled negative score, this gives

ε^=εθ​(zt​,∅)+(1+w)(εθ​(zt​,c)−εθ​(zt​,∅))=(1+w)εcond​−wεuncond​

One network learns both terms because the condition is replaced by the null token ∅ for about 10% of training examples. Libraries call 1+w the guidance scale; diffusers' default 7.5 is w=6.5.

Worked example
Latent diffusion's savings by hand

A 512×512 image; SD 1.x latent 64×64×4.

  • Positions the U-Net processes: 5122=262,144 in pixel space against 642=4,096 in latent space, 64 times fewer.
  • Self-attention cost grows with the square of the number of positions: 642=4,096 times less in latent space, which is what makes attention affordable at all.
  • Cross-attention at the 64×64 level: a 4,096×77 matrix of weights per head, every step.
  • Guidance doubles the U-Net work: conditional and unconditional predictions at every step, run as a batch of 2. 30 steps with guidance = 60 U-Net passes, plus one VAE decode.

Guidance by hand. εcond​=0.8, εuncond​=0.5, w=3: ε^=4×0.8−3×0.5=1.7, which is 0.5+4×0.3: the difference the prompt makes, amplified four times.

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Guidance is free quality."
    ✓ It costs a second network pass per step, and it trades diversity for adherence: the exact run after Step 3 shows the class's spread shrinking and its mean moving past the data as w grows.
  • ✗ "The diffusion model works on a small image."
    ✓ It works on VAE latents, which are not pictures; only the decoder turns them into pixels, and latents from one model family are meaningless to another.
  • ✗ "The text encoder is trained together with the diffusion model."
    ✓ In Stable Diffusion it is a frozen, separately trained encoder (CLIP, T5); the diffusion model learns to read it through cross-attention.

Step 1 · encoder/decoder

code/main.py stacks a toy 1-D "VAE" (a fixed scale-by-0.5 encoder and its exact inverse as the decoder, for demonstration; a real VAE would be a conv net) on top of the DDPM from Lesson 8.6 and adds class conditioning with classifier-free guidance. It shows that the same diffusion loss works whether you run on raw 1-D values or on encoded values. That is the key insight.

def encode(x):    return x * 0.5          # toy "compression" to smaller scale
def decode(z):    return z * 2.0

A real VAE has trained weights. For pedagogy, this linear map is enough to show that diffusion operates on z without caring about the original data space.

Step 3 · classifier-free guidance

During training, drop the class label 10% of the time (replace with a null token). At inference, compute both ϵcond​ and ϵuncond​, then:

eps_cond, eps_uncond = 0.8, 0.5            # the model's noise prediction with and without the prompt (one number here)
for w in (0, 1, 3, 7):
    eps_cfg = (1 + w) * eps_cond - w * eps_uncond
    print(f"w = {w}: eps_cfg = {eps_cfg:.1f}   (= eps_uncond + {1 + w} * (eps_cond - eps_uncond))")
What you should see
w = 0: eps_cfg = 0.8   (= eps_uncond + 1 * (eps_cond - eps_uncond))
w = 1: eps_cfg = 1.1   (= eps_uncond + 2 * (eps_cond - eps_uncond))
w = 3: eps_cfg = 1.7   (= eps_uncond + 4 * (eps_cond - eps_uncond))
w = 7: eps_cfg = 2.9   (= eps_uncond + 8 * (eps_cond - eps_uncond))

w=0 = no guidance (full diversity), w=3 = default, w=7+ = saturated / over-sharp.

Guidance, computed exactly

main.py's encode, decode and schedule (its main() trains a network, which this page does not run). Two overlapping classes, N(−1,0.82) and N(+1,0.82), are encoded to latents; because they are Gaussians, the conditional and unconditional noise predictors can be written down exactly. 4,000 samples of class 1 are drawn with DDPM's reverse chain at several guidance strengths and decoded.

import math, runpy, numpy as np
m = runpy.run_path("main.py")             # the lesson's encode/decode and schedule; main() is not run, because it trains
alphas, abar = m["make_schedule"](1000)
# Two classes in data space: class 0 ~ N(-1, 0.8^2), class 1 ~ N(+1, 0.8^2). Diffusion runs on z = encode(x).
mus = {0: m["encode"](-1.0), 1: m["encode"](1.0)}; sd = m["encode"](0.8)

def eps_gauss(z, ab, mu):                 # exact noise predictor if the data were one Gaussian N(mu, sd^2)
    return (z - math.sqrt(ab) * mu) * math.sqrt(1 - ab) / (ab * sd ** 2 + 1 - ab)
def eps_uncond(z, ab):                    # exact predictor for the 50/50 mixture of both classes (no label)
    v = ab * sd ** 2 + 1 - ab
    r = np.stack([np.exp(-(z - math.sqrt(ab) * mus[c]) ** 2 / (2 * v)) for c in (0, 1)]); r /= r.sum(0)
    return r[0] * eps_gauss(z, ab, mus[0]) + r[1] * eps_gauss(z, ab, mus[1])

def sample_class(c, w, n=4000, seed=0):
    g = np.random.default_rng(seed); z = g.normal(size=n)
    for t in range(999, -1, -1):
        e_c, e_u = eps_gauss(z, abar[t], mus[c]), eps_uncond(z, abar[t])
        eps = (1 + w) * e_c - w * e_u                         # classifier-free guidance
        b = 1 - alphas[t]
        z = (z - b / math.sqrt(1 - abar[t]) * eps) / math.sqrt(alphas[t])
        if t > 0: z = z + math.sqrt(b) * g.normal(size=n)
    return m["decode"](z)                                      # back to data space

print(f"class 1 in the data: mean +1.00, std 0.80, share below 0: {0.5 * math.erfc(1 / 0.8 / math.sqrt(2)):.3f}")
for w in (0, 1, 3, 7, 15):
    x = sample_class(1, w)
    print(f"w = {w:>2} (guidance scale {1 + w:>2}): mean {x.mean():+.2f}, std {x.std():.2f}, share below 0 {np.mean(x < 0):.3f}")
What you should see
class 1 in the data: mean +1.00, std 0.80, share below 0: 0.106
w =  0 (guidance scale  1): mean +0.99, std 0.81, share below 0 0.115
w =  1 (guidance scale  2): mean +1.36, std 0.66, share below 0 0.006
w =  3 (guidance scale  4): mean +1.66, std 0.61, share below 0 0.000
w =  7 (guidance scale  8): mean +1.93, std 0.60, share below 0 0.000
w = 15 (guidance scale 16): mean +2.18, std 0.60, share below 0 0.000
Reading this output

With w=0 (guidance scale 1) the sampler reproduces class 1 exactly: mean 0.99, spread 0.81, and 11.5% of samples on the wrong side of 0, where the two classes overlap, matching the data's 10.6%. Guidance moves the samples toward points that are typical of class 1 and unlike class 0: at w=1 almost none land below 0 and the spread narrows to 0.66; at w=7 (diffusers' usual scale is 7.5) the mean has moved to 1.93, nearly twice the data's, and at w=15 to 2.18. This is the formula box's p(z)p(c∣z)1+w: unambiguous examples are favoured, at the price of diversity and of realism, since the guided samples no longer follow the data distribution. In images the same push gives stronger prompt adherence and, at high scales, saturated, over-contrasted pictures. Encoding and decoding by main.py's 0.5 and 2.0 changes nothing: diffusion does not care which space it runs in.

Step 4 · text conditioning (concept, not code)

Replace the class label with a frozen text encoder output. Feed the text embedding to the U-Net via cross-attention:

import torch, torch.nn as nn
torch.manual_seed(0)
mha = nn.MultiheadAttention(320, 8, kdim=768, vdim=768, batch_first=True)   # SD 1.x's first-block width, CLIP's width
def CrossAttention(Q, K, V):
    out, weights = mha(Q, K, V)
    print(f"queries {tuple(Q.shape[1:])} from the image, keys/values {tuple(K.shape[1:])} from the text "
          f"-> attention weights {tuple(weights.shape[1:])}, each row sums to {weights[0, 0].sum():.2f}")
    return out

h = torch.randn(1, 64 * 64, 320)             # image features: one row per latent position
text_embed = torch.randn(1, 77, 768)         # the text encoder's output: 77 token vectors
h = h + CrossAttention(Q=h, K=text_embed, V=text_embed)
print("h after cross-attention:", tuple(h.shape))
What you should see
queries (4096, 320) from the image, keys/values (77, 768) from the text -> attention weights (4096, 77), each row sums to 1.00
h after cross-attention: (1, 4096, 320)

This is the only substantive difference between a class-conditional diffusion model and Stable Diffusion.

Pitfalls

  • VAE-scale mismatch. SD 1.x VAEs have a scaling constant (scaling_factor ≈ 0.18215) applied after encoding. Forgetting this makes the U-Net train on latents with wildly wrong variance. Every checkpoint ships one.
  • Text encoder silently missing. SD3 can run without its T5-XXL encoder to save memory (text_encoder_3=None in diffusers), but prompt adherence, especially for text inside images, drops noticeably. Check which encoders a pipeline actually loaded.
  • Mixing latent spaces. SDXL, SD3, Flux all use different VAEs. A LoRA trained for SDXL will not work on SD3, FLUX.1 or FLUX.2: the latents and the network both differ, and loaders report the mismatched weights.
  • CFG too high. w > 10 produces saturated, oily images and over-fits the prompt at the cost of diversity. The sweet spot is w = 3-7.
  • Negative prompts leaking. Empty negative prompt becomes the null token; a filled negative prompt becomes the ϵuncond​. These are not the same; some pipelines silently default to the null.

Break it on purpose

Swap the two terms of the guidance formula by mistake: eps = (1 + w) * eps_uncond - w * eps_cond. With w=3 in the run after Step 3, where do class-1 samples end up? (Solution B1.)

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Transformer backbones over VAE latents. FLUX.2 (32B, with a Mistral Small 3.1 text encoder), Qwen-Image, Z-Image and SD3.5 are diffusion transformers trained with flow matching; SDXL and SD 1.5 U-Nets remain widely used for their ecosystems of LoRAs and ControlNets.
  • Running big models on small GPUs. ComfyUI and diffusers offload text encoders, quantise the transformer (GGUF, NF4 with bitsandbytes, FP8; SVDQuant 4-bit in Nunchaku) and tile the VAE so FLUX-class models fit consumer GPUs (FLUX.2 [dev] needs over 80 GB unoffloaded, about 20 GB with 4-bit weights).
  • Guidance distilled into the model. FLUX.1-dev is guidance-distilled: it takes the guidance strength as an input and needs one pass per step instead of two, halving the cost of guidance.

Sources: diffusers, FLUX.2 in diffusers, FLUX.1, SD3.5, Qwen-Image, Nunchaku, BK-SDM.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Train a VAE once; run diffusion on its scaled latents; decode once at the end.
  • Text enters through cross-attention: queries from image positions, keys and values from frozen text-encoder tokens.
  • Guidance: ε^=(1+w)εcond​−wεuncond​; one network trained with 10% null conditions.
  • Guidance buys adherence with diversity and a second pass per step (unless distilled in).
  • Latents, VAEs, LoRAs and ControlNets belong to one model family.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Not really

Not really: the unguided image shows a fox-like animal in a red hat among coloured sparkles, with no snow, while the guided one shows a fox sitting in snow. Without guidance, sampling follows p(z∣c) as the model learned it, and a model trained on noisy web captions treats the prompt as a weak hint: many images in its training data barely match their captions. Guidance amplifies the difference between "with this prompt" and "without any prompt", which is the part of the prediction that the prompt is responsible for. That is why practically every text-to-image pipeline uses a guidance scale well above 1, or a model with guidance distilled in.

SolutionB1 · Pushed away from the class, past the other one

Swapping the terms gives εuncond​+(1+w)(εuncond​−εcond​): the sampler is now pushed away from class 1, amplified four times. Run with w=3, the class-1 samples land at a mean of about −6.4 with 99% of them below 0: not just on the other class's side but far outside the data, where neither class has any probability. This is exactly how negative prompts work, deliberately: the negative prompt's prediction takes the place of the unconditional one, so the sampler is pushed away from it. With the sign wrong on the main prompt, the image is pushed away from what you asked for.

Production note: running Flux-12B on an 8GB consumer GPU

the reference Flux integration is the canonical "I have a consumer GPU, can I ship this?" recipe. The trick is the same three-knob recipe production inference literature lists applied to a diffusion DiT:

  1. Staggered loading. Flux has four networks that never need to coexist in VRAM: T5-XXL text encoder (4.7B parameters, ~9.5 GB in bf16), CLIP-L (small), the 12B MMDiT, and the VAE. Encode the prompt first, delete the encoders, load the DiT, denoise, delete the DiT, load the VAE, decode. Consumer 8GB GPUs only fit one stage at a time.
  2. 4-bit quantization via bitsandbytes. BitsAndBytesConfig(load_in_4bit=True, bnb_4bit_compute_dtype=torch.bfloat16) on both the T5 encoder and the DiT. Cuts weight memory 4× from bf16, quality drop is imperceptible for text-to-image per Aritra's benchmarks (linked in the notebook).
  3. CPU offload. pipe.enable_model_cpu_offload() auto-swaps modules between CPU and GPU as each forward pass advances. Adds 10-20% latency but makes the pipeline run at all.

The memory accounting is: 4.7B T5 params × 0.5 bytes ≈ 2.4 GB quantized, 12 B params × 0.5 bytes = ~6 GB quantized DiT, plus activations. In stas00's terms this is the extreme-end of TP=1 inference — no model parallelism, maximum quantization. For production you'd run TP=2 or TP=4 on H100s; for a single dev laptop, this is the recipe.
