A large share of enterprise knowledge sits in PDFs, scans, slides, photos and calls. Document AI (also called intelligent document processing) turns that into text and structured fields that RAG, text-to-SQLlater · 14.80 and agents can use. Modern multimodal models have changed the field: a single model call can now read a messy invoice that needed a custom OCR model five years ago. But the engineering around the call (parsing, schemas, validation, measurement) decides whether the result is usable.

Why this came about

RAG systems failed quietly when documents were scanned, laid out in columns or full of tables, because simple text extraction garbled them. Better parsers, OCR and later vision-language models became their own part of the pipeline, since everything downstream depends on clean text.

In plain words first
Getting the words out before anything else

Before anything can be searched, documents must become clean text with their structure intact: headings, tables, page numbers. PDFs are layouts, not text; scans are pictures of text. Parsing extracts text and structure, with OCR or a vision model for scans. Errors here, such as tables flattened into nonsense or headers repeated on every page, silently poison everything downstream.

What is inside a PDF, and why parsing is hard

A web page or a Word file stores text as paragraphs, headings and tables. A PDF does not. A PDF is a set of drawing instructions: "put these letters at this position, in this font". It was designed to look identical on every screen and printer, and it keeps almost nothing about what the text means. That one fact explains every parsing problem in this lesson.

What a person sees What the file actually contains What goes wrong for RAG
A paragraph Pieces of text, each with an x and y position Usually fine
A table The same: pieces of text at positions, plus some drawn lines Rows and columns are lost; numbers detach from their labels
Two columns Pieces of text, in whatever order the software wrote them Lines from different columns are glued together
A heading Text in a larger font The outline of the document is lost, so chunks lose their context
A scanned page One picture. No text at all Nothing to extract until the picture is read by OCR
A chart or photo A picture The information is invisible to text search (Lesson 4.31)

Two terms you will meet everywhere. The text layer is the text stored in a digital PDF, the kind you can select with a mouse. OCR (optical character recognition) is software that looks at a picture of a page and works out which letters are on it; it is the only way into a scan, a fax or a phone photo.

Everything downstream depends on this step. If parsing glues two columns together or drops a table, no chunking strategy, embedding model or prompt can repair it, and the failure is silent: the system simply answers from wrong text.

Step 1 · Make a realistic document, then parse it

The lab creates its own invoice PDF so everything runs offline, then extracts its text the way any pipeline starts: with a PDF text layer.

import base64, io, json, re
from fpdf import FPDF
from pypdf import PdfReader

def make_invoice(path: str, number: str, gstin: str, lines: list[tuple[str, int, float]]):
    pdf = FPDF()
    pdf.add_page()
    pdf.set_font("Helvetica", "B", 16); pdf.cell(0, 10, "ShopKart Retail Pvt Ltd - Tax Invoice", new_x="LMARGIN", new_y="NEXT")
    pdf.set_font("Helvetica", size=10)
    for label, value in [("Invoice No", number), ("Date", "2026-10-21"), ("Seller GSTIN", gstin),
                         ("Bill To", "Ravi Kumar, Pune 411001")]:
        pdf.cell(0, 6, f"{label}: {value}", new_x="LMARGIN", new_y="NEXT")
    pdf.ln(4)
    pdf.set_font("Helvetica", "B", 10)
    for header, w in [("Item", 90), ("Qty", 20), ("Rate (INR)", 35), ("Amount (INR)", 35)]:
        pdf.cell(w, 7, header, border=1)
    pdf.ln()
    pdf.set_font("Helvetica", size=10)
    subtotal = 0.0
    for item, qty, rate in lines:
        amount = qty * rate; subtotal += amount
        for value, w in [(item, 90), (str(qty), 20), (f"{rate:,.2f}", 35), (f"{amount:,.2f}", 35)]:
            pdf.cell(w, 7, value, border=1)
        pdf.ln()
    gst = round(subtotal * 0.18, 2)
    for label, value in [("Subtotal", subtotal), ("GST 18%", gst), ("Total", subtotal + gst)]:
        pdf.cell(145, 7, label, align="R"); pdf.cell(35, 7, f"{value:,.2f}", border=1, new_x="LMARGIN", new_y="NEXT")
    pdf.output(path)

A digital PDF with a text layer is the easy case: the text is exact, but the table became lines of words and the structure is gone. Scanned documents have no text layer at all and need OCR, which Step 4 compares with the other approaches.

Step 2 · Put the table back together

The text is all there, but the table is now four lines of words. For a layout you know, you can rebuild it with a pattern, and then check it: an invoice has arithmetic that must hold, which is a free test of whether parsing worked.

ROW = re.compile(r"^(?P<item>.+?) (?P<qty>\d+) (?P<rate>[\d,]+\.\d{2}) (?P<amount>[\d,]+\.\d{2})$")
num = lambda s: float(s.replace(",", ""))

rows = [m.groupdict() for line in text.splitlines() if (m := ROW.match(line))]
print("| Item | Qty | Rate | Amount |\n|---|---|---|---|")
for r in rows:
    print(f"| {r['item']} | {r['qty']} | {r['rate']} | {r['amount']} |")

subtotal = num(re.search(r"Subtotal ([\d,.]+)", text).group(1))
print("each row: qty x rate = amount?", all(abs(int(r["qty"]) * num(r["rate"]) - num(r["amount"])) < 0.01 for r in rows))
print("rows add up to the subtotal? ", abs(sum(num(r["amount"]) for r in rows) - subtotal) < 0.01)
What you should see
| Item | Qty | Rate | Amount |
|---|---|---|---|

| Steel Jar 1.5L | 2 | 449.00 | 898.00 |

Reading this output

The pattern reads each line from the right: the last number is the amount, the one before it the rate, the one before that the quantity, and whatever is left is the item name. That is why "MixPro 750W Mixer Grinder" survives even though it contains a number. The rows are printed as a Markdown table, which is the usual way to pass a table to a model or to a chunker: the header row travels with the values.

The two checks are the important part. They cost nothing and they catch a misread digit, a missed row or a row split across two lines. Whenever a document has internal arithmetic (totals, counts, percentages that should add to 100), use it.

A hand-written pattern like this works for one layout from one supplier. A company receives invoices from thousands. That is the job of the layout parsers and document services in Step 4: they do the same reconstruction for layouts nobody wrote a pattern for.

Step 3 · Two columns: when the reading order is wrong

Tables are one way structure is lost. Reading order is the other, and it is harder to notice, because the output looks like perfectly good sentences.

import re
from fpdf import FPDF
from pypdf import PdfReader

LEFT  = ["Refund policy", "Refunds are accepted within", "7 days of delivery.", "Damaged items: within 30 days."]
RIGHT = ["Shipping", "Metro cities: 2 to 3 days.", "Other pin codes: 4 to 7 days.", "Express delivery costs INR 99."]

pdf = FPDF(); pdf.add_page(); pdf.set_font("Helvetica", size=11)
for row, (left, right) in enumerate(zip(LEFT, RIGHT)):        # many layout tools write a page row by row, like this
    pdf.text(15, 20 + row * 7, left)
    pdf.text(110, 20 + row * 7, right)
pdf.output("/tmp/two_columns.pdf")

page = PdfReader("/tmp/two_columns.pdf").pages[0]
print("--- text layer, in the order the file stores it")
print(page.extract_text())

pieces = []                                                    # (x, y, text) for every piece of text on the page
page.extract_text(visitor_text=lambda text, cm, tm, font, size: pieces.append((tm[4], tm[5], text.strip())) if text.strip() else None)
print("--- rebuilt using positions: left column first, then right")
for column in (lambda x: x < 100, lambda x: x >= 100):
    lines = sorted((p for p in pieces if column(p[0])), key=lambda p: -p[1])     # top of the page first (y grows upward)
    print(" ".join(text for _, _, text in lines))

--- rebuilt using positions: left column first, then right
Refund policy Refunds are accepted within 7 days of delivery. Damaged items: within 30 days. Shipping Metro cities: 2 to 3 days. Other pin codes: 4 to 7 days. Express delivery costs INR 99.
Reading this output

Read the second line of the first block: "Refunds are accepted within Metro cities: 2 to 3 days." No such sentence exists on the page. The file stored the page row by row, so the text layer came out row by row, and half a sentence from the refund column was joined to half a sentence from the shipping column. A RAG system that indexed this would retrieve it for a refund question and tell the customer something false, with a citation.

The second block fixes it using the one thing a PDF does store reliably: positions. Every piece of text has an x coordinate (how far across) and a y coordinate (how far up). Sorting the left column's pieces from top to bottom, then the right column's, restores the two paragraphs.

Here the column boundary (x = 100) was typed in by hand. A layout parser finds it by itself: it detects columns, headings, tables, lists, headers and footers from positions, fonts and drawn lines, often with a small vision model, and outputs Markdown or JSON with that structure marked. For anything beyond simple one-column documents, use one. The habit to keep from this step is the check: after parsing a new kind of document, read twenty parsed pages next to the originals before you index a million.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Reading order

Plain extraction often reads across both columns line by line, interleaving them, so sentences from different columns are mixed. Chunks built from that text mean nothing. Layout-aware parsing restores the reading order before chunking.

SolutionB1 · Scans have no text layer

An empty string or almost nothing, because a scan has no text layer. You need OCR or a vision-capable parser, then checks on the fields that matter (Lesson 4.30).

SolutionD1 · Validate what you parsed

Format and arithmetic checks: a GSTIN has a fixed format with a check character, and line items must sum to the total. Run validators after parsing, send failures to review, and record the parser and version with each document.

SolutionM1 · Arithmetic as a gate

Sum quantity times price for each line, compare with the parsed total within a rupee of rounding, and on mismatch store the document with status "needs review" instead of indexing it. Count rejections per source: a spike means the source format changed.

Check your understanding

Explain the mechanismWhy is document parsing often the biggest lever in RAG quality?

Worked answer. Retrieval and generation can only use what parsing extracted. Lost tables, mixed columns, missing headings and OCR errors make the right answer unretrievable or unreadable, and no embedding model fixes that. Parsing choices, such as layout-aware parsers, table-to-Markdown conversion and keeping headings, decide whether the facts reach the index intact.

A plausible wrong answer. "The vector databaselater · 14.71 matters most." Stores matter for scale; the content quality comes from parsing.

Follow-ups. How would you measure parsing quality?

Debug a failureAnswers about prices in tables are often wrong. What do you check?

Worked answer. How tables were parsed and chunked: whether they survived as tables (Markdown or row-per-chunk with headers), or were flattened into text that loses which number belongs to which column. Look at the stored chunk for a failing question. Fix with a table-aware parser and chunking that keeps tables whole or repeats headers.

A plausible wrong answer. "The model is bad at numbers." Usually it never received the table structure.

Follow-ups. When would you send the page image to a vision model instead?

Defend a designA managed document-parsing service or an open-source parser?

Worked answer. A managed service for messy, high-variety documents (scans, forms, complex tables) where accuracy matters and data can leave your boundary under contract. Open-source parsers for simple digital PDFs and HTML, for cost, or when data must stay in-house. Decide with a sample of real documents scored field by field.

A plausible wrong answer. "Always the cheapest parser." Parsing errors cost more downstream than the parser does.
