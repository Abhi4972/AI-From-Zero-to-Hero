In plain words first
The idea in one line

Gemini 3 Pro (November 2025) accepts 1M tokens of context, yet Google's own launch table puts it at 26.3% on 8-needle MRCR at 1M tokens. Advertised ≠ usable. Long-context evaluation tells you the actual capacity of the model you are shipping on.

The problem

In plain words first

A model's context window is how much text it can take in at once, measured in tokens. Vendors now advertise hundreds of thousands or millions. That number says what the model accepts, not what it uses well. Finding one planted sentence in a long document is easy for current models; connecting several facts spread through it, or finding a fact that is phrased differently from the question, is much harder, and gets harder as the text grows. This lesson builds the standard tests, a planted "needle" at different depths and lengths, several needles, a chain of facts and a needle with no words in common with the question, and runs them on a small local model so you can watch the gap between "found it" and "understood it" open up at only a few thousand tokens.

You have a 200-page contract. The model claims a 1M-token context. You paste the contract in and ask: "What is the termination clause?" The model answers — but answers from the cover page because the termination clause sits at 120k tokens deep, past where the model actually attends.

This is the context-capacity gap. Spec sheets say 1M tokens (Meta advertised 10M for Llama 4 Scout). How much of that is usable depends on the task, and has to be measured.

  • Retrieval (single needle in haystack): near-perfect up to the advertised max on frontier models.
  • Multi-hop / aggregation: degrades much earlier than retrieval, often well inside the advertised window.
  • Reasoning over dispersed facts: the first task to fail.

Long-context evaluation measures these axes. This lesson names the benchmarks, what each actually measures, and how to build a custom needle test for your domain.

The idea

Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH, 2023). Place a fact ("the magic word is pineapple") at a controlled depth in a long context. Ask the model to retrieve it. Sweep depth × length. The original long-context benchmark. Frontier models now saturate this; it is a necessary but not sufficient baseline.

RULER (Nvidia, 2024). 13 task types across 4 categories: retrieval (single / multi-key / multi-value), multi-hop tracing (variable tracking), aggregation (common word frequency), QA. Configurable context length (4k to 128k+). Reveals models that saturate NIAH but fail on multi-hop. In the 2024 release, only half of 17 models claiming 32k+ context maintained quality at 32k. RULER's effective length is the longest length at which a model still beats a fixed bar: Llama-2-7B's score at 4k, 85.6%.

LongBench v2 (2024). 503 multiple-choice questions, 8k-2M word contexts, six task categories: single-doc QA, multi-doc QA, long in-context learning, long dialogue, code repo, long structured data. The production benchmark for real-world long-context behavior.

MRCR (Multi-Round Co-reference Resolution). Introduced in Google's Michelangelo evaluations (2024) and released in a harder open version by OpenAI (2025). A long synthetic conversation contains 2, 4 or 8 identical requests (say, "write a poem about tapirs") among similar distractor requests, and the model must reproduce one of them, for example the second poem about tapirs. Scored by string similarity to the right reply. Exposes how many near-identical items a model can keep apart.

NoLiMa. "Non-lexical needle." The needle and the query share no literal overlap; retrieval requires one step of semantic reasoning (Modarressi et al., 2025). Of 13 models claiming at least 128k tokens, 11 fell below half of their short-context score at 32k; GPT-4o fell from 99.3% to 69.7%.

HELMET (Yen et al., 2024). A suite of application-style tasks (retrieval-augmented QA, generation with citations, passage re-ranking, many-shot in-context learning, long-document QA, summarisation, and synthetic recall) at controlled lengths up to 128k. Built because synthetic recall scores alone did not predict how models ranked on real tasks.

BABILong. Embeds bAbI reasoning chains inside irrelevant haystacks. Tests reasoning-in-a-haystack, not just retrieval.

The same idea as a formula

Effective length. With accuracy a(L) measured at context length L and a threshold τ,

Leff​(τ)=max{L:a(L)≥τ}

RULER uses τ=85.6%. Report Leff​ separately for retrieval and for reasoning tasks.

The depth × length grid. For depths d∈{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1} and lengths L, the cell value is the pass rate over k trials, a^(d,L)=k1​∑j​passj​. With k=1 every cell is a single coin flip; a 95% interval for a pass rate needs k in the tens.

Several needles. If each of m needles were found independently with probability p, finding all of them has probability

P(all m)=pm

so multi-needle accuracy falls even when nothing interacts. In practice needles also compete for attention, which makes it worse.

Lost in the middle. Liu et al. (2024) found a U-shaped curve: accuracy is highest when the relevant passage is at the start or end of the context, lowest in the middle.

What length costs. A transformer's prefill does about 2N floating-point operations per token for the weights (N parameters) plus about 4ndmodel​ℓ per token for attention over n earlier tokens (ℓ layers). The first term is linear in length, the second quadratic. The key/value cache needs

KV bytes=2×ℓ×hkv​×dhead​×n×bytes per value

which is why grouped-query attention (few key/value heads) matters for long contexts.

Worked example
Two needles, a cache and a crossover by hand

Many needles. A model finds a single needle 95% of the time. If eight needles were independent, it would find all eight with probability 0.958=0.66; with p=0.9, 0.98=0.43. A respectable single-needle score says little about an eight-needle one.

KV cache for the model used below. Qwen2.5-0.5B has ℓ=24 layers, hkv​=2 key/value heads of size 64. Per token: 2×24×2×64×2 bytes =12,288 bytes. At 32,000 tokens: 0.39 GB; at 1M tokens it would need 12.3 GB, more than the model's own weights by a factor of about 12.

When attention starts to dominate. The same model has about N=0.49×109 parameters, so the weights cost 2N≈109 operations per token. Attention costs 4×n×896×24≈86,000n. They are equal at n≈109/86,000≈11,600 tokens. Below that, doubling the length roughly doubles the time; well above it, doubling the length roughly quadruples it.

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "The model accepts 1M tokens, so it can use 1M tokens."
    ✓ The window is a limit, not a measurement. Usable length depends on the task and must be tested.
  • ✗ "It passes needle-in-a-haystack at every depth, so long context works."
    ✓ Plain NIAH is a lookup with a word match. Tasks that need a step of inference or several facts fail much earlier.
  • ✗ "Filler text doesn't matter."
    ✓ Repeated filler makes the needle stand out; related, coherent filler makes it harder. Use realistic text from your domain.

Idea 1 · What to actually report

  • Advertised context window. The spec-sheet number.
  • Effective retrieval length. NIAH pass at some threshold (e.g., 90%).
  • Effective reasoning length. Multi-hop or aggregation pass at that threshold.
  • Degradation curve. Accuracy vs context length, plotted per task type.

Two numbers for your spec sheet: retrieval-effective and reasoning-effective. Expect the reasoning-effective length to be much shorter than the advertised window, and measure it rather than assume a ratio.

Step 1 · a custom NIAH for your domain

See code/main.py. The skeleton:

def tokenize(text):
    return text.split()     # whitespace "tokens" for the sketch; Step 1's run below uses the model's real tokenizer


def build_haystack(filler_text, needle, depth_ratio, total_tokens):
    if not (0.0 <= depth_ratio <= 1.0):
        raise ValueError(f"depth_ratio must be in [0, 1], got {depth_ratio}")
    if total_tokens <= 0:
        raise ValueError(f"total_tokens must be positive, got {total_tokens}")

    filler_tokens = tokenize(filler_text)
    needle_tokens = tokenize(needle)
    if not filler_tokens:
        raise ValueError("filler_text produced no tokens")

    # Repeat filler until long enough to fill the haystack body.
    body_len = max(total_tokens - len(needle_tokens), 0)
    while len(filler_tokens) < body_len:
        filler_tokens = filler_tokens + filler_tokens
    filler_tokens = filler_tokens[:body_len]

    insert_at = min(int(body_len * depth_ratio), body_len)
    haystack = filler_tokens[:insert_at] + needle_tokens + filler_tokens[insert_at:]
    return " ".join(haystack)


def score_niah(model, haystack, question, expected):
    answer = model.complete(f"Context: {haystack}\nQ: {question}\nA:", max_tokens=50)
    return 1 if expected.lower() in answer.lower() else 0


h = build_haystack("the river flows past the old mill and the fields beyond.", "The magic word is pineapple.", 0.5, 40)
print(len(h.split()), "tokens:", h)

A real needle-in-a-haystack grid

Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, greedy decoding, inference only. The haystack is real news prose from the SummEval articles; the needle is "The special code for the archive room is NNNN." with a different code in every cell; the question comes after the text. Three depths × four lengths, one trial per cell.

import re, time, torch
from datasets import load_dataset
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM

name = "Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct"            # trained with a 32k-token context window
qtok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(name)
qlm = AutoModelForCausalLM.from_pretrained(name, dtype=torch.float32).eval()

# Haystack: real news prose (SummEval's CNN/DailyMail articles), not a repeated sentence.
news = load_dataset("mteb/summeval", split="test")
sentences = [s for i in range(100) for s in re.split(r"(?<=[.!?])\s+", news[i]["text"].replace("\n", " ")) if len(s) > 20]

def haystack(n_tokens, needles, depths, start=0):
    """About n_tokens of news sentences, with each needle sentence inserted at its relative depth."""
    body, count, i = [], 0, start
    while count < n_tokens:
        body.append(sentences[i % len(sentences)]); count += len(qtok(sentences[i % len(sentences)])["input_ids"]) + 1; i += 1
    for needle, d in sorted(zip(needles, depths), key=lambda x: -x[1]):   # insert deepest first so positions hold
        body.insert(round(d * len(body)), needle)
    return " ".join(body)

def ask(context, question, max_new_tokens=24):
    msgs = [{"role": "user", "content": f"{context}\n\nAnswer from the text above. {question}"}]
    ids = qtok.apply_chat_template(msgs, add_generation_prompt=True, return_tensors="pt", return_dict=True)["input_ids"]
    with torch.no_grad():
        out = qlm.generate(ids, max_new_tokens=max_new_tokens, do_sample=False)
    return qtok.decode(out[0, ids.shape[1]:], skip_special_tokens=True).strip(), ids.shape[1]

t0 = time.time()
lengths, depths = [500, 1000, 2000, 4000], [0.0, 0.5, 1.0]
codes = iter(["4817", "9203", "3561", "7748", "2095", "6634", "1482", "8306", "5179", "3920", "7051", "6268"])
print("depth   " + "".join(f"{n:>8}" for n in lengths) + "   (tokens of haystack; 1 = code found)")
grid, secs = {}, {n: [] for n in lengths}
for d in depths:
    row = []
    for n in lengths:
        code = next(codes)
        ctx = haystack(n, [f"The special code for the archive room is {code}."], [d], start=n)
        t = time.time()
        answer, n_in = ask(ctx, "What is the special code for the archive room?")
        secs[n].append(time.time() - t)
        row.append(int(code in answer)); grid[d, n] = answer
    print(f"{d:<6}  " + "".join(f"{x:>8}" for x in row))
print("seconds per question: " + ", ".join(f"{n} tokens {sum(v) / len(v):.1f}" for n, v in secs.items()))
for (d, n), a in grid.items():
    if not re.search(r"\d{4}", a):
        print(f"   depth {d}, {n} tokens: model said {a[:70]!r}")

Step 2 · a multi-needle variant

def build_multi_needle(filler, needles, total_tokens, depths=(0.1, 0.4, 0.7)):
    """Place each needle at its relative depth in total_tokens of filler (whitespace tokens)."""
    words = filler.split()
    while len(words) < total_tokens:
        words = words + words
    words = words[:total_tokens]
    for depth, needle in sorted(zip(depths, needles), reverse=True):   # deepest first, so earlier positions do not shift
        pos = int(total_tokens * depth)
        words = words[:pos] + needle.split() + words[pos:]
    return " ".join(words)


h = build_multi_needle("grass sky stone water wind light", ["MAGIC-1 pineapple", "MAGIC-2 compass", "MAGIC-3 whisper"], 100)
print([i for i, w in enumerate(h.split()) if w.startswith("MAGIC")])   # where the three needles landed

Questions like "What are the three magic words?" require retrieving all three. Single-needle success does not predict multi-needle success.

This version works on a word list, so each needle lands at its stated depth; the repository's sketch sliced a string by character counts while calling them tokens. The printed positions are 10, 42 and 74: each needle is two words long, so the later ones shift by two words per earlier needle.

Step 3 · multi-hop variable tracing (RULER-style)

haystack = """X1 = 42. ... (filler) ... X2 = X1 + 10. ... (filler) ... X3 = X2 * 2."""
question = "What is X3?"

The answer requires chaining three assignments (and here, arithmetic). RULER's version only copies values (VAR B = VAR A) and asks for every variable that ends up holding a given value. In RULER, models that are near-perfect on simple retrieval show large drops on tasks like this as length grows.

The word-matching needle passes at every length, as in the grid above. Three needles at once also pass here: each still shares the words "special codes" with the question. The non-lexical needle collapses: with nothing but the needle, the model names Yuki (the only person mentioned), but inside 1,000 tokens of news it fails, and at 2,000 it confidently names a footballer from one of the articles, Carlos Tevez. It fails because nothing in "Which character has been to Dresden?" matches "Semper Opera House" and it must know the opera house is in Dresden and connect the two. That is NoLiMa's finding at a far shorter length. Variable tracking degrades even without filler (two of three variables), and reaches zero at 2,000 tokens. The two tasks that need a step of reasoning are the ones that fail, and they fail at 1/16 of this model's 32k window.

Step 4 · LongBench v2 on your stack

from datasets import load_dataset
longbench = load_dataset("THUDM/LongBench-v2", split="train")     # 503 questions, about 465 MB of contexts


def eval_model_on_longbench(model, domain="Single-Document QA"):
    tasks = [x for x in longbench if x["domain"] == domain]
    correct = 0
    for x in tasks:
        prompt = (x["context"] + "\n\nQuestion: " + x["question"] +
                  "".join(f"\n({c}) {x['choice_' + c]}" for c in "ABCD") +
                  "\n\nAnswer with the letter of the correct choice.")
        answer = model.complete(prompt, max_tokens=5)
        letter = next((ch for ch in answer.strip() if ch in "ABCD"), None)
        correct += letter == x["answer"]          # answers are the letters A-D
    return correct / len(tasks)

Report per-category accuracy. Aggregate scores hide big task-level differences.

Not run here: the dataset is about 465 MB, and its contexts (8,000 words to 2 million) are far beyond what a CPU can process in a lesson. Break it on purpose

Plant two versions of one fact in the same haystack: "The special code for the archive room is 1111." and "Update: the special code for the archive room has been changed to 2222." Ask for the current code with the update placed after the original, then with the two sentences' positions swapped. Does the model follow the word "Update", or something else? (Solution B1.)

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Million-token windows are common; using them is not. Gemini 3 Pro (November 2025) accepts 1M tokens, and Google's own launch figures put it at 26.3% on 8-needle MRCR v2 at that length. Vendors now publish multi-needle and long-reasoning scores next to the window size.
  • "Context rot". Chroma's July 2025 report tested 18 models on deliberately simple tasks and found reliability falls as input grows, faster when the needle is phrased unlike the question, and with even one topically related distractor in the haystack.
  • Context management is a product feature. Prompt caching (OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini) makes long, repeated prefixes cheap, and agent platforms trim, summarise or clear old tool results (for example Anthropic's context editing, 2025) rather than letting the window fill.

Sources: MRCR dataset, Context Rot, Anthropic context management, Anthropic prompt caching, RULER.

Pitfalls

  • NIAH-only evaluation. Passing NIAH at 1M tokens says nothing about multi-hop. Always run RULER or a custom multi-hop test.
  • Uniform depth sampling. Many implementations only test depth=0.5. Test depth=0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 — the "lost in the middle" effect is real.
  • Lexical overlap with filler. If the needle shares keywords with the filler, retrieval becomes trivial. Use NoLiMa-style non-overlapping needles.
  • Ignoring latency. Prefill time grows with length, the attention part quadratically. On this page the 0.5B model needed about 19 seconds per question at 4,000 tokens on a CPU; a 1M-token prompt is a large computation even on data-centre GPUs. Measure time-to-first-token alongside accuracy.
  • Vendor-self-reported numbers. OpenAI, Google, Anthropic all publish their own scores. Always re-run independently on your use case.

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

The 2026 stack:

Situation Benchmark
Quick sanity check Custom NIAH at 3 depths × 3 lengths
Model selection for production RULER (13 tasks) at your target length
Real-world QA quality LongBench v2 single-doc-QA subset
Multi-hop reasoning BABILong or custom variable-tracing
Conversational / dialogue MRCR 8-needle at your target length
Model upgrade regression Fixed in-house NIAH + RULER harness, run on every new model

Rule of thumb for production: never trust a context window until you have NIAH + 1 reasoning task at your intended length.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • The context window is what a model accepts; the effective length is what it handles at a given accuracy, and it differs by task.
  • Plain NIAH is a lookup; it saturates early. Add needles without word overlap, several needles, and a multi-step task.
  • Sweep depth (start, middle, end) as well as length, with several trials per cell, on filler that looks like your data.
  • Long inputs cost time and memory: prefill grows linearly then quadratically, and the KV cache grows linearly with tokens.
  • Re-run your own harness on every model upgrade; do not rely on self-reported numbers.

Given a target model, target context length, and use case, output:

1. Tests. NIAH depth × length grid; RULER multi-hop; custom domain task.
2. Sampling. Depths 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 at each length.
3. Metrics. Retrieval pass rate; reasoning pass rate; time-to-first-token; cost-per-query.
4. Cutoff. Effective retrieval length (90% pass) and effective reasoning length (70% pass). Report both.
5. Regression. Fixed harness, rerun on every model upgrade, surface deltas.

Refuse to trust a context window from the model card alone. Refuse NIAH-only evaluation for any multi-hop workload. Refuse vendor self-reported long-context scores as independent evidence.

Exercises

  1. Easy. Build a NIAH with 3 depths (0.25, 0.5, 0.75) × 3 lengths (1k, 4k, 16k). Run on any model. Plot pass rate as a 3×3 heatmap.
  2. Medium. Add a 3-needle variant. Measure retrieval of all 3 at each length. Compare to single-needle pass rate at the same length.
  3. Hard. Construct a variable-tracing task (X1 → X2 → X3, with 3 hops) embedded in 64k of filler. Measure accuracy across 3 frontier models. Report effective reasoning length per model.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · The reasoning tasks fail first

Both tasks that need reasoning fail, and early. The non-lexical needle is answered when it is alone but missed from 1,000 tokens on; variable tracking is imperfect even with no filler and reaches zero at 2,000 tokens. The two needles that share words with their question, single or triple, pass at every length tested. A NIAH score would have described this model as fine at 2,000 tokens.

SolutionB1 · It follows position, not meaning

With the update placed after the original, the model answers 2222 ("the current special code for the archive room is 2222"). Swap the positions, so that the update comes first and the old statement last, and it answers 1111. The word "Update" did not decide; the later statement did. (Read literally, the swapped text is ambiguous, but the model shows no sign of noticing that.) Tests with superseded facts, such as revised contract clauses or changed settings, need both orders, and production systems should resolve versions before the model sees them.
