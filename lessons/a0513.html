The problem

In plain words first

Question answering (QA) means giving a direct answer to a question, such as "June 29, 2007", rather than a document that might contain it. The classic approach, extractive QA, is given a passage and points at the answer inside it. A model reads the question and passage together and scores every word as a possible start and end of the answer, and the best-scoring stretch of text is the answer. Since 2018 models can also say "this passage does not contain the answer". When there is no passage, a retriever first finds relevant ones in a large collection, which is the "R" in RAG (retrieval-augmented generation). This lesson writes out the span-prediction math, rebuilds the answer step that newer transformers versions removed, measures a real model on SQuAD 2.0 (including the questions it should refuse), and shows that in a retrieve-then-read system the retriever often limits the accuracy.

A user types "When did the first iPhone launch?" and expects "June 29, 2007." Not "Apple's history is long and varied." Not "2007" sitting in isolation with no sentence. A direct, grounded, correct answer.

Three architectures have dominated QA over the last decade.

  • Extractive QA. Given a question and a passage that is known to contain the answer, find the start and end indices of the answer span in the passage. SQuAD is the canonical benchmark.
  • Open-domain QA. The passage is not given. Retrieve the relevant passage first, then extract or generate an answer. This is the bedrock of every RAG pipeline today.
  • Generative / Closed-book QA. A large language model answers from its parametric memory. No retrieval. Fastest at inference, least reliable on facts.

The trend in 2026 is hybrid: retrieve the best few passages, then prompt a generative model to answer grounded in those passages. That is RAG, and lesson 14 covers the retrieval half in depth. This lesson builds the QA half.

The idea

Extractive. Encode question and passage together with a transformer (BERT family). Train two heads that predict start and end token indices of the answer. Loss is cross-entropy over valid positions. Output is a span from the passage. Never hallucinates (by construction), never handles questions the passage cannot answer (by construction).

Retrieval-augmented (RAG). Two stages. First, a retriever finds the top-k passages from a corpus. Second, a reader (extractive or generative) produces the answer using those passages. The retriever-reader split lets each be trained and evaluated independently. Modern RAG often adds a reranker between them.

Generative. A decoder-only LLM (GPT, Claude, Llama) answers from learned weights. No retrieval step. Excellent on common knowledge, catastrophic on rare or recent facts. The hallucination rate is inversely correlated with fact frequency in the pretraining data.

The same idea as a formula

Span prediction. The question and passage are encoded together, <s> question </s></s> passage </s>, giving one vector hi​ per token. Two learned vectors ws​,we​ score every token as a start and as an end:

P(start=i)=∑k​ews⊤​hk​ews⊤​hi​​,P(end=j)=∑k​ewe⊤​hk​ewe⊤​hj​​

Training minimises −logP(start=i∗)−logP(end=j∗) for the true span (i∗,j∗). At inference, the answer is the span with the best total logit, under two constraints: it must lie inside the passage, and i≤j<i+L for a maximum length L:

(i^,j^​)=argi≤j<i+Lmax​(ws⊤​hi​+we⊤​hj​)

No answer. SQuAD 2.0 models are trained to put both start and end on the first token for unanswerable questions, so null=ws⊤​h0​+we⊤​h0​. Answer only if the best span beats the null score by a margin τ, which you tune on held-out data to trade wrong answers against wrong refusals.

Metrics. After normalising both strings (lowercase, strip punctuation and the articles a, an, the), Exact Match is 1 if they are identical. Token F1 treats them as bags of tokens: precision =∣common∣/∣pred∣, recall =∣common∣/∣gold∣, F1​=2PR/(P+R). With several gold answers, take the best score over them. For an unanswerable question, both metrics are 1 only if the prediction is empty.

Retrieve then read. If the retriever puts the right passage in its top k with probability R@k and the reader is right on a correct passage with probability a, end-to-end accuracy is at most R@k⋅a: the reader cannot answer from a passage it never sees.

Worked example
EM and F1 by hand

Gold answer "June 29, 2007", prediction "on June 29th, 2007".

  • Normalise: gold tokens june 29 2007; prediction tokens on june 29th 2007 (punctuation removed; "on" is not an article, so it stays).
  • Exact Match: the strings differ, so EM = 0.
  • Common tokens: june, 2007, so 2. Precision =2/4=0.5, recall =2/3=0.667.
  • F1​=2×0.5×0.667/(0.5+0.667)=0.571.

A correct answer earns 0 EM and 0.57 F1 because of "on" and "th". That is why SQuAD reports both, and why production QA uses human or LLM judgement for answer correctness.

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Extractive QA cannot be wrong, because it copies from the passage."
    ✓ It cannot invent words, but it can point at the wrong span (a different date in the same passage), or answer a question the passage does not answer.
  • ✗ "The span score is the model's confidence."
    ✓ It is a sum of two logits, meaningful only relative to other spans and the no-answer score; calibrated probabilities need extra work.
  • ✗ "A better reader fixes a RAG system."
    ✓ If the retriever misses the passage, no reader can recover. Measure retrieval recall first.

Step 1 · extractive QA with a pretrained model

import torch
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForQuestionAnswering

model_id = "deepset/roberta-base-squad2"
qa_tok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(model_id)
qa_model = AutoModelForQuestionAnswering.from_pretrained(model_id).eval()


def qa(question, context, max_answer_len=30, handle_impossible_answer=True):
    enc = qa_tok(question, context, return_tensors="pt", truncation="only_second", max_length=384, return_offsets_mapping=True)
    offsets = enc.pop("offset_mapping")[0]
    with torch.no_grad():
        out = qa_model(**enc)
    start, end = out.start_logits[0], out.end_logits[0]          # one score per token for "answer starts / ends here"
    in_context = torch.tensor([sid == 1 for sid in enc.sequence_ids(0)])   # only passage tokens can be part of the answer
    best, best_span = -float("inf"), None
    for i in torch.nonzero(in_context).flatten().tolist():
        for j in range(i, min(i + max_answer_len, len(start))):
            if in_context[j] and start[i] + end[j] > best:
                best, best_span = (start[i] + end[j]).item(), (i, j)
    null = (start[0] + end[0]).item()                             # position 0 (<s>) stands for "no answer"
    if handle_impossible_answer and null > best:
        return {"score": null, "start": 0, "end": 0, "answer": ""}
    s, e = offsets[best_span[0]][0].item(), offsets[best_span[1]][1].item()
    return {"score": best, "start": s, "end": e, "answer": context[s:e]}


passage = (
    "Apple Inc. released the first iPhone on June 29, 2007. "
    "The device was announced by Steve Jobs at Macworld in January 2007."
)
question = "When was the first iPhone released?"

answer = qa(question=question, context=passage)
print(answer)

deepset/roberta-base-squad2 is trained on SQuAD 2.0, which includes unanswerable questions. transformers 5 removed the question-answering pipeline, so the block above (rewritten for this page) does what it did, with the model directly: score every start and end position, pick the best valid span, and compare it with the model's "no answer" score. The pipeline's old default returned the best span even when "no answer" scored higher; the function above returns an empty answer in that case unless you pass handle_impossible_answer=False. Always look at the score either way.

Step 2 · a retrieval-augmented pipeline (sketch)

from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
import numpy as np

encoder = SentenceTransformer("sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2")

corpus = [
    "Apple Inc. released the first iPhone on June 29, 2007.",
    "Macworld 2007 featured the iPhone announcement by Steve Jobs.",
    "Android launched in 2008 as Google's mobile operating system.",
    "The first iPod was released in 2001.",
]
corpus_embeddings = encoder.encode(corpus, normalize_embeddings=True)


def retrieve(question, top_k=2):
    q_emb = encoder.encode([question], normalize_embeddings=True)
    sims = (corpus_embeddings @ q_emb.T).squeeze()
    order = np.argsort(-sims)[:top_k]
    return [corpus[i] for i in order]


def answer(question):
    passages = retrieve(question, top_k=2)
    combined = " ".join(passages)
    return qa(question=question, context=combined)


print(answer("When was the first iPhone released?"))

Two-stage pipeline. Dense retriever (Sentence-BERT) finds relevant passages by semantic similarity. Extractive reader (RoBERTa-SQuAD) pulls the answer span from the combined top passages. Works on small corpora. For a million-document corpus, use FAISS or a vector database.

Break it on purpose

In qa, delete the in_context[j] check and the restriction to passage tokens, so any token, including the question's, can start or end the answer. Ask "When was the first iPhone released?" about a passage that does not mention the iPhone. What can go wrong? (Solution B1.)

Step 3 · generative with RAG

def rag_generate(question, llm):
    passages = retrieve(question, top_k=3)
    prompt = f"""Context:
{chr(10).join('- ' + p for p in passages)}

Question: {question}

Answer using only the context above. If the context does not contain the answer, say "I don't know."
"""
    return llm(prompt)

The prompt pattern matters. Explicitly telling the model to ground in the context and return "I don't know" when the context is insufficient measurably reduces unsupported answers compared with naive prompting, though by how much depends heavily on the model and the data, so measure it on your own questions. More elaborate patterns add citations, confidence scores, and structured extraction.

Step 4 · evaluation that reflects the real world

SQuAD uses Exact Match (EM) and token-level F1. EM is a strict match after normalization (lowercase, strip punctuation, remove articles) — either the prediction matches exactly or it scores 0. F1 is computed over token overlap between prediction and reference and gives partial credit. Both under-credit paraphrases: "June 29, 2007" vs "June 29th, 2007" typically gets 0 EM (the ordinal breaks normalization) but still earns substantial F1 from overlapping tokens.

For production QA:

  • Answer accuracy (LLM-judged or human-judged, since metrics do not capture semantic equivalence).
  • Citation accuracy. Does the cited passage actually support the answer? Trivial to check automatically with string match between generated citations and retrieved passages.
  • Refusal calibration. When the answer is not in the retrieved passages, does the system correctly say "I don't know"? Measure false confidence rate.
  • Retrieval recall. Before evaluating the reader, measure whether the retriever gets the right passage into the top-k. A reader cannot fix a missing passage.

Build · RAGAS: the 2026 production eval framework

RAGAS is a widely used open-source framework built for RAG systems. Its four core metrics use an LLM as the judge; three of them need no gold answer:

  • Faithfulness. Does each claim in the answer come from the retrieved context? Measured by having an LLM split the answer into individual claims and check each one against the context. Your primary hallucination metric.
  • Answer relevance. Does the answer address the question? Measured by generating hypothetical questions from the answer and comparing to the real question.
  • Context precision. Of the retrieved chunks, what fraction were actually relevant? Low precision = noise in prompt.
  • Context recall. Did the retrieved set contain all needed information? This one compares against a reference answer, so it needs a labelled set. Low recall = reader cannot succeed.

Reference-free scoring lets you evaluate on live production traffic without curated gold answers. Layer LLM-as-judge on top for open-ended questions where exact-match metrics are useless.

pip install ragas. Plug your retriever + reader. Get four scalars per query. Alert on regressions.

The 2026 stack.

Use case Recommended
Given passage, find answer span deepset/roberta-base-squad2
Over a fixed corpus, closed-book not acceptable RAG: dense retriever + LLM reader
Real-time over a document store RAG with hybrid (BM25 + dense) retriever + reranker (lesson 14)

import re, string, time
import pyarrow.parquet as pq
from collections import Counter
from huggingface_hub import hf_hub_download

squad = pq.read_table(hf_hub_download("rajpurkar/squad_v2", "squad_v2/validation-00000-of-00001.parquet", repo_type="dataset")).to_pylist()[:200]
def normalize(t):
    t = "".join(ch for ch in t.lower() if ch not in set(string.punctuation))
    return " ".join(w for w in t.split() if w not in ("a", "an", "the"))
def f1(pred, gold):
    p, g = normalize(pred).split(), normalize(gold).split()
    if not p or not g:
        return float(p == g)
    common = sum((Counter(p) & Counter(g)).values())
    return 0.0 if common == 0 else 2 * common / (len(p) + len(g))
def score(pred, golds):
    golds = golds or [""]
    return max(float(normalize(pred) == normalize(g)) for g in golds), max(f1(pred, g) for g in golds)

print(f"worked example: F1('on June 29th, 2007', 'June 29, 2007') = {f1('on June 29th, 2007', 'June 29, 2007'):.3f}")
start = time.time()
rows = []
for ex in squad:
    golds = ex["answers"]["text"]
    with_null, forced = qa(ex["question"], ex["context"]), qa(ex["question"], ex["context"], handle_impossible_answer=False)
    rows.append((bool(golds), score(with_null["answer"], golds), score(forced["answer"], golds)))
print(f"{len(rows)} questions ({sum(r[0] for r in rows)} answerable) in {time.time() - start:.0f} s")
for label, k in (("may answer 'no answer'", 1), ("must always give a span", 2)):
    for subset, name in ((lambda r: True, "all"), (lambda r: r[0], "answerable"), (lambda r: not r[0], "unanswerable")):
        sel = [r[k] for r in rows if subset(r)]
        print(f"   {label:24s} {name:13s} EM {100 * np.mean([s[0] for s in sel]):5.1f}   F1 {100 * np.mean([s[1] for s in sel]):5.1f}")

With the option to refuse, the model scores about 78 F1 on this sample (its published result on the full SQuAD 2.0 set is around 83). The interesting part is the trade-off. Forced to always answer, it does better on answerable questions (89 against 82 F1), because it no longer wrongly refuses some of them, but every unanswerable question now gets a confident wrong span and scores 0, so the overall F1 halves to about 40. The "no answer" threshold is a dial between those two kinds of error, and where to set it depends on what a wrong answer costs compared with a wrong refusal. In SQuAD 2.0 about half the questions are unanswerable; in production that share is unknown, which is why refusal calibration is a metric in its own right.

Retrieve, then read: where the errors come from

The 200 questions' passages (fewer, since several questions share a passage) become a small corpus. The lesson's dense retriever (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) looks for each answerable question's passage, and the reader answers from the top retrieved passage instead of the gold one.

from sentence_transformers import SentenceTransformer
encoder = SentenceTransformer("sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2")
corpus = sorted({ex["context"] for ex in squad})
corpus_emb = encoder.encode(corpus, normalize_embeddings=True, batch_size=32)
answerable = [ex for ex in squad if ex["answers"]["text"]]
q_emb = encoder.encode([ex["question"] for ex in answerable], normalize_embeddings=True, batch_size=32)
ranks = np.argsort(-(q_emb @ corpus_emb.T), axis=1)
gold_idx = np.array([corpus.index(ex["context"]) for ex in answerable])
for k in (1, 3, 5):
    print(f"retrieval recall@{k}: {np.mean([g in r[:k] for g, r in zip(gold_idx, ranks)]):.1%}   ({len(corpus)} passages)")
gold_f1, rag_f1 = [], []
for ex, r in zip(answerable, ranks):
    golds = ex["answers"]["text"]
    gold_f1.append(score(qa(ex["question"], ex["context"], handle_impossible_answer=False)["answer"], golds)[1])
    rag_f1.append(score(qa(ex["question"], corpus[r[0]], handle_impossible_answer=False)["answer"], golds)[1])
print(f"reader F1 given the gold passage: {100 * np.mean(gold_f1):.1f}   given the top retrieved passage: {100 * np.mean(rag_f1):.1f}")

Reading this output

Even on a tiny corpus of 38 Wikipedia paragraphs, the dense retriever puts the right passage first for only about three questions in four (73.6%), rising to 89% within the top 5. The reader's F1 falls from 89 with the gold passage to 66.5 with the top retrieved one, almost exactly the gold-passage score times the recall@1 (88.9×0.736=65.4): given the wrong passage, it answers confidently from the wrong text. This is the "retrieve then read" bound from the formula box, and it gets worse as the corpus grows, which is why the next lesson is entirely about retrieval, and why production systems pass several passages to the reader or rerank them first.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • RAG is the default architecture, and agentic RAG is the frontier. Instead of one retrieve-then-read pass, models now decide when to search, rewrite queries and read several sources in a loop (the "deep research" products of 2025), with citations to the passages used.
  • Long context did not make retrieval obsolete. Models with context windows of hundreds of thousands of tokens can read whole document sets, but benchmarks keep showing accuracy dropping for facts buried in the middle of long inputs, and cost grows with every token, so retrieval still decides what the model reads.
  • Evaluation is LLM-judged and claim-level. Frameworks such as RAGAS score faithfulness by checking each claim of the answer against the retrieved text, and the RAG evaluation literature splits errors into retrieval failures and generation failures, as the last experiment did.

Sources: RAGAS, Lost in the Middle.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Extractive QA predicts a start and an end token: softmax over positions, best valid span by summed logits.
  • SQuAD 2.0 adds "no answer": compare the best span with the null score and tune the margin.
  • EM and token F1 after normalisation; both under-credit correct paraphrases.
  • In retrieve-then-read, end-to-end accuracy is capped by retrieval recall; measure the retriever first.
  • RAG evaluation checks faithfulness, relevance and retrieval quality separately, increasingly with LLM judges.

  1. Easy. Set up the SQuAD extractive pipeline above on 10 Wikipedia passages. Hand-craft 10 questions. Measure how often the answer is correct. You should see 7-9 correct if passages and questions are clean.
  2. Medium. Add a refusal classifier. When the top retrieval score is below a threshold (say 0.3 cosine), return "I don't know" instead of calling the reader. Tune the threshold on a held-out set.
  3. Hard. Build a RAG pipeline over a 10,000-document corpus of your choice. Implement hybrid retrieval (BM25 + dense) with RRF fusion (see lesson 14). Measure answer accuracy with and without the hybrid step. Document which question types benefit most.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · It drops a lot

On the answerable half, forcing a span changes little, since the model would have answered anyway. On the unanswerable half, every question now gets a span, and every span scores 0, because the correct answer is "no answer". With about half the questions unanswerable, overall F1 falls by tens of points (the run prints both). The lesson: when a system can be asked things its sources do not contain, the ability to abstain is worth as much as the ability to answer.

SolutionB1 · The answer can come from the question

Without the restriction, the span can start or end in the question or on special tokens. Asked about a passage that does not mention the iPhone, the model may "answer" with a piece of the question itself, such as "the first iPhone", or with a span that runs from the question into the passage, which turns into a character range that makes no sense in the passage. The sequence_ids check is what makes extractive QA extractive: only passage tokens are valid answers, and the first token is reserved for "no answer".
