In plain words first
The idea in one line

Audio is an image of frequency over time. Whisper is a ViT that eats mel spectrograms and speaks back.

Terms you'll hear
Term What people say What it actually means
Mel spectrogram "Audio image" 2D representation: frequency bins on one axis, time frames on the other; log-scaled energy per cell.
Log-mel "What Whisper sees" Mel spectrogram passed through log; approximates human perception of loudness.
Frame "One time slice" A 25 ms window of samples; overlapping at 10 ms stride.

Faster-whisper "The production wrapper" CTranslate2 reimplementation; int8 quantization; 4× faster than OpenAI's reference.

The problem

In plain words first

Whisper turns speech into text with the same encoder–decoder transformer as a translation model. The audio is first turned into a picture: a log-mel spectrogram, which shows how much energy each band of pitches has in each 10-millisecond slice, much as the ear hears it. The encoder reads that picture (always a 30-second window), and the decoder writes text token by token, reading the audio through cross-attention. What it should do is chosen by a few special tokens at the start of the decoder's input: which language, and whether to transcribe or translate into English. This lesson runs the lesson's framing code, rebuilds Whisper's log-mel spectrogram in a dozen lines of NumPy and matches the official one, and has Whisper identify, transcribe and translate German speech, switching task by changing one token. Nothing is trained.

Before Whisper (OpenAI, Radford et al. 2022), state-of-the-art automatic speech recognition (ASR) meant wav2vec 2.0 and HuBERT — self-supervised feature extractors plus a fine-tuned head. High quality, expensive data pipelines, domain-brittle. Multilingual speech recognition needed separate models per language family.

Whisper made three bets:

  1. Train on everything. 680,000 hours of weakly-labeled audio scraped from the internet across 97 languages. No clean academic corpus. No phoneme labels.
  2. Multi-task single model. One decoder trained jointly on transcription, translation, voice activity detection, language ID, and timestamping via task tokens.
  3. Standard encoder-decoder transformer. Encoder consumes log-mel spectrograms. Decoder produces text tokens autoregressively. No vocoder, no CTC, no HMM.

The result: Whisper large-v3 is robust across accents, noise, and languages that have zero clean labeled data. It is the default speech front-end for every open-source voice assistant and most commercial ones in 2026.

The idea

Idea 1 · Step 1 — resample + window

Audio at 16 kHz. Clip/pad to 30 seconds. Compute log-mel spectrogram: 80 mel bins (128 in large-v3), 10 ms stride → 3,000 frames × 80 features. This is the "input image" that Whisper sees.

Idea 2 · Step 2 — convolutional stem

Two Conv1D layers with kernel 3 and stride 2 reduce the 3,000 frames to 1,500. Halves sequence length without adding a lot of parameters.

Idea 3 · Step 3 — encoder

A 32-layer (for large) transformer encoder over 1,500 timesteps. Sinusoidal positional encoding, self-attention, GELU FFN. Produces 1,500 × 1,280 hidden states.

Idea 4 · Step 4 — decoder

A 32-layer (for large) transformer decoder. It autoregressively produces tokens from a BPE vocabulary that is a superset of GPT-2's with a few audio-specific special tokens.

Idea 5 · Step 5 — task tokens

The decoder prompt starts with control tokens that tell the model what to do:

<|startoftranscript|>  <|en|>  <|transcribe|>  <|0.00|>

or

<|startoftranscript|>  <|fr|>  <|translate|>   <|0.00|>

The model was trained on this convention. You control task by prefix. The 2026 equivalent of instruction-tuning, but applied to speech.

Idea 6 · Step 6 — output

Beam search (width 5) with a log-prob threshold. Timestamps are predicted every 0.02 seconds of audio when the <|notimestamps|> token is absent.

Idea 7 · Whisper sizes

Model Params Layers d_model Heads VRAM (fp16)
Tiny 39M 4 384 6 ~1 GB

Large-v3-turbo (2024) cut the decoder from 32 layers to 4: much faster decoding for a small loss in accuracy, mostly on translation, which turbo was not trained for. That decode speed unlock is why Whisper-turbo is the default for real-time voice agents in 2026.

Idea 8 · What Whisper does not do

  • No diarization (who is speaking). Pair with pyannote for that.
  • No real-time streaming natively — the 30-second window is fixed. Modern wrappers (faster-whisper, WhisperX) bolt on streaming via VAD + overlap.
  • No long-form context beyond 30 s without external chunking. Works well in practice because human speech rarely needs long-range context for transcription.

The same idea as a formula

From waveform to log-mel. With samples x[n] at 16 kHz, a Hann window w of N=400 samples (25 ms) and hop H=160 (10 ms), the short-time Fourier transform of frame m is

X[m,k]=n=0∑N−1​x[n+mH]w[n]e−2πikn/N,k=0,…,N/2

The power ∣X[m,k]∣2 is pooled by 80 triangular mel filters F (spaced evenly on the mel scale mel(f)=2595log10​(1+f/700), which is roughly linear below 1 kHz and logarithmic above, like hearing), then

S=log10​max(F⊤∣X∣2, 10−10),S←max(S, maxS−8),S←(S+4)/4

(an 80 dB range, scaled to roughly [−1,1]). Thirty seconds give 30×100=3,000 frames.

Encoder. Two 1-D convolutions (kernel 3; stride 1, then 2) turn 80×3000 into d×1500: one position per 20 ms. Sinusoidal positions are added and L encoder blocks attend over all 1,500 positions, 15002=2.25 million scores per head per layer, padding included.

Decoder. A standard causal decoder with cross-attention to the encoder:

p(y∣audio)=t∏​p(yt​∣prompt<|sot|> <|lang|> <|task|> [<|notimestamps|>]​​, y<t​, E)

The task is chosen by which tokens are placed in the prompt; language identification is just the decoder's prediction for the position after <|startoftranscript|>. Timestamp tokens come in steps of 0.02 s, the encoder's resolution: 1,501 of them cover 0.00 to 30.00 s.

Worked example
One clip by hand

A 5.9-second English sentence at 16 kHz:

  • Samples: 5.86×16,000≈93,800, padded with zeros to 480,000 (30 s).
  • Frames: 3,000 of 10 ms, of which about 586 contain speech; after the stride-2 convolution, 1,500 encoder positions, about 293 of them speech.
  • The encoder still computes all 1,500 positions: 15002/2932≈26 times the attention scores the speech alone would need. That fixed 30-second window is the cost that Moonshine (variable-length input) and VAD-based chunking cut.
  • The decoder then runs once per output token, about 20 times for this sentence, each step attending over the 1,500 encoder vectors.
Check your mental model
  • ✗ "Whisper processes the waveform directly."
    ✓ It sees a log-mel spectrogram: 80 (or 128) mel bins every 10 ms, a time–frequency image.
  • ✗ "Short clips are cheaper for the encoder."
    ✓ Every input is padded to 30 seconds, so the encoder always processes 1,500 positions; only the decoder's work shrinks with less speech.
  • ✗ "Translation needs a separate model."
    ✓ The same weights translate into English when the prompt says <|translate|> instead of <|transcribe|> (the last run).

Step 1 · synthesize audio

See code/main.py. We don't train Whisper — we build the log-mel spectrogram pipeline + task-token prompt formatter. Those are the parts you actually touch in production.

Generate a 1-second sine wave at 440 Hz sampled at 16 kHz. 16,000 samples.

Step 2 · log-mel spectrogram (simplified)

Full mel spectrogram needs FFT. We do a simplified framing + per-frame energy version that shows the pipeline without requiring librosa:

def frame_signal(x, frame_size=400, hop=160):
    frames = []
    for start in range(0, len(x) - frame_size + 1, hop):
        frames.append(x[start:start + frame_size])
    return frames

Frame = 25 ms, hop = 10 ms. Matches Whisper's windowing. Per-frame energy stands in for mel bins for pedagogy.

Step 3 · pad to 30 s

Whisper always processes 30-second chunks. Pad (or clip) the spectrogram to 3,000 frames.

Step 4 · build the prompt tokens

def whisper_prompt(lang="en", task="transcribe", timestamps=True):
    tokens = ["<|startoftranscript|>", f"<|{lang}|>", f"<|{task}|>"]
    if not timestamps:
        tokens.append("<|notimestamps|>")
    return tokens

That is the whole task-control surface. A 4-token prefix.

Reading this output

One second gives 98 frames, not 100, because a full 400-sample frame must fit: ⌊(16000−400)/160⌋+1=98. Whisper's own extractor pads the signal by half a window on both sides ("centring"), so it gets exactly 100 per second and 3,000 for 30 s. The energies are identical because a steady sine wave has the same energy in every frame. The prompts show the whole control surface: a language token, a task token and optionally <|notimestamps|>.

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

import io
import numpy as np, soundfile as sf, torch
from datasets import load_dataset, Audio
from transformers import pipeline

# The "meeting.wav" of the original: here a real LibriSpeech clip (openai/whisper is not installed on this page).
libri = load_dataset("hf-internal-testing/librispeech_asr_dummy", "clean", split="validation").cast_column("audio", Audio(decode=False))
speech, sr = sf.read(io.BytesIO(libri[0]["audio"]["bytes"]), dtype="float32")
sf.write("meeting.wav", speech, sr)

asr = pipeline("automatic-speech-recognition", model="openai/whisper-base")
result = asr("meeting.wav", return_timestamps=True, generate_kwargs={"language": "en", "task": "transcribe"})
print(result["text"])
print(result["chunks"][0]["timestamp"])

When to pick Whisper in 2026:

  • Multilingual ASR with one model.
  • Robust transcription of noisy, diverse audio.
  • Research / prototype ASR — fastest starting point.

When to pick something else:

  • Ultra-low latency streaming on edge — Moonshine beats Whisper at matched quality.
  • Real-time conversational AI needing <200 ms — dedicated streaming ASR.
  • Speaker diarization — Whisper does not do this; bolt on pyannote.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from transformers import WhisperFeatureExtractor

fe = WhisperFeatureExtractor.from_pretrained("openai/whisper-base")
ref = fe(speech, sampling_rate=16000, return_tensors="np").input_features[0]      # (80, 3000)

# The same log-mel spectrogram in a few lines of NumPy (mel filter bank taken from the extractor).
x = np.pad(speech, (0, 30 * 16000 - len(speech)))                 # pad to 30 s = 480,000 samples
x = np.pad(x, 200, mode="reflect")                                # centre the 400-sample windows
win = np.hanning(401)[:-1]                                        # periodic Hann window, 25 ms
frames = np.lib.stride_tricks.sliding_window_view(x, 400)[::160]  # hop 160 samples = 10 ms
power = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(frames * win, axis=-1)) ** 2           # (3001, 201) frequency bins
mel = fe.mel_filters.T @ power[:-1].T                              # (80, 3000)
logmel = np.log10(np.maximum(mel, 1e-10))
logmel = np.maximum(logmel, logmel.max() - 8.0)                   # keep an 80 dB range
logmel = (logmel + 4.0) / 4.0                                     # Whisper's fixed scaling
print(f"audio: {len(speech) / 16000:.2f} s -> padded to 30 s -> log-mel {logmel.shape} (mel bins x 10 ms frames)")
print(f"ours vs WhisperFeatureExtractor: max difference {np.abs(logmel - ref).max():.1e}")
print(f"frames that contain speech: about {int(len(speech) / 160)} of 3000; the rest is padding")

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(9, 2.6))
ax.imshow(ref[:, :700], origin="lower", aspect="auto", cmap="magma")
ax.set_xlabel("frame (10 ms each); the first 7 s of the 30 s window"); ax.set_ylabel("mel bin")
ax.set_title(f"What Whisper's encoder sees: \"{libri[0]['text'][:60].capitalize()}...\"", fontsize=9)
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

Reading this output

The dozen lines of NumPy reproduce the official features to 10−5: Whisper's input really is the formula box, a framed, windowed power spectrum pooled into 80 mel bins, logged, clipped to an 80 dB range and scaled. The 5.9-second sentence fills about 585 of the 3,000 frames; everything after it is padding that the encoder still processes. In the picture, voiced sounds show as stacks of horizontal harmonics in the low bins, fricatives such as "s" and "sh" as broad energy high up, and the pauses between words as dark columns. large-v3 uses the same pipeline with 128 mel bins, which is why its feature extractor cannot be swapped with the smaller models'.

One model, three jobs, chosen by tokens

A German sentence synthesised with Meta's MMS text-to-speech, then Whisper-small: the encoder's output shape, the language-token probabilities at the first decoding step, and two generations with prompts that differ only in the task token.

from transformers import VitsModel, AutoTokenizer, WhisperForConditionalGeneration, WhisperProcessor

# German speech for the test, synthesised with Meta's MMS text-to-speech model.
ttok = AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained("facebook/mms-tts-deu")
tts = VitsModel.from_pretrained("facebook/mms-tts-deu").eval()
torch.manual_seed(0)
with torch.no_grad():
    de = tts(**ttok("Das Wetter ist heute schön, deshalb gehen wir am Nachmittag in den Park.", return_tensors="pt")).waveform[0].numpy()
de16 = np.interp(np.arange(0, len(de), tts.config.sampling_rate / 16000), np.arange(len(de)), de).astype("float32")

repo = "openai/whisper-small"
proc = WhisperProcessor.from_pretrained(repo)
wm = WhisperForConditionalGeneration.from_pretrained(repo).eval()
feats = proc(de16, sampling_rate=16000, return_tensors="pt").input_features
with torch.no_grad():
    enc_out = wm.model.encoder(feats).last_hidden_state
print(f"log-mel {tuple(feats.shape)} -> two convolutions (stride 1, then 2) + {wm.config.encoder_layers} encoder layers -> {tuple(enc_out.shape)}")

# Language identification: the decoder's first prediction after <|startoftranscript|>.
lang_ids = {l: proc.tokenizer.convert_tokens_to_ids(f"<|{l}|>") for l in ["de", "en", "nl", "fr", "sv"]}
with torch.no_grad():
    first = wm(input_features=feats, decoder_input_ids=torch.tensor([[wm.config.decoder_start_token_id]])).logits[0, -1]
p = first[list(lang_ids.values())].softmax(-1)
print("language token probabilities (among five):", ", ".join(f"{l} {v:.2f}" for l, v in zip(lang_ids, p)))

for task in ["transcribe", "translate"]:
    with torch.no_grad():
        out = wm.generate(input_features=feats, language="de", task=task, max_new_tokens=60, max_length=None)
    prompt = proc.tokenizer.convert_ids_to_tokens([wm.config.decoder_start_token_id] +
                                                  [i for _, i in proc.get_decoder_prompt_ids(language="de", task=task)])
    print(f"\ndecoder prompt {' '.join(prompt)}\n  -> {proc.tokenizer.decode(out[0], skip_special_tokens=True).strip()}")

Reading this output

The two convolutions turn the 80×3000 log-mel into 1,500 positions of width 768 (Whisper-small), one per 20 ms. Before writing a word, the decoder's first prediction already identifies the language: among the five language tokens, German gets essentially all the probability, which is exactly how detect_language works. The two generations then share everything, the audio, the encoder output and the weights, except one prompt token. With <|transcribe|> the output is German ("Das Wetter ist, heute schön…", with one stray comma); with <|translate|> it is a fluent English translation, "The weather is beautiful today, so we go to the park in the afternoon." Task selection by prompt tokens is the speech version of what instruction-tuned language models do with text prompts.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Whisper is the open baseline. large-v3 and large-v3-turbo run in production through faster-whisper (CTranslate2), whisper.cpp and transformers; NVIDIA's Parakeet and Canary models lead several English and multilingual benchmarks on the Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard.
  • Hosted speech-to-text moved to LLM-based models, for example OpenAI's gpt-4o-transcribe (2025), while open audio–language models such as Qwen2-Audio and Mistral's Voxtral reuse a Whisper-style encoder in front of a language model.
  • Pipelines around the model. Production systems add VAD (Silero), diarisation (pyannote) and word timestamps (WhisperX) around the recogniser (Phase 6).

Sources: Whisper, faster-whisper, whisper.cpp, Open ASR Leaderboard, OpenAI audio models, Voxtral.

Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Input: a log-mel spectrogram, 80 or 128 bins every 10 ms, always 30 s (3,000 frames).
  • A convolutional stem halves it to 1,500 positions; an encoder–decoder transformer does the rest.
  • Task, language and timestamps are chosen by tokens in the decoder's prompt; language ID is the first prediction.
  • The fixed 30 s window wastes encoder work on short clips; wrappers and newer models chunk or cut it.

Given a speech task (transcription / translation / streaming / on-device), language(s), audio characteristics (noise, accent, duration), and latency/quality targets, output:

1. Model choice. One of: faster-whisper large-v3-turbo (default production), whisper large-v3 (highest quality, multilingual), whisper medium (mid-tier), Moonshine base (edge), distil-whisper (2× faster English). One-sentence reason.
2. Quantization. int8_float16 (CPU default), float16 (GPU default), fp32 (research). Flag VRAM impact.
3. Decoding. Beam width (5 typical, 1 for streaming), temperature fallback schedule, log-prob threshold, no-speech threshold, VAD gate on/off.
4. Chunking. 30 s fixed window vs streaming chunks (typically 10 s with 2 s overlap) + VAD-based segmentation. Document post-merge strategy for overlaps.
5. Post-processing. Timestamp alignment (WhisperX forced alignment), punctuation restoration, diarization (pyannote). Flag which are required by the task.

Refuse to recommend plain OpenAI Whisper (reference implementation) for production — `faster-whisper` is 4× faster with identical outputs. Refuse to ship streaming ASR without VAD unless documented reason. Flag any single-speaker assumption when the input is likely multi-speaker.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · German text, then English text

Before generating, Whisper is essentially certain the audio is German: the language token <|de|> takes practically all the probability among the five checked. With <|transcribe|> it writes German, "Das Wetter ist, heute schön, deshalb gehen wir am Nachmittag in den Park." (one spurious comma); with <|translate|> it writes English, "The weather is beautiful today, so we go to the park in the afternoon." One token in the prompt switches the task; the audio, the encoder output and the weights are the same.

SolutionB1 · Slowed-down audio

Whisper assumes every sample array is at 16 kHz. Samples recorded at 44.1 kHz, read as 16 kHz, last 44.1/16=2.76 times longer and every frequency drops by the same factor: a voice at 200 Hz becomes 73 Hz, slow and deep. The spectrogram is stretched in time and squashed towards the low mel bins, nothing like the training data, so the transcript degrades into errors or hallucinated text; a long recording also spills over the 30 s window. Always resample to 16 kHz (librosa.resample, torchaudio.functional.resample) and pass sampling_rate=16000, which makes transformers check it.
