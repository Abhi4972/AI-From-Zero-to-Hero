In plain words first
The idea in one line

Neural nets do not consume raw waveforms well. They consume spectrograms. They consume mel spectrograms even better. Every ASR, TTS, and audio classifier in 2026 lives or dies by this single preprocessing choice.

The problem

In plain words first

A raw recording is 16,000 numbers a second, and the same word said twice gives completely different numbers. What stays similar is which frequencies are loud, moment by moment. A spectrogram measures exactly that: cut the sound into short overlapping slices (frames), take the Fourier transform of each, and stack the results into a picture with time across and frequency up. Speech models then make two adjustments that match hearing. They group frequencies into bands that are narrow at the low end and wide at the high end (the mel scale), and they take the logarithm, because loudness is heard on a ratio scale. The result, the log-mel spectrogram, is what Whisper and most other speech models read. This lesson builds each step, checks the result against Whisper's own feature extractor, and measures the trade-offs: frame length against time and frequency detail, and log-mel against MFCC.

Take a 10-second 16 kHz clip. That is 160,000 floats, all in [-1, 1], almost perfectly uncorrelated with the label "dog barking" or "the word cat". The raw waveform has the information but in a form the model cannot easily extract. Two identical phonemes spoken 100 ms apart have completely different raw samples.

A spectrogram fixes this. It collapses the temporal detail where human perception ignores it (microsecond jitter) and preserves the structure where perception attends (which frequencies are energetic, over time windows of ~10–25 ms).

Mel spectrograms push further. Human pitch judgments are not linear in Hz: the same step in Hz sounds smaller and smaller as frequency rises. The mel scale, built from listening experiments in the 1930s, is roughly linear below about 1 kHz and roughly logarithmic above, and spectrograms warp their frequency axis to match it. A mel-scaled spectrogram is the single most important feature in speech ML from 2010 through 2026.

The idea

STFT (Short-Time Fourier Transform). Slice the waveform into overlapping frames (typical: 25 ms window, 10 ms hop = 400 samples / 160 samples at 16 kHz). Multiply each frame by a window function (Hann is the default; Hamming slightly different tradeoff). FFT each frame. Stack the magnitude spectra into a matrix of shape (n_frames, n_freq_bins). That is your spectrogram.

Log-magnitude. Raw magnitudes span 5-6 orders of magnitude. Take log(|X| + 1e-6) or 20 * log10(|X|) to compress dynamic range. Every production pipeline uses log-magnitude, not raw magnitude.

Mel scale. Frequency f in Hz maps to mel m by m=2595⋅log10​(1+f/700). The mapping is roughly linear below 1 kHz and roughly logarithmic above. 80 mel bins covering 0–8 kHz is the standard ASR input.

Mel filterbank. A set of triangular filters spaced equally on the mel scale. Each filter is a weighted sum of adjacent FFT bins. Multiplying the STFT magnitude by the filterbank matrix gives the mel spectrogram in one matmul.

Log-mel spectrogram. log(mel_spec + 1e-10). Whisper, NVIDIA's Parakeet models and Meta's SeamlessM4T all start from log-mel or closely related log filterbank features. It is the standard speech front end.

MFCCs. Take the log-mel spectrogram, apply a DCT (type II), keep the first 13 coefficients. Decorrelates the features and compresses further. Dominant feature until about 2015 when CNNs/Transformers on raw log-mels caught up. Still used where compute is tiny (keyword spotting) and in classic speaker recognition: Kaldi's x-vector recipes use MFCCs, while ECAPA-TDNN usually takes log-mel filterbanks.

Resolution trade. Larger FFT = better frequency resolution but worse time resolution. 25 ms / 10 ms is the audio-ML default; 50 ms / 12.5 ms for music; 5 ms / 2 ms for transient detection (drum hits, plosives).

The same idea as a formula

STFT. With window w of length N and hop H, frame m and frequency bin k:

X[m,k]=n=0∑N−1​x[mH+n]w[n]e−2πikn/N,k=0,…,N/2

A signal of T samples gives 1+⌊(T−N)/H⌋ frames. The power spectrogram is P[m,k]=∣X[m,k]∣2.

Mel scale (the HTK formula used here) and its inverse:

mel(f)=2595log10​(1+700f​),f(mel)=700(10mel/2595−1)

Its slope, dfdmel​=(700+f)ln102595​, is 1.61 mel per Hz at 0 Hz and 0.24 at 4 kHz: low frequencies are stretched, high ones compressed.

Filterbank. Place M+2 points evenly in mel between fmin​ and fmax​, convert back to Hz (f0​,…,fM+1​), and build triangle m rising from fm−1​ to a peak at fm​ and falling to fm+1​:

Hm​(f)=max(0,min(fm​−fm−1​f−fm−1​​, fm+1​−fm​fm+1​−f​))

Stacking Hm​ at the bin frequencies into a matrix F (M×(N/2+1)), the mel spectrogram is one product, S=PF⊤, and the log-mel is log(S+ϵ).

MFCC. A DCT-II across the M log-mel values of each frame, keeping the first J (usually 13):

cj​=m=0∑M−1​logSm​cos(Mπj(m+21​)​)

c0​ is the overall log energy; low cj​ describe the broad shape of the spectrum (the vocal tract's resonances), high cj​ fine ripple.

Time against frequency. A window of N samples spans N/fs​ seconds and gives bins fs​/N Hz apart. A Hann window's main peak is 4 bins wide, so two tones are separated only if they are about 2 bins or more apart. Finer frequency detail needs longer windows, which blur events in time: Δt⋅Δf cannot be made small in both.

Worked example
Frames, mel spacing and an empty filter by hand

Frames. 10 s at 16 kHz with N=400, H=160: 1+⌊(160000−400)/160⌋=1+997=998 frames, about 100 per second.

Mel points. mel(8000)=2595log10​(1+8000/700)=2595×1.0944=2840 mel. For 80 filters, 82 points are 2840/81=35.1 mel apart.

How wide is 35 mel in Hz? Near 0 Hz, 35.1/1.61=22 Hz. Near 4 kHz, 35.1/0.24=146 Hz. With n_fft = 400 at 16 kHz the FFT bins are 16000/400=40 Hz apart, so at the bottom of the scale two neighbouring mel points can round to the same FFT bin. A triangle whose edges are the same bin has no width, and the filter is all zeros: the run under Step 5 finds four of them. Fixes: fractional triangle edges (what librosa does), fewer mels, or a longer FFT.

Numbers per second. Waveform 16,000; STFT 201×100=20,100; 80 log-mels 8,000; 13 MFCCs 1,300.

Check your mental model
  • ✗ "The spectrogram throws away information, so models should use raw audio."
    ✓ The magnitude spectrogram drops phase, which matters little for recognising speech; models that learn from raw audio (wav2vec 2.0) learn something spectrogram-like in their first layers anyway.
  • ✗ "More mel bins are always better."
    ✓ Each mel bin must cover at least one FFT bin. With a 400-point FFT, 80 bins already leave the lowest filters narrower than an FFT bin.
  • ✗ "Log-mel is log-mel."
    ✓ Log base, the floor value, clipping and normalisation all differ between toolkits. A model must see exactly the features it was trained on.

Step 1 · frame the waveform

def frame(signal, frame_len, hop):
    n = 1 + (len(signal) - frame_len) // hop
    return [signal[i * hop : i * hop + frame_len] for i in range(n)]

A 10-second 16 kHz clip with frame_len=400, hop=160 yields 998 frames.

Step 2 · Hann window

import math

def hann(N):
    return [0.5 * (1 - math.cos(2 * math.pi * n / (N - 1))) for n in range(N)]

Multiply element-wise before the FFT. Removes spectral leakage caused by truncating at non-zero endpoints.

This is the symmetric Hann window (zero at both ends). torch.stft and librosa use the periodic form, hann(N + 1)[:-1], which repeats perfectly when frames overlap; the difference is tiny but shows up when you compare against a reference implementation.

Step 3 · STFT magnitude

def stft_magnitude(signal, frame_len=400, hop=160):
    win = hann(frame_len)
    frames = frame(signal, frame_len, hop)
    return [dft_mag([w * s for w, s in zip(win, f)]) for f in frames]   # main.py's dft_mag: |DFT|, bins 0..N/2

Production uses torch.stft or librosa.stft (FFT-backed, vectorized). The loop here is pedagogical; it runs on short clips in code/main.py.

The repository's version called magnitudes(dft(...)); main.py provides the same computation as dft_mag, which returns only bins 0…N/2.

Step 4 · mel filterbank

def hz_to_mel(f):
    return 2595.0 * math.log10(1.0 + f / 700.0)

def mel_to_hz(m):
    return 700.0 * (10 ** (m / 2595.0) - 1)

def mel_filterbank(n_mels, n_fft, sr, fmin=0, fmax=None):
    fmax = fmax or sr / 2
    mels = [hz_to_mel(fmin) + (hz_to_mel(fmax) - hz_to_mel(fmin)) * i / (n_mels + 1)
            for i in range(n_mels + 2)]
    hzs = [mel_to_hz(m) for m in mels]
    bins = [int(h * n_fft / sr) for h in hzs]
    fb = [[0.0] * (n_fft // 2 + 1) for _ in range(n_mels)]
    for m in range(n_mels):
        for k in range(bins[m], bins[m + 1]):
            fb[m][k] = (k - bins[m]) / max(1, bins[m + 1] - bins[m])
        for k in range(bins[m + 1], bins[m + 2]):
            fb[m][k] = (bins[m + 2] - k) / max(1, bins[m + 2] - bins[m + 1])
    return fb

80 mels covering 0–8 kHz with n_fft=400 gives an (80, 201) matrix. Multiply the (n_frames, 201) STFT magnitude by the transpose to get (n_frames, 80) mel spectrogram.

Step 5 · log-mel

def log_mel(mel_spec, eps=1e-10):
    return [[math.log(max(v, eps)) for v in frame] for frame in mel_spec]

Common alternatives: librosa.power_to_db (reference-normalized dB), 10 * log10(power + eps). Whisper uses a more involved clip + normalize routine (see Whisper's log_mel_spectrogram).

def chirp_fixed(f0, f1, sr, seconds, amp=0.5):
    """Linear sweep with the right phase: the frequency is the derivative of phase / 2π."""

The tone's peak lands at exactly 2,000 Hz, and the filterbank widths grow from 1–2 FFT bins at the bottom to 9–12 at the top: mel spacing in action. But the chirp's peak mel bin rises to 39 and then falls back to 16. The corrected run explains it: main.py computes sin(2πf(t)t) with f(t) rising linearly, and the instantaneous frequency of that is the derivative of the phase, f0​+2(f1​−f0​)t/T, so the sweep really goes to 7,800 Hz. Everything above 4 kHz (the Nyquist frequency at 8 kHz) aliases back down: the same folding as Lesson 6.1. With the phase computed as 2π(f0​t+(f1​−f0​)t2/(2T)) the peak rises steadily to about 3.8 kHz.

import numpy as np
import soundfile as sf
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from datasets import load_dataset, Audio

libri = load_dataset("hf-internal-testing/librispeech_asr_dummy", "clean", split="validation").cast_column("audio", Audio(decode=False))
def speech_clip(i):
    wav, sr = sf.read(io.BytesIO(libri[i]["audio"]["bytes"]), dtype="float32")
    return wav, sr, libri[i]["text"]

wav, sr, text = speech_clip(1)
N_FFT, HOP = 400, 160                                       # 25 ms window, 10 ms hop at 16 kHz
win = np.hanning(N_FFT + 1)[:-1]                            # "periodic" Hann, as torch.stft and librosa use
frames = np.lib.stride_tricks.sliding_window_view(wav, N_FFT)[::HOP]
power = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(frames * win, axis=1)) ** 2       # (n_frames, 201)
import librosa
fb_lesson = np.array(mel_filterbank(80, N_FFT, sr))          # the lesson's filterbank, (80, 201)
empty = np.where(fb_lesson.sum(1) == 0)[0]

The lesson's filterbank matches librosa's HTK-formula filterbank closely on average (mean difference 0.005) but not exactly: single entries differ by up to 1.0, where a snapped triangle puts its full peak on a different bin, because it snaps triangle corners to whole bins. librosa's default is a different design (Slaney's, which Whisper also uses): triangles normalised to equal area, so peaks are small numbers, not 1. The Whisper recipe, centred STFT with reflect padding, power spectrum, Slaney mel filters, log10​, clip to 8 below the maximum, (x+4)/4, padded to 30 seconds (3,000 frames), reproduces the reference to four decimal places. Change any one of those details and the numbers move: this is the "dB vs log" and "normalisation drift" pitfall made concrete.

Step 6 · MFCCs

def dct_ii(x, n_coeffs):
    N = len(x)
    return [
        sum(x[n] * math.cos(math.pi * k * (2 * n + 1) / (2 * N)) for n in range(N))
        for k in range(n_coeffs)
    ]

Apply DCT to each log-mel frame, keep the first 13 coefficients. That is your MFCC matrix. The first coefficient is usually dropped (it encodes overall energy).

Why MFCCs, and what the window length costs

Correlation between features over 40 utterances, for 80 log-mels and for 13 MFCCs. Then a test signal, two tones 40 Hz apart plus a click, through windows of 5, 25 and 100 ms.

# Why MFCCs: neighbouring log-mel bins move together; DCT coefficients much less so.
feats_mel, feats_mfcc = [], []
for i in range(40):
    w, _, _ = speech_clip(i)
    fr = np.lib.stride_tricks.sliding_window_view(w, 400)[::160]
    lm = np.log((np.abs(np.fft.rfft(fr * win, axis=1)) ** 2) @ fb.T + 1e-10)
    feats_mel.append(lm); feats_mfcc.append(lm @ C.T)
def mean_offdiag_corr(X):
    R = np.corrcoef(X, rowvar=False); k = R.shape[0]
    return np.abs(R[~np.eye(k, dtype=bool)]).mean(), np.abs(np.diag(R, 1)).mean()
for name, X in [("log-mel, 80 bins", np.vstack(feats_mel)), ("MFCC, 13 coefficients", np.vstack(feats_mfcc))]:
    allc, adj = mean_offdiag_corr(X)
    print(f"{name:22s} mean |correlation| between features {allc:.2f}, between neighbours {adj:.2f}")

# Time versus frequency resolution: a click and two tones 40 Hz apart, three window lengths.
sr16 = 16000; n = np.arange(sr16)
sig = np.sin(2 * np.pi * 1000 * n / sr16) + np.sin(2 * np.pi * 1040 * n / sr16)
sig[8000] += 30.0                                                          # a click at 0.5 s
for ms in [5, 25, 100]:
    L = int(sr16 * ms / 1000); h = L // 4
    S = np.abs(np.fft.rfft(np.lib.stride_tricks.sliding_window_view(sig, L)[::h] * np.hanning(L), axis=1))
    col = S[len(S) // 4]                                                   # a frame away from the click
    k1, k2, km = round(1000 * L / sr16), round(1040 * L / sr16), round(1020 * L / sr16)
    resolved = L / sr16 * 40 >= 2 and col[km] < 0.7 * min(col[k1], col[k2])
    hf = S[:, int(6000 * L / sr16):].sum(1)                                # energy above 6 kHz: only the click has any
    spread_ms = (hf > 0.1 * hf.max()).sum() * h / sr16 * 1000
    print(f"window {ms:3d} ms: bins {sr16 / L:5.1f} Hz apart -> 1000/1040 Hz {'separate' if resolved else 'merged'}; "
          f"click smeared over about {spread_ms:.0f} ms")

window   5 ms: bins 200.0 Hz apart -> 1000/1040 Hz merged; click smeared over about 4 ms
window  25 ms: bins  40.0 Hz apart -> 1000/1040 Hz merged; click smeared over about 19 ms
window 100 ms: bins  10.0 Hz apart -> 1000/1040 Hz separate; click smeared over about 75 ms
Reading this output

Neighbouring log-mel bins correlate at 0.96 on average: they carry nearly the same information, which mattered for older models (Gaussian mixtures with diagonal covariances) that assume independent features. After the DCT, the average correlation between MFCCs is 0.17. Neural networks cope with correlated inputs, which is one reason log-mels took over. The second half is the resolution trade in numbers: a 5 ms window places the click within about 4 ms but cannot separate tones 40 Hz apart; 25 ms, the speech default, still merges them (bins 40 Hz apart, main peak 4 bins wide) and smears the click over about 19 ms; 100 ms separates the tones and smears the click over 75 ms. Speech sits in the middle because phonemes last tens of milliseconds and harmonics are 100 Hz or more apart.

Use it: the same idea with real libraries

The 2026 stack:

Task Features
ASR (Whisper, Parakeet, SeamlessM4T) 80 log-mels (128 in Whisper large-v3), 10 ms hop, 25 ms window
TTS acoustic model (VITS, F5-TTS, Kokoro) 80–100 mels (F5-TTS: 100 at 24 kHz), hop about 10–12 ms
Audio classification (AST, PANNs, BEATs) 128 log-mels for AST and BEATs, 64 for PANNs; 10 ms hop
Speaker embedding (ECAPA-TDNN, WavLM) 80 log-mels or raw-waveform SSL
Music generation (MusicGen, Stable Audio) Learned codec latents, not mels: EnCodec tokens (MusicGen), a continuous autoencoder latent (Stable Audio)
Keyword spotting 10–40 MFCCs for tiny devices

Rule of thumb: if you are not working on music, start with 80 log-mels. The burden of proof is on any deviation.

Industry practice
What's current (2024–2026)
  • Whisper's front end is the de facto standard. Whisper large-v3 and its distilled and "turbo" variants read 128 log-mel bins at 16 kHz with a 25 ms window and 10 ms hop; many open ASR and audio-language models reuse WhisperFeatureExtractor unchanged so that they can start from Whisper's encoder.
  • Use the model's own extractor. Hugging Face transformers ships a feature extractor with every speech model (Whisper, wav2vec 2.0, Parakeet through NeMo), and production code calls it rather than re-implementing the recipe.

Sources: Whisper audio.py, Whisper large-v3 model card, WhisperFeatureExtractor docs.

Pitfalls that still ship in 2026

  • Mel count mismatch. Training with 80 mels, inference with 128 mels. Silent failure. Log the feature shape at both ends.
  • Sample-rate mismatch upstream. Mels computed at 22.05 kHz look different from 16 kHz. Fix SR before featurization.
  • dB vs log. Whisper uses log10 of the mel power, clipped to 8 below the maximum and mapped with (x + 4) / 4. Natural-log or dB features differ by a constant factor and a different normalisation. Use the model's own feature extractor, as the run under Step 5 shows.
  • Normalization drift. Per-utterance normalization during training, global normalization during inference. A production bug that can sharply raise WER.
  • Leakage from padding. Zero-padding the end of a clip produces a flat spectrum in the trailing frames. Pad symmetrically or replicate.
Recap
What to keep from this lesson
  • Spectrogram = frames (25 ms, 10 ms hop) × Hann window × FFT, magnitude or power.
  • Mel filterbank = triangles evenly spaced in mel; one matrix product turns the STFT into a mel spectrogram; then take the log.
  • MFCC = DCT of log-mel, first 13 coefficients; decorrelated and compact, now mostly for tiny devices.
  • Longer windows resolve frequency, shorter windows resolve time; speech uses 25 ms.
  • Feature details (mel formula, normalisation, log base, clipping, padding) must match the model exactly: use its own extractor.

Solutions

SolutionP1 · Up, then down

No. The peak rises to the top of the scale and then falls back, because the chirp's phase formula doubles its frequency sweep: it really reaches 7,800 Hz, and everything above 4 kHz aliases down. Computing the phase as the integral of the frequency, 2π(f0​t+(f1​−f0​)t2/(2T)), gives the intended 200 Hz to 4 kHz sweep.

SolutionB1 · More mels than the FFT can feed

21 of the 128 filters are all zeros. With 128 mels the points are 2840/129=22 mel apart, about 14 Hz at the bottom of the scale against 40 Hz between FFT bins, so many low triangles collapse onto a single bin. Their log-mel rows are a constant floor value, identical in every frame: wasted inputs at best, a train/test mismatch at worst. Whisper's filterbank (librosa's Slaney design) has none empty: it spaces filters linearly below 1 kHz, about 23 Hz apart here, and gives every triangle fractional edges, so each one still overlaps part of an FFT bin. (librosa's HTK version at 128 mels leaves 4 empty and prints a warning.)
